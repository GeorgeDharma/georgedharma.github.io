The research question

Were shares in state-controlled banks more resilient around MSCI announcements?

I compared Indonesian central-state-controlled banks with other listed conventional banks around the January and October 2026 announcements.

01 · Main finding

The answer depends
on the comparison.

The study did not find consistently smaller observed share-return losses for state-controlled banks. Relative abnormal-return differences were positive with the Jakarta benchmark and negative with the Singapore benchmark.

02 · Approach

From market data
to a tested comparison.

  1. 01
    Build the samples. Compare 36 banks in January and 39 in October, including five central-state-controlled bank securities in each sample.
  2. 02
    Measure the response. Separate observed return sums from cumulative abnormal returns, using the Jakarta index, Singapore index and a combined model. Adjust for bank size using log total assets.
  3. 03
    Challenge the result. Check alternative event windows, individual-bank influence, overlapping announcements and assumptions about uncertainty.

03 · Interpretation

Evidence with
clear limits.

  • Benchmark and event-window choices can change the interpretation.
  • The state-controlled group contains only five securities, including linked ownership between AGRO and BRI.
  • The October announcement coincided with trading-rule changes, limiting attribution to the MSCI event.
  • Uncertainty estimates depend on stated assumptions. The analysis is exploratory and does not establish a causal benefit of state control.

Master’s research

Abstract

International Finance

This thesis investigates whether shareholders of banks under Indonesian central-state control were more resilient than shareholders of other listed conventional banks around the January 27 and October 15, 2026 MSCI announcements. The exploratory event study covers 36 banks in January and 39 in October, including five state-controlled bank securities in each sample. Shareholder resilience is assessed using observed sums of daily returns and cumulative abnormal returns (CARs) based on the Jakarta Composite Index (JKSE), Singapore’s Straits Times Index (STI), and their combination. Comparisons are also adjusted for bank size using the natural logarithm of pre-event total assets.

After adjusting for bank size, the observed-return difference between state-controlled banks and other banks was +0.02 percentage points in January and −3.77 percentage points in October over the primary three-day windows. All differences are state banks minus other banks. The size-adjusted CAR differences were +6.91 and +2.85 percentage points under JKSE, and −0.27 and −3.46 percentage points under STI. January’s positive JKSE and combined-model differences were supported by the 95% uncertainty ranges under the historical-variance assumption, but became less clear when event-period innovation variance was doubled. October’s CAR comparisons remained uncertain under every variance scenario examined.

The primary-window results therefore do not show that state-controlled banks consistently experienced smaller share-return losses. The interpretation depends on the return benchmark and event window. The study is exploratory and does not establish a causal benefit of state control for shareholder resilience.
