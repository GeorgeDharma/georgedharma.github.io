Project 05 / Portfolio construction · Risk management

Equity volatility targeting Trading frequency & drawdown

I tested how frequently a US equity and Treasury bill portfolio needs to trade to pursue a 10% annual volatility target.

Analysis by George Dharma · US daily research returns · 2006–2025 · 5,031 trading sessions

Read the analysis ↓Project files ↓

How much trading does the target need?

I would use the five-point band when controlling turnover and drawdown matters most. It waits for a five-percentage-point gap between actual and desired equity weights. The portfolio traded materially less while its worst drawdown stayed close to daily targeting.

One signal, three trading rules

Each rule estimates volatility from 63 equity returns ending two sessions earlier. The desired equity allocation is capped at 100%, cash earns the Treasury bill return and costs are charged to equity trades. I also tested fixed 60% equity and fully invested benchmarks.

What held up across decades?

Band turnover was about 108% in each half of the sample. Its growth advantage over daily targeting appeared in the second decade. A lagged signal also leaves the portfolio exposed when volatility changes suddenly.
