Project 02 / M&A · Transaction modelling

DSV / SchenkerAcquisition financing & value creation

I reconstructed DSV’s acquisition from the information available at signing. The investment case turns on how much of the freight network savings the buyer can deliver and retain.

September 2024 signing case · Analyst-modelled savings and purchase accounting

Read the analysis ↓Project files ↓

What would justify the signing price?

I would support the price if DSV could retain about €600m of annual savings by year five. The base plan delivers €700m. If it delivers 85% of that plan and savings then erode by 5% a year, buyer NPV falls to −€28m.

How I valued the transaction

I valued Schenker’s standalone operations at €12.21bn, then modelled the savings separately so they can fade as customers renegotiate or staff leave. The analysis reserves the full €1bn German investment programme in addition to ordinary capex and links financing to a combined earnings forecast.

What I would monitor

Adjusted earnings can rise even as the buyer loses value, as the downside case illustrates. I would track savings retained after customer discounts and replacement costs. The stress case also needs €0.78bn beyond the assumed revolving facility by year three.
