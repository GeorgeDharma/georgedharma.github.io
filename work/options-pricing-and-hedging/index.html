Project 10 / Python · Equity derivatives

European putOption pricing & hedge rebalancing

I priced a European put and simulated a desk short 100 contracts. I tested how far its stock hedge could drift before the saving in trading costs came with a material increase in tail losses.

One-year short option book · 30,000 simulated paths per scenario · Costs of 5 bp

Read the analysis ↓Project files ↓

When should the desk trade?

I would rebalance when delta moves by 2.5 points. For this book, that means the desired hedge differs from actual holdings by 250 shares. At that point the desk trades back to its full theoretical hedge.

Putting a price on fewer trades

I tracked the option premium, stock position, financing and execution costs to cash settlement. Each of five hedge policies faced identical stock paths. The base simulation uses 20% implied and realised volatility. Separate scenarios increase realised volatility or introduce price jumps.

Where rebalancing stops helping

With 30% realised volatility and the option still priced at 20%, the mean loss in the worst 5% of paths reaches $34,979 under the 2.5-point band. With jumps it reaches $57,059. A tighter trigger can change execution costs, but a price gap exposes the desk between trades.
