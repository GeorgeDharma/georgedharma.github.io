Pricing a share-exchange collar before a merger closes

Calculate seller proceeds and buyer share issuance throughout a collar.

Theory and terminology

Merger exchange-ratio collars

fixed value · fixed exchange ratio · caps and floors

What you should be able to explain

  • Calculate seller proceeds and buyer share issuance throughout a collar.
  • Identify which shareholders bear a price move in each interval.

Two promises a buyer might make

A fixed exchange ratio promises a number of buyer shares for each target share. Its value floats with the buyer’s market price from signing to closing. A fixed-value promise changes the share count to deliver an agreed amount. That gives sellers price certainty but can force the buyer to issue many shares after a price fall. Existing buyer owners then keep a smaller stake in the combined business.

A fixed-value collar limits that adjustment. At the lower buyer-price boundary the contract sets the largest share issuance. At the upper boundary it sets the smallest. Economics change at these two points. Plot seller value on the vertical axis against the buyer price on the horizontal axis. A flat middle and two rising outer segments should emerge.

Read the pricing clause into the model

An agreement may use a multi-day average, identify the exchange, set the observation dates and specify rounding. An average can differ from the quoted price on delivery day. Calculate the number of shares using the contract’s reference price. To estimate what the seller can then sell them for, use the actual closing market price.

The clause can also give a party a right to walk away or renegotiate. That changes the probability of closing and cannot be represented by simply extending a line on the graph. The examples assume completion, one class of common shares, and no cash component or walk-away rights.

Show both boards what moves

A seller board compares proceeds per target share with the standalone alternative. A buyer board examines the new ownership percentage, voting dilution and the number of shares it must authorize. These are separate from earnings accretion. More shares can be affordable if the business acquired earns enough, while a low relative issuance can still purchase a disappointing asset.

Model one price below, at and above each boundary. At the two boundaries the middle and outer formulas must meet. A jump usually signals that the price bounds and exchange-ratio bounds were paired in the wrong order.

Method

Build the result

r = min(K/L, max(K/U, K/P)); proceeds per target share = r × S.

K is the agreed currency amount per target share, L and U are the lower and upper buyer reference prices, P is the actual reference price, S is the buyer price on delivery and r is buyer shares delivered per target share. All prices use the same currency.

  1. Divide the promised value by the reference price to obtain the unconstrained ratio.
  2. Floor that ratio at K/U and cap it at K/L. A high price buys fewer shares.
  3. Multiply the final ratio by each target share count for issuance and by the delivery-day price for value.
Worked case

The board tests three closing prices

A buyer with 100 million shares promises $48 per share for 10 million target shares. The buyer-price corridor is $40–$60. In each state the reference and delivery prices coincide.

Working

  1. The maximum ratio is 48/40 = 1.20. The minimum is 48/60 = 0.80.
  2. At $32, issue 12 million shares. Each seller share receives 1.20 × $32 = $38.40. Former target owners hold 12/112 = 10.71%.
  3. At $50 the ratio is 48/50 = 0.96. Issue 9.6 million shares worth $48 per target share. Former target owners hold 9.6/109.6 = 8.76%.
  4. At $75 the floor fixes issuance at 8 million shares. Each target share receives $60. The sellers hold 8/108 = 7.41%.

Decision and interpretation

A seller requiring a guaranteed $45 sees a shortfall in the $32 state. A board could seek a cash top-up, which would add a financing requirement.

Quick check
With a $30 fixed-value offer, buyer-price bounds of $20 and $25, and a reference and delivery price of $16, what does each target share receive?

The maximum ratio is 30/20 = 1.50. Below the corridor the ratio stops growing, leaving 1.50 × $16 = $24.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A different buyer targets €36 with price boundaries of €24 and €30. Reference and delivery prices are equal. Calculate proceeds at €20, €28.80 and €36.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The allowed ratio ranges from 36/30 = 1.20 to 36/24 = 1.50.
  2. At €20 the cap gives 1.50 × 20 = €30. At €28.80 the ratio is 1.25 and proceeds are €36.
  3. At €36 the floor gives 1.20 × 36 = €43.20. The target participates in the buyer’s upside above €30.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How much protection does an offer of “$48 in buyer shares” give the seller?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The contract defines a reference share price and a permitted range of exchange ratios. Inside this collar the buyer adjusts its share count to deliver $48. Outside the range the share count stops adjusting, so the seller receives less or more as the buyer’s share price moves.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if the reference is €28.80 but the buyer closes at €26 once the ratio has been fixed?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The agreed reference fixes 1.25 shares. Their market value at delivery is €32.50. The fixing-to-delivery price change creates a €3.50 shortfall against the headline amount.
