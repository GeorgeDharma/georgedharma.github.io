Standalone value and control

Value the target before combining it with the buyer. Separate improvements available to any new controller from gains available only to this particular combination.

The starting point

Start by imagining the target tomorrow with its current strategy and no offer. The present value of that expected cash is its standalone value under existing management. A buyer can ask what any competent new owner could improve, such as collecting overdue invoices.

A buyer-specific benefit requires this buyer’s separate assets or relationships. Splitting the two sources matters in an auction. Several bidders may be able to release overdue cash, while only one owns the distribution route needed for a particular delivery saving.

Theory and method

Probability-weighted value of corporate control

Status quo valuation · Optimal control value

A target’s share price already reflects takeover speculation. How much control value can a buyer legitimately add to that price?

A poorly run depot may be worth €40m if its present plan continues and €50m if any competent owner implements a turnaround. A €43m traded value can reflect a market that already assigns some chance to the turnaround. Adding the full €10m improvement to that observed value would count part of the same change twice. Buyer-specific distribution gains must be valued separately because another controller might achieve the operating turnaround without having the buyer’s network.

How the result is built

M = V₀ + p(V₁ − V₀). Remaining control uplift to V₁ = (1 − p)(V₁ − V₀)

M is observed equity value, V₀ equity under unchanged policy, V₁ equity under improved policy and p the probability weight on improvement. All values are in the same currency at one date.

  1. Begin with two possible equity payoffs already expressed in comparable present values. Weight unchanged policy by 1 − p and improved policy by p.
  2. Collect the terms. The observed premium over V₀ equals p times the full policy improvement. If the two policy values differ, the implied weight is (M − V₀)/(V₁ − V₀).
  3. Subtract M from V₁ to find the improvement the market has not already capitalized. Add buyer-specific benefits only where they are incremental to both policy scenarios.

When the reasoning holds

  • The two values use consistent risk treatment and exhaust the modeled states. A financing shock, private information or unrelated mispricing can make the implied p misleading.
  • An improved policy must be feasible after implementation costs. An asset sale already assumed under the current plan belongs in V₀.

Terms in this lesson
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Enterprise value
Value of the business operations attributable to its capital providers. A bridge adjusts it to the amount due to shareholders.
How to read the notation
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.

All notation examples

First example

A target is worth €40m under current management. Any new owner could release €4m from idle assets. Your routes add another €6m after integration costs. Your no-gain bid is €50m. A rival without the routes reaches only €44m on these assumptions.

Value three different versions of the company

Start with cash the target can distribute under its current policies. Next value it under realistic changes any capable owner could make. Finally estimate extra cash requiring this particular buyer’s technology, distribution or assets. Give each improvement a home in exactly one version.

Control value is the difference between the first two valuations when control enables the improvements. The economic gain is the change in cash or risk, not the appearance of the word control in the agreement. If current managers are already implementing the changes, their benefit belongs in the original forecast.

Connect forecasts to the type of claim

A value based on cash available to shareholders is an equity value. A value based on operations before financing is an enterprise value. Compare a proposed share purchase with equity value. To use operating value, adjust for debt, eligible cash and other claims consistently.

One common shortcut multiplies a target’s share price by a historical control premium. It conceals why a premium might exist and can double count expectations already priced by the market. A concrete forecast identifies which changes are achievable, who can implement them and why a competitor might value the target differently.

Competing bidders set the bargaining range

A buyer-specific ceiling is the highest price leaving that buyer with zero gain under its assumptions. A rival with fewer unique benefits may still bid strongly for improvements available to everybody. A seller’s alternatives affect the likely price between the competing valuations.

The greatest theoretical improvement is rarely a certainty. A turnaround may depend on labour agreements, capital expenditure or time. Probability-weight the distinct outcomes or model risk consistently in the discounting. Do not count a saving as certain and then omit the conditions for achieving it.

Method

Build a control-value bridge

  1. Fix the baseline

    List the actions assumed under existing management and the date, currency and claim represented by each forecast. Keep the standalone plan intact for comparison.

  2. Isolate change

    Recalculate the value of cash resulting from each feasible policy change, net of required investment. Explain which owner has access to each change.

  3. Set a conditional ceiling

    Add only improvements available to the particular bidder. Subtract that bidder’s execution costs and compare the resulting equity figure with cash offered for the shares.

Worked case

A garage and its two bidders

A garage’s operations are worth €26m under current management. It has €5m debt and €1m surplus cash. Any new controller can free €3m net value by disposing of an idle line. A local fleet owner can additionally save €2m after contract and integration costs.

Working

  1. Standalone equity is €26m − €5m + €1m = €22m. This is the claim purchased when a bidder acquires all outstanding shares.
  2. A financial bidder can reach €25m. The fleet owner can reach €27m because its route density supplies an additional €2m unavailable to the financial bidder.
  3. At a €26m cash offer, the seller gains €4m over the current-policy equity value. The fleet buyer retains €1m. The financial buyer would lose €1m on its own assumptions.

Interpretation

If the original €26m operating forecast already included the idle-line disposal, both ceilings are €3m lower. Inspect the business plan before carrying the change into an offer.

A common mistake

Adding a generic control premium after explicitly valuing the improvements a controller could make.

How to correct it

Follow each proposed change from operational driver to cash flow. A second label for the same sale or saving creates no additional value.

Quick check
A target is already valued at €23m assuming its empty site is sold for €3m. Your firm can also save €2m through shared deliveries. What is your maximum bid before any buyer gain?

The €23m already includes the site sale. Adding it again would count one improvement twice. Only the additional delivery saving takes the maximum to €25m.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A target’s existing equity is worth €45m including €2m of approved warehouse sales. Any new controller can remove waste worth €4m. Only bidder A can improve distribution worth €5m, at €1m transition cost.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate the ceilings for A and a bidder without the distribution benefit.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Who gains at an A offer of €51m? State the relevant assumption.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

A can pay €45m + €4m + €5m − €1m = €53m. The competing bidder can pay €49m. The €2m sale is already in €45m.

Part B 3 marks

Sellers gain €6m and A gains €2m relative to current-policy value. These sum to €8m of new value. This assumes the distribution benefit is incremental to waste removal and all terms refer to equity at one date.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A target’s share price already reflects takeover speculation. How much control value can a buyer legitimately add to that price?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would first value the independent firm under existing policy and under an achievable better policy. With €40m and €50m as those two values, a €43m market value implies a 30% weight on improvement under the two-state assumptions. A certain turnaround takes us from €43m to €50m, a remaining €7m. Only an additional advantage unique to our combination can justify a higher economic ceiling.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Your exclusive distribution right adds €4m, but the same €2m warehouse saving appears both in that estimate and in the turnaround. What is the bidder ceiling?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Start at the full-control €50m. The distribution estimate contains €2m already counted, leaving €2m incremental. The bidder’s ceiling is €52m on these assumptions. At €43m today the total uplift is €9m, comprising the remaining €7m control uplift and €2m of unique gain.
