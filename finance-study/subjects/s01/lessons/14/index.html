Selling, spinning off or partially listing a division

Reconcile parent cash and shareholder value under three separation structures.

Theory and terminology

Divestiture transaction structures

asset sale · spin-off · equity carve-out

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile parent cash and shareholder value under three separation structures.
  • Calculate the minimum sale offer that matches a spin-off after leakage.

Identify who owns what after closing

A trade sale transfers the unit to an outside buyer. The seller receives the agreed consideration and can repay debt or reinvest it. In a pro rata spin-off, each existing parent investor receives subsidiary shares in proportion to their holding and initially owns pieces of both businesses. The basic distribution raises no cash for the parent.

In an equity carve-out the public buys a portion of the subsidiary. A secondary sale pays the parent for existing subsidiary shares. A primary sale issues new subsidiary shares and the cash goes to the subsidiary. Always locate the proceeds before using them to pay a parent obligation. The parent may retain a controlling block.

Compare claims at one valuation date

Begin with equity values after debt or start from enterprise values and bridge each to equity. Use a consistent basis for the retained business. A buyer may pay for synergies unavailable to public shareholders. Independence may instead let the division attract talent, set its own investment budget or escape costly internal constraints. State which of these changes is already in the valuation.

Separate one-time fees from ongoing costs. A stranded cost is an overhead payment the parent still makes after the revenue leaves. Only its unavoidable incremental present value belongs in the route comparison. Taxes depend on the jurisdiction and specific structure. Use transaction-specific estimates and distinguish taxes observed at the company from investor-level taxes.

Find a decision threshold

Track shareholder value and parent liquidity in different columns. A distribution can deliver substantial value with no parent proceeds. A carve-out retained block can look valuable on paper while the parent faces restrictions on selling it. Do not assign a new control premium to both the shares sold and the stake kept.

A banker can solve backwards for a minimum cash offer. Add the cash-route leakage to the value obtainable by distribution. A board negotiating with a buyer can compare an improved offer to that threshold and separately test cash needs, execution risk and whether the division can stand alone.

Method

Build the result

Incremental sale value = gross equity proceeds − sale leakage; spin value = separated equity value − spin leakage.

Both values accrue to current owners in the same currency at the same date. Leakage includes all incremental taxes, fees and the present value of residual costs that are outside the initial values.

  1. Place the retained parent business on the same basis under both routes.
  2. Calculate route-specific value net of leakage. Compare the result with keeping the division.
  3. For a secondary carve-out, add cash from the fraction sold to the value of the retained stake, then deduct route costs once.
Worked case

A parent separates its diagnostics unit

Illustrative equity values in $ millions. Parent operations excluding diagnostics are worth 420 before route-specific leakage. Keeping the unit contributes 142. A buyer offers 175 cash. A fully separated unit is worth 174. All deductions below are present values.

Working

  1. Keeping the group intact gives 420 + 142 = 562.
  2. The cash sale costs 8 in fees, 11 in tax and 9 in remaining overhead. Incremental value is 175 − 8 − 11 − 9 = 147. Combined owner value is 567.
  3. A spin costs 12 to complete and leaves 8 of overhead. Incremental value is 174 − 12 − 8 = 154. Owners receive interests worth 574 in total.
  4. As a separate alternative, sell 30% of existing subsidiary shares at the 174 valuation. Cash is 52.2 and the 70% stake is worth 121.8. Deduct total assumed carve-out leakage of 15 once to reach 159, giving owner value of 579.

Decision and interpretation

The carve-out has the highest modeled owner value but raises only 52.2 gross parent cash. A firm needing 100 cash at closing would have to finance the balance or choose the trade sale. The retained stake is exposed to future market pricing.

Quick check
A subsidiary issues new shares for €40m and the parent sells none of its shares. Which entity receives the subscription cash?

The issuer receives the primary proceeds. A secondary sale of parent-owned shares would instead deliver cash to the parent.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A company can sell its unit for £115m, with £4m fees, £7m tax and £6m stranded-cost present value. A spin gives owners subsidiary equity worth £106m, incurs £5m fees and £2m stranded costs. How high must a bid be to match the spin?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The spin delivers 106 − 5 − 2 = £99m of incremental owner value.
  2. The existing sale offer delivers 115 − 4 − 7 − 6 = £98m.
  3. The break-even gross sale price is 99 + 4 + 7 + 6 = £116m, assuming leakage is unchanged as the offer increases.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why might a board prefer to distribute shares in a division even when a buyer offers cash immediately?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Shareholders receive an interest in the separated business and keep its future upside. A sale exchanges that interest for cash after taxes and fees. Comparing both on a common date, including the costs left behind, can favor the distribution even if only the sale meets a near-term cash target.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the parent must deliver £80m cash next month, how does the recommendation change?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The spin delivers shares to owners. The sale leaves £104m after fee and tax at closing, before future overhead. The board could rationally accept the lower owner value when alternative near-term liquidity costs more than £1m.
