Deal process and control contests

Compare negotiated mergers, tender offers and auctions. Evaluate takeover defences and incentives of boards, managers, bidders and minority owners under the stated case rules.

The starting point

A negotiated acquisition begins with discussions between buyer and target. In a tender offer, the bidder invites shareholders to sell under stated terms. In an auction, several parties can compete. Access to diligence, time to respond and the conditions attached to each bid affect the outcome.

Compare what shareholders receive if an offer closes with what happens if it fails. The highest advertised price can come with financing or approval conditions. Managers may prefer the offer that preserves their jobs, making it important to explain how the board compared terms.

Theory and method

Winner’s curse in common-value takeover auctions

Conditional valuation · Common and private value

Why should a bidder update its valuation upon learning that it won the auction?

Several buyers independently estimate the amount of recoverable cash in a target’s receivables. That component has much the same value whichever bidder acquires it. Winning is more likely when a bidder’s estimate is unusually high relative to other bidders’ information. The event of winning can therefore change the distribution of the common value. A competitor’s willingness to walk away becomes information. Unique cost savings available only to this buyer are a separate private-value component.

How the result is built

E[V given win] = Σᵢ Vᵢ × Pr(win given i) × Pr(i) / Pr(win)

Vᵢ is the common euro value in state i, Pr(i) is its prior probability, Pr(win given i) is the chance of winning in that state, and E is a probability-weighted mean. Pr(win) adds all state-and-win probabilities.

  1. Start from values €80m and €120m, each with probability one-half. Before using information from the award, the expected common value is €100m.
  2. Suppose the proposed bid wins 80% of the time in the low-value state and 20% in the high state. The joint probabilities of those states and winning are 0.4 and 0.1, so total winning probability is 0.5.
  3. Among wins, the low-value state has 0.4/0.5 = 80% weight. Expected common value conditional on winning is €88m. A €94m bid loses €6m per win on this component.

When the reasoning holds

  • The conditional win probabilities belong to the particular bidding policy. Changing the bid can change the information revealed by a win.
  • There must be a material common-value element and informative competition. Exclusive synergy whose size the bidder knows may justify a higher private valuation.
Terms in this lesson
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
First example

In a simplified auction, failure leaves shares worth €30. Bid A offers €44 with a 75% chance of closing. Bid B offers €42 with a 95% chance. Ignoring delay and risk preferences, expected proceeds are €40.50 for A and €41.40 for B.

How the sale process changes the outcome

In a negotiated transaction a bidder can learn about the business before fixing its price and tailor contractual protections. An auction may create competition and reveal alternatives, but limits time for investigation and can raise the risk of winning on overoptimistic assumptions. A tender offer asks owners directly to sell under specified conditions.

Control rights and board procedures vary across jurisdictions. Reconstruct the rules stated for a case, including acceptance thresholds, approvals, voting eligibility and the treatment of remaining owners. A bidder owning a large minority can influence a company without having the automatic power to implement every desired action.

Compare conditional offers on one date

Shareholders care about payment if an offer closes, what remains if it fails, timing and any costs. A price conditional on financing is not equivalent to the same amount in cash today. Compute an expected outcome under stated probabilities, then discount any later outcome to a common date.

An expected value is not a guaranteed payment. Shareholders with liquidity needs or concentrated holdings may value certainty differently. A board can ask a bidder for a committed financing package or clearer conditions to address completion risk, instead of arguing about headline price alone.

Whose incentives shape a defence?

A defence might give a board time to solicit a stronger competing bid or protect management from replacement. Examine the governance process, independent advice, treatment of minority shareholders and realistic alternative offers. Delay has an opportunity cost if the original bid can expire.

A rival’s intervention can increase the target’s bargaining power but reduce expected value to the original bidder. Separately evaluate buyer and seller decisions. A bid that maximizes the target’s expected outcome may exceed the original buyer’s reservation price.

Method

Evaluate two offers

  1. Specify outcomes

    List proceeds per share at closing or failure, any break fees actually received by that group of owners, assumed probabilities and dates. Include the no-deal alternative.

  2. Value each path

    Calculate probability-weighted proceeds and discount if dates differ. Show how much completion probability would be needed for a conditional offer to beat a certain one.

  3. Explain execution

    Identify who can change the decisive probability or timing and how. Show what supporting document or binding condition would make the estimate credible.

Worked case

A tender with a financing condition

An offer pays €42 per share in one year if financed, and the shares would be worth €28 then if it fails. A second buyer pays €36 today with certainty. Assume a 5% one-year discount rate and an 80% conditional completion probability.

Working

  1. Expected proceeds in one year equal 0.80 × €42 + 0.20 × €28 = €39.20. Their present value is €37.33, above €36.
  2. For indifference at €36 today, next year’s expected proceeds must be €37.80. Solve €28 + €14p = €37.80 to obtain p = 70%.
  3. If a bank withdraws its funding commitment and probability falls to 60%, expected proceeds are €36.40 in one year or €34.67 today. The certain offer then leads.

Interpretation

These calculations assume one discount rate represents the stated comparison. Differing downside exposures or a different no-deal value can change the ranking.

A common mistake

Comparing the conditional cash price with the certain cash price and dismissing financing or shareholder approvals as procedural details.

How to correct it

Value failure as a genuine branch. If probabilities are uncertain, report an indifference threshold and identify which evidence would move the estimate across it.

Quick check
Shareholders can take €31 with certain completion or €35 with a 60% chance of completion. Failure leaves €24. Under a simple expected-value comparison, which offer is worth more?

The conditional offer has expected proceeds of 0.60 × €35 + 0.40 × €24 = €30.60. The comparison would change if failure value, timing or completion chances changed.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

One bidder offers €50 in a year on success, otherwise shares are worth €30 in a year. Another offers €40 now. Use a 5% discount rate.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find the minimum completion probability required to match the immediate offer.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    A director backing the conditional bid expects to keep her job if it closes. What independent checks would you request?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The later expected payment must equal €42. Set €30 + €20p = €42 and solve p = 60%. Above 60% the conditional offer has the higher expected present value in this model.

Part B 3 marks

Request documentary financing evidence and an independent assessment of completion risk and fallback value. Review conflict handling and the comparison with the €40 certainty. Personal benefit is evidence of a conflict, not proof the offer is inferior.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why should a bidder update its valuation upon learning that it won the auction?

I would separate what other informed bidders can also assess from what is unique to us. Their decision to stop bidding is informative about common receivables or demand. Interviewer’s follow-up

Our distribution network produces €10m certain private synergy whenever we win. Does the €94m bid still destroy expected value?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Adding the €10m unique benefit raises our conditional value to €98m. Against €94m, expected surplus is €4m per win before bid costs, or €2m per auction at a 50% win probability. Any unavoidable preparation fee above €2m would make entering unattractive on these assumptions.
