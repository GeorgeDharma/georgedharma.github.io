Financing and earnings per share

Build sources and uses, shares issued and interest expense. A deal can raise earnings per share while destroying value if the buyer pays too much.

The starting point

Earnings per share, or EPS, divides reported profit available to ordinary shareholders by the number of shares. Financing an acquisition with debt adds interest expense. Paying with shares increases the denominator. Either can change EPS even when the bargain itself creates no wealth.

A separate check compares the economic value received with the price paid. Analysts build a funding table showing where every euro comes from and where it goes. That exposes fees, refinancing and borrowing costs that disappear in a simple addition of the two firms’ profits.

Theory and method

EPS bootstrapping in a stock-financed acquisition

Accretion and dilution · Earnings-yield test

Why can a bidder with a high price-to-earnings ratio show EPS accretion without increasing total operating value?

An acquirer issuing shares at a high price can buy a given amount of earnings with relatively few new shares. The combined company’s earnings per share then increase mechanically if the earnings purchased per euro exceed the bidder’s own earnings per euro. A lower multiple on the target can reflect slow growth or greater risk. Buying those earnings and presenting one year’s EPS cannot by itself establish that economic value was created for the original owners.

How the result is built

Accretion when Eₜ/P > Eᵦ/B

Eₜ and Eᵦ are target and bidder annual earnings in euros. P is the reference euro value of the share offer at the initial bidder share price, and B is initial bidder market equity in euros. The fractions are annual earnings yields.

  1. Let bidder share count be N and initial price b = B/N. A reference purchase amount P implies an issue of P/b new shares under a fixed share count set at that price.
  2. Compare combined earnings per share, (Eᵦ + Eₜ)/(N + P/b), with Eᵦ/N. Cross multiplying cancels N × Eᵦ and leaves N × Eₜ > Eᵦ × P/b.
  3. Use bN = B to obtain the yield comparison. With €10m bidder earnings on €200m equity and €3m target earnings at a €45m headline offer, 6.67% exceeds 5%.

When the reasoning holds

  • The comparison assumes no financing charges, integration or acquisition-accounting earnings changes and a full same-period contribution. Actual pro forma EPS can include all three.
  • This test uses the initial bidder price to set the issue count. Actual consideration in a fixed-ratio deal changes with the combined share price.
Terms in this lesson
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
First example

A buyer earns €10m on 10m shares. It borrows €30m at 5% to buy a target earning €2m. Ignoring taxes and other changes, combined EPS is (€10m + €2m − €1.5m)/10m = €1.05. If the target is only worth €26m and offers no gains, the buyer loses €4m in value.

A share is a fraction of the profits

Earnings per share divides profit available to ordinary owners by the relevant number of shares. For a simple full-year pro forma comparison, add target earnings, subtract new financing costs after any stated tax effect, include forecast changes to earnings and divide by the post-deal count. State if a partial year or diluted share count is required.

An accretive deal raises earnings per share. A dilutive one lowers it. The arithmetic responds to the earnings yield of the acquired firm, the price of debt or newly issued shares and accounting charges. It says little about the duration or risk of those earnings.

Why price and accounting profit can disagree

A buyer with highly valued shares can exchange a small number for a company whose profits trade cheaply. Even with unchanged combined operating cash, earnings per share may rise. Existing holders still lose if they surrender a larger fraction of combined economic value than the acquired assets contribute.

Debt funding can produce a similar illusion. Interest may be low this year while principal remains due later. The purchase price is exchanged for an asset and does not pass through current earnings as an immediate expense. To test value, model the cash available to investors relative to what they paid.

Keep the funding table separate

List uses such as purchase consideration, refinancing and fees against debt, cash on hand and any new equity. Cash drawn from the buyer’s balance sheet also has an opportunity cost. Consistently distinguish recurring interest and recurring operating profit from one-time integration expenses.

A credible comparison reports assumptions about tax deductibility, share issue dates and any acquisition-accounting charges. A temporary EPS increase can reverse once a teaser loan rate resets or a new asset is depreciated. Look at the profile across several years before interpreting an earnings metric.

Method

Compute two different answers

  1. Build the numerator

    Start from profits available to shareholders of both firms and deduct incremental after-tax interest or other stated recurring charges. Explain treatment of any flat or one-time item.

  2. Build the denominator

    Keep the original share count for an all-debt deal. Add newly issued shares for an equity deal. Match the period implied by the question.

  3. Value the exchange

    Compare the value of the acquired claim plus net economic benefits with the value given up. Test whether the two conclusions agree and explain any difference in their mechanics.

Worked case

A cash bid at cheap funding

A listed buyer earns €24m on 12m shares. It borrows €60m at 4% to buy a target earning €4m. Interest is fully deductible at 25%. The target’s economic equity value is €52m, with net operating gains worth €3m. Ignore other effects including financing subsidies in the value comparison.

Working

  1. Annual interest is €2.4m and the after-tax charge is €1.8m. Pro forma profit becomes €24m + €4m − €1.8m = €26.2m.
  2. EPS rises from €2.00 to €26.2m/12m = €2.1833, an increase of 9.17%. No new shares were issued.
  3. The buyer pays €60m for value of €55m. Existing shareholders lose €5m under these assumptions. This conclusion uses the economic cash-flow valuations, not the target’s single year earnings.

Interpretation

An interest-rate increase would shrink EPS even if the agreed purchase price and long-term operating plans did not change. An examiner should be able to reproduce each of the two tests separately.

A common mistake

Including debt proceeds in shareholder profit when preparing the pro forma income calculation.

How to correct it

Borrowing creates cash and an obligation at closing. Only the period’s relevant interest and other income-statement items affect the earnings numerator in this simple calculation.

Quick check
A bidder’s EPS rises after a deal. Which additional calculation addresses whether the purchase created value for its existing investors?

EPS records profit per share. It does not charge the acquisition price against one year’s profit. Comparing the economic value acquired with the price directly tests overpayment.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A buyer earns €8m on 4m shares. For an all-debt purchase it pays €25m and incurs €1m annual interest after tax. The target earns €2m. Target and net synergies together are worth €23m.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate pro forma EPS and the percentage change.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Calculate buyer value creation and explain the differing findings.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

EPS moves from €2.00 to (€8m + €2m − €1m)/4m = €2.25. That is a 12.5% increase on the original €2.00 base.

Part B 3 marks

The owner loses €2m because €23m is acquired for €25m. This year's €1m incremental accounting earnings do not deduct the entire price or measure the risk and duration of future receipts.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why can a bidder with a high price-to-earnings ratio show EPS accretion without increasing total operating value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would explain the price of the earnings the buyer issues and the price of the earnings it buys. Issuing our shares at 20 times earnings to acquire a target at 15 times raises the earnings backing each enlarged share in this example. The value test requires a separate share-count and cash-flow valuation. If the target’s equity is only worth €36m, the old buyers’ surviving stake falls below its original €200m.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Which level of target annual earnings would make EPS exactly unchanged on the €45m headline offer?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The target would need the bidder’s 5% earnings yield on €45m, giving €2.25m of annual earnings. Then combined EPS is €12.25m/6.125m = €2.00. This equality still says nothing about how sustainable those target profits are.
