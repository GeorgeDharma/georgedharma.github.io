Building a precedent-transaction valuation for a buyer’s board

Construct consistently measured acquisition multiples from publicly stated terms.

In this lecture

  1. Select deals before looking at the answer
  2. Spread the transaction on a consistent basis
  3. Turn observations into a bidding discussion
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. The same enterprise price can imply different multiples
  6. A buyer screens four industrial acquisitions

  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Precedent transactions in a merger bid

unaffected price · control premium · buyer limit

What you should be able to explain

  • Construct consistently measured acquisition multiples from publicly stated terms.
  • Apply a screened range to the target and compare the result with its unaffected price and buyer-specific limit.

Select deals before looking at the answer

A precedent transaction is an earlier acquisition offered as evidence of what buyers have been willing to pay. Screen by products, geography, size, margins, customer economics, ownership transferred and date. Interest rates, growth expectations and forced-sale conditions change the relevance of an old observation. A price paid for a small minority stake with limited rights can be a poor comparator for buying legal control.

Create an inclusion log alongside the financial table. Record the reason for each exclusion before choosing the valuation range. Closed and announced-but-uncompleted deals can answer different questions. Record deal status and use the publicly available information that existed at the selected date. A failed deal does not demonstrate an executable closing price.

Spread the transaction on a consistent basis

A sale announcement may report the cheque written to shareholders, a headline enterprise value including debt, or only the value of a block acquired. Identify the basis first. For a simple full acquisition, start from total equity consideration, add target debt assumed and subtract usable cash acquired. Check leases, minority interests, contingent payments and pensions where material. Counting debt twice inflates the multiple.

Use the last twelve months of comparable operating earnings available before announcement. EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization. Reconcile adjustments and lease accounting. A buyer may announce a “synergy-adjusted” multiple by adding expected savings to the denominator. Remove those savings when matching ordinary standalone EBITDA peers unless every observation uses compatible measures.

Turn observations into a bidding discussion

Line up the calculated multiples and inspect the low, middle and high observations. Apply a defensible range to the target’s corresponding EBITDA, subtract its net debt and other senior claims, then divide by diluted target shares. Use low and high as scenarios. A median from a thin sample has limited precision.

A control sale can already embed bargaining, competitive tension and a share of expected synergies. Adding a standard takeover premium to its implied equity value can count those elements twice. Compare the inferred equity price with the target’s unaffected market price before credible leaks, the independent valuation and your own maximum affordable bid.

Method

Build the result

Deal multiple = (full equity consideration + debt assumed − usable cash)/standalone EBITDA; implied share price = (selected multiple × target EBITDA − target net debt)/diluted target shares.

Equity consideration and debt are values on a common currency and date. Usable cash is cash available with the target. EBITDA uses the matched reference period. Net debt means debt less usable cash. The simplified cases have no other enterprise-to-equity adjustments.

  1. Rebuild full acquisition enterprise value from the offer terms, identifying cash versus enterprise-value headlines.
  2. Calculate comparable multiples and document exclusions. Select an economically defensible range.
  3. Apply that range to target operating earnings, bridge to common equity, and compare with the unaffected price without adding another control premium.
Worked case

A buyer screens four industrial acquisitions

Three recent full-control deals have the following equity price, debt, cash and standalone EBITDA, in $m. A has 198, 32, 10 and 22. B has 174, 24, 8 and 20. C has 207, 21, 8 and 20. A fourth transaction bought a minority stake with different rights. The target has $18m EBITDA, $33m debt, $8m cash, 10m diluted shares and an unaffected share price of $12.50.

Working

  1. A’s enterprise price is 198 + 32 − 10 = $220m, or 10.0× EBITDA. B gives $190m or 9.5×. C gives $220m or 11.0×.
  2. The full-control range is 9.5–11.0×, with 10.0× as median. Exclude the minority deal on rights and control grounds before examining whether it raises or lowers the result.
  3. Applying the range produces $171m–$198m of target enterprise value. Subtract $25m net debt to reach $146m–$173m of equity, or $14.60–$17.30 per diluted share.
  4. At the median the implied price is (10 × 18 − 25)/10 = $15.50. Relative to $12.50 that implies a 24% premium.

Decision and interpretation

Give the board a range and each deal’s selection rationale. If the buyer’s standalone-plus-synergy valuation caps its bid at $15, a $15.50 observed median does not create the missing value.

Quick check
A $100m full equity purchase includes a target with $20m debt, $5m cash and $10m standalone EBITDA. What is the simple acquisition multiple?

Enterprise price is 100 + 20 − 5 = $115m, so the matched enterprise-value-to-EBITDA multiple is 11.5×.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Two full acquisitions have equity/debt/cash/EBITDA of €120m/€25m/€5m/€14m and €112m/€20m/€6m/€14m. A target has €12m EBITDA, €18m net debt, 6m diluted shares and an unaffected price of €14. Calculate the range and midpoint premium.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. The rebuilt enterprise prices are €140m and €126m. The multiples are 10× and 9×.
  2. The €108m–€120m implied enterprise values translate into €90m–€102m common equity and €15–€17 per share.
  3. The €16 midpoint implies a premium of 16/14 − 1 = 14.29%. This comparison does not call for a second premium.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can an analyst use the prices buyers actually paid for other businesses to evaluate a bid?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Choose control deals for economically similar businesses. Rebuild the full enterprise purchase price and divide each by an operating measure from the same period on the same accounting basis. Apply the resulting range to the target, bridge from enterprise to equity and test the price against standalone value and the buyer’s own potential synergies.

Interviewer’s follow-up

How would you handle a third announcement stating “€150m transaction value including €30m debt” but saying nothing about target cash?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Identify whether €150m is already enterprise value, obtain the release or agreement and document how cash was treated. Do not add the €30m again. Show the observation separately or exclude it until a consistent basis is established.
