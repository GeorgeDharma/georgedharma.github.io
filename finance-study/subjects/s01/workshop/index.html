Professional workshop · S01

Acquire the depot network or license its routing system?

Northbank Distribution must choose between acquiring all ordinary shares of Quay Routes and signing a mutually exclusive licence worth €1.50m to Northbank today. The seller requests €65m cash. Prepare a signed-off valuation, financing and earnings model as of 31 December Year 0, then specify a maximum bid and the evidence that could change it. All figures in millions of euros unless indicated. Forecasts, companies and terms are fictional.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Corporate development associate preparing a buyer investment committee paper
What to submit
A buyer-side investment committee recommendation with a standalone valuation, incremental synergy schedule, sources and uses, debt covenant test, EPS bridge, threshold price and downside decision.

From inputs to decision

01

Normalize Quay’s earning power

The seller’s insurance settlement arrived once. Leaving it in a perpetuity would capitalize a historical cheque as if it arrived every year.

  1. Remove the €2m one-time gain from €14m operating profit, leaving €12m normalized EBIT. EBIT is operating profit before interest and tax.
  2. Apply 25% tax to normalized EBIT to obtain €9m operating profit after tax. Add €3m depreciation because it is noncash, deduct €4m physical replacement investment and €1m capitalized software renewals. Operating working capital stays constant.

Normalized free cash flow to the operations is €7m per year. Target normalized EBITDA is €12m EBIT plus €3m depreciation, or €15m.

02

Bridge operating value to the shares being bought

The seller owns the shares. Using operating value as the cash price and then paying the old loan a second time would inflate the consideration.

  1. At zero growth a €7m annual cash flow starting in one year is worth €7m/10% = €70m for the operating business.
  2. Deduct €15m old debt and add €5m usable cash to get €60m standalone equity, or €12 per target share. The separate unaffected quoted price of €11 implies €55m market equity.

Use €60m as the economic standalone benchmark and €55m as the unaffected market benchmark.

03

Value the actual benefits available only through ownership

The licence provides a credible alternative and the seller’s premium competes for the combination gains. The acquisition case needs dated cash, including what it takes to get the benefit.

  1. Discount the four €3.30m savings at 10%. Their current values are €3.000m, €2.727m, €2.479m and €2.254m. Add them before rounding, giving €10.461m.
  2. Deduct €2m integration paid today for €8.461m net operating synergy. Deduct the separate €1m deal fee once when measuring buyer gain, leaving €7.461m of distributable acquisition benefit.

An unconstrained zero-gain price is €67.461m for the target equity. A buyout must also beat the €1.50m licensing option.

04

Calculate offer premiums, allocation and walk-away price

A target can command a large quoted premium while leaving sufficient economic value to a bidder. Define which benchmark each premium uses.

  1. The €65m ask is €13 per share. Relative to €11 unaffected it is an 18.18% market premium. Relative to economic standalone value the seller gains €65m − €60m = €5m.
  2. Buyer gain is €60m + €7.461m − €65m = €2.461m. Deduct €1.50m of alternative value to find only €0.961m of advantage over licensing. The maximum competitive bid is €60m + €7.461m − €1.50m = €65.961m.

The current price passes narrowly. The combined gains check is €5m seller gain plus €2.461m buyer gain equals €7.461m after integration and fees.

05

Fund closing and test the lender’s actual covenant

An economically attractive deal can still breach a financing constraint. The cash required to close includes share consideration, costs and the old loan payoff.

  1. Uses are €65m to sellers, €15m refinancing, €2m integration and €1m fees, totaling €83m. Sources are €5m target cash, €24m buyer cash and €54m new debt, also €83m.
  2. Buyer net debt rises from €60m by €24m when its own cash is spent and by €54m of new borrowing. The old target debt is repaid and no target cash remains. Combined net debt is €138m. Eligible EBITDA is €38m + €15m = €53m.

Leverage is 138/53 = 2.604 times. Threshold net debt is €145.75m, giving €7.75m headroom. The economic ceiling binds before this funding headroom.

06

Build the recurring EPS bridge as a separate test

The board wants to understand year-one reported earning power per share. Interest depends on the actual financing, while target interest on the retired loan should disappear from the projection.

  1. Annual new cash interest is 5% × €54m = €2.7m. Incremental recurring earnings before synergy are (€12m target EBIT − €2.7m new interest) × 75% = €6.975m.
  2. Add €3.30m already after tax for integration savings. Add the €10.275m incremental profit to €30m standalone buyer earnings. Divide €40.275m by 20m unchanged shares.

Recurring EPS is €2.01375 versus €1.50, up 34.25%. The price ceiling still comes from incremental discounted cash. Report upfront costs separately when preparing actual period accounts.

07

Break the strongest acquisition assumption

The supplier agreement expires after year four. A delayed migration permanently forfeits the first year of savings, rather than shifting all four payments forward.

  1. Remove the €3.30m year-one cash, reducing present value by exactly €3m. Net benefit after integration and fees falls from €7.461m to €4.461m.
  2. At the €65m price, acquisition gain becomes −€0.539m against doing nothing and −€2.039m relative to licensing. The downside maximum price to beat the licence falls to €62.961m.

Request a binding migration timetable, evidence from comparable depots and customer-retention testing. Link any conditional price payment to savings the buyer can verify.

08

Reconcile and send the mandate

A signing decision should survive a handover to a second analyst. Cross-check the flow of cash, claims and control rights before an offer is released.

  1. Confirm sources minus uses equals zero, buyer and seller gains sum to acquisition surplus, both valuations use the same date and the licence is deducted once. Verify no operating line already includes the €3.30m purchasing benefit.
  2. Round a board-approved hard limit down to €65.95m if bids are made in €0.05m steps. At €65m proceed only subject to diligence on the four dated savings and funding availability. Without that evidence renegotiate protection or take the licence.

Submit a conditional offer mandate for €65.00m with no discretion to exceed €65.95m and a clearly identified integration downside.

Completed document

Investment committee decision paper | Quay Routes

Approval requested

Authorize a conditional €65m all-cash offer for all five million Quay shares. Retain the routing licence as an executable alternative. Fix delegated bidding authority at €65.95m, rounded down from the €65.961m calculated indifference price.

Value and ownership

We value Quay equity at €60m standalone. Four years of procurement savings support €7.461m of incremental value after one-off cash costs. At the ask, sellers retain €5m of this value and Northbank retains €2.461m, only €0.961m above licensing.

Funding and earnings

Refinancing and closing payments require €83m. A €54m acquisition loan balances the settlement. Closing leverage of 2.604 times is below the 2.75 limit. Recurring EPS accretion of 34.25% is reported separately from our bid valuation.

Conditional release and monitoring

Do not release an unconditional bid until procurement signs the migration milestones and treasury confirms the €54m draw and covenant calculation. Losing year-one savings would leave us €2.039m worse off than licensing. If that risk cannot be addressed, seek a contingent payment or take the licence.

Academic and professional references
  • Aswath Damodaran, Acquisitions and Takeovers

    Standalone value, synergy, control, share exchanges and buyer pricing.

  • Grossman and Hart, Take-Over Bids, the Free Rider Problem and the Theory of the Corporation

    Dispersed ownership and the incentives facing non-pivotal target shareholders.

  • Richard Roll, The Hubris Hypothesis of Corporate Takeovers

    Overconfident bidders as an explanation for takeover premiums.

  • Michael Jensen, Agency Costs of Free Cash Flow, Corporate Finance, and Takeovers

    Managerial discretion over free cash flow and incentives to acquire.
