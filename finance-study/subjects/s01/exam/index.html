Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

An acquisition or a licensing agreement

Boreal considers a cash bid for a data business worth €50m standalone. A controller could cut waste worth €4m today. Boreal could also earn €3.3m at each of the next two year-ends by using its distribution network, at €1m integration expense today. Discount the incremental flows at 10%. A mutually exclusive licence would give Boreal €2m net value. The target has an executable €54m offer. All amounts are on an equity basis.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

Paying with shares and reporting earnings

A bidder has 8m shares, equity worth €80m and €8m annual profit. The target has 2m shares, equity worth €16m and €4m profit. The bidder offers 1.5 shares per target share. Economic synergy is €3m of present value. Assume the current year’s combined profit is simply the sum of the standalone profits, with no charges or debt change.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Competing offers across currencies

A bidder must pay 160m local units in a year if approval succeeds. It forward-buys 80m units at 16 units per euro for that date. A rival offers the seller €9.7m immediately. At the assumed year-end spot rate of 20 units per euro the target is worth €6m in a failed-deal state. Conditional completion probability is 75%, with a 5% euro discount rate. Assume that the seller receives local currency and does no hedging, the bidder holds the entire forward, and fallback value is measured at year-end.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

From market expectation to realized performance

At announcement a bidder worth €1,000m falls 1% against a +0.5% benchmark. A €200m target rises 14% against a +1% benchmark. One year later combined operating cash rises from €20m to €25m before a €1.4m integration cash payment. Independents with similar product lines report 12% cash growth. A new competitor entered only the combined firm’s market after closing.

Case 1 · 10 marks

An acquisition or a licensing agreement

Part A 4 marks

Calculate Boreal’s maximum price relative to doing nothing and relative to licensing.

Distribution adds €3.3m/1.10 + €3.3m/1.10² − €1m = €4.727m (2). Together with the control improvement the no-gain ceiling is €58.727m (1). Give up the €2m licence gain only if price is no higher than €56.727m (1).

Part B 3 marks

At a €55m bid, divide gains relative to standalone and relative to alternatives.

Sellers gain €5m versus standalone and €1m versus their rival offer (1). Boreal gains €3.727m versus doing nothing (1), or €1.727m above the licensing option (1).

Part C 3 marks

The target’s own board was already funded to eliminate the waste and the standalone €50m already includes the €4m benefit. Reassess the sale.

Remove the double count (1). Revised ceiling versus licensing is €50m + €4.727m − €2m = €52.727m (1), below the executable €54m. Boreal should pursue the licence on these assumptions (1).

Case 2 · 10 marks

Paying with shares and reporting earnings

Part A 4 marks

Compute the ownership split, combined share price and consideration.

Issue 3m shares and total 11m (1). Target owners have 3/11 and original owners 8/11 (1). Combined equity is €99m, or €9 per share (1). Sellers receive €27m (1).

Part B 3 marks

Find the original owners’ gain or loss and reconcile it with total value created.

Original owners hold €72m rather than €80m, a loss of €8m (1). Sellers gain €27m − €16m = €11m (1). The €11m gain and €8m loss sum to €3m synergy (1).

Part C 3 marks

Find the EPS change and explain why it differs from owner value.

Old EPS is €1. New EPS is €12m/11m = €1.0909, up 9.09% (2). Sellers received an economically rich fraction of the firm even though adding the target’s profit raised earnings per share (1).

Case 3 · 10 marks

Competing offers across currencies

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the successful bidder’s euro cash cost at that spot with and without its hedge.

The forward portion costs €5m and 80m units at spot cost €4m, totalling €9m (2). Entirely unhedged, the bidder would pay €8m (1).

Part B 4 marks

Compare both offers from the seller’s perspective and explain why the bidder’s hedge is excluded.

A successful 160m payment is €8m at spot (1). The seller’s expected cash-equivalent value in one year is 0.75 × €8m + 0.25 × €6m = €7.5m, or €7.143m today (2). The €9.7m rival leads and the bidder’s private hedge does not transfer to the seller (1).

Part C 3 marks

If approval fails at that spot, calculate the hedge outcome and name one additional execution exposure.

The bidder pays €5m for 80m units worth €4m, a €1m loss (2). Delayed approval beyond the hedge date could require rolling the contract at a new rate or financing an interim currency balance (1).

Case 4 · 10 marks

From market expectation to realized performance

Part A 4 marks

Calculate the bidder, target and combined abnormal reactions in euros and the combined percentage.

Bidder abnormal return is −1.5% or −€15m (1). Target abnormal return is +13% or +€26m (1). Combined increase is €11m (1), or €11m/€1,200m = 0.917% (1).

Part B 3 marks

Use peers to estimate the year’s operating outperformance after integration cost.

The no-deal proxy is €20m × 1.12 = €22.4m (1). Actual net cash is €25m − €1.4m = €23.6m (1). Estimated outperformance is €1.2m (1).

Part C 3 marks

Can either calculation prove that the deal generated €11m of realized operating gains? Identify a concrete improvement to the test.

The announcement is a market expectation and can include other news (1). One year’s net cash is neither lifetime value nor cleanly causal in the presence of different competition (1). Match product lines or regions exposed to the same competitor and examine prior trends, then value the future net cash profile (1).
