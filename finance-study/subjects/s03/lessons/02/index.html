Reconcile the data

Tie management accounts to the ledger and signed statements. Track periods, entities, adjustments and unexplained reconciling items.

The starting point

A management report, general ledger and signed financial statements can show different totals because they cover different periods, entities or adjustments. Fix those boundaries first. Reconciliation means building a bridge that explains every difference using traceable entries.

Check both directions. A report can tie to the ledger while the ledger omits a subsidiary or an adjusting entry. Mark unexplained amounts explicitly until supporting evidence reconciles them. A convenient line called “other” can make a spreadsheet balance while leaving the underlying question open.

Theory and method

Receivables control-account reconciliation

Subledger-to-ledger tie-out · Roll-forward

How would you identify an unexplained reduction in debtors while group revenue appears to reconcile?

A ledger can show the cash expected from customers as a single total while the receivables subledger lists individual open invoices. A roll-forward asks what economic events moved that account from beginning to end. Customer billings create claims, collections extinguish them, and valid credits or write-offs remove amounts for different reasons. If the movement does not reconcile, a plug figure disguises which party owes money or which period earned the revenue.

How the result is built

AR₁ = AR₀ + B − C − R − W + J

AR₀ and AR₁ are opening and closing gross receivables, B credit billings, C cash applied, R credit notes, W write-offs, and J separately evidenced other net movements. All are euros for the same entity and period.

  1. Start with €3m open. Credit billings of €8m bring amounts to collect to €11m. Apply €7.2m verified cash to leave €3.8m.
  2. Subtract €0.3m valid credits and €0.1m write-offs, leaving €3.4m. A reported gross closing balance of €3.2m leaves a negative €0.2m unexplained movement.
  3. Find the missing cause using unapplied cash, duplicate extraction, FX or manual journal entries. Reconcile invoice-level open amounts to the control balance after classifying this movement.

When the reasoning holds

  • The roll-forward uses gross receivables. Substituting a net-of-loss-allowance balance introduces the allowance movement as an artificial discrepancy.
  • Use one currency or separately identify translation effects. A foreign subsidiary can show a euro movement without any new bill or customer payment.
Terms in this lesson
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
First example

A dashboard shows €14.0m in sales. It includes €0.8m internal sales eliminated in the group accounts. A €0.1m year-end adjustment adds external sales. The bridge predicts €13.3m. Signed group sales of €13.2m leave a €0.1m difference still to investigate.

Numbers that balance can mean different things

Management accounts can show a division’s trading performance while statutory consolidated statements describe all controlled entities under the relevant reporting framework. A single euro difference can arise from an omitted company, a month cut off at a different date, foreign-currency translation or inclusion of internal group sales.

A reconciliation explains the difference between two totals with identifiable entries. First harmonize scope and definitions. Matching two reports created by the same system proves they agree with each other, not that either one captures every delivery or obligation. Independent evidence becomes useful once the population is understood.

From the transaction to the signed accounts

A ledger is the detailed record of entries. A trial balance lists accounts and their debit or credit balances. Management reporting groups those accounts into lines useful for decisions. Statutory statements may then add consolidation eliminations, external reporting adjustments and audit entries.

Follow the direction of the bridge. Removing an internal sale reduces consolidated revenue, while adding a previously excluded external subsidiary increases it. At the end, calculate a residual. An unresolved difference belongs in a visible open-items line with an owner, not a rounded plug treated as resolved.

Data quality changes what can be sampled

Obtain a reconciliation for each material period, not just a convenient annual total. Two different errors may cancel over a year. A December accrual reversed in January can distort the trend used for a valuation even though the two-year sum matches perfectly.

Before selecting transactions, verify record counts, unique identifiers, date boundaries and signs. Select some entries from the ledger into source documents to test recorded activity, and some from dispatch logs into the ledger to test completeness. Those directions answer different questions.

Method

Create a reconciliation file

  1. Fix the boundaries

    Record entities, currencies, accounting standard, period start and end, and whether each source is before or after consolidation. Use a consistent unit.

  2. Build a signed bridge

    Make each line a documented adjustment with an explicit plus or minus sign. Reference its entry identifiers or consolidation schedule and capture who approved it.

  3. Chase the residual

    Reconcile to the authoritative counterpart and isolate every unexplained amount. Inspect individual months and high-risk populations even when the full-year residual is zero.

Worked case

From store reporting to group revenue

Store dashboards total €22m for the calendar year. They exclude a newly purchased division with €1.8m external sales during the consolidated period. They include €0.9m internal sales. Signed statements also reflect a €0.2m return reserve absent from the dashboards.

Working

  1. Begin at €22m and add €1.8m to reach €23.8m on the required entity perimeter. Obtain the acquisition-date schedule to confirm the included months.
  2. Subtract internal revenue of €0.9m and the return reserve of €0.2m to reach €22.7m. Each line needs evidence even though both reduce reported revenue.
  3. If signed group sales are €22.5m, an unexplained €0.2m still separates the sources. Check consolidation entries, period cut-offs and whether the reserve was applied to the proper transactions.

Interpretation

A €0.2m residual is not automatically an overstatement. Until resolved, it is a difference for which neither the responsible report nor the appropriate correction has been established.

A common mistake

Inserting an unsupported adjustment to match a published total and using the resulting monthly series as verified.

How to correct it

Preserve the open difference and test both coverage and timing. A management series may reconcile annually while misallocating revenue between months.

Quick check
Accounts show €9.0m of sales before eliminating €0.4m of internal sales. Signed group sales are €8.5m. What remains unexplained?

The adjustment explains €0.4m of the €0.5m gap. The predicted group total is €8.6m, leaving €0.1m that still needs an identified cause.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

The ledger shows €12.9m revenue before group eliminations. Internal sales are €0.6m, and a documented year-end external sale adds €0.2m. Published revenue is €12.4m.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Construct the bridge and identify the residual.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Why does agreeing ten ledger invoices to customer orders not close the issue?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Expected group revenue is €12.9m − €0.6m + €0.2m = €12.5m. Published revenue is €0.1m lower, still unexplained.

Part B 3 marks

That test addresses selected recorded sales. It does not verify entity scope, omitted sales, all consolidation entries or timing. Investigate the consolidation worksheet and trace independent delivery records into the ledger.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How would you identify an unexplained reduction in debtors while group revenue appears to reconcile?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would reconcile the balance using actual billing, cash application, credits and write-offs. That gives €3.4m here, €0.2m above the reported figure. I would keep the difference open and trace manual postings, FX and customer remittances until I can identify its effect. A genuine write-off, an error in the cash import and a change in perimeter have very different implications for earnings and price.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Management finds an unrecorded credit note for €0.2m in the adjustment file. What further check separates an explanation from a justified correction?

Compare with the follow-up answer

I would confirm the note relates to a real credit owed, verify its transaction and reporting dates, then trace where it entered the final receivables and revenue. A document explaining the arithmetic still needs an appropriate economic reason and accounting period.
