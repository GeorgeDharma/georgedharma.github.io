Building standalone earnings for a carved-out business

Build a three-column earnings bridge using shared-service allocations and replacement costs.

In this lecture

  2. Cost the business at three dates
  3. Account for the seller’s remaining obligations
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. A higher transitional bill can depress earnings temporarily
  6. A laboratory migrates off group systems
  7. Independent exam problem
  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Standalone cost adjustments in carve-outs

stranded costs · transition services agreement · TSA

What you should be able to explain

  • Build a three-column earnings bridge using shared-service allocations and replacement costs.
  • Distinguish the seller’s stranded cost, the buyer’s transition expense and one-time migration cash.

Draw the boundary around the activity being sold

A carve-out is a business separated from a larger group. Its legal perimeter can differ from its economic perimeter. A division might employ all its technicians but use the parent’s payroll application, warehouse lease and credit insurance. Map each function to the people, contracts, assets and liabilities that will transfer. Reconcile historical revenue and direct costs to the group ledger using stable identifiers.

For a historical statement, an allocation estimates the service the business consumed as part of the group. A support desk may keep tickets by division. A square-footage driver may suit facilities. Choose a driver with a credible link to demand. A sensible allocation of yesterday’s shared cost can still be a poor estimate of tomorrow’s independent cost.

Cost the business at three dates

A standalone estimate asks what it would cost to operate independently at a specified scale. Obtain a staffing plan and vendors’ quotes. Trace whether the historical division already paid a relevant employee or software license. Replacing an entire allocation while retaining an overlapping direct expense would charge the business twice. Separate savings that only this buyer can realize into a buyer-synergy column.

A transition services agreement, or TSA, purchases temporary help from the former parent. Price each service for its actual duration. An exit can require data migration, retraining or several weeks of parallel operation. Recurring replacement cost belongs in sustainable earnings. Nonrecurring migration cash matters to funding even when the sustainable earnings bridge excludes it.

Account for the seller’s remaining obligations

A stranded cost stays with the seller after the division leaves. Examples include a five-year lease with no break clause and a shared team whose payroll cannot shrink immediately. The seller can recover some of it through temporary TSA receipts. On the buyer side, record the price paid for the TSA. On the seller side, show the cash receipts and the cost of obligations it retains.

Assign a service owner, an end date and evidence to each replacement. Where a software license cannot transfer, request the vendor quote and implementation lead time. A lower quoted annual price cannot justify assuming an earlier exit from the TSA than the systems team can deliver.

Method

Build the result

Independent EBITDA = E + A − R; transition EBITDA = E + A − T − Rₜ.

EBITDA is operating profit before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. E is reported annual divisional EBITDA after relevant allocated cost A. R is annual replacement cost, T is the TSA bill during transition and Rₜ is replacement cost incurred during that same forecast year. All are currency amounts.

  1. Reconcile E and identify exactly which charges A can be removed.
  2. Cost each service independently and allocate the start dates and months of overlap explicitly.
  3. Compute steady-state and transition-year EBITDA separately. Reconcile one-time cash requirements below the operating bridge.
Worked case

A laboratory migrates off group systems

Reported divisional EBITDA is $20m after $5m of group support charges. All these services will be replaced. The annual outside package costs $7m. A four-month TSA costs $0.75m per month, after which the full package starts. Migration consumes $2m in separate one-time cash. Revenue and other costs stay constant.

Working

  1. Earnings before shared support are 20 + 5 = $25m.
  2. Independent sustainable EBITDA is 25 − 7 = $18m.
  3. Transition support totals 4 × 0.75 + 8/12 × 7 = $7.67m. Transition EBITDA is about $17.33m.
  4. The year-one amount after migration cash is $15.33m before tax, investment and working-capital changes. If the seller can remove only $2m of the original $5m cost, it retains $3m annually before TSA receipts or other mitigation.

Decision and interpretation

The buyer can underwrite $18m of sustainable earnings if the quoted package covers every function. Its year-one funding plan must cover both the transition burden and migration.

Quick check
Reported EBITDA of $9m includes a $2m allocation for a service fully replaced at $3m per year. What is independent EBITDA?

Add back the $2m historical cost already charged, then deduct the full $3m replacement. Nine plus two minus three is eight.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A renewables division has €13m of EBITDA after €3.6m of removable support charges. Replacement costs €4.8m per year. It buys six months of TSA at €0.55m monthly and six months of replacement services. Calculate independent and transition EBITDA.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Pre-support earnings are 13 + 3.6 = €16.6m.
  2. Independent EBITDA is 16.6 − 4.8 = €11.8m.
  3. Transition EBITDA is 16.6 − 3.3 − 2.4 = €10.9m. The temporary difference is €0.9m.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How do you estimate the earnings a division can generate after it leaves its parent?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Start with reconciled divisional earnings and identify shared services already charged. Reverse only allocations for services being replaced, then charge the cost of providing each service independently. Prepare a separate transition-year forecast using the actual contract dates, plus a schedule for nonrecurring cash outlays.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if replacement staff must start one month before the TSA ends, with the year-end unchanged?

Compare with the follow-up answer

One additional month of replacement costs 4.8/12 = €0.4m. Transition EBITDA falls to €10.5m. Independent annual EBITDA stays at €11.8m because the overlap ends.
