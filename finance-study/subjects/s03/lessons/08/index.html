Price mechanisms

A completion-accounts clause adjusts the price at closing. A locked-box mechanism fixes a reference balance sheet and controls specified value leakage.

The starting point

Signing and closing can be months apart. Completion accounts measure selected balances at closing and adjust the price using an agreed formula. That makes the final amount sensitive to delivered debt, cash and working capital, so definitions and the dispute process matter.

A locked-box agreement instead fixes the price using an earlier balance sheet. The parties then regulate transfers out of the company between the reference date and closing. The contract identifies prohibited leakage, permitted payments and any compensation for time.

Theory and method

Locked-box equity ticker and leakage reconciliation

Value accrual · Completion-accounts true-up

When can the seller receive more at closing even though a locked-box share price was fixed six months earlier?

A fixed earlier balance sheet can transfer the economic benefit of subsequent trading to the buyer before legal closing. The parties may compensate the seller for time by agreeing an equity ticker, calculated separately from permitted or prohibited transfers to the seller. The amount accrues from a stated reference date to closing under a stated day count. The buyer should trace cash paid out and identify whether the contract already permits it before seeking reimbursement.

How the result is built

Settlement = P₀ + P₀ × i × d/y − L

P₀ is locked-box reference equity price, i the annual contractual simple ticker rate, d elapsed days, y denominator days per year and L recoverable prohibited leakage in euros. This example assumes no other adjustments.

  1. With P₀ = €40m, i = 6%, d = 90 and y = 360, the time payment is €0.60m. The rate and base must come from the agreement.
  2. A €0.25m prohibited dividend is credited euro for euro. A separate €0.10m related-party payment expressly permitted on arm’s-length terms produces no further deduction.
  3. The model settles €40m + €0.60m − €0.25m = €40.35m. If the dividend is physically repaid separately, reconcile gross cash and final net proceeds without taking a second deduction.

When the reasoning holds

  • The contract uses a simple ticker on the original equity price. Agreements may use a daily amount, compounding or no ticker at all.
  • Permitted leakage is specifically defined and within agreed limits. A larger payment or different beneficiary could change recoverability.
Terms in this lesson
Working capital
Money tied up in day-to-day operations. For a deal, agree which receivables, stock and payables count.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Covenant
A promise or condition in a financing agreement, such as maintaining an agreed level of cash.
First example

Suppose a locked-box price is €18m. A €0.4m dividend to the seller after the reference date is prohibited and recoverable euro for euro. All else equal, €0.4m must be repaid or credited under the agreed remedy. Ordinary stock purchases on business terms are different under these assumptions.

What can change between signing and closing?

A business continues to collect cash, buy stock and repay borrowings between the offer and legal transfer. Parties can agree how that movement affects their settlement. Completion accounts measure specified balances at closing, typically followed by a final reconciliation after the buyer obtains reliable records.

A locked box uses an earlier agreed balance sheet to fix an equity price, subject to the contractual leakage and other agreed terms. The seller may receive a separately negotiated time-related amount. Identify exactly what is fixed, what can still adjust and when each party bears commercial changes.

Design a completion calculation

At closing the seller may receive an estimated price. An agreed process prepares and reviews the final accounts under stated definitions, accounting policies and dispute hierarchy. The final adjustment is the difference between final and provisional equity prices. It is not the entire final debt balance again.

Use the agreed reference for each term. A €1m rise in debt lowers equity, a €1m rise in eligible cash raises it, and a €1m working-capital surplus can increase the price under a common formula. The direction comes from the contract, not from who prepared the accounts.

Identify leakage in a locked box

Leakage usually concerns specified value transferred to the seller or connected parties after the reference date, such as an unpermitted dividend. Ordinary payroll or buying inventory is generally economically different. A payment to a connected supplier could be legitimate if permitted and at agreed commercial terms.

Evidence includes bank transfers, related-party ledgers, shareholder decisions and the approved list of permitted payments. A decline in sales after the locked-box date is an operating event and does not automatically become seller leakage. Check covenants and remedies before making a claim.

Method

Read the settlement clause

  1. Mark the dates

    Identify reference date, signing, closing and final reconciliation. Decide which balance sheet or transfer each clause measures and when cash is owed.

  2. Recalculate from scratch

    Build provisional and final prices independently, or derive a signed bridge with one line per change. For a fixed price, identify only adjustments the agreed mechanism permits.

  3. Find the remedy

    State whether the result changes closing consideration, creates a post-closing repayment or belongs in an alternative contractual claim. Track possible overlap.

Worked case

Two ways to settle a parts supplier

A completion formula starts from €60m operating value. Provisional net debt is €8m and working capital equals its €6m target. Final net debt is €9.2m and final working capital is €6.5m. Assume euro-for-euro adjustments and no other items.

Working

  1. Provisional equity price is €52m. Final price is €60m − €9.2m + (€6.5m − €6m) = €51.3m.
  2. Relative to the initial €52m payment, €0.7m is returned to the buyer. The €1.2m higher net debt is partly offset by the €0.5m additional working capital.
  3. In a separate locked-box alternative, suppose the agreed fixed price is €52m and the only recoverable leakage is a €0.3m prohibited dividend. The seller’s net proceeds are €51.7m, regardless of the closing balances under these hypothetical terms.

Interpretation

The two mechanisms allocate different risks. Choosing the larger outcome after the fact would disregard the contract each party originally accepted.

A common mistake

Applying a full completion-accounts adjustment and a locked-box leakage claim to the same cash dividend without checking the agreed mechanism.

How to correct it

Identify the operative clause and whether the single transfer already changed cash in a price adjustment. Calculate contractual consequences once.

Quick check
A contract requires a final closing statement and adds €1 for each €1 by which closing working capital exceeds its target. What mechanism is described?

The price moves with the actual balance at closing. A locked box relies on an earlier balance sheet and regulates subsequent transfers through its leakage provisions.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

Provisional equity price is €20m. Final net debt is €0.6m lower than estimated, while actual working capital falls €0.4m short of the provisional level. A common completion formula applies.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    What is the final price and who pays the difference?

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Under a separate €20m locked box, an approved €0.1m payment and prohibited €0.25m dividend occur. What follows?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Lower net debt raises price by €0.6m and the working-capital change lowers it by €0.4m. Final equity price is €20.2m, requiring €0.2m more for the seller.

Part B 3 marks

Under euro-for-euro recovery of prohibited leakage only, the €0.25m dividend is repaid or credited, leaving €19.75m. The permitted €0.1m does not generate a claim.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

When can the seller receive more at closing even though a locked-box share price was fixed six months earlier?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would read the equity accrual first. Ninety days at 6% adds €0.60m under this 360-day convention. Then I would review the leakage register. The prohibited dividend reduces settlement by €0.25m, giving €40.35m, while a documented permitted supplier payment stays outside the adjustment. I would reconcile both the actual bank transfer and any separate seller reimbursement.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Closing slips a further 30 days. Trading cash falls €0.5m with no prohibited transfer. What changes under these terms?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The ticker earns another €40m × 6% × 30/360 = €0.20m, lifting modeled net settlement to €40.55m. The operating cash decline alone creates no leakage claim in this fixed-price formula. Check separately whether a clearly specified operating covenant or other protection was breached.
