Net debt and debt-like items

Define debt, cash and obligations economically similar to borrowing. Track items across earnings, net debt and working capital to avoid double counting.

The starting point

An agreed operating-business price, often called enterprise value, usually needs adjustment to calculate what shareholders receive. In a simple cash-free, debt-free deal, add eligible cash and subtract debt and agreed debt-like obligations. Apply the exact contract definitions and signs.

An unusual payable can be a financing obligation, an operating balance or excluded under specific terms. Trace where it already enters the calculation. Deducting the same amount in both debt and the working-capital adjustment transfers value twice for one economic obligation.

Theory and method

Enterprise-to-equity bridge and reclassification invariance

Debt-like items · Working-capital reclassification

Moving an overdue payable into net debt appears to lower the price twice. How would you prove where the duplication arose?

A buyer agrees to pay for operating assets with ordinary supplier credit already reflected in a normal working-capital target. An unusual €0.8m payable belongs to financing under the negotiated definition. If it is deducted as debt and remains in actual operating payables, the price falls twice. Reclassifying a closing balance can leave the same economic deduction when its historical benchmark was zero, but the historical normal amount matters if that balance existed throughout the reference period.

How the result is built

Equity = EV + C − D + A − T

Equity is shareholder cash consideration, EV operating value, C eligible cash, D agreed debt and debt-like items, A actual defined working capital and T its agreed target, all in euros.

  1. Start with EV €50m, C €2m, other D €10m. Normal working capital excluding the exceptional payable is €4m delivered and €4m target. Treating the €0.8m item as debt yields €41.2m.
  2. If the item remains an operating payable instead and was absent historically, A becomes €3.2m, T stays €4m and D stays €10m. The same €41.2m follows.
  3. Charging €10.8m debt while measuring A at €3.2m reduces equity to €40.4m. Trace the €0.8m difference to an account entering both deductions.

When the reasoning holds

  • Historical target conditions matter. If the payable normally averaged €0.3m, reclassification of both actual and target changes the relative working-capital effect.
  • The agreed contract controls treatment. A diagnostic bridge can identify duplicate economics without automatically establishing a legal adjustment.
Terms in this lesson
Working capital
Money tied up in day-to-day operations. For a deal, agree which receivables, stock and payables count.
Enterprise value
Value of the business operations attributable to its capital providers. A bridge adjusts it to the amount due to shareholders.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
First example

A business is valued at €50m. Closing figures include €4m of eligible cash, €12m of loans and a €1m settlement payable agreed to be debt-like and excluded from working capital. With working capital on target, shareholders receive €50m + €4m − €12m − €1m = €41m.

Which claims come with the shares?

Equity holders own the residual after other claims. When a price is quoted for operations, a buyer needs a bridge to shareholder proceeds reflecting what cash is delivered and what obligations are assumed. Start with funded debt and identify other claims whose economic effect resembles a financing obligation.

Contract definitions matter. Cash pledged to another creditor, deposits held for customers and money trapped in another entity may not be fully available to the buyer. A shareholder loan may be repaid, transferred or capitalized before closing. Trace each item to who ultimately receives or pays cash.

Why operating bills are treated differently

Ordinary unpaid supplier invoices continually arise in operating and are generally reflected in a working-capital mechanism where agreed. An overdue legacy settlement, an exceptional shareholder liability or unpaid transaction fee may instead be defined as debt-like. Classification depends on the terms and the cash-flow economics of the specific balance.

Changing a classification without updating both the actual balance and historical working-capital target can create an artificial price effect. Build a grid showing each material account’s location in earnings, debt, working capital and forecast cash. More than one location can be valid only when genuinely different effects are being measured.

Estimate uncertain and related items

An indemnity, pension obligation or tax dispute can have uncertain payout, duration and enforceability. One may be valued probabilistically, excluded by contract, or protected separately. Do not automatically convert an accounting provision into an identical cash deduction. Inspect timing, probability and the contract’s remedy.

Distinguish the same cash flow appearing twice from two effects of the same event. A one-time legal expense can be added back to recurring earnings while an actual unpaid settlement reduces closing equity, if they concern future earning power and a distinctly payable amount. Explain the traces.

Method

Construct an enterprise-to-equity bridge

  1. Inventory claims

    Start from a legal-entity balance sheet and supporting bank or lender confirmations. Identify cash availability, principal, accrued interest and settlement obligations as defined for closing.

  2. Assign one consistent treatment

    Map each closing cash claim into debt, operating working capital, a separate remedy or exclusion. Recalculate historical peg inputs when required for consistency.

  3. Verify the economics

    Check that operating value and bridge refer to the same perimeter, date and claim. State any item depending on unresolved evidence rather than presenting a false exact price.

Worked case

A specialist insurer-service provider

Agreed operating value is €80m. Bank funds total €9m, of which €2m is client money excluded under the deal. Funded debt is €14m. A €1.5m deferred purchase payment is debt-like. The seller’s unpaid fees are €0.5m and excluded from working capital. Normal operating working capital is delivered.

Working

  1. Eligible cash is €7m. Funded debt plus debt-like obligations equals €14m + €1.5m + €0.5m = €16m. Defined net debt is €9m.
  2. Equity proceeds are €80m + €7m − €16m = €71m. The client cash is also outside the operating working-capital calculation under the stated case.
  3. Had the €0.5m fee already reduced actual working capital against a comparable target, the current price would be too low by a duplicate €0.5m deduction unless corrected under the agreed treatment.

Interpretation

Whether funds sit in a bank account is insufficient evidence of beneficial ownership. Trust terms or the client agreement can determine whose cash the buyer receives.

A common mistake

Adding every positive bank balance and deducting all booked liabilities, including normal operating payables already priced via working capital.

How to correct it

Check ownership and accessibility of cash. Reconcile claims to their contract treatments and distinguish operating liabilities from assumed financing obligations.

Quick check
A €0.6m payable already reduced the price through net debt. It also reduced actual working capital below the target by €0.6m. The contract intends one deduction. What correction follows?

The same claim has already reduced what shareholders receive. Align actual working capital and its target on a consistent basis so the pricing bridge captures the obligation once.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

The price of operations is €46m. There is €5m cash including €1m pledged and excluded, €11m borrowing, €0.8m accrued interest defined as debt-like and €0.4m excess operating working capital.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    What is the equity price under these terms?

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    A seller says the interest has already reduced income and should never be deducted. Evaluate.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Eligible cash is €4m. Equity equals €46m + €4m − €11m − €0.8m + €0.4m = €38.6m. The pledged €1m receives no add-back under the case terms.

Part B 3 marks

Historical interest is a financing cost in the income statement and may be outside the operating measure used to set €46m. Unpaid accrued interest is a distinct closing payment. Check the operating valuation and whether €11m already includes it.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Moving an overdue payable into net debt appears to lower the price twice. How would you prove where the duplication arose?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would rebuild the bridge under each treatment and trace the historical peg. A financing deduction and an operating shortfall can be economically equivalent if this payable has no normal historical balance. Here both supported treatments give €41.2m. Only the mixed calculation produces €40.4m. I would show the contract owner the duplicate account and quantify any historical average before proposing a correction.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The payable averaged €0.3m in the target history and the agreement expressly moves it to debt in both periods. What changes versus keeping it operating?

Compare with the follow-up answer

As an operating balance it lowers actual by €0.8m and target by €0.3m, so the incremental shortfall is €0.5m. Debt treatment deducts the full €0.8m while removing both operating balances. The resulting €0.3m difference reflects the historical funding allowance that disappeared.
