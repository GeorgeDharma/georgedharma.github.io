Consolidating a step acquisition and measuring minority ownership

Calculate prior-stake remeasurement and goodwill when a minority investment becomes a controlled business.

Theory and terminology

Step acquisitions and non-controlling interests

IFRS 3 · partial goodwill · full goodwill · previously held interest

What you should be able to explain

  • Calculate prior-stake remeasurement and goodwill when a minority investment becomes a controlled business.
  • Reconcile cash paid, consolidated net assets and the interest retained by outside shareholders.

Find the event that changes the reporting boundary

A parent controls a business when its rights give it power over relevant activities, exposure or rights to variable returns, and the ability to use that power to affect its returns. A majority of ordinary voting stock is often informative; shareholder agreements and substantive rights can change the result. Here the transactions unequivocally confer control on the stated date.

Before a step acquisition an investor might report an investment in an associate as a single line. When control first passes, remove that investment line and bring the controlled business’s assets and liabilities into the group accounts. Separately identify the non-controlling interest, or NCI, meaning the equity held by other owners. Consolidating 100% of revenue or assets can therefore coexist with attribution of only part of net profit to the parent.

Measure what the newly controlling owner already held

A buyer may have bought its first stake years ago at a different valuation. Under the illustrated IFRS acquisition method, remeasure that prior equity interest at fair value on the control date. The difference from its carrying amount is recognized in profit or loss or other comprehensive income as appropriate. Previous amounts in other comprehensive income follow the rules applicable to a disposal of that interest.

For these cases assume a conventional equity-accounted prior stake, no accumulated other comprehensive income, and a remeasurement gain recorded in profit or loss. The gain generally is not cash generated on the control date. Using the carrying amount instead of fair value understates or overstates the goodwill residual as well.

Choose a permitted NCI measure and reconcile

For qualifying present ownership interests entitling holders to a proportionate share of net assets on liquidation, the acquisition method permits transaction-by-transaction measurement at fair value or at the outsiders’ share of recognized identifiable net assets. Measuring NCI at fair value is often called full goodwill. The proportionate-net-assets method is often called partial goodwill. Other types of NCI may have different requirements.

The 100% identifiable net assets entering consolidation are unchanged by that choice. A higher initial NCI increases goodwill dollar for dollar, all else equal. An NCI fair value may differ from a simple gross-up of the price paid for a controlling block because control rights or liquidity differ. The choice changes reported amounts without changing the cash the parent actually paid.

Method

Build the result

Goodwill = C + H + N − A; prior-stake gain in the stipulated case = H − B.

C is fair value of new consideration at control, H fair value of the previous holding, N the chosen measure of eligible NCI, A 100% of identifiable net assets acquired and B the prior carrying amount. Under proportionate measurement N = outside ownership fraction × A. All values use the acquisition-date currency.

  1. Establish the control date and reconcile the percentages bought, previously held and still outside.
  2. Remeasure the old stake and determine the 100% acquisition-date net asset values. Calculate N under the applicable election.
  3. Reconcile identifiable net assets plus goodwill to consideration, old stake and NCI. Report actual cash paid separately.
Worked case

A distributor moves from associate to subsidiary

A parent owns 20% of a distributor with carrying amount £14m and acquisition-date fair value £22m. It pays £66m for another 55% and gains control. Other investors hold the remaining 25% in qualifying ordinary shares with fair value £24m. Identifiable assets at acquisition are £122m and liabilities £32m. Ignore tax and previously accumulated other comprehensive income.

Working

  1. The parent controls 75%. Net identifiable assets acquired are 122 − 32 = £90m. Remeasuring the prior stake gives a £22m − £14m = £8m noncash gain under the stipulated prior accounting.
  2. With fair-value NCI, goodwill is 66 + 22 + 24 − 90 = £22m. Check that £90m net assets plus £22m goodwill equal the £112m measured claims and consideration.
  3. With proportionate NCI, the outside share is 25% × £90m = £22.5m. Goodwill is 66 + 22 + 22.5 − 90 = £20.5m. Both NCI and goodwill fall by £1.5m.
  4. Actual new cash paid is £66m with either choice. If subsequent post-acquisition profit is £12m after all consolidation adjustments, ordinary profit allocation is £9m parent and £3m NCI on these simple rights.

Decision and interpretation

The board can reconcile reported asset growth and the £8m noncash gain without describing either as new operating cash. Substantive rights and independent valuation of the remaining stake are material inputs.

Quick check
In an acquisition of control, eligible NCI is initially measured €3m higher under fair-value measurement than under proportionate measurement. What changes, all else equal?

NCI is added in the goodwill formula. A €3m increase in its measured amount increases goodwill by €3m while identifiable net assets and the cash cheque stay fixed.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A company’s old 30% associate has a €20m carrying amount and €24m fair value. It pays €48m for another 50%, obtaining control. Eligible outside shares have €17m fair value. Net identifiable assets are €70m. Use the same prior-accounting assumptions. Compute both goodwill measures and the prior gain.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The buyer now controls 80%. The prior-stake remeasurement gain is 24 − 20 = €4m.
  2. Fair-value NCI produces goodwill of 48 + 24 + 17 − 70 = €19m.
  3. Proportionate NCI is 20% × 70 = €14m. The resulting goodwill is €16m, a €3m difference corresponding exactly to the NCI difference.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a company buy only another 55% of a business and put 100% of its assets on the consolidated balance sheet?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Obtaining control changes the reporting boundary. The group recognizes all the acquired business’s identifiable assets and liabilities, measures its prior holding at fair value and presents the outside owners’ interest separately in equity. The cash paid for the additional stake is only one term in acquisition-date goodwill.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose instead the parent already controlled 80% and then purchased another 10%, retaining control. Would it perform another acquisition-date remeasurement?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Under the consolidated reporting rules illustrated here the additional purchase is an equity transaction with owners. Adjust the parent and NCI equity accounts as appropriate. A change in ownership without a change of control does not create fresh acquisition goodwill.
