Accounting for earn-outs and seller employment payments

Value a contingent cash payment and reconcile acquisition-date goodwill.

Theory and terminology

Contingent acquisition consideration

earn-out · seller compensation · acquisition accounting

What you should be able to explain

  • Value a contingent cash payment and reconcile acquisition-date goodwill.
  • Explain how a later performance event, qualifying acquisition-date evidence and a service condition change the accounting.

Read what earns the payment

An earn-out gives sellers more consideration if a defined outcome occurs after completion. It can bridge different beliefs about future cash flow. Read the beneficiary, payment date, performance measure and cancellation clauses. If all sellers receive the same amount per share regardless of future employment, the payment can be part of the acquisition price.

If a seller must remain employed and automatically forfeits a payment on leaving, a substantive continued-service requirement points to compensation for that service. Evaluate the arrangement and facts, including whether the service condition is substantive. Separate service pay from what the buyer transferred for the business. Otherwise goodwill can absorb an employee cost that belongs in later periods.

Measure the acquisition-date exchange

In the IFRS acquisition method illustrated by AASB 3, measure genuine contingent consideration at fair value on the acquisition date. The contractual maximum is an upper bound on nominal payment. A valuation considers possible outcomes, time to payment and market-participant risk assumptions. The simple binary cases here supply an agreed probability and discount rate. More complex thresholds can require simulations.

Add acquisition-date fair value to the other consideration. In a 100% purchase with no earlier ownership, goodwill equals total consideration less the acquisition-date fair value of identifiable net assets. Deduct assumed liabilities when computing those net assets. Opening goodwill is a residual and the earn-out liability is a separate entry.

Follow the liability after control passes

For cash contingent consideration classified as a financial liability, remeasure fair value at subsequent reporting dates. A change due to new post-close trading goes to profit or loss. Contingent consideration that actually qualifies as equity follows different subsequent accounting and is settled within equity. Promising to settle in shares does not by itself establish equity classification.

A measurement-period adjustment asks a different question. If initial accounting was provisional and new information establishes facts that existed at acquisition, adjust the acquisition-date numbers retrospectively as required. That period ends when the needed information arrives or cannot be obtained, and is at most one year. Winning a genuinely new contract after the acquisition is a later event.

Method

Build the result

FV₀ = p × M/(1 + r)ⁿ; goodwill = C + FV₀ − N.

FV₀ is the acquisition-date value of an illustrative cash liability, p the assumed probability of the sole positive payout M, r its annual discount rate and n years until settlement. C is cash paid at closing and N the identifiable net assets acquired, all in one currency.

  1. Calculate the probability-weighted payment, then discount to the transaction date. Assume the supplied rate and probability incorporate risk consistently.
  2. Enter the resulting amount once in total consideration and once as a liability in the opening entries.
  3. Compare later fair value with the liability carrying amount. Identify whether the change reflects a new event, an eligible measurement-period adjustment or simple settlement.
Worked case

A licence milestone changes the buyer’s reported result

A buyer pays $54m for 100% of a business with $46m of identifiable net assets. All former owners are entitled to an extra $12m in two years if a commercial milestone is met. At closing the probability is 60.5% and the annual discount rate is 10%. One year later post-close sales raise the probability to 77%.

Working

  1. At close the expected payout is 0.605 × 12 = $7.26m. Discount for two years to 7.26/1.10² = $6m.
  2. Total consideration is $60m. Recognize $14m of goodwill and a separate $6m contingent liability.
  3. After year one, the expected payout is $9.24m. Its one-year discounted amount is 9.24/1.10 = $8.40m. Record a $2.40m increase in the liability through profit or loss in this simplified case.
  4. If the milestone succeeds and $12m is paid at maturity, the remaining increase is $3.60m before settlement. Paying the recorded balance extinguishes the liability. Opening goodwill is still $14m, assuming no other adjustment or impairment.

Decision and interpretation

The acquisition model should carry a cash range up to $12m even though the opening liability is $6m. The board also needs a bridge explaining how a successful milestone can reduce reported post-close profit.

Quick check
A cash earn-out liability rises by £1m solely because the acquired company won a new contract after completion. What normally changes?

New post-close performance changes the financial liability’s fair value. The amount is recognized through profit or loss under the acquisition rules used here.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

An acquirer pays €30m cash for net assets worth €25m. An independent seller can receive €8m one year later with probability 52.5%. Use a 5% discount rate. Subsequent trading changes the liability value to €6.3m. Find the opening goodwill and later effect.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Expected payout is 8 × 0.525 = €4.2m. Opening fair value is 4.2/1.05 = €4m.
  2. Opening goodwill is 30 + 4 − 25 = €9m.
  3. The later liability increases from €4m to €6.3m, producing a €2.3m expense from new conditions.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why can a target doing better than expected produce an expense for its buyer?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A seller earn-out can leave the buyer with a financial liability. Better post-close performance makes the expected payment larger. Under the acquisition rules illustrated here that increase goes through profit or loss, even as the purchased business improves.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the agreement instead automatically cancels the entire bonus if the former owner leaves their substantive two-year employment. What changes?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Evaluate the continued-service arrangement as remuneration. For the stated substantive service condition the payment is compensation for post-close work. With no other consideration the opening goodwill on these figures is 30 − 25 = €5m. Recognize the service-related expense under the applicable remuneration rules.
