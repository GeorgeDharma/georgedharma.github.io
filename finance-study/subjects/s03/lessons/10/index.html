Findings and uncertainty

Distinguish evidence, company explanations, unresolved requests and estimated exposure. A weakly supported adjustment can matter more than a small precisely measured one.

The starting point

A useful finding distinguishes the amount verified, the explanation offered and the evidence still missing. A payment appearing in the ledger is an observation. Whether it will recur next year is an inference that needs contracts, history or an operational explanation.

Show the range of exposure and how the decision changes across it. A narrow uncertainty may be immaterial. A wide uncertainty can change the price ceiling or require a condition before closing. Assign responsibility for the remaining evidence and avoid presenting a midpoint as a measured fact.

Theory and method

Expected value of perfect information in diligence

Value of information · Decision threshold

How much should the buyer spend to verify a disputed customer contract before it can walk away?

A buyer has a good deal if a customer renews and a bad one if it leaves. Verification only helps if the result can change the buyer’s action or price before signing. Calculate the best attainable decision using the evidence currently available. Then give the buyer perfect knowledge of each outcome and allow it to choose again. The improvement is an upper bound on the cost of investigation. A real test with mistakes or delays is worth less.

How the result is built

EVPI = Σₛ pₛ maxₐ U(a,s) − maxₐ Σₛ pₛ U(a,s)

EVPI is expected value of perfect information in euros, s denotes possible states, pₛ each state’s probability, a a feasible action and U(a,s) its euro payoff. max selects the best of the available actions.

  1. Buying yields +€3m on renewal with 60% probability and −€2m otherwise. Walking yields zero. Before extra evidence the better action is buying with expected value €1m.
  2. Knowing the state first allows buying only on renewal. That selective policy generates 60% × €3m = €1.8m expected value before test cost.
  3. Perfect information improves value by €0.8m. A €0.25m definitive test delivers €0.55m net improvement over the best current choice if the opportunity remains open.

When the reasoning holds

  • The buyer retains the right to act differently after the evidence. When signing is irrevocable the same test can have little pre-decision value.
  • The test result must address the uncertain economic state. Verifying that a contract existed last year can leave next year’s renewal unresolved.
Terms in this lesson
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
First example

A contract dispute could cost €0.2m–€1.0m under two documented scenarios. A €0.6m midpoint is not an expected cost until the probabilities are supported. A buyer can model both outcomes and test whether the bid still works in the larger one.

Facts and estimates have different evidential weight

A good findings report lets a reader reproduce the evidence before accepting the recommendation. A signed settlement specifies an obligation. A manager’s statement that settlement is likely is a hypothesis. A range produced by multiplying contract volumes by a disputed rebate rate is an estimate, with assumptions that should remain visible.

Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence. If a requested side letter has not arrived, the analyst cannot value it at zero simply because the data room lacks it. Nor should an unverified rumour be deducted as a fixed debt. Describe what changes in each credible state.

Quantify sensitivity with a decision in mind

The useful number may be a threshold, not an expected value. If a bid creates €1m of headroom before an unresolved claim, find the claim size or probability that consumes that headroom. With a single cash claim paid today, expected exposure is probability multiplied by amount, only when the probability has evidential support.

A valuation multiple amplifies a recurring earnings issue, whereas a fixed one-time payment often affects equity euro for euro. Treating them identically can hide the most important issue. Check dependence if several findings stem from one customer contract or one disputed accounting choice.

Select a response and an evidence owner

Choose a mechanism matching the problem. Missing proof of ownership may need resolution before closing. A clearly defined contingent tax cost might be allocated by a collectible indemnity. Falling sustainable earnings usually require a revised forecast or price. The decision must show which risk the buyer still bears.

Document who should obtain the outstanding evidence and by when. A statement that a claim is “covered” should identify the cap, deductible, time limit and ability to collect. If the buyer keeps the downside above a cap, show it explicitly in the sensitivity.

Method

Write the finding

  1. Lead with the observed fact

    Name the dates, affected population, records inspected and coverage limitations. Put management’s interpretation on its own line and note any contrary evidence.

  2. Express the exposure

    Separate recurring and one-time consequences, calculate credible scenarios and identify the input most capable of changing the bid. Label observed, assumed and unresolved amounts.

  3. Tie it to a decision

    Recommend one action that follows from the evidence available now. Identify a new fact or threshold that would change that action.

Worked case

An uncertain sales rebate

A supplier might owe a customer €2m, or nothing, for past rebates. The deal produces €0.8m buyer gain before this item. The possible payment is immediate. A separate €0.1m annual servicing cost is verified and should reduce sustainable earnings at 5 times. No overlap exists.

Working

  1. The recurring servicing cost reduces indicated value by €0.5m, leaving €0.3m headroom. This is conceptually separate from the old rebate.
  2. Break-even probability for an uncovered €2m payment is €0.3m/€2m = 15% under an expected-value decision. At 25%, expected rebate exposure is €0.5m and expected buyer gain becomes −€0.2m.
  3. If a fully collectible indemnity caps buyer cash loss at €0.4m, expected buyer exposure at 25% would be €0.1m and expected gain €0.2m. Inspect the cap wording and seller’s ability to pay.

Interpretation

A positive expected value can still hide an unaffordable downside. Compare the maximum cash loss and payment timing with the buyer’s financing capacity.

A common mistake

Assigning a 50% probability because there are two possible outcomes.

How to correct it

A binary outcome gives two states, not equal probabilities. Use supported estimates or show the break-even probability and request evidence that could place the case on one side.

Quick check
Management estimates a €2m refund, but the underlying agreement and customer confirmation are missing. How should diligence present the figure?

An unsupported claim has no automatic probability. Separating verified amounts from conditional ones lets the decision-maker see the consequence of missing evidence.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A bid leaves €0.9m headroom. A verified contract cost reduces future operating profit by €0.1m annually. Use 4 times recurring profit. A separate €1m historical dispute could become fully payable immediately.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    What is the break-even dispute probability after the verified adjustment?

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    The seller offers to reimburse no more than €0.3m if the dispute is lost. What changes?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The recurring shortfall costs €0.4m of value, leaving €0.5m headroom. A €1m binary payment consumes that in expectation at 50%, ignoring discounting and other risks.

Part B 3 marks

Conditional buyer loss falls to €0.7m if reimbursable and collectible. Break-even probability becomes €0.5m/€0.7m = 71.4%. Confirm claim scope, expiry and seller capacity before using it.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How much should the buyer spend to verify a disputed customer contract before it can walk away?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would compare two decision policies. On current evidence it is rational to buy because expected gain is €1m. Perfect advance knowledge produces €1.8m by allowing the buyer to acquire only after renewal. That leaves up to €0.8m available to pay for verification. If the actual test is slower or noisy I would model its possible readings before authorizing even that much.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The buyer has already promised to buy on identical terms in either state. Would a perfect renewal test still have €0.8m of decision value?

Compare with the follow-up answer

For the fixed buy-versus-walk decision the test can no longer avoid the loss, so that €0.8m benefit disappears. It might still inform a new action, such as customer retention investment or financing. Calculate the payoff of that remaining choice separately rather than preserving the original information value.
