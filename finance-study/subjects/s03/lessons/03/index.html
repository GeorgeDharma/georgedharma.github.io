Sustainable earnings

Separate normal business costs from defensible one-off items. Repeated exceptional expenses can be part of the cost of running the business.

The starting point

Sustainable earnings estimate what the present operations can reasonably earn under ordinary conditions. Begin with reported profit and examine individual items. A factory fire may be genuinely unusual. Repeated reorganization charges may be a routine cost of running a changing business.

An adjustment also needs a replacement-cost test. A founder working for free makes reported profit look high if a paid manager will be needed after the sale. Keep future buyer-specific savings separate from the target’s recurring earnings so the buyer can value them independently.

Theory and method

Quality-of-earnings normalization and run-rate adjustment

Adjusted EBITDA bridge · Standalone replacement cost

Management wants a full-year add-back for the annual salary of an eliminated role. What amount would you accept?

A manufacturer removes a senior role halfway through its accounting year. Its last twelve months still include six months of salary. Annualizing the new organization means adjusting the amount actually embedded in that period. That differs from forecasting the benefit for a future twelve-month interval. If a consultant must replace some work, the sustainable saving is smaller again. Track the historical charge, effective change date, replacement cost and any unfulfilled measures before accepting an add-back.

How the result is built

Normalized EBITDA = reported EBITDA − nonrecurring gains + verified nonrecurring costs + embedded obsolete cost − missing ongoing replacement cost

EBITDA means earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Each term is a euro amount for the same twelve-month period. Embedded obsolete cost is the portion still contained in that measured period.

  1. A role cost €0.24m annually and ended six months ago. Reported EBITDA includes only €0.12m of its historic salary. Removing a full €0.24m would reverse €0.12m twice.
  2. The replacement consultant costs €0.08m per full year, but €0.04m is already recorded for the last six months. Add the €0.12m old charge and deduct only the missing €0.04m new charge.
  3. The net run-rate adjustment is +€0.08m for this measured period. At 8 times, that contributes €0.64m of indicated operating value, subject to durability.

When the reasoning holds

  • The original role has been genuinely removed. An open vacancy for substantially the same work is a future expense requiring a defensible replacement estimate.
  • The consultant covers the required duties on sustainable terms. A temporary discount or deferred recruitment creates a different normalized cost.
Terms in this lesson
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Check how the seller calculates and adjusts this measure.
Synergy
Extra value expected from combining businesses, such as a saving attainable only after a merger.
First example

A company reports €4m of operating profit after a verified €0.3m nonrecurring relocation. The founder is unpaid, but a replacement manager would cost €0.2m. The normalized estimate is €4.1m. Adding back the relocation alone would leave the continuing management cost out.

Recurring is an economic judgment

A seller’s adjusted profit often starts with earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization, abbreviated EBITDA. Removing financing and certain noncash charges can help compare operations, but EBITDA still depends on accounting choices and can exclude large real demands for investment.

Normalizing earnings asks what these operations would earn under a defensible ongoing arrangement. A charge with an exceptional label may recur every year as a succession of restructuring programmes. Conversely, a single relocation can be genuinely unusual if the move is complete and future costs reflect the new premises.

Set a sustainable replacement cost

The buyer needs the people, buildings and systems required to operate independently. If an owner pays herself no salary, replace that missing expense with a realistic market cost. If the seller charges unusually high rent, examine the binding lease and when a market-rate alternative is achievable.

A buyer’s ability to close another site is buyer-specific synergy. Putting that saving into normalized standalone earnings and then adding it again in the integration model inflates the case. Maintain independent columns for historical normalization, achievable standalone changes and changes available only after combination.

Link past adjustments to forecast cash

An accepted add-back is a change to an earnings indicator. It does not reverse a real payment made in the past or erase an outstanding claim. If a one-time lawsuit produced a valid cash payable, adjusting recurring earnings and recognizing the cash obligation can both be appropriate if they represent different effects.

Build a track record over several years. Check invoices, board decisions, staffing needs and subsequent trading. Where the future cost depends on uncertain volume or an expiring subsidy, present a range and explain why a single favourable year is unrepresentative.

Method

Test an adjustment schedule

  1. Start with reported earnings

    Tie the opening measure to verified statements. State which expenses it excludes by definition before accepting seller adjustments, since an excluded expense cannot be added back again.

  2. Apply symmetric treatment

    Remove nonrecurring gains as well as genuinely nonrecurring costs. Replace missing ordinary expenses and quantify changes needed to operate independently.

  3. Translate cautiously

    Recalculate the indicated operating price at the case multiple. Then test capital expenditure, working capital and cash obligations separately before forming a view on value.

Worked case

Buying a family-owned repair network

Reported EBITDA is €5.4m. It includes a €0.7m nonrecurring lease surrender gain, €0.3m verified completed relocation cost and €0.2m recurrent “strategic review” fees. The unpaid owner performs a role costing €0.25m to replace.

Working

  1. Start from €5.4m, subtract the €0.7m gain and reverse €0.3m for the completed relocation. Maintaining the strategic work is an ordinary ongoing cost in the case, so its €0.2m stays expensed.
  2. Deduct the €0.25m replacement salary to obtain €4.75m sustainable EBITDA. At 8 times, indicated operating value is €38m.
  3. Using the reported €5.4m would give €43.2m, a €5.2m difference. Investigate whether the relocation also permanently raised rent before accepting the adjusted figure without another change.

Interpretation

A multiple is an assumption about growth, future investment and risk. The €38m indication does not independently establish that comparable businesses deserve eight times EBITDA.

A common mistake

Automatically reversing every cost a seller calls exceptional while leaving all favourable one-off gains in earnings.

How to correct it

Require evidence of nonrecurrence and apply the same principle to gains, costs and missing replacement labour.

Quick check
Operating profit of €2m includes a nonrecurring €0.25m gain on a property sale and a verified €0.10m one-off expense. What is normalized operating profit?

Remove the unusual gain and reverse the unusual cost. The arithmetic is €2.00m − €0.25m + €0.10m. The amounts move in opposite directions.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

An owner reports €3.2m EBITDA after a genuine €0.4m flood cleanup. It contains a €0.25m one-off grant and no expense for a recurring €0.15m administrative role. Assume replacement is necessary.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute sustainable EBITDA and indicated value at 7 times.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What if flood damage happens frequently and annual prevention costs €0.12m?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

€3.2m + €0.4m − €0.25m − €0.15m = €3.2m. At seven times that is €22.4m. Offset in the total does not make the adjustments individually irrelevant.

Part B 3 marks

Check flood history, insurance terms and the prevention plan. If €0.12m is a necessary recurring preventive expense after the one-time cleanup, reduce sustainable earnings to €3.08m and the indication to €21.56m.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Management wants a full-year add-back for the annual salary of an eliminated role. What amount would you accept?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would reconstruct the actual payroll month by month. Six months of old salary contribute €0.12m, and the period already includes €0.04m of a full €0.08m replacement cost. I would accept €0.08m net, provided the duties have transferred permanently. For the buyer I would show the evidence and the €1.28m price difference versus the seller’s requested adjustment.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The buyer could absorb the consulting work using its own team after completion. Does that change standalone normalized EBITDA?

Compare with the follow-up answer

I would retain the necessary independent consultant in the standalone analysis. The buyer can separately model its own incremental staff capacity and any extra cost as a combination benefit. Combining these two lenses without tracing the baseline would allow the same saving to support two price increases.
