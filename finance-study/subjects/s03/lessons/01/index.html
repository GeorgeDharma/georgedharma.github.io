Financial Due Diligence and Transaction Accounting
Advanced finance / Lesson 1 of 13

Define the scope

Connect each diligence question to a valuation or contractual decision. A possible earnings issue and a verified cash obligation require different evidence.

The starting point

A buyer has limited time to inspect a business. Start with the decision that could change. Will an issue alter expected profit, increase the cash needed at closing, require a guarantee or make the buyer withdraw? The answer determines which documents and time periods matter.

Set a threshold for deeper investigation, but leave room for small amounts with wider consequences. A small unexplained payment each month can reveal a much larger contractual commitment. Record the claim, how to test it, who provides the evidence and the date a decision is needed.

Theory and method

Financial-statement assertions and risk-based diligence scoping

Occurrence · Completeness · Cut-off

A seller offers 200 invoices to prove its customer-revenue forecast. What does that population let you test?

A distributor can provide genuine invoices for transactions already in its ledger while omitting returns or losing its largest customer next month. Each potential error calls for a different test. Start with the buyer’s decision, then specify the assertion about an account, a date or a future event. Direction matters. Moving from recorded sales to delivery evidence tests whether recorded activity occurred. Moving from delivery logs to recorded sales tests whether the activity was included.

How the result is built

Decision → assertion → population → direction of test → proposed adjustment or open item

Decision identifies a price or contractual term in euros. Assertion names the property to test. Population specifies the entities and period. Direction identifies the independent starting record. The final field explains the decision consequence.

  1. If €0.30m of claimed annual profit might disappear and the price is seven times recurring earnings, the issue can move the operating indication by €2.10m.
  2. For occurrence, begin at recorded invoices and find matching dispatch and acceptance. For completeness start from independent dispatch or return records and trace into accounting entries.
  3. For forecast durability, inspect contract expiry, independently supported renewal progress and customer contribution. Estimate the profit at risk and state which evidence could lift or lower it.

When the reasoning holds

  • The independent source must cover the same entities and dates. An incomplete shipping log cannot establish that all unbilled activity was found.
  • A targeted unusual-item sample can reveal a mechanism. Extrapolating its error frequency to all trading requires a suitable sampling design.
Terms in this lesson
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
First example

A seller says €90,000 of monthly rent disappears after the deal. Inspect the lease term and termination rights, then inspect the buyer’s plans for the site. A five-year obligation with no usable alternative is a very different finding from a cancellable contract on an empty building.

A diligence question needs a decision

A financial review has a deadline and a buyer with limited investigative time. Start with the decision that could change. If earnings depend on a distributor renewing next year, the decision is whether the business justifies the proposed price under plausible renewal outcomes. A list of all invoices would be an expensive first request.

Set the perimeter before testing. Identify the legal entities, operations, dates and currencies included in the sale. A parent’s report may include a profitable division that will stay with the seller. No amount of detailed margin analysis rescues an incorrectly defined asset.

Different claims require different records

An assertion that sales occurred can be tested with contracts, fulfilment evidence and collections. An assertion that a customer will remain requires renewal terms, history, concentration and evidence of demand. A ledger can show how much was recorded without establishing either performance or repeatability.

Evidence has dimensions of independence, completeness and relevance. A signed contract may establish a cancellation right, but a later signed amendment may supersede it. A random invoice sample can estimate frequency. A deliberate sample of unusual transactions can reveal mechanisms but cannot establish their frequency without further work.

Allocate effort by decision value

Materiality measures how a finding might influence a decision. Repeated small charges can matter at a large valuation multiple. A low-probability event can matter if it stops the business operating. Set quantitative thresholds alongside qualitative triggers such as unreliable records or conditions essential to completing the purchase.

Assign an owner, deadline and consequence to missing evidence. A buyer might condition completion on evidence of a licence, model an ordinary commercial uncertainty in cash flows, or seek protection for a past liability. A request matters when its answer could genuinely change the decision.

Method

Draft a focused request

  1. Define the dependency

    Write the claim needed for the buyer’s forecast to hold. Narrow it to one population and period and identify the assumptions it affects.

  2. Choose a test

    Specify the record, the independent comparison and what result would refute the claim. Ask for evidence proportionate to the possible price effect.

  3. State the outcome

    Link credible findings to a changed input, a contract term or a decision to stop. Document what still cannot be inferred even if the test passes.

Worked case

A maintenance company’s contract renewal

A buyer uses 7 times recurring operating earnings. A €4m contract contributes a 25% margin. The seller assumes certain renewal, while the buyer’s initial case gives renewal a 60% chance. Assume no substitute sales and apply the same multiple to the recurring earnings difference.

Working

  1. Annual profit at risk is €4m × 25% = €1m. A loss could reduce the multiple-based operating value by €7m, holding everything else fixed.
  2. Relative to the seller’s case, a 40% loss probability implies €0.4m lower expected operating earnings and €2.8m less indicated operating value in this simplified model.
  3. Request the signed contract and renewal history, the procurement timetable and direct renewal evidence if available. A later price cut could preserve sales but lower margin, so a simple yes/no renewal test has limits.

Interpretation

The €2.8m is scenario arithmetic, not an observed claim. Contract remaining life and the evidence for the 60% probability should be challenged before treating it as a bid adjustment.

A common mistake

Requesting proof of historical billing as the only evidence that a forecast renewal is secure.

How to correct it

Historical billing tests past activity. Contract rights and ongoing commercial discussions address the distinct question of whether profitable revenue can continue.

Quick check
Management says a large invoice is a one-off advisory cost that should be added back to profit. Which evidence addresses that claim most directly?

The work performed and the history of comparable bills explain whether that expense is likely to recur. Cash on hand and the return target answer different questions.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A branch proposed for sale shares headquarters with an excluded division. Its forecast assumes €0.5m annual support cost, but an independent quote is €0.8m. The buyer values the operation at 6 times recurring earnings.

    Quantify the indication and frame a focused request.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The €0.3m annual gap implies €1.8m lower operating value at the same multiple. Request the service list, existing allocations and comparable replacement quotes covering the same responsibilities.

Part B 3 marks

The external quote may include extra services, or a transition contract may cover some costs temporarily. Separate recurring stand-alone needs from temporary transition payments and price each on its own timing.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A seller offers 200 invoices to prove its customer-revenue forecast. What does that population let you test?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would accept the invoices as one test of recorded revenue, provided the selection and period are clear. Then I would request the independent dispatch and returns population and the contracts supporting the forward forecast. A set chosen by the seller cannot prove completeness or retention. I would prioritize the additional work by its €2.10m potential price consequence and make an unresolved finding visible to the buyer.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Only the top ten customers are checked, covering 70% of revenue. Can you say 70% of all accounting errors were tested?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. The coverage percentage describes revenue value, while error patterns may concentrate in small customers, new accounts or year-end returns. I would state the coverage precisely, add tests drawn from the remaining population and separately test the process that could generate omitted transactions.
