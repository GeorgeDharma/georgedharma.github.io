Acquisition accounting

Recognize eligible assets and liabilities at the measurement basis the applicable standard requires. In a simple 100% purchase, goodwill is the residual after identifiable net assets.

The starting point

After buying a business, the acquirer must explain what it acquired in its accounts. Under the acquisition method, identify the assets and liabilities separately and measure them under the applicable rules. A qualifying customer relationship or brand can have a separately recognized value even if absent from the seller’s books.

For a simple purchase of 100% with cash, with no prior holding or non-controlling interest, goodwill is the price less recognized identifiable net assets. It is a residual accounting amount. A large residual does not, by itself, prove the buyer secured valuable synergies.

Theory and method

IFRS 3 acquisition method with deferred tax

Purchase price allocation · Taxable temporary difference

Why can recognizing a €4m customer-contract asset reduce goodwill by less than €4m?

A buyer pays a single price for a business, then allocates it among identifiable assets and liabilities. A separately recognized customer contract can receive an accounting value even when the acquisition leaves its tax basis at zero. Future recovery of that carrying amount can therefore have a tax consequence. In the stated case recognizing the asset increases identifiable net assets while an associated deferred tax liability partially offsets that increase. The remaining purchase-price residual becomes goodwill.

How the result is built

Change in goodwill = −ΔA + τ(ΔA − ΔTB)

ΔA is the increase in recognized asset carrying amount, ΔTB the increase in its tax basis, τ the applicable tax rate. The goodwill change is in euros for a 100% acquisition with unchanged consideration and other measurements.

  1. Before the identification, consideration is €20m and recognizable net assets €12m, giving €8m goodwill. Add €4m accounting value for a qualifying contract.
  2. With no tax basis step-up and 25% tax, the €4m taxable temporary difference creates €1m deferred tax liability under the stated recognition assumptions.
  3. Net assets become €12m + €4m − €1m = €15m. Goodwill becomes €5m, down €3m. The €20m cash purchase price is unchanged.

When the reasoning holds

  • The business combination and taxable temporary difference in the case require recognition. Different recognition exceptions, tax jurisdictions or asset purchases cannot be assumed to share the same treatment.
  • Consideration, prior holdings and non-controlling interests are fixed here. Those components require separate measurement in more complex combinations.
Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Goodwill
In a simple purchase of an entire company, the part of the purchase price above the measured value of identifiable net assets.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Synergy
Extra value expected from combining businesses, such as a saving attainable only after a merger.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
First example

A buyer pays €12m. Identifiable assets measured for the acquisition total €14m and recognized liabilities total €5m, including any relevant tax effects. Net identifiable assets are €9m. The remaining €3m goes to goodwill in this simple case.

Identify the acquirer and the measurement date

Buying enough voting shares can give one enterprise control over another. For a business combination within the applicable acquisition-method rules, the acquirer recognizes the identifiable assets and liabilities it acquires at the acquisition date using the prescribed measurement bases, commonly acquisition-date fair value subject to exceptions.

Determine what has actually been purchased. An asset purchase can have different accounting from acquisition of a business. Establish the control date and identify any pre-existing relationship, previous shareholding or remaining outside shareholders. These facts change the calculation beyond the simple full cash acquisition used first.

What counts separately from goodwill?

Identifiable assets include qualifying intangibles such as contractual customer relationships or patents even if the seller did not previously show them. Recognition and measurement requirements are specific. The analyst cannot simply recognize every positive synergy as an asset to eliminate goodwill or understate identifiable liabilities to improve a ratio.

Goodwill is the residual after recognized identifiable net assets in a simple wholly owned cash acquisition. More complex cases can include the measured non-controlling interest and fair value of a previously held interest. A bargain purchase may require reassessment before recognizing a gain under the applicable rules.

Accounting allocations affect later earnings

Assigning more value to a finite-life asset can raise subsequent amortization expense. That accounting charge affects reported earnings even where the allocation itself creates no extra cash on the acquisition date. Valuation, contractual purchase price and later financial reporting each answer distinct questions.

Tax bases can differ from accounting acquisition-date values and create deferred tax effects. Do not apply a universal adjustment without case assumptions. Similarly, expected future integration spending ordinarily requires a separate assessment of whether a present acquired obligation exists. A management plan alone does not establish one.

Method

Build the allocation

  1. Define consideration

    Measure the consideration under the case facts at the acquisition date. Keep purchase consideration, separate transactions and later operating costs distinct.

  2. Recognize net assets

    List identifiable assets and assumed liabilities, using stated measurement bases and any tax or other required adjustments. Subtract liabilities from assets to determine recognized net assets.

  3. Reconcile the residual

    Compare consideration and the other required ownership components with net assets. Show a subsequent earnings consequence separately from cash paid.

Worked case

A software distributor’s customer contracts

The buyer pays €32m cash for 100% of a business, with no earlier holding. Identifiable assets at acquisition-date measures are €24m before a separately identifiable €6m customer-contract asset. Recognized liabilities are €8m, including any relevant tax effects. The contracts have a three-year useful life with straight-line amortization and no residual value.

Working

  1. Identifiable assets total €30m and net identifiable assets are €22m after €8m liabilities. Goodwill is €32m − €22m = €10m.
  2. Omitting the qualifying €6m contract asset would overstate goodwill at €16m under the other unchanged assumptions. The total cash consideration remains €32m.
  3. The contract asset creates €2m annual amortization over three full years. That charge reduces subsequent accounting profit under the case assumptions, while the acquisition-date cash payment does not recur annually.

Interpretation

Do not infer €10m of future operating savings merely because the accounting residual is €10m. The residual can reflect several economic elements including the price paid.

A common mistake

Treating seller book equity as acquired net assets without examining fair-value changes or identifiable intangibles.

How to correct it

Build an acquisition-date schedule under the applicable measurement rules. State whether the simplified 100% calculation fits the shareholding facts.

Quick check
A buyer pays €20m for 100%. Identifiable assets are €23m and liabilities are €7m. Under the simplified assumptions, what goodwill is recognized?

Identifiable net assets equal €23m − €7m = €16m. The €20m price exceeds this by €4m. This calculation assumes no earlier stake or outside ownership.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A buyer pays €15m for 100%. Recognized assets are €18m, liabilities €7m. A newly identified patent worth €2m was omitted from that asset figure. Ignore tax and other changes.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute corrected goodwill and show what omission would do.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    If the patent is amortized equally over four years with no residual value, distinguish earnings and cash.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Assets become €20m and net assets €13m. Goodwill is €2m. Omitting the patent would give €4m goodwill, overstating it by €2m.

Part B 3 marks

Annual amortization is €0.5m over four full years, reducing accounting earnings before tax. It does not create a new €0.5m annual acquisition payment. The €15m consideration was paid at purchase.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why can recognizing a €4m customer-contract asset reduce goodwill by less than €4m?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would start by asking whether the acquired contract has a separate tax basis. Here it adds €4m in recognized assets, but the €1m deferred tax liability leaves only €3m more identifiable net assets. Because consideration stays at €20m, goodwill falls from €8m to €5m. That is why a buyer needs accounting and tax workstreams to reconcile the allocation.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The tax authority instead grants a full €4m tax-basis increase. Assume no other changes. What happens?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The new carrying amount and tax basis rise by the same amount, leaving zero incremental taxable temporary difference. Net assets rise the full €4m to €16m, and goodwill falls to €4m. Any subsequent tax deductions belong in the separate tax and cash-flow forecasts.
