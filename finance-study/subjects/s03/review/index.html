New and expanded lessons

S03.11

Building standalone earnings for a carved-out business

The quality-of-earnings and cash-conversion lessons normalize a whole business. They do not build historical, transition-year and independent earnings for a division that shared its former parent’s systems and staff.

  • Build a three-column earnings bridge using shared-service allocations and replacement costs.
  • Distinguish the seller’s stranded cost, the buyer’s transition expense and one-time migration cash.

S03.12

Accounting for earn-outs and seller employment payments

The acquisition-method lesson calculates goodwill and deferred tax. It does not allocate the change in a contingent purchase price between acquisition-date goodwill and later operating-period earnings.

  • Value a contingent cash payment and reconcile acquisition-date goodwill.
  • Explain how a later performance event, qualifying acquisition-date evidence and a service condition change the accounting.

S03.13

Consolidating a step acquisition and measuring minority ownership

The acquisition lesson names a non-controlling interest and a previously held stake as terms in the goodwill formula, then works a full purchase. This chapter measures each term and compares the permitted goodwill choices for eligible shares.

  • Calculate prior-stake remeasurement and goodwill when a minority investment becomes a controlled business.
  • Reconcile cash paid, consolidated net assets and the interest retained by outside shareholders.

Public benchmarks

The Hong Kong Polytechnic University

AF4106 Advanced Financial Accounting

The syllabus asks students to compare business combination structures, identify the reporting entity and prepare consolidated accounts at the acquisition date, including partially owned groups.

KPMG International Standards Group

Combined and/or carve-out financial statements

The guide examines how to draw the reporting boundary, identify balances and allocate shared expenses in historical financial statements. Its examples use operating drivers such as time spent and headcount.

KPMG

Separation in practice

The separation guide connects the standalone operating model to service agreements, cost allocations and stranded costs that must be identified on the seller side.

Australian Accounting Standards Board

AASB 3 Business Combinations, paragraphs 19, 32, 39–50, 58 and B55

The public Australian standard addresses eligible non-controlling interests, previously acquired stakes, contingent consideration, measurement-period changes and separate employment arrangements. Its acquisition-method requirements incorporate IFRS 3 for the commercial transactions considered here.

Australian Accounting Standards Board

AASB 10 Consolidated Financial Statements, accounting requirements, paragraphs 19–25

AASB 10 identifies control as the consolidation boundary, locates non-controlling interests within equity and treats changes in an already controlled subsidiary without loss of control as transactions with owners.
