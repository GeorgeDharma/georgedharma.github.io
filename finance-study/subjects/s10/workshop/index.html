Professional workshop · S10

Credit approval for a three-year district solar loan

Meridian District Solar is a fictional project selling power under a five-year municipal offtake agreement. The sponsor requests €26m of senior debt repayable within its first three operating years. Prepare the lender's decision, a sculpted repayment and reserve schedule, the completion support required and a first-year operating downside. The committee wants signed cash-flow definitions and evidence of who absorbs each residual risk before it commits. All amounts are euro cash amounts.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Project-finance credit analyst on an infrastructure lender's underwriting team
What to submit
Deliver a project credit application and indicative facility terms for the infrastructure credit committee. The cash-flow exhibit must reconcile opening debt, annual interest and principal, debt-service coverage, reserve top-ups and distributable cash, including final reserve release.

From inputs to decision

01

Define the lender's perimeter

The applicant is the project company. A municipal contract gives a source of revenue, while sponsor recourse is limited to the stated completion undertaking.

  1. Map project collections from the municipal buyer into the company account and cash expenses to the operator. Senior principal and interest are paid from cash after the stated operating costs. The guarantee can support specified pre-acceptance overruns but cannot make up lower post-completion volume.
  2. Use the first three annual cash receipts for sizing. Years four and five provide a contractual tail after scheduled repayment but contribute no assumed cash to the three-year loan-life calculation. Verify the counterparty's credit and payment timing separately.

The underwritten primary source is contracted and collected operating cash. Treat the sponsor's promise and future tail as separately documented protection with finite eligibility.

02

Build annual operating cash available for debt service

The senior schedule requires cash before debt and reserves. Distinguish the unit margin from fixed receipts and payments.

  1. Each MWh sold contributes €150 − €40 = €110 before the fixed items. The €2m availability fee and €2m fixed cost offset under the base assumptions, leaving annual CFADS equal to output times €110.
  2. Year-one total receipts are €15m energy + €2m availability, and total costs €4m variable + €2m fixed, producing €11m. Repeating this calculation for years two and three gives €12.10m and €13.31m.

Annual CFADS is €11m, €12.1m and €13.31m. Keep operating CFADS separate from the later release of restricted reserve cash.

03

Size supportable principal and test the sponsor request

The 1.25 coverage target limits the scheduled payment in each period. The principal today is the present value of those payments at the loan rate.

  1. The payment ceilings are €11m/1.25 = €8.8m, €12.1m/1.25 = €9.68m and €13.31m/1.25 = €10.648m. Discount at 10% to closing. The three values are €8m, €8m and €8m.
  2. Maximum supported senior debt totals €24m. The €26m request exceeds capacity by €2m. The PV of eligible CFADS is €10m + €10m + €10m = €30m. LLCR excluding reserves is 30/24 = 1.25, while at €26m it would be only 1.154.

Recommend a €24m facility subject to completion and operating conditions. Require equity for any additional construction financing and the separately funded reserve.

04

Roll the outstanding loan to zero

A lender needs a contractual schedule of interest and principal. A table of total coverage alone cannot verify whether the final balance clears.

  1. In year one, interest on €24m is €2.4m. Service €8.8m covers €6.4m principal, leaving €17.6m. Year two interest €1.76m and service €9.68m repay €7.92m, leaving €9.68m.
  2. Year three interest on €9.68m is €0.968m. The €10.648m final payment clears €9.68m principal. Principal repaid across the schedule is €6.4m + €7.92m + €9.68m = €24m.

Debt service covers each year's interest and clears at final maturity. Annual DSCR is exactly 1.25 before distribution and reserve movements.

05

Reconcile financial closing and the reserve waterfall

Reserves protect payment timing but require real cash. Treating the initial reserve as an operating expense or including it twice in debt sizing would distort the decision.

  1. Opening reserve equals 50% of €8.8m or €4.4m. Uses at close are €28m construction plus €4.4m reserve = €32.4m. Sources are €24m senior and €8.4m sponsor equity. An uncalled guarantee is not cash in this base.
  2. At end year one the target rises to €4.84m, requiring €0.44m. Distributable cash is €11m − €8.8m − €0.44m = €1.76m. At end year two the target rises to €5.324m, requiring €0.484m. Cash available for distribution is €12.1m − €9.68m − €0.484m = €1.936m.

At maturity €13.31m − €10.648m leaves €2.662m operating surplus. Add the €5.324m reserve release only after the debt is fully discharged, giving €7.986m that year under the stated scope.

06

Run a physically defined downside and completion overrun

Volume risk can reduce coverage even at a fixed tariff. Distinguish the risk owned by the project after completion from a qualifying construction issue.

  1. An output fall to 80,000 MWh cuts year-one CFADS to €8.8m because €110 contribution is lost on 20,000 MWh. Scheduled service remains €8.8m and DSCR falls to 1.00. Senior payment is just covered, but no cash is left for the €0.44m reserve top-up and the distribution test of 1.20 also fails.
  2. If this is the only downside, loan-life CFADS PV falls €2.2m/1.10 = €2m to €28m. LLCR remains 28/24 = 1.167 even though year one's distribution and reserve conditions failed. This illustrates the timing difference between the measures.

Separately, a qualifying €2m construction overrun triggers at most €1.5m sponsor support under the stated guarantee, leaving €0.5m to bridge. A post-completion €2m operating shortfall would not trigger this promise.

07

Approve with enforceable conditions and reporting

The useful output is a decision that can be turned into documentation and monitored at each payment date.

  1. Before first draw require binding equity of €8.4m, funded €4.4m reserve at the agreed date, a certified cost-to-complete report and enforceable guarantee within its documented limit. Obtain executed offtake and assignment of project receivables with a clear payment timetable.
  2. During operation require metered output, invoice collections and actual CFADS reporting, an updated reserve balance and a forward production test before distributions. Clarify remedy and cure periods for reserve deficiencies separately from failure to pay scheduled debt.

Record the decision to reduce the request by €2m. Any increase depends on committed additional cash, a revised contract or lower required coverage expressly approved by the credit committee.

Completed document

Infrastructure credit application and indicative decision

Recommendation

Approve €24m senior term debt with final maturity at the end of operating year three, subject to the stated documents. Decline the excess €2m requested. Each sculpted payment is fully covered 1.25 times in the base case. Reserves are excluded from sizing.

Sources and security

Require €8.4m paid-in equity and a €4.4m equity-funded opening DSRA. Obtain assignment of project receipts and sponsor completion support capped at €1.5m. This package does not cover after-completion demand or payment failure by the offtaker.

Operating controls

The distribution floor is 1.20 alongside full DSRA funding. At 80,000 MWh the first service is paid but DSCR falls to 1.00, reserve funding misses €0.44m and no cash may be distributed. Request a recovery and liquidity plan before curing restrictions.

Residual risks

Confirm actual availability conditions, operator and municipal payment capacity, and the legal process to step in after material default. Stress the ramp to 121,000 MWh and check that the two-year contractual tail is enforceable even though sizing takes no credit for it.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

If the committee requires 1.40 times annual coverage instead of 1.25, what maximum principal does the same projected cash support?

See the full solution

The PV of eligible CFADS remains €30m. With constant coverage and identical loan discount rate, capacity is €30m/1.40 = €21.4286m. Recalculate reserve and equity needs on the new schedule before closing.

Task 2

Year-two tariff falls from €150 to €140 per MWh, with volume and all costs unchanged. What is year-two DSCR on the approved payment schedule?

See the full solution

Cash contribution falls to €100 per MWh on 110,000 MWh, so CFADS is €11m. Divide by scheduled €9.68m for 1.13636 times. This fails the 1.20 distribution test.

Task 3

What year-one output is needed to reach 1.20 times DSCR on €8.8m scheduled service when energy contribution remains €110 per MWh?

See the full solution

CFADS must reach €8.8m × 1.20 = €10.56m. Divide by €110 per MWh for 96,000 MWh. The reserve test remains an additional distribution condition.

Task 4

Can the sponsor count its uncalled €1.5m guarantee toward the €8.4m base equity requirement?

The transaction requires cash for €28m construction and €4.4m reserves. The guarantee pays only on qualifying overruns and expires at acceptance. Replacing base equity with it would leave ordinary closing uses unfunded and count contingent support for an ineligible use.
