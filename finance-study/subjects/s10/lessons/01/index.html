Project structure

Map the project company, sponsor, contractor, customer and lenders. Identify which cash flow or guarantee supports each party’s claim.

The starting point

A wind farm can be owned by a company formed for that project alone. The sponsors put money into this project company. Lenders advance money to it, a contractor builds the farm and a customer may contract to buy its electricity.

Limited recourse means lenders principally rely on project cash and the rights specified in the financing documents. A sponsor can commit to particular support, such as funding completion overruns up to a cap. The sponsor's entire balance sheet does not automatically secure every repayment.

Theory and method

Limited-recourse project finance

Special-purpose vehicle · Creditor recovery waterfall

A sponsor owns 100% of the project company. Why might the senior lender still lose money on default?

A separate company borrows to build a regulated terminal. Its lender can recover cash from specified project assets and call only those sponsor promises that remain enforceable under the transaction documents. Equity ownership alone gives the lender no unrestricted claim to the parent's cash. To estimate downside, trace what is realized after enforcement costs, how competing creditors rank and whether a guarantee covers this trigger. The recovery cap also prevents counting one loan twice when guarantees and collateral overlap.

How the result is built

Senior recovery = min[D, max(0, A − E − P) + G]; loss = D − recovery

D is the senior claim, A gross realizable project proceeds, E enforcement costs, P claims payable earlier, and G collectible eligible deficiency guarantee after applying its trigger and contractual cap. All amounts use euros.

  1. A senior claim of €30m is backed by €25m of realizable assets. Enforcement uses €2m and higher-ranking claims use €4m, leaving €19m for this lender.
  2. A sponsor guarantee collectible in this default covers €5m of the remaining deficiency. The lender can recover €24m, leaving a €6m loss.
  3. If realizable proceeds rise enough to cover the claim before guarantee, the amount assignable to the lender stays capped at €30m. Any excess follows lower priorities.

When the reasoning holds

  • The stipulated ranking and guarantee must be enforceable in the relevant jurisdiction. Real-world legal priority and trigger wording can differ.
  • Use recoverable cash after disposal delays and costs. A book valuation before default may exceed proceeds actually available to creditors.
Terms in this lesson
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
First example

A project company borrows €40m, with a sponsor guarantee covering up to €3m of qualifying construction overruns only. A €5m qualifying overrun can trigger at most €3m under that promise. An operating revenue shortfall years later is a different risk.

Why put one project in its own company?

A project company contracts to build and operate a specific asset. Its separate accounts let lenders trace collections, costs and claims without assuming that every unrelated sponsor business will support them. That ring-fencing can improve monitoring but does not eliminate construction or commercial risk.

Corporate finance relies broadly on the borrower group's available resources. Limited-recourse project lending instead specifies when, how much and for how long sponsors can be called upon. An explicit completion guarantee can coexist with limited recourse after completion.

Contracts transmit the risk

Map the engineering and construction contractor, operator, customer, lenders, public authority and insurers. For every key risk, identify both the contract that transfers it and the party's financial ability to perform when it matters. A promise by a thinly capitalized contractor may have little recovery value.

Examine interface risks. A constructor may certify mechanical completion while the customer requires a longer performance test before paying. The uncovered period belongs somewhere even when each individual contract appears comprehensive.

Claims, priority and step-in rights

Senior creditors generally negotiate priority over distributions to equity under the transaction documents. Sponsor funds, subordinated loans and contingency reserves can absorb losses before senior debt, depending on their terms. Do not infer economic priority from the name of an instrument alone.

Step-in rights can allow a lender or nominated operator to remedy a project default before the authority terminates a key contract. Their usefulness depends on notice periods, approvals and whether a capable replacement operator exists.

Method

Drawing the financing map

  1. Trace uses and sources

    Match construction, reserves and financing costs with committed equity, loans and any grants by draw date.

  2. Trace claims to cash

    Show which customer pays which entity, where cash is held and which expenses and claims take priority.

  3. Trace residual exposure

    Follow each guarantee through trigger, expiry and cap, then identify losses without a committed payer.

Worked case

A small regional data centre

A project requires €70m in construction uses and €5m of reserves. Sponsors commit €25m equity and lenders commit €50m senior debt. A separate guarantee covers qualifying construction overruns up to €4m and expires at acceptance.

Working

  1. Base sources equal €75m and match €75m of base uses. The guarantee is contingent support, so it should not be counted as a separate €4m cash source for the already balanced base case.
  2. A qualifying €6m overrun before acceptance draws at most €4m under the guarantee. The remaining €2m requires a contingency, added sponsor funds or a revised financing agreement.
  3. A €6m operating shortfall after acceptance does not trigger this construction guarantee. Its effect falls through reserves, permitted support and the operating-period priority of claims.

Interpretation

A lender deciding whether to commit should test both timing and enforceability of the support, including how the remaining gap could delay acceptance or prevent an operating start.

A common mistake

Adding an undrawn guarantee as unconditional cash can make an unfunded project look fully financed.

How to correct it

Maintain a base sources-and-uses table plus a separate schedule of contingent promises with their precise triggers.

Quick check
A lender to a port can draw a sponsor guarantee only for documented construction costs before completion. Which shortfall falls within that stated promise?

Recourse follows the trigger, time limit and amount written into the guarantee. Cash generated by the project and other promises would have to be assessed separately for operating losses.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A port costs €120m and requires €6m cash reserves. Investors commit €36m cash and banks €90m. A parent guarantees up to €5m of qualifying overruns before the completion certificate only.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Reconcile the financing and find the residual gap after an €8m qualifying overrun before the certificate.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What changes if the same €8m is lost to low traffic after certification? Name one document the lender would inspect.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Cash sources of €126m equal base uses. The guarantee covers €5m of the overrun, leaving €3m uncovered before considering other resources.

Part B 3 marks

The stated guarantee has expired and traffic misses its trigger. Inspect the operating revenue agreement, reserve and liquidity provisions or other operating guarantees to identify available protection.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A sponsor owns 100% of the project company. Why might the senior lender still lose money on default?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask which entity borrowed and exactly what recourse the sponsor signed. Here the €25m assets leave €19m after costs and earlier claims. The triggered €5m promise brings recovery to €24m against €30m owed. The lender loses €6m despite full sponsor ownership. I would inspect guarantee expiry, guarantor capacity and enforceable rights before entering that recovery in a credit paper.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the sponsor promise expires at acceptance and this loss occurs six months later. How does recovery change?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Keeping the other assumptions fixed, eligible guarantee recovery becomes zero. Senior recovery drops from €24m to €19m and loss rises to €11m. An unrelated claim against a sponsor would require its own legal and factual basis.
