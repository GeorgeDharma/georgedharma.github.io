Revenue and operating contracts

Compare merchant revenue with an offtake agreement. Fixed pricing can reduce one risk while introducing exposure to the buyer’s credit quality.

The starting point

A power plant selling electricity on a spot market faces the market price on the day it generates. An offtake agreement is a contract with a buyer for the plant's output. An agreed price can stabilize revenue per unit while production and collection can still vary.

Read the volume commitment, indexation, payment schedule and buyer's ability to pay. An obligation to buy all electricity produced differs from an obligation to pay for minimum volume even when demand falls. A contract can also expire before the project debt matures.

Theory and method

Tariff indexation and availability payments

Escalation formula · Contracted revenue

A concession contract says its tariff is inflation-linked. How much project cash protection does that statement actually provide?

A water operator signs a tariff that follows inflation only on the portion linked to wages and energy. The remaining portion stays fixed and performance deductions may reduce the entire payment. The economic hedge therefore depends on the index weights, lag and measured availability, as well as the cost mix. A lender can translate each clause into a payment formula and compare it with the input costs actually paid. A generic inflation label cannot establish full pass-through.

How the result is built

Payment = Q × T₀[(1−w) + w(Iₜ/I₀)] × a

Q is eligible service units, T₀ initial euros per unit, w contractual fraction indexed, I₀ and Iₜ reference and current index observations under the contract, and a earned availability or performance factor between zero and one.

  1. The baseline is 20,000 units at €50 and 60% of the tariff follows an index rising from 100 to 110. Revised unit tariff is €50 × (0.4 + 0.6 × 1.10) = €53.
  2. Full-performance revenue would be €1.06m. At 95% earned availability the payment is €1.007m.
  3. Compare this with €1m baseline at full availability. An underlying 10% index rise produces only a €7,000 payment gain when both clauses operate.

When the reasoning holds

  • The performance factor is assumed multiplicative across the entire billed amount as written. A fixed deduction or independent availability component gives a different result.
  • Use the specified index date and eligible quantity. A one-year lag or unavailable output changes cash precisely when the operator needs protection.
Terms in this lesson
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
First example

A solar farm receives €50 for every megawatt-hour it delivers, with no minimum-volume guarantee. At 12,000 MWh it earns €600,000. Cloud cover cutting output to 9,000 MWh lowers revenue to €450,000 even if the customer pays every invoice in full.

Who is paid for what?

A merchant project earns the prevailing market price for what it sells. A volume-based offtake agreement can fix a unit price or link it to an index. An availability contract can pay for making an asset operational even when actual customer demand is low, subject to service deductions.

A take-or-pay clause may commit a buyer to a minimum payment, but read what counts as available capacity and who bears outages or curtailment. Different contract conditions can produce different revenues from the same physical production.

Matching contract currency and duration

A stable local-currency tariff is not stable dollar income when debt or equipment is priced in dollars. Indexation can pass through some input inflation, sometimes with a lag or cap. Model the actual formula and reset dates against the project's payment needs.

A ten-year purchase agreement supporting twenty-year debt creates a merchant tail. Future market prices after expiry and the likelihood of renewal can materially affect loan sizing, even if near-term payments look secure.

Counterparty and operating interactions

A contracted tariff has value only to the extent the buyer pays. A state-owned buyer's obligations are not automatically the government's own debt. Inspect its payment record, available guarantees, termination payments and dispute provisions.

Fixed operating and maintenance charges increase sensitivity to low output. Variable fuel costs and tariff escalation can offset or compound one another. Trace which party can control maintenance downtime and whether the payment regime rewards prompt restoration.

Method

Forecasting contracted receipts

  1. Separate volume and availability

    Identify paid units or contracted capacity, performance deductions and which party controls the cause of a shortfall.

  2. Apply price and timing

    Apply floors, caps, indexation dates, currency and payment delays in the order written.

  3. Stress the payer

    Test both a delay and a permanent failure to collect. Compare contract expiry with debt maturity.

Worked case

A plant with a minimum commitment

A utility agrees to pay €60 per MWh on the larger of energy taken or 8,000 MWh, provided the plant makes at least 8,000 MWh available. If availability is lower, the utility pays for energy actually taken. No other revenue is assumed.

Working

  1. With 9,000 available and 6,500 taken, the minimum applies. Billed revenue is 8,000 × €60 = €480,000, even though fewer units were dispatched.
  2. With just 7,000 available and 6,500 taken, the condition fails. Revenue is 6,500 × €60 = €390,000, a €90,000 difference driven by project availability.
  3. If the utility takes ninety days to settle an approved €480,000 invoice, the project has a receivable rather than immediate debt-service cash. Loan due dates remain unchanged.

Interpretation

The contract transfers part of demand risk, leaves operational risk with the plant and introduces exposure to the buyer's payment performance.

A common mistake

Assuming every fixed-price offtake agreement guarantees a fixed total annual revenue erases volume and credit risk.

How to correct it

Write the payment as a function of available output, dispatched output, price and collection terms before projecting cash.

Quick check
A public utility agrees to buy all actual output at a fixed tariff for eight years. Which risk plainly remains with the producer under that payment formula?

The agreed tariff fixes the price per unit. Fewer units still produce less revenue, and collection also depends on the buyer paying what it owes.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A waste facility receives an annual €2m availability fee multiplied by measured availability, plus €10 for each tonne handled. It handles 50,000 tonnes and achieves 90% availability.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate total billed revenue and its breakdown.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    If handled tonnes fall 20% while availability stays fixed, find the revenue change and identify a remaining risk after billing.

Part A 3 marks

Availability revenue is €1.8m and volume revenue is €0.5m, totaling €2.3m.

Part B 3 marks

Volume revenue falls €0.1m to €0.4m and total becomes €2.2m. Collection delay or failure by the payer can still reduce cash available for debt service.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A concession contract says its tariff is inflation-linked. How much project cash protection does that statement actually provide?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would read the tariff formula. Here only sixty percent moves with inflation, so a 10% index rise takes the tariff from €50 to €53. With 95% earned availability, yearly revenue on 20,000 units reaches just €1.007m. I would compare that cash with actual energy and wage escalation and confirm when the off-taker pays the approved invoice.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The index resets next year, but today's cost increase and 95% performance apply now. What payment is earned this year?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The applied index ratio remains one for this payment. The €50 tariff on 20,000 units at 95% earns €950,000. The reset lag reduces current receipts €57,000 relative to the €1.007m contemporaneously indexed case.
