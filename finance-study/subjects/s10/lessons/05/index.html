Debt sizing and sculpting

Debt service can follow project cash generation subject to a coverage target. A weak individual period can constrain borrowing even with a strong average.

The starting point

A seasonal power project earns more in windy quarters than in still ones. A lender can shape interest and principal payments to suit expected collections. This is called debt sculpting. A minimum debt-service coverage ratio sets the required cushion in each tested period.

Divide each period's cash available for debt service by the required coverage ratio to find its maximum allowable service. Principal must still fit after paying interest. The loan amount depends on the whole payment schedule, its interest rate and any final repayment limits.

Theory and method

Debt-service and loan-life coverage ratios

DSCR · LLCR

Could a loan show 1.5 times coverage over its life and still miss its first installment?

A country's new reservoir will generate far more water sales after pipes reach the second district. The total discounted cash can justify lending while the first payment is poorly matched to the ramp-up. The loan-life coverage ratio compares discounted cash available up to final loan maturity with outstanding debt. The debt-service coverage ratio tests actual cash against each installment. Both are useful because a strong late year may not supply cash on an early payment date.

How the result is built

DSCRₜ = Cₜ/Dₜ; LLCR₀ = [Σₜ Cₜ/(1+r)ᵗ]/B₀

Cₜ is eligible cash available for debt service in year t, Dₜ scheduled principal plus interest, r annual loan discount rate, B₀ current debt outstanding, and Σₜ includes years through final loan maturity only. The simplified definition excludes reserves.

  1. Assume two years, €10m debt and 10% loan rate. CFADS is €3.30m then €14.52m. Its present value is 3.30/1.1 + 14.52/1.1² = €15m.
  2. LLCR is 15/10 = 1.50. A valid repayment schedule of €6m and €5.5m has PV €10m but first-year DSCR is only 3.30/6 = 0.55.
  3. Sculpt 1.5 times coverage by dividing each year by 1.5. Service becomes €2.2m and €9.68m. Its PV is €10m and both period tests become 1.5.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use CFADS and discount rate specified in finance documents. Some LLCR conventions include qualifying reserve balances or different discounting.
  • Sculpting must be contractually permitted, with future cash available before loan expiry. A late optimistic volume assumption can endanger the larger final repayment.
Terms in this lesson
Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
Cash available to meet scheduled debt payments divided by the principal and interest due for the period.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
How to read the notation
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.

All notation examples

First example

Cash available in two years is €12m and €18m. A 1.5-times minimum coverage permits service of €8m and €12m respectively. Setting €10m in both years gives average coverage of 1.5 but breaches the first-year test.

Debt sizing as a present-value problem

A required debt-service coverage ratio is cash available divided by scheduled principal plus interest over a specified period. Dividing projected CFADS by the target gives the total service each period can support. This is a ceiling on a payment, not an initial principal amount.

If the interest rate is fixed and there are no fees, reserve adjustments or final balloon, discount the sequence of allowable service at that rate. Its present value is the largest starting loan supported by exactly that repayment stream under those assumptions.

Sculpting interest and principal

Begin each period with the outstanding principal. Calculate interest on that balance for the period. Subtract interest from allowable service to find principal repaid, then carry the reduced balance forward. Negative scheduled principal flags capitalization or an infeasible schedule unless explicitly permitted.

A lender may require a margin between final loan repayment and contract expiry, called a loan tail. This can create a lower sizing limit than average coverage suggests. A loan-life coverage ratio compares the present value of remaining eligible cash with the remaining debt balance.

The weakest period may bind

A level-payment loan is designed around constant service and can fail a low-output year even when a sculpted loan fits. A reserve or grace period may shift cash across dates, but their permitted use, funding cost and repayment must be modelled.

Stress resource availability, tariffs, operating costs and interest jointly. Increased borrowing rates raise interest on a floating-rate loan and reduce the amount initially supported by a given service sequence. Distinguish a fall in capacity from a breach on an existing facility.

Method

Sizing and checking a loan

  1. Set period caps

    Calculate allowed payments for each period under the stated coverage test, dates and measured cash definition.

  2. Discount service

    Discount those payments at the loan rate to the draw date to derive a starting balance.

  3. Roll forward

    Split each payment into interest and principal, verify closing balance and inspect coverage for each period individually.

Worked case

A two-year production ramp

A project expects €12.6m then €15.75m CFADS at the end of years one and two. The target coverage ratio is 1.5 throughout. Loan rate is 5% annually, with equal annual accrual periods and no fees.

Working

  1. Allowable service is €8.4m and €10.5m. Starting loan capacity equals €8.4m/1.05 + €10.5m/1.05² = €17.5238m.
  2. First-year interest is about €0.8762m. The €8.4m payment therefore repays about €7.5238m principal, leaving exactly €10m for the final year.
  3. Second-year interest is €0.5m. A €10.5m payment clears the remaining principal and gives 1.5 coverage again. Borrowing the undiscounted €18.9m would leave principal outstanding.

Interpretation

Compare the result with sponsor funding needs and independent limits on leverage. Debt capacity under this one test does not ensure lenders will advance the full amount.

An annual coverage pass can conceal a missed instalment

Delaying €0.10m of Q1 principal until Q2 removes that specific gap in this simple case, provided the lender agrees and interest costs do not change. A forecast with customer-by-customer collection dates is the next check.

A common mistake

Dividing total life-of-project cash by a ratio and calling the result debt principal overlooks timing and interest.

How to correct it

Establish permitted service by period, discount it and verify the closing loan schedule rather than relying on an average.

Quick check
A project expects €9m cash available next year and must maintain coverage of at least 1.25 times. If €2m interest is due, what maximum principal can it repay under that test?

Total service can be €9m ÷ 1.25 = €7.2m. Deduct the €2m interest to leave €5.2m for principal in that year.

Independent exam problem

A two-year project has CFADS of €11m then €12.1m, coverage target 1.1 and annual loan rate 10%. Payments occur at year-end. Assume no fees or residual loan.

    Calculate the two service ceilings and maximum initial loan.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Show year-one interest and closing balance, and confirm the final payment clears debt.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Could a loan show 1.5 times coverage over its life and still miss its first installment?

Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. The project has €15m of discounted eligible cash against €10m debt, so LLCR is 1.5. Under the first schedule €6m falls due against €3.3m cash, and the project has a €2.7m immediate gap. Reshaping payments to €2.2m and €9.68m makes both annual DSCRs 1.5 without changing initial loan size. I would stress the final cash before accepting that larger later payment.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Only half of the second year's €14.52m materializes under the sculpted schedule. What happens to year-two DSCR?

Compare with the follow-up answer

CFADS falls to €7.26m against €9.68m service, giving 0.75 times coverage and a €2.42m cash shortfall. Initial forecast alignment did not remove output risk. A lender should test the ramp-up and available support.
