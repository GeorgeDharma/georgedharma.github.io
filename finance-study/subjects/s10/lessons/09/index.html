Climate and sustainability

Translate physical damage or a policy transition into price, output or cost. Define a development indicator with a baseline and an independent way to verify it.

The starting point

A flood can close a factory and cut output. A carbon price can raise operating costs even when the factory sustains no physical damage. Translate each climate scenario into the time, quantity and price that enter the project's cash model.

A sustainability claim also needs a measurable outcome and a credible baseline. Record the boundary, period and verification method. For avoided emissions, a change in the local electricity mix can change the estimated benefit even if the project generates the same amount.

Theory and method

GHG Protocol project accounting

Baseline scenario · Primary and secondary effects

How could a project generate the promised output while its reported emissions reduction declines?

A rail electrification project displaces a diesel journey but uses electricity whose emissions depend on the supplying grid. The comparison requires a realistic alternative journey and a common service quantity. Additional activities such as feeder travel can create significant emissions outside the immediate equipment boundary. A fall in baseline emissions, extra project energy use or newly identified secondary effects can reduce estimated impact although the trains still run as scheduled. Record cash revenue and tonnes of reductions separately.

How the result is built

ER = Q(eᵦ − eₚ) − S

ER is estimated net emissions reduction in tonnes CO₂-equivalent, Q comparable service units, eᵦ and eₚ tonnes per unit for baseline and project under a shared boundary, and S incremental material secondary emissions not already counted.

  1. One million passenger journeys displace baseline emissions of 0.0020 tonnes each and produce 0.0008 tonnes each on the project under the stated scope.
  2. The direct reduction is 1,000,000 × 0.0012 = 1,200 tonnes. Additional feeder trips create 180 net tonnes beyond the two intensity estimates.
  3. Net estimated reduction is 1,020 tonnes. If cleaner baseline vehicles reduce baseline intensity to 0.0017, the result falls to 720 tonnes, holding other terms fixed.

When the reasoning holds

  • The baseline must describe a plausible without-project service for the same passengers. Some journeys might be newly generated and require a separate counterfactual.
  • Exclude overlap between the activity factors and secondary effects. Double counting construction, feeder travel or electricity emissions distorts the result.
Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Counterfactual
What would probably have happened without the event, decision or policy being studied.
First example

A small plant displaces 10,000 MWh of grid electricity. Assume baseline emissions of 0.5 tonnes per MWh and project emissions of 0.1. Estimated emissions avoided are 10,000 × (0.5 − 0.1) = 4,000 tonnes for the period.

From physical climate to cash

A flood hazard is not a complete loss estimate. Exposure depends on assets in the affected place, vulnerability on their susceptibility to damage, and resilience on protection, insurance and recovery time. Lost sales can exceed repair spending when a critical asset is closed for months.

Model insurance deductibles, caps, exclusions and time to settlement. An expected insurance receivable can reduce ultimate loss while leaving a funding gap during rebuilding. Damage and lost revenue should be linked to the same physical scenario.

Transition risk and pass-through

A carbon price, efficiency standard or customer shift can alter costs, output or asset life without damaging equipment. A fee per tonne is multiplied by emissions within its scope. The financial effect depends on whether the offtake contract allows price adjustments and whether demand responds.

Different transition assumptions need internally consistent energy prices, technology and demand. Stress policy delay as well as acceleration. An apparently green project can still depend on a carbon-intensive supply chain or a short-lived policy incentive.

Measuring and attributing avoided emissions

Specify project boundary, functional unit, time period and counterfactual technology. An emissions factor converts output or fuel use into emissions. A falling grid factor can reduce avoided emissions over time even as the project produces at the same level.

Use measured output and independently supportable baseline factors. Avoid counting the same environmental benefit both as the project's own emissions reduction claim and as another party's exclusive credit. Financial income from selling certificates is distinct from the physical number of tonnes avoided.

Method

Translating a scenario

  1. Frame the driver

    Identify the hazard, output loss, carbon exposure or policy change, including when it occurs and what remains fixed.

  2. Trace cash and outcomes

    Calculate production, tariffs, costs, repair, recovery and debt-service effects on their proper dates.

  3. Verify the indicator

    Define the baseline, unit, measurement source and attribution rule, recording uncertainty or changes in the baseline.

Worked case

A district heating retrofit

A plant supplies 30,000 MWh yearly. Old heat would emit 0.45 tonnes per MWh. The new plant emits 0.15 within the same boundary. Imagine a €50 carbon fee on the new plant's own emissions. Its fixed-price contract has no pass-through.

Working

  1. Avoided emissions relative to the stated baseline are 30,000 × (0.45 − 0.15) = 9,000 tonnes. The new plant still emits 4,500 tonnes.
  2. Its hypothetical annual carbon bill is 4,500 × €50 = €225,000. One must use actual taxable project emissions rather than subtracting a carbon bill calculated on avoided emissions.
  3. If the baseline cleans to 0.30, annual avoided emissions fall to 4,500 tonnes at unchanged output. The project's carbon bill stays €225,000 because its own emissions factor has not changed.

Interpretation

Separating counterfactual impact from the cash contract lets readers understand why a lower environmental benefit does not automatically change a particular invoice.

A common mistake

Treating all avoided emissions as cash income assumes a crediting contract and an eligible buyer without evidence.

How to correct it

Report physically verified outcomes separately from contracted financial receipts and state who may claim any credit.

Quick check
A generator supplies 20,000 MWh. The baseline emits 0.4 tonnes per MWh and the project emits 0.1 within the same boundary. What is estimated avoided emissions?

Multiply the difference of 0.3 tonnes per MWh by 20,000 MWh. Check the grid baseline and metered generation before reporting an actual result.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A solar facility supplies 40,000 MWh. Grid baseline is 0.50 tonnes per MWh and project emissions are 0.05 on the same boundary. Flooding cuts output 25%, with the factors unchanged.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate base and stressed avoided emissions.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Each lost MWh would have earned €60 and cost €10 of genuinely avoidable variable expense. Find lost contribution and one additional cash exposure.

Part A 3 marks

The difference is 0.45 tonnes per MWh. Base avoided emissions are 18,000 tonnes. Stressed output is 30,000 MWh and avoided emissions 13,500 tonnes.

Part B 3 marks

The 10,000 MWh reduction loses €500,000 contribution. Repair, deductibles, timing of insurance receipts or a fixed debt payment during the outage can worsen liquidity.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How could a project generate the promised output while its reported emissions reduction declines?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would check the counterfactual and the boundary before blaming operations. One million journeys yield 1,200 tonnes of direct savings against the original baseline, then 1,020 after feeder emissions. If alternative vehicles become cleaner, the same project service avoids only 720 tonnes. Actual revenue from any credits also depends on monitoring, entitlement, verification and a sales contract.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Twenty percent of journeys would never have been made otherwise. Is applying the original diesel baseline to every trip defensible?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. The induced 200,000 trips require their own baseline, potentially zero direct travel emissions for that activity. Separate existing and induced journeys, retain their actual project electricity and incremental effects, and document how the demand counterfactual was established.
