Financial and economic appraisal

Financial value uses cash accruing to investors. Economic appraisal can include external benefits and costs, requiring explicit assumptions and a counterfactual.

The starting point

A bus line can save commuters hours without collecting enough fares to cover its costs. Financial appraisal measures cash flowing to a defined investor or project. Economic appraisal asks what real resources and benefits change for the population covered by the analysis.

Compare building the project with a plausible alternative over the same period. Discount incremental benefits and costs to a common date. Treat transfers between parties within the chosen boundary consistently, and check whether charging fares and valuing all passenger benefits would count the same benefit twice.

Theory and method

The rule of half in transport appraisal

Consumer surplus · Generated demand

A faster bus route attracts 200 new daily riders. Why is it usually wrong to give every new rider the full benefit received by someone already travelling?

An existing traveller enjoys the full fall in generalized trip cost, which includes money and the value of time. Newly induced travellers are only willing to travel after the improvement. The first of those users values the trip almost at the old cost, while the last values it near the lower new cost. With locally linear demand, the average surplus for the new users is half the change in generalized cost. A public appraisal measures that gain alongside resource costs, producer effects and externalities.

How the result is built

ΔCS ≈ Q₀Δc + ½(Q₁ − Q₀)Δc = ½(Q₀ + Q₁)Δc

ΔCS is consumer-surplus gain in euros per period, Q₀ and Q₁ trips before and after, and Δc the positive fall in generalized euro cost per trip. The second term represents additional users.

  1. Daily trips rise from 1,000 to 1,200 as generalized cost falls from €7 to €5. Existing users gain 1,000 × €2 = €2,000 daily.
  2. The 200 additional users gain 0.5 × 200 × €2 = €200 under linear demand. Total user benefit is €2,200 daily.
  3. Add incremental operator surplus and appropriate external costs on a consistent boundary. If fares already enter generalized cost, reconcile fare receipts rather than counting them twice.

When the reasoning holds

  • The linear-demand approximation applies over the price or time change, with comparable trips and other determinants held sufficiently stable. A large nonlinear response warrants explicit demand integration.
  • Separate genuine generated travel from transfers from other routes or modes. The appropriate alternative costs of diverted travellers can differ.
Terms in this lesson
Counterfactual
What would probably have happened without the event, decision or policy being studied.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
First example

A public depot costs €12m today. The present value of its net operating cash receipts is €9m, so financial value is minus €3m. If separately measured, additional benefits to third parties have a present value of €5m and there are no other adjustments, economic value is positive €2m.

Define whose welfare is being measured

Financial cash flow identifies amounts accruing to a project, lender or equity investor. Economic appraisal measures changes in real resources and welfare for a defined population. Decide whether the perspective is local, national or global before treating a payment as an internal transfer.

A fare paid by a resident to a publicly owned transit operator within the boundary is a transfer between them. The passenger's total benefit and the resources used to provide the service still matter. Adding the entire fare revenue to a gross willingness-to-pay benefit can double-count value.

Shadow prices and the counterfactual

A market price can be adjusted when subsidies, taxes, rationing or external effects drive a wedge between what users pay and the opportunity cost to society. A shadow price should be justified by evidence of that alternative use, rather than picked to force a positive result.

Assess the next best feasible option. An upgraded road can divert traffic from another route, reducing benefits attributed to altogether new trips. Compare with what would happen without the project, including maintenance and demographic changes that would occur anyway.

Discounting and distribution

Put real benefits and costs in constant purchasing power and use a real discount rate, or use nominal cash with a consistent nominal rate. Report important distributional consequences even when the aggregate economic present value is positive.

A financially weak project with social benefits may justify public support, but only if a practical funding mechanism exists and the benefits survive sensitivity tests. A bankable project can have negative social value when pollution or displacement costs are substantial.

Method

Building an economic bridge

  1. Set the baseline

    Define affected parties, geography, horizon and the realistic alternative, with common assumptions across both options.

  2. Reconcile resource flows

    Identify transfers, value genuine costs and external benefits, and ensure that each welfare change is counted once.

  3. Test decision thresholds

    Discount incremental impacts, change uncertain shadow prices and record which groups gain or lose.

Worked case

A bridge with toll income and time savings

Assume all figures are present values and all parties are domestic. A bridge uses €50m of construction and operating resources. Users' gross time and travel benefits are valued at €70m before tolls. They pay the operator €25m in tolls. Local harm is €8m.

Working

  1. The operator's simple financial value is €25m − €50m = minus €25m before subsidy or financing. Users receive €70m of gross benefit and pay €25m, leaving €45m user net benefit.
  2. Adding operator value and user benefit cancels the internal toll. Deduct €8m external harm, giving −€25m + €45m − €8m = €12m of economic value.
  3. Directly taking gross benefit minus resource and external costs also gives €70m − €50m − €8m = €12m. Counting the toll again atop gross benefits would overstate the result by €25m.

Interpretation

Someone must still finance the operator's cash gap. A positive economic value does not put the €25m gap into the project company's bank account.

A common mistake

Treating a subsidy as a new social benefit confuses the movement of money with additional goods, services or welfare.

How to correct it

Trace the taxpayer or donor on the other side within the chosen boundary, then identify any real behavioural or resource change.

Quick check
A plant has financial net present value of €3m. Unpriced local pollution imposes new costs with present value €5m. Assuming everything else is already valued appropriately, what is economic net present value?

The pollution uses economic resources or harms people outside the plant's accounts. Deduct its €5m present cost once from the €3m financial value.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A bus link costs €38m in resources, gives residents €52m gross travel benefits, collects €16m internal fares and creates €5m pollution costs. All are present values on the same basis.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate operator financial value, passengers' net benefit and total economic value.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    At what pollution cost would economic value reach zero? Does changing an internal fare alone change it if use is unchanged?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Operator financial value is €16m − €38m = minus €22m. Passenger net benefit is €36m. Deducting €5m harm gives €9m economic value.

Part B 3 marks

The threshold is €52m − €38m = €14m. A pure internal fare transfer cancels at unchanged use. Real demand or resource responses could change economic value.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A faster bus route attracts 200 new daily riders. Why is it usually wrong to give every new rider the full benefit received by someone already travelling?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The pre-existing thousand users gain the full two-euro improvement, giving €2,000 a day. The 200 marginal users have willingness to pay spread across the two-euro interval, so under linear demand they gain one euro each on average. Total user benefit is €2,200 a day. I would separately identify diverted customers and changes in fares, operator costs, pollution and congestion before deciding economic viability.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Only 100 of the 200 extra trips are generated. The other 100 switch routes and each saves a verified €1.50 against its actual previous route. What is revised user benefit?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Existing users still gain €2,000. Generated trips gain €100 using the half rule and diverted trips gain €150 using their specific comparison. Total user benefit is €2,250 daily under these separate baseline assumptions.
