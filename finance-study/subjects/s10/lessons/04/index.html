Cash-flow waterfall

Start from operating cash, taxes and required investment. Allocate the cash available to debt service, reserve accounts and distributions in contractual order.

The starting point

A project may generate cash while its shareholders receive nothing that month. A cash-flow waterfall is the contractual order of payments. It often gives operating needs, taxes and senior debt priority over distributions to the owners.

Begin with actual collections, deduct the costs and required investment specified in the agreement, then allocate the rest in order. Cash held in a restricted reserve remains an asset but may be unavailable for dividends. A covenant can further block distributions.

Theory and method

Debt service reserve account

DSRA funding waterfall · Distribution lock-up

A project pays every installment this year. Can a rising next-year installment still prevent its owners taking a dividend?

A lender can require a reserve sized to part of the next scheduled payment. Owners may receive cash only after the reserve is at the contractual target. Rising next-year service can therefore absorb today's operating surplus even when current scheduled debt is paid in full. A separate coverage lock-up can also retain residual cash. The same euro cannot simultaneously support the next payment and be distributed, so the reserve roll-forward and distribution test belong on separate lines.

How the result is built

R* = kDnext; top-up = max(0, R* − Ropen); dividend ≤ max(0, C − Dnow − top-up)

R* is target reserve, k the fraction of next-period debt service Dnext, Ropen existing reserve, C operating cash available for service and Dnow current contractual service. All cash amounts use euros.

  1. A project has €8m cash available, pays €5m current service and holds €1m in reserve. Next year's service is €6m and the reserve target equals half.
  2. The new target is €3m, requiring a €2m top-up. After scheduled service the €3m surplus splits into €2m reserve and at most €1m dividend.
  3. If next service rises to €8m, target becomes €4m. Funding the extra €3m consumes all current surplus even though current coverage remains 8/5 = 1.6.

When the reasoning holds

  • The assumed reserve covenant uses next year's scheduled cash. Contracts can instead refer to months or prior service. Read the definition and date.
  • The dividend is a ceiling before other tests. A forward coverage lock-up or unpaid senior amount can block an otherwise positive balance.
Terms in this lesson
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Covenant
A promise or condition in a financing agreement, such as maintaining an agreed level of cash.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
First example

A water project collects €10m. Operating costs, tax and required investment consume €4m, leaving €6m for debt service. Senior debt service takes €4m and a required reserve top-up takes €1m. The remaining €1m can go to owners if other tests pass.

Define the cash entering the waterfall

Cash available for debt service, often abbreviated CFADS, is a contractual measure. It generally begins with operating collections after costs, taxes and specified maintenance or investment needs. A change in receivables can make operating profit diverge from cash even when the asset is running normally.

Define whether the waterfall begins before or after a reserve movement and whether a receipt is allowed into the debt-service calculation. The same reserve cannot be included both in CFADS and again as separate support without double counting.

Seniority and restricted amounts

Senior interest and scheduled principal often have priority, followed by reserve replenishment, junior claims and distributions under the stated documents. A debt-service reserve account holds cash for a specified future payment. Its balance can support lenders while being restricted to a particular use.

A cash sweep may dedicate a share of surplus to accelerated debt repayment. A distribution lock-up can retain earnings inside the project if a coverage test fails. Triggering a lock-up need not be a payment default. Look up each test separately.

Timing changes who can be paid

A project may have sufficient annual cash but fail a quarterly payment because seasonal revenue arrives after the due date. Build the waterfall at the contractual testing frequency. Explicitly carry opening cash and reserve balances to the next period.

A reserve draw can bridge a temporary gap. It cannot repair permanently inadequate cash generation, and later top-up obligations can reduce distributions or increase debt. Analyse the first period after a reserve withdrawal as carefully as the withdrawal itself.

Method

Running a contractual waterfall

  1. Establish unrestricted operating cash

    Reconcile accounting figures to actual collections and specified costs. Keep reserve balances separate from operating inflows.

  2. Apply priorities

    Allocate cash down the contractual sequence, allowing only permitted reserve draws or retained cash to cover shortfalls.

  3. Test distributions

    Check both forward and backward coverage requirements, reserve targets and sweep terms before identifying cash owners can receive.

Worked case

A reserve is replenished before dividends

A water facility collects €15m in a year. Operating costs, taxes and required maintenance total €7m. Senior annual debt service is €5m. The reserve opens at €0.6m against a required €1.2m target. Half the remaining surplus after reserve top-up must repay extra debt.

Working

  1. CFADS is €8m, giving initial scheduled coverage of €8m/€5m = 1.6 times. Scheduled debt service leaves €3m.
  2. Topping the reserve up by €0.6m leaves €2.4m. Half is swept to extra principal, so €1.2m is available for distribution before any other lock-up.
  3. Closing reserve is €1.2m, separate from the €1.2m distribution. Total cash allocation reconciles to €8m as €5m scheduled, €0.6m reserve, €1.2m sweep and €1.2m distribution.

Interpretation

If a forward-looking test blocks distributions, the €1.2m stays inside the project under its rules. That does not imply a failure to have paid the €5m senior service.

A common mistake

Leaving a reserve top-up in both operating costs and post-debt allocations exaggerates the cash shortage.

How to correct it

Mark each line by cash account and waterfall priority, then reconcile opening cash, inflows, outflows and closing balances.

Quick check
A stated waterfall has €5m available for debt service, €3.6m senior payments and a mandatory €0.9m reserve top-up. How much remains for an otherwise permitted distribution?

Pay €3.6m first and reserve €0.9m. Restricted cash cannot be included in the owner's current payout under the stated order.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A plant has €9m CFADS, €6m senior service, a €2m reserve target and €1.2m already in reserve. It must sweep 25% of surplus after top-up to extra debt. All other distribution tests pass.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate reserve top-up, sweep and permissible distribution.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    A following quarter's CFADS is €1.4m against €1.8m debt service. If reserves may cover the gap, what is the immediate effect?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Scheduled debt leaves €3m. Top-up is €0.8m, leaving €2.2m. Sweep equals €0.55m and the remaining €1.65m may be distributed.

Part B 3 marks

Draw €0.4m from the €2m reserve. Debt service is paid and reserve falls to €1.6m. Later top-up requirements and distributions need recalculating.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A project pays every installment this year. Can a rising next-year installment still prevent its owners taking a dividend?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. The contract determines how much forward debt service must remain ring-fenced. Current coverage is healthy at 1.6, but if next service reaches €8m the reserve must hold €4m. The €1m already in it needs a €3m top-up, which equals all current surplus. I would show that loss of distribution capacity separately from a current payment failure.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If current cash falls to €4.5m against €5m due and reserve draws are permitted solely for scheduled debt, which balance is at risk?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Draw €0.5m from the reserve to pay scheduled service. The opening €1m reserve falls to €0.5m, before any future replenishment. Owners receive nothing and the forward reserve deficiency increases. Whether the deficiency triggers a separate breach depends on the cure provisions.
