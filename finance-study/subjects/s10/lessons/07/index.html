Development and blended finance

Evaluate what changes because concessional funding is provided. Distinguish crowding in private capital from subsidizing a project that would proceed anyway.

The starting point

A public development lender can supply patient or discounted finance where private investors cannot absorb a specific risk. Blended finance combines development resources with commercial funding. To judge its purpose, ask what the project would achieve without the public contribution.

Additionality concerns the difference the intervention makes. Private capital may be mobilized because a project can now borrow for longer, serve poorer areas or complete a first demonstration. Count commitments carefully and compare the supported transaction with realistic commercial alternatives.

Theory and method

Grant-equivalent concessionality

Financial additionality · Concessional loan grant element

A donor offers a €10m two-year interest-free loan. Is its subsidy €10m, and does the subsidy prove more people will be served?

A development agency pays cash today and expects to recover principal later. The euro value of the concession is the difference between today's disbursement and the discounted value of the repayments using a specified reference rate. This gives a measure of financial softness, which can be compared across repayment structures. A different question asks what commercial financing and service coverage would have occurred without it. A subsidized loan can largely replace market funding that was already available.

How the result is built

GE = L − Σₜ Pₜ/(1+r)ᵗ; grant element = GE/L

L is initial loan disbursement in euros, Pₜ each stipulated repayment, r annual comparison discount rate, t time in years, Σ sums repayments and GE is the euro grant equivalent under this convention.

  1. Advance €10m today and recover €10m in two years. At a teaching reference rate of 10%, repayment PV is €10m/1.1² = €8.264m.
  2. Grant equivalent is €10m − €8.264m = €1.736m. The grant element is about 17.36%. The public agency has advanced the whole €10m in cash despite the smaller subsidy measure.
  3. Compare the supported contract with documented commercial offers and expected service coverage. The €1.736m estimate alone cannot identify households or financing newly enabled.

When the reasoning holds

  • State the purpose and discount rate. Official grant-element conventions can prescribe rates, while economic subsidy valuation may use market-comparable risk and terms.
  • This illustration assumes full repayment and no fees. Default risk, grace or fees alter the repayment PV or the economic cost being measured.
Terms in this lesson
Blended finance
Combining public or philanthropic funding with commercial funding to support a development objective.
Counterfactual
What would probably have happened without the event, decision or policy being studied.
First example

A clean-water project attracts €8m in private loans after a €2m public loss buffer. The observed private-to-public ratio is four to one. If firm offers for the same €8m on the same terms already existed, the ratio alone offers weak evidence that the buffer mobilized extra money.

Two separate counterfactuals

Financial additionality asks what financing terms, risk-bearing capacity or private participation change because development finance is supplied. Development additionality asks what outcomes for people or the environment change. Securing a larger loan does not establish that additional households gain a service.

A credible baseline specifies what would have happened without the support, including commercially viable alternatives. Investors may already have intended to fund the initiative, while the subsidy improves returns without expanding coverage. Evidence matters more than the transaction's label.

First-loss capital and risk sharing

A first-loss tranche absorbs project losses before a senior tranche, up to its stated size. This can change the private lender's expected losses even when total project risk is unchanged. The public investor's commitment and its expected loss are different quantities.

Use minimal concessionality as a design question. Compare competitively obtained commercial terms, test a smaller guarantee or longer maturity, and explain why the proposed support changes a binding constraint. Assess whether it may crowd out another lender or nearby investment.

Measuring additional outcomes

Ratios of total private commitments to public funds are useful descriptions but are sensitive to what is counted. Attribute only mobilized financing plausibly linked to support and disclose whether amounts are committed, drawn or refinanced. Two institutions must not each claim the same entire financing contribution.

Select outcomes with a baseline, timeframe, observed users and a mechanism connecting finance to results. Compare with plausible alternative beneficiaries or rollout areas and consider displacement. Counting installed capacity alone does not demonstrate sustained access or affordability.

Method

Evaluating a blended transaction

  1. Identify the obstacle

    Use lender offers, demand analysis or contractual constraints to show what prevents the unassisted project.

  2. Map tranches and losses

    Calculate which provider bears each loss in plausible scenarios and what terms remain for commercial investors.

  3. Test the counterfactual

    Compare actual financing and observable development outcomes with credible alternatives and a feasible lower-support structure.

Worked case

A first-loss layer for rural connections

A €10m portfolio has €2m of public junior capital and €8m private senior money. Total portfolio losses are zero with 60% probability, €1m with 30% and €4m with 10%. Junior capital absorbs losses first.

Working

  1. For €1m loss, junior bears €1m. For €4m loss, junior bears its full €2m and senior bears €2m. Thus expected junior loss is €0.30m + €0.20m = €0.50m.
  2. Expected senior loss is 0.10 × €2m = €0.20m. The €0.70m sum matches expected project loss before it is divided between tranches.
  3. The observed private-to-public ratio is four. If comparable projects already secured €6m private funds without support, claiming the entire €8m as newly mobilized would need additional counterfactual evidence.

Interpretation

Test whether new households received viable connections and kept them working at affordable tariffs. A financing ratio alone cannot answer those questions.

A common mistake

Calling the full face value of a contingent first-loss tranche its expected cost confuses maximum risk capacity with probability-weighted loss.

How to correct it

Show commitment, expected loss and stressed loss separately, including discounting when payment timing matters.

Quick check
A developer requests a subsidy for a commercially attractive solar farm. Which evidence most directly tests whether support changes the outcome?

Additionality is a comparison with a counterfactual. Funding totals describe the supported transaction but cannot on their own show which investment, access or development outcomes changed.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A €6m portfolio has €1.5m public first-loss cover. Loss is zero with 70%, €1m with 20%, or €3m with 10% probability. Remaining loss is private.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Allocate expected losses across public and private parties.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Which evidence could establish development additionality beyond loss allocation?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Public bears €1m in the middle state and €1.5m in the high state. Expected public loss is €0.35m. Expected private loss is 0.10 × €1.5m = €0.15m.

Part B 3 marks

Compare service coverage, affordability or reliability with a credible unsupported rollout. Verify delivered outcomes and identify why private lenders or sponsors would not otherwise have produced them.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A donor offers a €10m two-year interest-free loan. Is its subsidy €10m, and does the subsidy prove more people will be served?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

No. I would report the €10m cash advanced and the approximately €1.736m grant equivalent separately. I would then test whether the project could obtain comparable financing, perhaps for fewer customers or a shorter term. Only evidence on that alternative, plus verified service outcomes, supports a claim that the concession expanded access. A large loan commitment by itself answers neither question.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The same €10m is instead repaid after one year with no interest. How does grant equivalent change at the same rate?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Repayment PV becomes €10m/1.10 = €9.091m. Grant equivalent falls to about €0.909m or 9.09% of the loan, because the agency gives up the use of its cash for one year rather than two.
