Development appraisal and the residual land bid

Derive a maximum land offer from staged property development cash flows and purchaser costs.

In this lecture

  1. Start with what can legally be built and sold
  2. Solve for the unknown
  3. Turn an appraisal into a bid
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. The same development supports different land bids at different hurdle rates
  6. A developer bids for a parcel for apartment sales
  7. Independent exam problem

Theory and terminology

Residual land valuation

property development appraisal · required return

What you should be able to explain

  • Derive a maximum land offer from staged property development cash flows and purchaser costs.
  • Distinguish the price of land from project NPV and quantify a delay in unit sales.

Start with what can legally be built and sold

A site has development potential when a feasible scheme can earn more than its non-land costs and the return required for taking risk. Gross development value is the estimated proceeds from the completed development. Establish the permitted unit count, likely sale prices and actual payment schedule before valuing the land.

Construction, infrastructure obligations, professional fees, marketing and a realistic contingency use part of the proceeds. Unsold apartments produce no sale cash until closing. A claimed sale price and an agreed reservation can still differ from the ultimate proceeds if buyers can withdraw.

Solve for the unknown

A simple residual starts with completed value and subtracts all development costs plus a specified profit allowance. A timed DCF instead asks which land price reduces NPV to zero at the required project return. Make each assumption once. In this unlevered case the hurdle rate already compensates the developer for time and risk.

Purchaser costs may scale with the land price. A 5% acquisition allowance means that buying a €1m site costs €1.05m before fixed fees. Subtract fixed fees before dividing the available present value by 1.05. Real offices would investigate taxes, financing and planning terms for the particular purchase.

Turn an appraisal into a bid

At an offered land price below the modeled ceiling, positive NPV is the present-value headroom relative to the hurdle. That headroom can be small beside a delay in sales or a change in build cost. Calculate a second timeline while keeping the source of the change explicit.

The ceiling is what this investor can afford under its return target. A market valuation also needs comparable land transactions, achievable sales assumptions and a market-participant view. A financing facility can change equity timing, but it does not eliminate the project’s exposure to delayed sales.

Method

Build the result

Maximum land price = [Σₜ CFₜ/(1+r)ᵗ − C₀]/(1+a).

CFₜ is post-selling-cost development receipts less non-land project expenditure at the end of year t, in euros. r is the annual unlevered return target. C₀ is fixed non-land expenditure at purchase and a is acquisition cost as a fraction of the land price. Σₜ sums the future cash flows.

  1. Build a separate cash line for each period, including build contingency and selling fees when paid.
  2. Discount future net receipts, then subtract purchase-date costs that do not depend on the price of land.
  3. Set the remaining amount equal to land price plus its percentage purchaser costs. For an actual asking price, subtract the resulting all-in land outflow to obtain project NPV.
Worked case

A developer bids for a parcel for apartment sales

All figures are €m. Gross sales settle €3 in year one and €12 in year two. Selling costs are 3% of gross sales. Building costs €6 and €3 respectively, with 5% contingency on each. Infrastructure costs €0.40 in year two. Purchase-date design and fixed closing expenses total €0.60. Purchaser costs equal 5% of land price. Hurdle is 12%. Seller asks €2.50m.

Working

  1. Year-one cash is 3 × 0.97 − 6 × 1.05 = −€3.39m. Year-two cash is 12 × 0.97 − 3 × 1.05 − 0.40 = €8.09m.
  2. The present value of both flows is −3.39/1.12 + 8.09/1.12² = €3.42251m. After fixed costs €2.82251m remains for land plus its percentage fees.
  3. Maximum land price is 2.82251/1.05 = €2.68811m. At the €2.50m ask, NPV is 2.82251 − 2.50 × 1.05 = €0.19751m.
  4. If year-two net sales of €11.64m slip to year three while year-two costs remain due, cash is −€3.55m in year two and +€11.64m in year three. Repeating the calculation gives a €1.74124m land ceiling and −€0.79670m NPV at the ask.

Decision and interpretation

The base bid meets the return target, but a one-year sale delay erases the headroom. Seek substantiated settlement dates or negotiate a lower or contingent land price before releasing a binding offer.

Quick check
After discounting all project receipts and deducting fixed non-land expenses, a developer has €3.30m available for land and purchaser costs. Those costs equal 10% of land price. What is the maximum bid?

Land plus purchaser costs is 1.10 times the bid. Dividing €3.30m by 1.10 gives €3.00m land and €0.30m purchaser costs.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A one-year development sells for €10m at completion and pays €0.5m selling expense and €4.5m construction costs that day. It incurs €0.5m of non-land expenses today. Purchaser costs are 10% of land price and the return target is 10%. Find the ceiling and evaluate a €3.8m ask.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The completion cash flow is 10 − 0.5 − 4.5 = €5m. Present value is €5m/1.10 = €4.54545m.
  2. After fixed costs, €4.04545m is available for land and purchaser costs. Maximum stated land price is €4.04545m/1.10 = €3.67769m.
  3. The €3.8m ask costs €4.18m including purchaser costs. NPV is €4.04545m − €4.18m = −€0.13455m, below the specified return requirement.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How much can a developer pay for a site while meeting a required return if buyers may settle a year late?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would project net sale receipts and development costs by date, discount them at the developer’s required return and solve for land plus purchaser costs. The example supports €2.688m if sales settle on schedule, but only €1.741m if the second tranche settles one year later. The €2.50m ask clears one test and fails the other, so the credibility of the settlement date matters to the bid.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The seller requests a percentage of future sale prices instead of an upfront price. Can you simply replace the land price with the same nominal amount?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Value the contingent payment on its contractual thresholds, timing and costs in each scenario. It shifts exposure to achieved sales and may postpone cash outflow. Rebuild both the discounted distribution to the seller and the developer’s retained project NPV before agreeing equivalent terms.
