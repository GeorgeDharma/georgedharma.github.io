Alternative structures

Compare a conventional project loan with an asset- or use-based sukuk structure. Trace contractual payments and rights under the stated case documents.

The starting point

A conventional loan entitles a lender to repayment under a debt contract. Sukuk are certificates whose rights and payments depend on a specified asset, use or investment arrangement. For an ijara, or lease-based, structure trace the lessor, the user of the asset and the rental flows.

Start with who holds which rights, then identify who must pay, when, and what happens if payment stops. A reference to equipment or land does not by itself establish investors' ability to sell that property. Recourse and enforceability depend on the particular documents and legal setting.

Theory and method

Sukuk al-ijara lease structure

Lease-based sukuk · Purchase undertaking

Two ijara certificates promise the same rent and final purchase price. Which could carry greater credit risk?

A transport operator obtains asset use through a lease, and certificate holders receive distributions funded by the agreed rental stream. A final purchase undertaking can support redemption. Investors' recovery depends on which property or use rights were effectively transferred, the obligor's ability to pay and the remedies after a default. Two structures with the same expected performing cash flows can therefore have different downside values. Trace the right to each payment before assigning probability or recovery.

How the result is built

V = PV(expected net rentals) + PV(expected enforceable terminal proceeds)

V is today's value under the specified valuation convention, PV discounts dated euros at the stated rate, net rentals are collected lease payments after allowed costs, and terminal proceeds depend on enforceable recovery and redemption rights.

  1. On €10m face, assume one-year net rent of €0.6m is certain and final redemption pays €10m with probability 90%. In the 10% default state Structure A recovers €7m, Structure B €3m.
  2. Expected terminal amounts are €9.7m and €9.3m. With the same stipulated 5% discount convention, A is (€0.6m + €9.7m)/1.05 = €9.810m.
  3. B is (€0.6m + €9.3m)/1.05 = €9.429m. The roughly €0.381m difference arises solely from the stated enforceable default recoveries.

When the reasoning holds

  • The rent is assumed recoverable in both states. If lessee default also interrupts rent, include that dependence explicitly.
  • The exercise values the supplied scenario weights and rate. Market pricing requires consistent risk adjustments, and enforceability must be verified under actual documents.
Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Sukuk
Islamic finance certificates linked to an underlying asset, use or investment. The contract determines holders’ rights and payments.
Counterparty
The other party to a financial contract or trade.
How to read the notation
Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.

First example

In a simplified lease structure, a special-purpose issuer receives €5m rent from an operator, pays €0.4m specified expenses and distributes €4.6m to certificate holders. The operator's credit quality affects that cash even if the equipment is physically intact.

Mapping a lease-based certificate

In a simplified ijara transaction, investors subscribe for certificates issued through a special-purpose entity. The structure acquires specified rights in a tangible asset or its use and leases those rights to an operator. Rent supports periodic distributions under the documents.

Identify the timeline of subscription, acquisition, lease, distributions and end-of-term transfer or purchase undertaking. Investor rights and payments can depend on asset condition, lessee credit, agency arrangements and enforceability. One structure cannot represent every form of sukuk.

Asset rights and recovery

A transaction can reference a physical asset without giving investors unrestricted ability to sell it. An asset-backed structure with effective transfer and enforceable access to assets can differ materially from an asset-based structure where practical recovery depends on an obligor's undertaking.

Ask who holds title, beneficial interests or rights of use. Which party bears maintenance and damage, and whether independent recovery is available after default. The answers require the governing documents and local legal framework. The name on the certificate does not answer them.

Comparing economic exposures

For comparison with a conventional loan, trace the payment amounts, dates, currency and counterparty risk borne by each investor. Similar promised yields can hide different remedies, insurance obligations, residual value and conditional payments.

A purchase undertaking may specify a final price subject to stated conditions. If enforceability or obligor credit is weak, the projected maturity receipt deserves a downside case. Separate the expected operational cash from enforceable legal rights.

Method

Following investor cash flows

  1. Draw the parties

    Identify issuer or trustee, investors, asset seller, lessee, service agent and any purchase undertaking provider.

  2. Trace priority and costs

    Relate collected rent to required expenses, reserves and investor distributions rather than equating gross rent with net investor return.

  3. Model distress

    State which rights remain accessible if rent stops, who can exercise them and what recovery follows under explicit assumptions.

Worked case

Lease cash with two recovery routes

A trust holds lease rights supporting €2.4m annual rent, €0.3m annual specified servicing costs and €20m of certificates. For one period, assume full collections and no reserve movements or other expenses.

Working

  1. Cash available to distribute is €2.1m, or 10.5% of the €20m face amount for the year. That ratio measures this cash distribution, not a guaranteed lifetime yield.
  2. Under a stress assumption the operator pays only €1.2m, while servicing cost stays €0.3m. Available distribution becomes €0.9m, or 4.5% of face.
  3. At maturity one hypothetical legal structure permits enforceable net asset-sale recovery of €14m. Another allows only a claim on a distressed obligor with €9m expected recovery. The same asset description can support substantially different downside cash.

Interpretation

Do not add both recoveries unless the actual documents establish genuinely additive claims and rule out double recovery. Separate yield calculations from enforceability analysis.

A common mistake

Assuming certificate holders can seize the project simply because a tangible asset is mentioned can overstate downside recovery.

How to correct it

Verify the interest transferred, available enforcement route, creditor priority and any separately enforceable undertaking.

Quick check
A prospectus mentions a port asset and an operator's purchase undertaking at maturity. What should an analyst inspect to assess repayment rights?

Those linked terms identify the source of periodic and final payments and the remedies after non-payment. Similar expected cash flows can rest on different legal claims.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A simplified lease issuer collects €3.6m rent on €30m face, pays €0.4m specified costs and places €0.2m into a required reserve. Ignore other cash flows.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate the current distribution and its percentage of face.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Rent falls by a quarter while costs and reserve requirements stay fixed. Recalculate, then name evidence needed for any claim about recovery of principal.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Distribution is €3.0m, or 10% of face. The €0.2m reserve remains restricted under the stated terms.

Part B 3 marks

Rent becomes €2.7m and distribution €2.1m, or 7%. Inspect title or beneficial rights, purchase undertaking, priority and enforcement documents before estimating principal recovery.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two ijara certificates promise the same rent and final purchase price. Which could carry greater credit risk?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would examine the transfer documents and remedies. Both certificates pay the same €0.6m rent in this exercise, and both redeem at €10m in the good state. In default A has €7m recovery while B has €3m. Applying the same weights and discounting leaves A worth about €381,000 more. The label ijara and matching headline distributions do not resolve that legal and credit distinction.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the market cannot verify the claimed asset rights in A, how would you present the decision?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Show the two recovery cases and the €0.381m valuation range rather than silently assuming €7m recovery. Require confirmation of transfer, priority, control of sale proceeds and the purchase undertaking. Until then the high-recovery price needs explicit qualification.
