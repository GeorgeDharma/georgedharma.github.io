Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

Marginal use of a pottery kiln

A studio can hire out its kiln for €110 this evening or fire up to 20 of its own plates. Own plates face single-price demand P = 40 − Q in euros per plate and cost €8 each to make. Operating tonight requires an additional €80 setup cost, avoidable on renting the kiln out. An old €250 design fee is unrecoverable.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

Bus fares and congestion

A bus operator tries fares of €6 and €8. Comparable periods yield 140 and 100 trips respectively. Each trip costs €2 in private resources and causes €1 of external crowding harm. Treat these data as an observed interval, with no other cost change.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Pricing a pool and monitoring a borrower

A lender’s pool contains equally many safe borrowers who always repay and risky borrowers who repay with probability 0.60. There is zero default recovery. Both types accept a promised €110, but at €140 only the risky type accepts. Separately, a funded firm chooses between €105 for sure and €160 or zero with equal chances, with existing debt of €90.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Financing an irreversible installation

A contractor has €30 now and an alternative yielding zero. A special installation costs €50 now. The contractor must borrow the €20 shortfall at 10% for one year. Treat the €30 of own funds as otherwise earning zero and assume no time preference. If installed, the client obtains €88 gross benefit next year. Without a prior contract the parties split the €88 equally and the client makes no other payment.

Case 1 · 10 marks

Marginal use of a pottery kiln

Part A 3 marks

Find the best positive own-production output and price.

Revenue is 40Q − Q² and marginal revenue equals 40 − 2Q. Equating to marginal cost €8 yields Q = 16, within capacity. Price is €24. Award for the revenue relation, equation and feasible price/quantity.

Part B 4 marks

Compare own production with leasing the kiln and explain the design fee.

Own production contribution net of setup is (€24 − €8) × 16 − €80 = €176. Leasing yields €110. Own production wins by €66. The design fee was spent under both alternatives and is sunk. Award for the two alternatives, difference and treatment of the past cost.

Part C 3 marks

If capacity falls to 8 plates, does the decision change?

The unconstrained optimum exceeds capacity. At Q = 8, P = €32, so profit after setup is (€32 − €8) × 8 − €80 = €112. This is €2 above leasing. The decision stays the same but is sensitive to minor extra costs.

Case 2 · 10 marks

Bus fares and congestion

Part A 4 marks

Calculate midpoint elasticity and revenue at each price.

Quantity changes −40/120 = −33.33% and price changes 2/7 ≈ 28.57%. Elasticity is about −1.167. Revenues are €840 and €800. Award for both midpoint changes, elasticity and revenues.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate private contribution at both prices and compare the rankings by revenue and contribution.

At €6, contribution = (€6 − €2) × 140 = €560. At €8 it is (€8 − €2) × 100 = €600. Revenue favours the lower fare but profit before fixed costs favours the higher by €40. Two marks for contributions and one for interpretation.

Part C 3 marks

A €1 per-trip crowding fee is introduced. Recalculate operator contribution, and explain why this alone does not rank social welfare.

The figures become (€6 − €3) × 140 = €420 and (€8 − €3) × 100 = €500. The fee changes the private calculation. Consumers’ benefits and the underlying real crowding harm also matter to social welfare, and fee receipts are transfers.

Case 3 · 10 marks

Pricing a pool and monitoring a borrower

Part A 3 marks

Calculate expected repayment per funded loan at the two quoted terms. Identify the mechanism.

At €110 expected probability is 0.5 × 1 + 0.5 × 0.60 = 0.80, giving €88. At €140 only risky applicants remain, giving €84. The decrease follows adverse selection before contracting. Award for each expectation and identifying the timing.

Part B 4 marks

For the funded firm, compare the two choices for its owner, creditor and for total value.

Safe plan gives €15 to the owner, €90 to the creditor and €105 total. Risky plan gives the owner €70 or zero, expected €35, while the creditor receives €90 or zero, expected €45. Total €80. The owner prefers risk, contrary to the higher total value of the safe plan. Award for each party and correct project interpretation.

Part C 3 marks

Would a collectible €45 owner guarantee payable only on failure induce the safe plan? Distinguish this mechanism from the first part.

Risky owner payoff falls to €35 − 0.5 × €45 = €12.50. The sure €15 is now preferred. The guarantee changes a hidden project choice after funding. The quoted loan terms changed who accepted before funding.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Financing an irreversible installation

Part A 3 marks

Would the contractor invest under equal division? Show its resources and future debt service.

The contractor spends €30 today and owes €22 next year. It receives €44 under equal sharing, leaving €22 after debt service compared with keeping €30 if it declined. Net disadvantage is €8. It declines. Award for financing, payment and comparison.

Part B 4 marks

Find a future payment that both parties strictly prefer to no installation and give the interval of such payments.

The contractor must recover €30 own funds plus €22 debt service, requiring more than €52. The client needs to pay less than €88. Any price strictly between €52 and €88 works. For example, €60 leaves the contractor €8 better off and the client €28. Award for both bounds, example and payoffs.

Part C 3 marks

Suppose the contractor’s borrowing cap is €15 and the client can advance €5 today. Explain how timing alters feasibility and give a feasible financing structure.

Without an advance, €30 + €15 falls short by €5. A €5 advance plus €30 own cash and €15 debt covers €50. Debt service is then €16.50. A later payment such as €55 recovers the contractor’s own contribution and debt and leaves both better off after including the advance. Other consistent feasible splits earn credit.

Additional exam cases

After the core paper, test the additional lectures below. Each case opens with an answer space and its own worked solution. Use the worked solutions to assess these cases separately from the 40-mark paper.

  1. F02.11 · Who really pays a tax on each sale?

    In another market the inverse curves are buyer willingness to pay Pb = 50 − Q/2 and seller willingness to accept Ps = 5 + Q/4, in pounds. Work out prices, quantity, receipts and deadweight loss after a £6 tax.

  2. F02.12 · Operate at a loss, suspend, or leave the market?

    A seasonal kiosk can serve either 400 guests or none. At £9 per guest the variable cost for 400 is £3,100. Its £900 licence fee is nonrefundable for this season, but renewal would be voluntary at the same fee. Decide today and for next season, assuming identical demand.

  3. F02.13 · Untangle a price change from a loss of purchasing power

    A different household spends half of its £160 budget on books and half on meals. Books fall from £4 to £2. Meals remain £2. Use old-bundle compensation to split the change in book purchases.
