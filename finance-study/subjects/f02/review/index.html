New and expanded lessons

F02.11

Who really pays a tax on each sale?

F02 works elasticity, market structure and external costs. Solving jointly for the price paid, price received, quantity, government receipts and lost gains from trade gives the statutory tax a complete market-equilibrium treatment.

  • Solve a market with a specific tax collected on each unit traded.
  • Distinguish tax receipts, economic incidence and deadweight loss.

F02.12

Operate at a loss, suspend, or leave the market?

The course already compares marginal revenue and cost and illustrates an avoidable setup cost. A full price-taking cost schedule separates the short-run shutdown threshold from the long-run break-even price and traces the effect of entry.

  • Use total, variable and fixed costs to choose output and decide on temporary shutdown.
  • Explain when economic losses imply exit and when entry can erode economic profit.

F02.13

Untangle a price change from a loss of purchasing power

F02 introduces choices between present and future consumption. This lesson gives the same-period household problem a worked decomposition using an explicit budget that can still buy the original bundle at the new prices.

  • Construct a compensating budget that makes the original bundle affordable.
  • Separate a total change in quantity into a substitution effect and an income effect.
