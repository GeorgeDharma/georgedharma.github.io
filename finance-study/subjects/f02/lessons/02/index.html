Marginal choice

Compare the extra benefit and cost of the next unit. A profitable business can still lose money by expanding one unit too far.

The starting point

A hotel has one empty room tonight. Yesterday’s building cost and salaries may matter for long-term viability, but the immediate decision concerns what changes if the hotel sells this room. That change is the margin.

Compare marginal benefit with marginal cost. Include lost sales from other customers, extra labour and capacity that could have another use. An average cost that spreads the whole building bill across rooms is a different measure.

Theory and method

Shadow price of a capacity constraint

Marginal value · Constrained optimization

How much is one more hour of a fully booked machine worth if two orders compete for it?

A print shop allocates machine hours between jobs that earn different contributions. The most attractive available work goes first. An extra hour creates value only if it can be sold to an order the shop would otherwise refuse, which makes the value depend on where current capacity sits. A shadow price measures the improvement in the best feasible objective when the binding limit is relaxed slightly. Once all attractive demand is served, further capacity can have little value.

How the result is built

Marginal value of one extra hour = V(H+1) − V(H).

H is usable machine hours in the period. V(H) is the largest feasible contribution in euros at capacity H after variable job costs. The difference is a discrete shadow value in euros per extra hour.

  1. The first two hours of work earn €70 each and the next three €40 each. Any further work would earn zero. Sort compatible jobs by contribution per machine hour.
  2. At three hours use two high-value hours and one €40 hour. Best contribution is €180. At four hours it is €220.
  3. The fourth hour therefore adds €40. The sixth hour adds zero because the five profitable hours have already been supplied.

When the reasoning holds

  • Jobs are divisible into the stated one-hour blocks without setup interactions. A two-hour indivisible batch could change which mix fits.
  • Machine hours are the only binding constraint and the demand list is credible. If operators are unavailable, buying machine time alone may create no extra output.
Terms in this lesson
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
First example

A late guest offers €75. Cleaning and utilities add €18, and no other guest would take the room. Accepting adds €57 to profit. If a certain €90 guest would be displaced and both guests cost the same to serve, taking the €75 offer instead costs €15 of contribution.

Incremental revenue and cost

A restaurant is considering opening for one more evening each week. The question is how the restaurant’s total receipts and costs change with that evening. Include wages for a new shift, extra power and food. Allocated shares of unchanged monthly rent will affect the restaurant’s accounts but do not change the immediate choice.

Marginal analysis compares two nearby plans. When units are indivisible, compare total outcomes with and without the next unit. For a smooth quantity choice, the familiar condition marginal benefit equals marginal cost describes an interior optimum under suitable curvature. At a capacity limit, or where costs jump, the best feasible quantity can occur at a boundary.

A fixed cost can become avoidable

A cost is fixed relative to a specified range and time horizon. Suppose a kitchen can serve 50 customers with one cook but needs an entire second shift for customer 51. The apparent cost of an isolated sandwich understates the cost of crossing that staffing threshold. Calculate the whole block of extra orders if they come together.

The reverse situation also occurs. Closing for one day might save ingredients yet leave all contracted staffing costs payable. A decision to close an entire location for a year can release those commitments. Use the resource change associated with the choice in front of you and explain which commitments persist.

Capacity and displaced business

If an additional low-price booking uses the last available slot, include the contribution sacrificed on a displaced full-price booking. Use contribution after the costs avoided by losing it. If displacement is uncertain, estimate the expected value of the alternative on the available information at the decision date.

A positive marginal contribution can justify a temporary order without implying that its price supports the business over the long run. The business eventually needs revenue to replace equipment and finance new commitments. State explicitly whether you are solving a one-off utilization decision or a continuing pricing decision.

Method

Compare complete plans

  1. Construct the base case

    Record demand served, resources committed and total contribution without the new activity.

  2. Build the change

    Include the whole change in receipts and avoidable costs, plus displaced contribution or a step increase in capacity costs.

  3. Make the decision

    Choose the plan with higher expected contribution for the stated horizon and show the condition that would reverse it.

Worked case

A print shop’s night shift

A customer offers €1,200 to print an urgent brochure. Paper costs €340 and a night crew costs €420. Taking the order would use a reserved slot that has a 60% chance of being used by a client generating €600 contribution after its own variable costs.

Working

  1. The urgent brochure contributes €1,200 − €340 − €420 = €440 before allowing for displacement.
  2. Expected sacrifice on the reserved slot is 0.60 × €600 = €360. Net expected improvement is €80, assuming equal timing and risk neutrality.
  3. If the chance of regular demand rises above €440/€600 = 73.33%, displacement makes the urgent order unattractive. At equality the choices tie.

Interpretation

The existing daytime rent is already committed under both alternatives. Including it only in the brochure calculation would bias the decision.

A common mistake

An analyst treats a positive sales price as a positive contribution.

How to correct it

List all additional resources and the best alternative use of constrained capacity. A positive receipt can accompany a fall in total profit.

Quick check
With no displacement, a new housekeeping overtime charge of €60 on top of the €18 makes the €75 offer worth what?

The extra cost becomes €78. The hotel compares the fee with that cost and loses €3 by accepting.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A cold store earns €4 contribution per extra box before a special cooling charge. It can take 20 additional boxes at that margin using existing equipment, or 40 boxes requiring a one-off €95 cooling shift. It must choose one plan or neither.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate incremental contribution for both offers and choose.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    At what maximum cooling charge would the 40-box offer be at least as good as 20? Explain why average rent per box is irrelevant here.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Twenty boxes add 20 × €4 = €80. Forty add 40 × €4 − €95 = €65. Choose 20, which beats both €65 and zero.

Part B 4 marks

Solve €160 − charge ≥ €80, giving at most €80. Both offers use space already rented on unchanged terms, so allocating rent across boxes does not change the comparison.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How much is one more hour of a fully booked machine worth if two orders compete for it?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would solve the order allocation with and without the extra hour. At three hours the shop earns €180 and at four €220, so the added capacity is worth €40 before its own acquisition cost. I would accept a €30 overtime quote for that fourth hour. The same quote for hour six would be uneconomic, since no extra profitable order is waiting.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A contractor sells three extra hours as one indivisible package for €95 when capacity is already three. Would you buy?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The package takes capacity from three to six. Contribution moves from €180 to €260, a gain of €80. Paying €95 would reduce profit by €15. Applying the initial €40 shadow price to all three hours overstates value when the profitable work runs out.
