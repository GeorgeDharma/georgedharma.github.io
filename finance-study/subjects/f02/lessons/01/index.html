Opportunity cost

Using capital for a warehouse means giving up the next-best use of that money. The economic cost includes that forgone benefit.

The starting point

A family owns an empty shop and wants to use it for a bakery. Paying no rent to a landlord makes the shop look free. Yet using it means giving up the income a reliable tenant would pay.

Opportunity cost is the benefit of the best feasible alternative forgone. Count only alternatives that compete for the same resource and use comparable amounts after costs. A past payment that cannot be recovered is a sunk cost and does not change with today’s choice.

Theory and method

Comparative advantage and the production possibility frontier

Opportunity cost · Gains from specialization

One analyst is faster at both valuation and credit work. Why might a team still assign valuation work to the slower analyst?

Two analysts devote a morning to one of two kinds of file. Each can produce a different mix, so shifting an hour between tasks sacrifices a different number of completed files. Absolute speed tells a manager who can produce more in a particular task. Comparative advantage looks at what else that person must give up. Keeping the analyst with the largest sacrifice on the job where their time matters most can raise the team’s total output.

How the result is built

Opportunity cost of one valuation = credit files forgone / valuation files gained.

Each number is a change in completed files over the same time interval. A production possibility frontier is the boundary of feasible combinations of completed valuation and credit files.

  1. In four hours Ada can complete eight valuations or sixteen credit files. One valuation costs her two credit files.
  2. In the same four hours Ben can complete four valuations or four credit files. One valuation costs him one credit file.
  3. If the team must supply four valuations, assigning them to Ben leaves Ada free to produce sixteen credit files. Assigning them to Ada consumes half her day, leaving her eight credit files plus Ben’s four, or twelve.

When the reasoning holds

  • Both types of file meet the same quality standard and tasks can be handed over. Extensive rework could consume the four-file gain.
  • Productivity is constant across the small workload and there is demand for the credit files. Idle credit capacity would lower their true opportunity cost.
Terms in this lesson
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
First example

The shop could earn €12,000 a year in net rent. The bakery would earn €18,000 after wages and supplies, before charging for the space. With equal risk and timing, using the shop for the bakery produces €6,000 more per year than leasing it.

The choice determines the cost

Suppose a furniture maker already owns a workshop. Using it on a new contract requires no cheque for rent, but occupying it prevents a tenant from paying for it. Economic cost measures resources given up by choosing one action rather than the best feasible alternative. Accounting records alone do not always reveal that sacrifice.

List mutually exclusive choices and say whose decision is being evaluated. Using a worker’s evening to make a product might displace paid overtime, family time or nothing the worker values equally. The relevant cost is the best available alternative for that evening, not the sum of every imaginable alternative.

Sunk costs, recoverable costs and space

A paid design fee with no resale value is sunk when deciding whether to produce the designed item. One can still decide whether to incur future production costs. By contrast, material bought years ago can have a current opportunity cost if it could be sold or would otherwise have to be replaced.

Ask what would actually happen to each resource if the project were declined. If specialized stock would be discarded for a disposal fee, using it can even avoid a cost. If the same stock is needed for a confirmed alternative order, using it here could force a replacement purchase. Its historical invoice answers neither question by itself.

When a constraint binds

A machine running at capacity puts a value on each additional hour. An order using that time may displace the contribution from another order. Contribution is revenue less costs that change when the activity occurs. Idle capacity has a different opportunity cost, provided accepting the order creates no other credible sacrifice.

A calculation can be correct for today but wrong for a longer decision. A salaried worker may be available for one short special order with no new cash cost. Repeated commitments can require hiring or displace regular work. State the decision horizon and show the assumption making the resource available at that horizon.

Method

Price the next-best alternative

  1. Map the counterfactual

    Describe what happens to each scarce resource if the proposed activity is declined.

  2. Count only avoidable differences

    Include foregone contribution or proceeds, additional spending and any cost avoided. Ignore past payments common to both choices.

  3. Test availability

    Check that the proposed alternative is permitted, feasible and supported by demand. A theoretical rental quote without a willing tenant is weak evidence.

Worked case

An unused production die

A factory paid €14,000 for a custom die last year. A dealer now offers €3,000 for it. Using it once will exhaust it. A customer offers €9,500 for an order requiring the die, €2,800 of materials and €1,900 of extra labour.

Working

  1. Declining the order yields €3,000 from the dealer. Accepting generates €9,500 − €2,800 − €1,900 = €4,800 cash from production.
  2. The order is €1,800 better than selling the die. Its relevant cost includes €3,000 foregone sale proceeds, not the historical €14,000.
  3. The minimum acceptable price on these assumptions is €2,800 + €1,900 + €3,000 = €7,700. A rush shipment costing €2,100 would reverse the decision.

Interpretation

This evaluation compares the same dates and ignores taxes. A later payment or uncertain dealer bid would need a comparable present value or risk treatment.

A common mistake

A student adds both the resale value and original purchase price of the same asset.

How to correct it

Identify what each number changes between alternatives. Keep the current sacrificed resale proceeds and remove the past, irreversible purchase payment.

Quick check
If the best tenant raises the offer to €20,000 with the same conditions, which use has the higher annual economic benefit?

Both €18,000 and €20,000 are available only by committing the same shop. Subtracting the bakery income from the rent gives the €2,000 advantage.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A venue spent €4,000 investigating a festival. It can now earn €7,500 in cash from the festival after new direct costs, let the space to a tenant for €6,000 net, or leave it idle for no return.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate the festival’s opportunity cost and incremental gain.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Suppose cancellation of a reusable exhibition is also required, but the venue cannot both lease the space and run the exhibition. Exhibition contribution would be €7,900. Redo the decision.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

The best feasible alternative is the €6,000 lease. The festival gives up that income and gains €7,500 − €6,000 = €1,500 relative to it. The €4,000 spent already is sunk.

Part B 4 marks

The relevant alternative becomes the exhibition alone, worth €7,900. The festival is €400 worse. Adding €6,000 and €7,900 together would assume the incompatible activities could both occur.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

One analyst is faster at both valuation and credit work. Why might a team still assign valuation work to the slower analyst?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would allocate the four valuations to Ben. Ada is absolutely faster at both tasks, but each valuation she takes on gives up two credit files while Ben gives up only one. By assigning valuation to Ben we still deliver four and can produce sixteen credit files instead of twelve. I would check review quality and actual demand before translating that production gain into financial value.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if Ben needs one hour of Ada’s review time for his four completed valuations?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Ada then has three hours left for credit at four files an hour, producing twelve. Ben’s four valuations plus twelve credit files now tie the other plan. Additional coordination or quality differences could decide the assignment. Review time belongs in the feasible production set.
