Bargaining and games

Compare each party’s alternatives if negotiations fail. A good project can collapse when parties cannot credibly commit to what they promise.

The starting point

A factory and a nearby supplier can both profit by trading together. Whether they agree also depends on what each can earn elsewhere. Those alternatives set the minimum gains each party will accept.

A bargaining surplus is the value created above both parties’ alternatives. The split can depend on bargaining power and commitment. If someone must invest first and the investment cannot be reused, the other party may later demand better terms. Anticipating that risk can deter investment.

Theory and method

Nash bargaining solution

Disagreement payoff · Bargaining power

Two partners can create €180 of value together. How would you distinguish splitting that total from splitting the surplus over their alternatives?

A contractor can earn €50 elsewhere while a distributor can earn €30. If they cooperate, the total available is €180. Each must prefer the bargain to its own outside option, so the negotiable gain is only €100. The Nash bargaining solution gives a formal division of these gains under specified bargaining assumptions. Different powers change the split of the remaining gain, while outside opportunities set the initial claims each party must meet.

How the result is built

With transferable surplus S = V − dA − dB, payoffs are dA + αS and dB + (1−α)S.

V is total joint value in euros. dA and dB are the disagreement payoffs, S the surplus over them and α the contractor’s bargaining weight between 0 and 1.

  1. Let x be A’s gain over its alternative, leaving S−x for B. The weighted Nash product is x^α(S−x)^(1−α).
  2. Maximizing its logarithm equates α/x to (1−α)/(S−x), giving x = αS for positive surplus.
  3. At α=0.60, allocate €60 of gain to the contractor and €40 to the distributor. Adding alternatives produces final payoffs €110 and €70.

When the reasoning holds

  • Total value can be transferred between parties and the sharing model is appropriate. Nontransferable benefits or risk attitudes can require bargaining over utilities instead of euros.
  • The disagreement payoffs are attainable if this negotiation fails. An empty threat cannot support the assumed bargaining position.

Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
First example

A supplier can earn €25 elsewhere and a buyer can earn €35. Their partnership generates €100 total, leaving €40 above their €60 combined alternatives. Equal division of that extra €40 yields €45 for the supplier and €55 for the buyer.

Money on and off the bargaining table

A baker and a delivery firm earn more by working together than apart. Their outside options are attainable payoffs if negotiations break down. A deal must generally offer each party at least its outside option to secure voluntary agreement. The remaining surplus can be divided in many ways.

A negotiated price is only a transfer between the parties when it does not change total value, tax or incentives. Write total surplus first, then the distribution. If either side has a stronger alternative, the available bargaining surplus shrinks and the feasible set of agreements changes. A claim about fairness is separate from a calculation of willingness to accept.

Solve sequential choices backward

Some bargains span several decisions. A supplier may have to build a specialized tool today before bargaining over its use tomorrow. Backward induction means finding what the parties will choose at the final stage, then using those payoffs to evaluate the earlier decision. A promised threat matters only if carrying it out would be sensible when the time arrives.

A relationship-specific investment can be worthwhile jointly yet unappealing to the party that must pay for it. After the investment is sunk, a partner can negotiate for a share of the created value. Anticipating this hold-up, the investor may refuse to invest. A credible minimum purchase or compensation for cancellation can sometimes improve both parties’ outcomes.

Information and enforceability

Even mutually profitable trades can fail if each side privately knows different information, cannot commit, or suspects the other will exploit a sunk investment. Observing a failed deal therefore does not alone prove the underlying project lacked value. Separate a negative total surplus from a difficulty dividing or verifying a positive one.

Before treating a long-term contract as a solution, ask whether the promised quality, quantity and dates can be verified by someone independent. A detailed contract can increase negotiation costs and reduce flexibility. An analytical answer connects the proposed term to the exact point at which incentives otherwise diverge.

Method

Solve an investment-and-bargain game

  1. Draw the decision order

    Record when investment becomes irreversible, when parties negotiate, and which payoffs are available after rejection.

  2. Allocate final surplus

    At the bargaining stage compute outside options and the agreed sharing rule, excluding already sunk costs from that stage’s decision.

  3. Move backward

    Deduct the investment cost from the investing party’s future payoff and from total value. Ask if that party will start the process.

Worked case

A specialist packaging mold

A supplier can spend €50 on a buyer-specific mold. If made, one transaction produces €80 in gross joint benefit and no other variable cost. The mold has no salvage use and both parties otherwise earn zero. Once it exists, assume equal bargaining shares.

Working

  1. After spending €50 the cost is sunk. The available gross €80 is shared €40 to each party.
  2. Before investing, the supplier expects €40 − €50 = −€10 and declines. Joint net benefit would have been €30 if the mold were built.
  3. An enforceable prior contract allocating €55 of the gross benefit to the supplier leaves €5 after its investment and €25 for the buyer. Both can gain relative to no deal.

Interpretation

The €55 is a division of the same €80. It changes willingness to invest without creating an additional €55 of resources.

A common mistake

Past investment is deducted again from the offer a party should accept after the investment is irrevocably made.

How to correct it

Write payoffs at each decision date. Include investment cost in the initial decision, and include only alternatives still available at the later bargaining stage.

Quick check
If the buyer’s alternative improves to €55 with all other values fixed, how much joint surplus remains?

The alternatives now sum to €80. The partnership still creates €100, so only €20 remains to negotiate above them.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

An installer must spend €36 before a customer can receive gross benefits of €60. Both otherwise earn zero. After installation they would divide gross benefit equally unless an enforceable contract specifies another split.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find the joint and private incentives under the equal division rule.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Find the range of gross payments to the installer allowing each party to break even or better, and one institutional obstacle.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Joint net benefit is €60 − €36 = €24. Installer anticipates €30 − €36 = −€6 and declines. Customer gains €30 only if installation occurs.

Part B 4 marks

A payment between €36 and €60 inclusive covers the installer and leaves the customer non-negative benefit. Strict gains require an interior payment. If quality cannot be verified or cancellation terms cannot be enforced, the promised division may fail.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two partners can create €180 of value together. How would you distinguish splitting that total from splitting the surplus over their alternatives?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would subtract the real alternatives from the €180 before splitting anything. The initial surplus is €100. With contractor weight 60%, payoffs are €110 and €70 after restoring the respective outside options. This gives a transparent forecast under the bargaining model, while the bargaining weight itself needs justification from the negotiation setting or contract.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If joint value drops to €75, can the original sharing formula produce a voluntary deal?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The alternatives total €80, so cooperation destroys €5 compared with disagreement. Any feasible split of €75 leaves at least one party below its fallback. The positive-surplus bargaining solution no longer describes a mutually beneficial agreement unless some additional benefit or transfer from outside the pair exists.
