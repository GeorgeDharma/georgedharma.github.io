Professional workshop · F02

A ferry operator’s reserved-seat contract

A coastal ferry operator must choose how to use one 45-seat sailing. The commercial director can sell all seats to walk-up passengers at a common posted fare or reserve 20 seats for an excursion operator and price the remainder to walk-up passengers. A community agreement also asks management to show the effects on passengers and residents. Prepare the base pricing, the contract floor, a late-cancellation sensitivity, and a negotiator’s mandate. All prices are euros for this sailing. Treat the demand curve as continuous when optimizing and use whole seats at the resulting solutions.

From inputs to decision

01

Establish the real choice

The walking-customer price and amount displaced must be optimized separately under each capacity choice, and the alternative charter is an opportunity to compare once.

  1. With only walk-up sales, quantity can be at most 45. Accepting the contract removes 20 seats before any walk-up price is chosen and leaves capacity 25.
  2. Operating costs include the €500 only on sailing, plus €150 if the contract is signed. The charter earns €900 net when chosen. It is a separate alternative, so avoid subtracting €900 from one operating alternative while leaving it inside another.

Three comparable operator payoffs will be reported for charter, walk-up-only and contracted service.

02

Optimize unconstrained walk-up sales

Single-price ticket sales create a revenue loss on existing customers when a further ticket requires a lower price.

  1. Walk-up revenue is Q(100−Q), so marginal revenue is 100−2Q. Equate this to €20 marginal cost to obtain Q=40 and fare P=€60. This lies within the available 45 seats.
  2. Walk-up contribution before sailing expense is 40 × (€60−€20) = €1,600. Deduct €500 for €1,100 net, or €200 above the charter.

Walk-up monopoly output leaves five seats empty because cutting the common price further lowers total profit.

03

Reprice retail after the reservation

Displacement cannot be measured by multiplying twenty seats by the original walk-up contribution. Some seats were originally empty and remaining customers now face a different fare.

  1. The unconstrained retail optimum remains 40, but only 25 retail seats are available. The marginal revenue at 25 is €50, above the €20 marginal cost, so selling all 25 is optimal.
  2. The fare clearing 25 is €75. Retail contribution becomes 25 × (€75−€20) = €1,375. The fall from €1,600 is €225. The group displaces 15 actual retail sales while raising the price on the remaining 25.

The commercial sacrifice from the block is €225 in retail contribution, plus the €150 dedicated contract cost.

04

Price the group and set a negotiating floor

The decision depends on what the operator retains after carrying the group and compensating the retail opportunity cost.

  1. At €55 each, a full group adds 20 × (€55−€20) = €700 contribution. Full operating income is €1,375 + €700 − €500 − €150 = €1,425, a €325 premium to walk-up-only.
  2. For a price G per group seat, require €1,375 + 20(G−€20) − €650 ≥ €1,100. The floor is €38.75 a seat when attendance and payment are certain. The guest group values a seat at €68, implying room to bargain above the floor if its total willingness to pay can be captured.

Authorize the quoted €55 on guaranteed attendance and use €38.75 as the break-even floor before any risk buffer.

05

Separate the community welfare question

A fare is a transfer between customer and operator. Social value includes passenger gains above fares, operator net income and real crowding harm under the stated boundary.

  1. For linear demand, walk-up consumer surplus is one-half × quantity × (maximum willingness to pay minus fare). The open sailing yields 0.5 × 40 × (€100−€60) = €800. The contract yields 0.5 × 25 × (€100−€75) = €312.50 for walk-ups and 20 × (€68−€55) = €260 for the group.
  2. Net local harm is €320 for 40 open passengers or €360 for 45 contracted passengers. Total measured welfare is €1,100 + €800 − €320 = €1,580 open, and €1,425 + €312.50 + €260 − €360 = €1,637.50 contracted.

The measured social advantage is €57.50, much smaller than the operator’s €325 gain. Walk-up patrons lose €487.50 of consumer surplus.

06

Stress late cancellation without reselling seats

A late no-show affects capacity use, ticket cash and operating costs differently. Assuming all variable costs persist would overstate the loss, while reselling locked seats would understate it.

  1. If the group cancels, the 25 walk-up customers still generate €1,375 and the €650 fixed and committed support costs remain. Operating income falls to €725. The difference from attendance is the €700 group contribution.
  2. At 75% attendance, expected operator income is 0.75 × €1,425 + 0.25 × €725 = €1,250. Against a €1,100 walk-up fallback the expected premium is €150. Solve €725 + €700p ≥ €1,100 for break-even attendance p ≥ 53.57%.

The commercial decision survives the stated cancellation likelihood. The €725 possible outcome still falls €175 below the charter payoff.

07

Distinguish retained risk from an enforceable payment

A signed reservation can protect against no-shows if its cancellation payment is collectible. The operator must know when a no-show becomes irreversible and who carries the payment risk.

  1. If management wants the cancellation outcome itself to equal the €1,100 walk-up alternative, a collectible €375 cancellation fee raises €725 to €1,100. With attendance the regular commercial terms still apply.
  2. Verify whether deposits are refundable, whether credit checks support collection and whether legal terms or passenger demand would change under a new fee. A penalty that no partner would accept has no negotiating value.

Recommend the quoted contract with a deposit or explicit cancellation terms where feasible, preserving the €38.75 no-risk floor as a reference.

Completed document

Commercial director’s contract decision

Approval

Approve the 20-seat block at €55 per seat on the stated demand. Price the 25 remaining walk-up seats at €75. Full attendance generates €1,425 versus €1,100 from the best walk-up fare and €900 net from the outside charter.

Negotiating mandate

With guaranteed attendance and payment, €38.75 is the commercial break-even group fare. At the offered €55 we earn €325 above walk-up-only when the group attends. Keep any proposed discount separate from a change in cancellation protection because each changes the amount of risk retained.

Cancellation exposure

At 75% late attendance, expected income is €1,250. A no-show yields only €725. Request collection evidence and a deposit or fee of €375 if management wants the cancellation state itself to match the retail fallback. Re-estimate attendance if a fee changes partner behaviour.

Community reporting

The base contract increases measured welfare by €57.50 but cuts walk-up consumer surplus by €487.50. Group passengers gain €260 and the operator gains €325, while external harm rises by €40. Report this distribution to the community liaison so the lower total of walk-up seats is visible.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

At €55 per group passenger, what minimum attendance probability makes expected operator income match the €1,100 retail fallback under the no-fee cancellation terms?

See the full solution

Cancellation income is €725 and attendance adds €700. Solve 725 + 700p = 1,100. The result is 375/700 = 0.535714, or 53.57% attendance. A probability below this level makes the retail option better on expected operator income.

Task 2

If local harm increases from €8 to €12 for every passenger, by how much does the contracted base sailing outperform the retail-only sailing in measured welfare?

See the full solution

The contract carries five more passengers. Increasing the harm rate by €4 reduces its earlier €57.50 advantage by €20. The new advantage is €37.50. Private fares and actual revenues are unchanged under this counterfactual.

Task 3

If cancellation probability is 40% and contractual fares stay €55, what is the minimum collectible cancellation fee needed to make expected income at least €1,200?

See the full solution

With no fee the expectation is 0.60 × €1,425 + 0.40 × €725 = €1,145. The needed €55 of expected incremental income comes from the 40% cancellation state. The fee must therefore reach €55/0.40 = €137.50.

Task 4

Why should a community official avoid treating the entire €325 rise in operator income as a net gain to residents?

See the full solution

The operator benefits partly from charging the remaining walk-up customers more. The €487.50 loss of their surplus, €260 group surplus and €40 extra external harm also enter the common boundary. Summing the changes yields €325 − €487.50 + €260 − €40 = €57.50.
