Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

An export bond and a currency shock

A euro fund owns one two-year zero-coupon dollar bond redeeming $121. Its annual yield starts at 10% and the currency quote at €0.80 per dollar. Immediately after, yield becomes 11% and the currency quote €0.82. Ignore the passage of time and all costs.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A hedge and an erroneous risk estimate

Two contracts have per-lot exposure columns (2, 1) and (1, −1). The desk needs total exposure (7, 2). A proposed covariance matrix for its two factor shocks has diagonal entries 0.09 and both off-diagonal entries 0.10.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Forecasting from a fitted market exposure

A portfolio’s daily return y and two-day market signal x are measured in percentage points, with three observed (x,y) pairs (−2, −2), (0, 1), (2, 4). A new sample has identical x but the final y observation is revised from 4 to 1.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

An order-routing limit with a shadow price

Route ten continuous trade blocks across two venues with modeled euro cost C = x² + 4y² and x + y = 10. Both quantities are nonnegative. A second scenario caps x at 6. Ignore fixed fees.

Case 1 · 10 marks

An export bond and a currency shock

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the initial dollar value and the local slope with respect to decimal yield. State the units.

$121/1.10² = $100 (1). The slope formula is −2 × 121/(1 + y)³ (1), giving −$181.82 per unit of decimal yield at 10% (1).

Part B 4 marks

Use a joint first-order approximation to estimate the change in euro value.

The yield contribution is €0.80 × (−181.82) × 0.01 = −€1.4546 (2). The currency contribution is $100 × €0.02 per dollar = +€2 (1). Total estimate is +€0.5454 (1).

Part C 3 marks

Calculate the exact euro simple return and explain the difference from the approximation.

Final value is 0.82 × 121/1.11² ≈ €80.5292 (1). Against €80 the simple return is about +0.66% (1). Bond curvature and currency-price interaction are omitted by the first-order calculation (1).

Case 2 · 10 marks

A hedge and an erroneous risk estimate

Part A 3 marks

Solve the holdings and check the target in both rows.

2a + b = 7 and a − b = 2 (1). Substitution gives a = 3 and b = 1 (1). The two exposures are 6 + 1 = 7 and 3 − 1 = 2 (1).

Part B 3 marks

Estimate the gain in a scenario of +1 first shock and −2 second shocks, using both the holdings and the row totals.

By rows the gain is 7 − 2 × 2 = 3 (1). The first contract gains 2 − 2 = 0 and the second gains 1 + 2 = 3 (1), so holdings give 3 × 0 + 1 × 3 = 3 (1).

Part C 4 marks

Test the covariance matrix and explain the risk of feeding it to an optimizer.

Eigenvalues are 0.19 and −0.01 (2). For exposures (0.5, −0.5), variance is −0.005 (1). An optimizer could exploit a spurious negative-risk direction. Check units and data alignment and rebuild a positive-semidefinite estimate (1).

Case 3 · 10 marks

Forecasting from a fitted market exposure

Part A 3 marks

Fit a line with intercept using the original three points.

Means are 0 and 1 (1). The covariance numerator is 12 and x deviation sum of squares is 8 (1), so slope is 1.5 and intercept 1 (1).

Part B 4 marks

Re-estimate after the revision. Explain why the original residuals provide little assurance about forecast stability.

New y mean is zero (1), cross-product sum is 6, giving slope 0.75 and intercept zero (1). Fitted return at x = 1 consequently falls from 2.5 to 0.75 (1). A zero original residual sum of squares on three points does not establish stable coefficients (1).

Part C 3 marks

The desk also includes z = −2x in a multivariable model. Diagnose identification and show a coefficient pair reproducing the revised slope.

The two columns are dependent (1). Only b_x − 2b_z = 0.75 is identified (1). For example b_x = 1.75 and b_z = 0.5 satisfy it, as do 0.75 and zero (1).

Case 4 · 10 marks

An order-routing limit with a shadow price

Part A 3 marks

Find the unconstrained-with-respect-to-cap allocation and total cost.

Marginal costs are 2x and 8y (1). Setting them equal implies x = 4y, hence x = 8 and y = 2 (1). Cost is 64 + 16 = €80 (1).

Part B 4 marks

Find the capped allocation and the local euro value of an extra block of capacity at venue x.

The optimizer is x = 6 and y = 4, yielding €100 (2). A small extra x displaces y. The saved marginal cost is 8 × 4 − 2 × 6 = €20 per extra block of cap (2).

Part C 3 marks

Predict the saving from 0.1 block of extra cap and compare with exact repricing. Why is the comparison close?

First-order saving is €2 (1). Exact cost at (6.1, 3.9) is 37.21 + 60.84 = €98.05, a €1.95 saving (1). Positive curvature of cost along the feasible path explains the €0.05 difference (1).
