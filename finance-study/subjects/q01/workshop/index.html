Professional workshop · Q01

Authorize a whole-lot rates hedge under a liquidity cap

At market close the treasury book would lose €27,000 from a standard short-end yield shock and €15,000 from a standard long-end yield shock. Two instruments can offset those local sensitivities, but trading can currently obtain only two lots of the second instrument. Find the best executable hedge under the declared risk objective, compare it with the unconstrained exact solution and write an authorization ticket with residual-risk triggers. Derive all trades from the supplied sensitivity matrix.

From inputs to decision

01

Build the correct target and unit map

A short-end loss is a negative sensitivity. A hedge that cancels it must add a positive gain under the identical shock. Sign and per-lot conventions determine which side of the market to trade.

  1. Write residual e = (−27, −15) + (4x + y, x + 3y), with x lots of A and y lots of B. Both entries are thousands of euros per named ten-basis-point shock.
  2. A simultaneous parallel +10 bp move applies each shock once and gives the local marked change e₁ + e₂. A steepener with short yields down 10 bp and long yields up 10 bp gives −e₁ + e₂.

Carry forward a target hedge exposure of (+27, +15) in the defined units. A fee is a separate, upfront cash expense.

02

Solve the unconstrained exact hedge

The exact solution identifies the missing risk direction and establishes what the market-access limit prevents.

  1. Set 4x + y = 27 and x + 3y = 15. Substituting y = 27 − 4x gives x + 81 − 12x = 15, so x = 6 and y = 3.
  2. Verify (24 + 3, 6 + 9) = (27, 15). The two columns are independent because their determinant is 4 × 3 − 1 × 1 = 11. The trade would cost 6 × 40 + 3 × 95 = €525.

The zero-residual hedge cannot currently trade because it requires three B lots. Report this gap instead of silently rounding B down.

03

Find a continuous lower bound for each permitted B size

Enumerating every integer combination is possible here, but solving the conditional continuous problem exposes why a nearby integer is optimal and supplies a reproducible method for larger books.

  1. At fixed y minimize J(x)=(4x + y − 27)² + (x + 3y − 15)². Setting its derivative to zero gives 4(4x + y − 27) + x + 3y − 15 = 0, hence x*=(123 − 7y)/17.
  2. For y=0,1,2 the real-valued minimizers are 7.2353, 6.8235 and 6.4118 respectively. They lie inside the available interval for A. Since each conditional loss is strictly convex in x, only the adjacent feasible integers need examination.

The B=2 relaxed solution leaves residuals +0.6471 and −2.5882. Fractional size is a diagnostic comparison and cannot be sent to the dealer.

04

Certify the integer optimum

The risk objective can be nonseparable after rounding, so rounding each component of the infeasible exact hedge would not itself prove optimality. Check the exact permissible candidates by B bucket.

  1. For y=0, x=7 leaves (1,−8) and risk score 65, while x=8 gives (5,−7) and score 74. For y=1, x=6 leaves (−2,−6), score 40, while x=7 gives (2,−5), score 29.
  2. For y=2, x=6 leaves (−1,−3), score 10, while x=7 gives (3,−2), score 13. The convex conditional certificate rules out all farther A holdings. The minimum across the three buckets is 10.

Submit buys of 6 A and 2 B. The mark-to-standard-shock sensitivity score of 10 is measured in squared thousands of euros and is not a probability-weighted value-at-risk.

05

Calculate scenario exposures and immediate costs

The risk manager signs against cash-valued limits. Convert each approved position back into bucket and scenario P&L before comparing with tolerances.

  1. The bucket checks are |−1| ≤ 3 and |−3| ≤ 3. Parallel up gives −1 − 3 = −€4k, exactly at its limit. A short-down and long-up steepener gives +1 − 3 = −€2k.
  2. The one-off fees are 6 × €40 + 2 × €95 = €430. A parallel +20 bp move, treated only as a scaled local scenario, gives approximately −€8,000 before costs. Exact large moves need full repricing.

The long-end and parallel limits both have zero headroom. Fees reduce economic P&L but the policy explicitly assesses market shocks before those fees.

06

Stress execution and model sensitivity

Treasury needs an escalation rule that responds to a missing fill or a change in contract sensitivity. The nominal solution has no room for a small long-end miss.

  1. If only one B lot fills alongside six A lots, residuals are (−2,−6) and the long-end limit fails. Even the best attainable whole-lot risk score at B=1 is 29 with residuals (2,−5), which still breaches the long-end limit.
  2. If B’s long-end sensitivity falls from +3 to +2.5 per lot with all trades filled, long residual becomes −15 + 6 + 5 = −4. Parallel shock becomes −5. Both limits fail. Pause certification and rerun using current sensitivities.

Ask execution to notify the risk manager on any partial B fill or sensitivity refresh. No alternative valid hedge should be presumed feasible under changed inputs.

07

Price an additional liquidity opportunity

Comparing the exact hedge with the authorized one turns a vague request for extra trading capacity into a measurable choice.

  1. An additional B lot at the original sensitivities adds (+1,+3), removing both residuals from the (6,2) book. Upfront cost rises €95 while the squared score falls from 10 to zero.
  2. Before obtaining that lot verify executable price, fees and continued validity of the exposure table. The reduction of €4,000 in a single parallel stress is a scenario improvement, not guaranteed revenue to be compared as if certain with the €95 fee.

Recommend pursuing the additional lot subject to live prices and limits, while authorizing only the available two B lots now.

Completed document

Treasury hedge authorization

Order

Buy 6 lots of A and 2 lots of B, subject to current quoted sensitivities and both B fills. Estimated execution fees are €430. The full parallel sensitivity improves from −€42,000 to −€4,000 for a +10 bp move.

Hedge quality

The minimum executable objective is 10 squared thousands of euros. An exact independent two-instrument solution exists at (6,3). Current liquidity caps prevent that trade, and the conditional convex calculations plus the whole-lot comparison certify today’s optimum.

Risk and limits

Long-end sensitivity and the parallel +10 bp limit sit exactly on their floors. A modeled short-down/long-up steepener costs €2,000 before fees. This worksheet captures local bucket exposures and does not replace full repricing of nonlinear or large curve shocks.

Monitoring

Immediately rebuild the exposure certificate after partial fills, a change in lot specification or any meaningful shift in the long sensitivity of B. Seek approval for a third B lot when tradable and verify its fee and modeled contribution before executing.
