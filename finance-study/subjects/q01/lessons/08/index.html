Least squares

Express regression as a projection that minimizes squared error. Collinear explanatory variables make separate coefficient estimates unreliable.

The starting point

A risk analyst wants a straight line relating an asset return to a market move. For each candidate line, calculate predicted values and the residuals, the differences between observations and predictions. Least squares selects coefficients that minimize the sum of the squared residuals. Squaring gives large misses extra weight.

With ordinary unweighted least squares, the fitted values form a projection onto the span of the explanatory columns. At a solution, residuals have zero inner product with each column. If one column can be obtained exactly from the others, several coefficient sets may imply identical predictions.

Theory and method

QR decomposition and the least-squares projection

Normal equations · Orthogonal projection

Why would a quantitative analyst use a QR factorization rather than explicitly invert XᵀX to fit hedge coefficients?

A desk wants the smallest squared difference between a target payoff and payouts from several traded hedges. Only payoff patterns spanned by the hedge columns can be reproduced. QR constructs orthogonal axes for that span, projects the target onto those axes, then translates the coordinates back to the original instruments. This avoids forming a matrix that squares the design’s condition number and makes the unhedgeable residual visible.

How the result is built

X = QR, Rb = Qᵀy, e = y − QQᵀy

X is the full-column-rank scenario-by-instrument payoff matrix, Q has orthonormal columns, R is square upper triangular, y target euro payoffs, b hedge holdings, e euro residual vector and ᵀ transpose.

  1. For any candidate b split y into its projection QQᵀy and an orthogonal residual. The two squared-error components add.
  2. The residual outside the column space is fixed. Minimize the component inside by solving the triangular system Rb = Qᵀy.
  3. If the sole column is (1,1,0) and y=(1,3,4), projection gives (2,2,0) and residual (−1,1,4). Their inner product is zero.

When the reasoning holds

  • This simple triangular solve requires independent columns. Rank-revealing QR or an SVD is appropriate when inputs appear redundant.
  • Ordinary Euclidean squared error weights scenarios equally and penalizes euro errors quadratically. Probability or tail-based weights alter the chosen projection.
Terms in this lesson
Regression
A way of estimating how an outcome varies with one or more observed inputs.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Percentage point
The difference between two percentages. A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises one percentage point.
How to read the notation
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.
Summation · ∑
The symbol asks you to add the terms for the listed items. For weights of 0.6 and 0.4 and returns of 5% and 2%, ∑ wᵢrᵢ means 0.6 × 5% + 0.4 × 2% = 3.8%. A standalone capital sigma (Σ) can also name a covariance matrix in portfolio equations.
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
Transpose and multiplication · wᵀr
A transpose turns a column into a row. Multiplying that row by a column pairs their entries and adds the products. Weights (0.75, 0.25) multiplied this way by returns (4%, 8%) produce a portfolio return of 5%.

All notation examples

First example

Fit y = bx with no intercept to the pairs (1, 3) and (2, 5). The slope is (1 × 3 + 2 × 5)/(1² + 2²) = 2.6. The residuals are 0.4 and −0.2, giving 1 × 0.4 + 2 × (−0.2) = 0.

Fit the best line under a stated loss

A desk has pairs of market moves x and asset moves y and wants fitted values a + bx. Ordinary least squares chooses intercept a and slope b to minimize the sum of the squared vertical errors. Units, sample and observation weights define which errors count most in a particular fit.

Large deviations receive disproportionate influence because doubling a residual quadruples its squared loss. If a data error makes one daily return huge, the fitted line may tilt toward that observation. Inspecting the underlying trades serves an economic purpose beyond checking fit statistics.

Obtain the normal equations

Differentiating the squared-error sum with respect to a gives the condition Σ(yᵢ − a − bxᵢ) = 0. Rearranging yields a = ȳ − b x̄. Differentiation with respect to b gives Σxᵢ(yᵢ − a − bxᵢ) = 0. Substituting for a solves for b as Σ(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)/Σ(xᵢ − x̄)².

The denominator measures variation in x. When it is zero an intercept and slope cannot be separately identified. In matrix form the residual vector is perpendicular to each included explanatory column, Xᵀ(y − Xb) = 0. These equations define projection without requiring independent residuals for the purely algebraic fit.

What the fit can establish

The coefficient describes a fitted association for the chosen sample. A market factor omitted from the regression can change the economic reading of its slope. Estimating sampling uncertainty additionally requires assumptions about the shocks, including dependence across dates. A tiny sum of squared residuals gives neither causal identification nor future stability.

If two regressors move nearly together, fitted values can be stable while their separate coefficients are highly sensitive. A numerical solver using QR or singular values can expose ill-conditioning more reliably than explicitly forming and inverting XᵀX.

Method

Fit and verify

  1. Center the data

    Calculate x and y means from the same observations. Tabulate deviations and products so the covariance numerator and variation denominator can be inspected.

  2. Solve both coefficients

    Compute the slope then set the intercept so fitted values average to the observed y mean. Compare coefficients in the original return units.

  3. Inspect orthogonality

    Check that residuals sum to zero with an intercept and that their x-weighted sum is zero. Investigate influential observations before interpreting the model.

Worked case

A three-month factor fit

Market excess returns in percentage points are −1, 0 and 1. A portfolio’s corresponding excess returns are −1, 2 and 3. Fit a line with an intercept and give the next-month prediction if the market returns 0.5 point.

Working

  1. The mean market move is zero and mean portfolio move is 4/3. The numerator is (−1)(−1) + 0 × 2 + 1 × 3 = 4 because the regressor is centered. The denominator equals 2.
  2. The slope is 2 and the intercept 4/3. Fitted values are −2/3, 4/3 and 10/3. Residuals are −1/3, +2/3 and −1/3, summing to zero.
  3. The x-weighted residual sum is +1/3 − 1/3 = 0. At a 0.5-point market move the fitted portfolio return is 4/3 + 1 = 7/3 points.

Interpretation

Three observations leave very little information about uncertainty. A mechanically correct fit can still be unsuitable for position sizing.

A common mistake

A fitted intercept is the return the strategy will earn for sure whenever the market stands still.

How to correct it

The regression estimates a conditional fitted value. Residual variability, parameter uncertainty, missing factors and the limited range of market moves remain relevant to a prediction.

Quick check
A regression includes x and 2x as its only explanatory columns. What can observations distinguish?

Writing b₁x + b₂(2x) = (b₁ + 2b₂)x shows what can be estimated. Changing the two coefficients while holding their combination fixed leaves all predictions unchanged.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Observations are (x,y) = (1,1), (2,3), (3,2). Fit an ordinary line with an intercept.

  1. Part A · 5 marks

    Derive the estimates and residuals.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    A researcher adds a predictor z = 3x. Explain which aspect remains estimable.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 5 marks

Means are 2 and 2. The cross-product sum is 1 and the squared x-deviation sum is 2, giving b = 0.5 and a = 1. Fitted values are 1.5, 2, 2.5 and residuals −0.5, 1, −0.5.

Part B 3 marks

The combined slope on x remains estimable as b_x + 3b_z. Separate coefficients are unidentified because a change in one can be offset in the other without changing any fitted value.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why would a quantitative analyst use a QR factorization rather than explicitly invert XᵀX to fit hedge coefficients?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would fit via QR or a suitable numerical least-squares routine so that the projection is explicit and I avoid forming XᵀX. In this example the €4 third-state error remains for any holdings. Looking both at orthogonality and the economic scenario behind large residuals tells the desk where a second, genuinely different hedge could help.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If all confidence is placed on the third scenario, will reweighting it create a better third-scenario fit?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The available hedge pays zero there, so no choice of its quantity can fit that payout. Weighting changes the objective and can affect other coefficients when scenarios are spanned. It cannot manufacture a missing payoff direction.
