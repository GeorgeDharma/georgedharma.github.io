Eigenvalues and covariance

Eigenvectors can identify common directions of movement. Small or negative estimated eigenvalues can make optimization noisy or economically impossible.

The starting point

A covariance matrix records how returns tend to move together. Its diagonal entries are variances, and each off-diagonal entry measures the joint movement of two returns. For portfolio weights w, the quadratic expression wᵀΣw is the variance of the weighted return, so it must be at least zero.

An eigenvector identifies a direction whose orientation the matrix preserves. The associated eigenvalue measures variance along that direction when the vector has unit length. Small eigenvalues can make an optimizer treat an estimated combination as almost riskless. Negative eigenvalues reveal an internally inconsistent covariance estimate, apart from tiny rounding errors.

Theory and method

Spectral theorem and principal component risk

Principal component analysis · Positive-semidefinite covariance

A risk report says its first principal component explains 80% of market variance. Does it therefore contribute 80% of this portfolio’s variance?

Principal component analysis rotates the market’s covariance matrix into mutually uncorrelated directions. The market-level variance fraction divides one component’s eigenvalue by the sum of all eigenvalues. A portfolio has its own exposure to each direction. It can be almost orthogonal to the largest component or deliberately load on it. Its risk shares therefore depend jointly on component variances and the squared sizes of its specific loadings.

How the result is built

Var(wᵀr) = Σᵢ λᵢ(vᵢᵀw)²

r is a vector of decimal asset returns, w portfolio exposures, vᵢ orthonormal covariance eigenvectors, λᵢ their variances, ᵀ transpose, and Var the portfolio variance in squared-return units.

  1. A symmetric covariance matrix decomposes as Σ = VΛVᵀ. Substitute this into wᵀΣw.
  2. The coordinates Vᵀw are the portfolio’s component loadings. Because the rotated factors are uncorrelated, variance is the weighted sum of squared loadings.
  3. With eigenvalues 0.08 and 0.02 and portfolio loadings 0.25 and 1, contributions are 0.005 and 0.020. The market’s 80% component supplies only 20% of this portfolio’s 0.025 variance.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use orthonormal loadings consistent with the covariance matrix rather than a separately normalized portfolio. Rescaling a factor changes the loading units.
  • Estimated principal directions may be unstable, especially when eigenvalues are close or the market regime changes. Review later exposures and out-of-sample risk.
Terms in this lesson
Eigenvector
A direction that a linear transformation stretches or shrinks without changing its direction. In finance it can describe common movements in several assets.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
Transpose and multiplication · wᵀr
A transpose turns a column into a row. Multiplying that row by a column pairs their entries and adds the products. Weights (0.75, 0.25) multiplied this way by returns (4%, 8%) produce a portfolio return of 5%.
Summation · ∑
The symbol asks you to add the terms for the listed items. For weights of 0.6 and 0.4 and returns of 5% and 2%, ∑ wᵢrᵢ means 0.6 × 5% + 0.4 × 2% = 3.8%. A standalone capital sigma (Σ) can also name a covariance matrix in portfolio equations.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

Two assets have variances 0.04 and covariance 0.03. Their equal-weight portfolio variance is ¼ × (0.04 + 0.03 + 0.03 + 0.04) = 0.035. Reversing one weight to −½ gives 0.005. Common movement largely cancels in the second position.

Rotate the axes of risk

Suppose two share returns usually rise and fall together. Holding equal long positions emphasizes this common movement. Holding one long and the other short can emphasize differences between them. An eigenvector gives a systematically chosen direction along which a covariance matrix acts by multiplication by one number.

For a symmetric covariance matrix Σ, solve Σv = λv with v nonzero. If v has unit length, vᵀΣv = λ measures the return variance of that direction, using v as exposures. A unit-length vector can have weights that do not sum to one, so its variance scale depends on the normalization.

Derive the two common directions

Take a matrix with common variance a on the diagonal and covariance b off the diagonal. Multiplication by (1, 1) produces (a + b, a + b). Multiplication by (1, −1) produces (a − b, −a + b). Thus the eigenvalues are a + b and a − b.

The sum of these eigenvalues is 2a, equal to the sum of the original variances. If b is positive, the common direction explains more total variation. An ordinary equal-weight investment uses (½, ½). Its variance is (a + b)/2 because the squared length of this vector is one half.

When an estimate gives impossible risk

For any holdings w, wᵀΣw is the variance of a random return and cannot be negative. A symmetric matrix with this property is positive semidefinite. Pairwise covariances computed on incompatible samples can assemble a matrix that violates it even when each input looks individually sensible.

One response is to estimate every covariance from a consistent panel of observations. Another is to use shrinkage toward a defensible valid matrix. Replacing tiny negative eigenvalues caused by numerical errors may be suitable after diagnosing their size. A substantial negative variance deserves a data and method investigation.

Method

Test an estimated matrix

  1. Check basic structure

    Use the same data frequency and currency across entries and verify symmetry. The diagonal contains individual variances and must be nonnegative.

  2. Find risk directions

    In the equal-variance two-asset case compute a + b and a − b. For a larger matrix use a symmetric eigensolver and inspect small values.

  3. Interpret the loading scale

    Report how eigenvectors are normalized, which exposures they combine and the variance fraction λ divided by the sum of eigenvalues.

Worked case

A common bank factor

Two bank shares each have annual variance 0.09, and their covariance is 0.06. A risk officer compares the volatility of an equal long portfolio with a long-short holding of equal gross size.

Working

  1. The eigenvalues are 0.15 and 0.03. The directional variance share of the common move is 0.15/(0.15 + 0.03) = 83.33%.
  2. The equal long holding (0.5, 0.5) has variance 0.15/2 = 0.075 and volatility √0.075 ≈ 27.39%.
  3. The holding (0.5, −0.5) has variance 0.03/2 = 0.015 and volatility about 12.25%. It cancels part of the common movement while retaining relative-share risk.

Interpretation

The long-short holding has zero net initial risky weight and gross risky weight one. Comparing volatility required keeping the gross scale constant.

A common mistake

The largest principal component must have a causal interpretation, such as an interest-rate shock.

How to correct it

Eigenvectors describe measured joint variation. Connecting a statistical component to an economic shock requires separate evidence on its loadings, timing, stability and identification.

Quick check
A fitted covariance matrix gives variance −0.002 for a proposed hedge. What should the analyst do?

Variance measures the expected square of a deviation and cannot be negative. Pairwise estimates assembled from different samples can be inconsistent even if their separate inputs look plausible.

Independent exam problem

A proposed covariance matrix has variances 0.04 and covariance 0.05 for both off-diagonal entries.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find its two eigenvalues and exhibit an impossible portfolio variance.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Describe two checks before using these estimates in an optimizer.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

The eigenvalues are 0.09 and −0.01. The position (0.5, −0.5) has variance −0.005. A negative variance fails the positive-semidefinite condition.

Part B 4 marks

Verify common dates, return units and treatment of missing prices, then estimate a consistent covariance matrix. Inspect eigenvalues and recalculate example portfolio variances after correction. An optimizer can otherwise chase an illusory low-risk direction.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A risk report says its first principal component explains 80% of market variance. Does it therefore contribute 80% of this portfolio’s variance?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

No. The 80% figure refers to aggregate covariance variation across the input market returns. Our portfolio carries just 0.25 units of that direction and one unit of the second. Its first-component risk share is therefore 0.005/0.025, or 20%. I would show both loadings and eigenvalues before recommending a factor hedge.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can the sign of an eigenvector reversal change the reported variance contribution?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The loading changes sign when the eigenvector does, but its square is unchanged. Signed stress scenarios require a consistent economic orientation for interpretation. Reversing a vector and its named shock together leaves its scenario economics intact.
