Theory and terminology

Cholesky factorization for correlated simulation

covariance · correlated shocks · positive definiteness

What you should be able to explain

  • Factor a two-asset covariance matrix by matching its entries.
  • Generate a dependent pair of shocks from independent draws and validate portfolio variance.

What a matrix square root does

A covariance matrix records uncertainty about returns over one stated horizon. Its diagonal entries are variances. Its off-diagonal entries record how each pair moves around their respective means. Generating each return with its own volatility and an independent random number preserves the diagonal but would erase the off-diagonal information.

Start instead with independent, mean-zero, unit-variance draws. Let each return combine the draws with different weights. A weight attached to the same draw in two returns makes them co-move. Normal input draws produce jointly normal returns. The covariance identity itself needs only the stated means, variances and lack of correlation.

Build the factor from three identities

For two assets let the lower triangular factor have first row (a, 0) and second row (b, c). The resulting variances are a² and b²+c². The covariance is ab. Choose a positive first loading, use the target covariance to determine b, then allocate the unexplained second variance to c².

A positive definite matrix gives a unique lower triangular factor with positive diagonal in a fixed asset ordering. Around a perfect correlation, the unexplained variance can approach zero. Exact perfect dependence permits a lower-rank representation. A materially negative remainder signals an inconsistent input matrix and calls for investigating the estimates.

Check the object that matters to the desk

An individual draw need not resemble the correlation target. Correlation describes a distribution across draws. Validate the factor analytically, then compare a large simulated sample’s means, covariances and representative portfolio volatilities to their theoretical values. Document the horizon, currency basis and whether inputs represent total returns or innovations.

Changing the ordering changes the triangular loadings and the meaning of a particular shock vector. With Gaussian input and a consistent permutation, the reordered model implies the same joint distribution. Outside joint normality the covariance matrix alone leaves joint tail behaviour undetermined. Independently multiplying normals cannot reproduce arbitrary crash dependence.

Method

Build the result

r = μ + Lz; Cov(r) = LLᵀ = Σ

r and μ are horizon returns and their means in decimals. z contains standardized random shocks. L has return units, its transpose is Lᵀ, and Σ contains covariances in squared-return units.

  1. Center the return to obtain r−μ=Lz. Taking an outer product gives (r−μ)(r−μ)ᵀ=LzzᵀLᵀ.
  2. Independent standardized draws have E[zzᵀ] equal to the identity matrix. Taking expectations leaves LLᵀ.
  3. For a portfolio with exposure weights w, its variance is wᵀΣw. Equivalently sum the squared entries of wᵀL, each the portfolio loading on one independent shock.
Worked case

Two related one-year market shocks

A scenario desk models two assets with annual volatilities 20% and 15%, correlation +0.60 and zero mean shocks. Portfolio weights are 40% and 60%. Use standardized draws z₁=1 and z₂=−0.5.

Working

  1. The target variances are 0.20²=0.0400 and 0.15²=0.0225. Their covariance is 0.60×0.20×0.15=0.0180.
  2. First loading a=0.20. Shared loading b=0.018/0.20=0.09. Remaining loading c=√(0.0225−0.09²)=0.12.
  3. The two shocks are 0.20 and 0.09−0.06=0.03. The portfolio outcome on this draw is 0.4×20%+0.6×3%=9.8%.
  4. The portfolio's two independent loadings are 0.4×0.20+0.6×0.09=0.134 and 0.6×0.12=0.072. Their squared sum, 0.02314, gives annual volatility of 15.21%.

Decision and interpretation

Approve the covariance implementation if both matrix and portfolio checks reproduce these targets within the specified numerical tolerance. The single portfolio gain says nothing by itself about predicted crash losses.

Quick check
A model divides off-diagonal covariance by the first asset's volatility to get a negative shared loading. What does that sign mean?

The product of the two common loadings is their covariance. A negative loading can coexist with positive variance and a positive definite covariance matrix.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A hedge pair has volatilities of 10% and 20%, correlation −0.80 and zero means. Construct a factor with positive diagonal and generate the shocks for z=(−1, 0.5).

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Covariance is −0.80×0.10×0.20=−0.016. Set the first row to (0.10,0).
  2. The shared loading is −0.016/0.10=−0.16 and the remaining loading is √(0.04−0.0256)=0.12. The second row is (−0.16,0.12).
  3. The first shock is −10%. The second is (−0.16)(−1)+0.12×0.5=+22%. Opposite signs are plausible under negative correlation.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a risk analyst simulate both assets moving together when the random-number generator supplies independent values?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I give the assets a shared shock and an independent residual shock. The coefficients come from a factor of the covariance matrix. Multiplying the factor by its transpose must reconstruct the target, including its off-diagonal covariance.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if estimated correlation is reported as +1.04?

Compare with the follow-up answer

That number cannot be a correlation. The two-asset matrix would imply negative residual variance in the factorization. Check data alignment and the covariance estimator before using the simulated risk.
