New and expanded lessons

C01.11

Dornbusch overshooting after a monetary surprise

The existing monetary and FX lessons show how a rate cut pressures a peg and how uncovered interest parity relates expected currency movement to interest rates. They do not trace a floating currency's impact jump beyond its new long-run level and subsequent appreciation while domestic prices adjust.

  • Derive the spot quote required by a specified interest differential and future exchange-rate anchor.
  • Distinguish an impact depreciation, a temporary overshoot and subsequent convergence using one quote convention.

C01.12

Optimum currency areas and adjustment to asymmetric shocks

The peg and trilemma lessons develop national policy choices. They leave open what replaces a national exchange rate after two regions adopt the same currency and how to distinguish pooled income risk from the union's aggregate economic loss.

  • Assess a shared currency against the similarity of member shocks and the channels available for adjustment.
  • Construct a budget-neutral transfer rule and distinguish local income insurance from recovery of union output.

C01.13

Trade-weighted nominal and real effective exchange rates

Bilateral PPP and the export/import terms of trade are already taught. A national analyst still needs to combine several partner currencies and inflation rates, state the index direction and distinguish a change in relative consumer prices from an export-price index.

  • Build a geometric nominal effective exchange-rate index using explicit trade weights.
  • Adjust the index for partner inflation and explain what a real appreciation can and cannot establish.

C01.14

Foreign direct investment, entry modes and the external accounts

The accounts lessons separate stocks from transactions, and the corporate courses assess overseas projects and acquisitions. They did not connect the voting-power threshold with Dunning's entry framework and a reconciliation of an affiliate's retained earnings to its recorded investment position.

  • Classify ordinary cross-border shareholdings and explain why a firm might build or buy a local business.
  • Separate cash dividends, reinvested earnings, investment transactions and valuation changes in an outward or inward FDI position.

Public benchmarks

Northwestern University, United States

ECON 362 International Finance, Spring 2019 syllabus

The exchange-rate sequence links long-run purchasing power to sticky-price overshooting, then considers intervention, crises, cross-border risk sharing and global imbalances.

European Central Bank

“New” views on the optimum currency area theory: what is EMU telling us?

Francesco Paolo Mongelli examines how mobility, price flexibility, correlated shocks and fiscal integration affect adjustment inside a shared currency.

Bank for International Settlements

Effective exchange rates

The official methodology uses geometric trade weights for nominal indices and relative consumer prices for real indices. Its convention assigns an increase to an appreciation.

University of Washington

GEOG 349, Modes of International Business

The university lecture compares exporting, licensing and investment, and develops Dunning's ownership, location and internalization reasoning using firm assets, the host market and contracting costs.

Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development

OECD Benchmark Definition of Foreign Direct Investment, Fifth Edition (2025)

The benchmark sets the voting-power test, distinguishes new establishments from acquisitions and separates transactions, valuation changes and reclassifications in the reconciliation of direct-investment positions.

International Monetary Fund

Integrated Balance of Payments and International Investment Position Manual, Seventh Edition, paragraphs 8.15 and 12.48–12.50

The manual attributes undistributed earnings to the immediate direct investor and records equal reinvestment in the financial account, distinguishing the investor's income from its cash dividend.
