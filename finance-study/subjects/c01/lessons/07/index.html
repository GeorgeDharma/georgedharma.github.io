The policy trilemma

A hard peg, free capital movement and independent monetary policy cannot all be maintained in the standard model. Identify which choice is constrained.

The starting point

A government may want a fixed exchange rate, free international capital movement and a policy rate tailored to its economy. The policy trilemma says these three ambitions cannot be held fully together in the standard model.

With a credible peg, free capital mobility and otherwise equivalent risk, investors can move toward the higher return without expecting a currency loss. Maintaining a rate difference then puts pressure on the peg. Letting the currency move or limiting capital mobility changes that trade-off.

Theory and method

Mundell–Fleming model and monetary trilemma

Impossible trinity · Policy effectiveness under fixed and floating rates

In a small open economy with mobile capital and sticky prices, why can the same monetary expansion behave differently under a hard peg and a float?

A small economy cuts its short rate below a foreign benchmark while investors can move funds freely. Residents try to obtain higher foreign returns. Under a credible peg the central bank must meet currency demand at the promised rate. Under a float the exchange rate can move, affecting import prices and demand for domestic exports. The Mundell–Fleming short-run model tracks how these market responses interact with national spending and domestic monetary policy.

How the result is built

i ≈ i* under a credible permanent peg, comparable safe assets and perfect capital mobility

i is the short domestic interest rate and i* the foreign rate for comparable instruments of the same maturity. The approximation states a model equilibrium condition under the listed assumptions.

  1. An open-market expansion initially lowers the home rate at existing output and prices. Capital outflow produces selling pressure on domestic currency.
  2. Under a peg, the central bank supplies foreign currency and receives domestic money. In the textbook unsterilized adjustment this reverses the attempted monetary expansion.
  3. Under a float the domestic currency can depreciate. If net export demand responds as assumed, spending and output rise while monetary policy need not defend a fixed quote.

When the reasoning holds

  • The model is short run with sticky prices and a small economy taking the world rate as given. Inflation adjustment or a large country's influence on global rates changes the channels.
  • A devaluation expectation or country risk premium permits domestic interest to differ from the foreign rate. Perfectly substitutable safe deposits are a benchmark.
Terms in this lesson
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
How to read the notation
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.

First example

Suppose the anchor currency pays 4% and a country with a credible peg cuts to 2% on comparable deposits. Investors can switch abroad. To protect the peg the local central bank may have to sell foreign reserves and buy domestic currency.

Replicate the investor's currency choice

Consider two one-year deposits, one local and one in the anchor currency. If the peg is believed to hold and principal can be moved freely, a local investor can convert, earn the foreign interest and return at the same exchange rate. Two comparable deposits then face direct competition.

Suppose local interest is lowered while anchor interest remains higher. Investors have an incentive to move funds abroad. Meeting the demand for anchor currency at the peg depletes reserves. Holding the rate difference, the peg and free movement indefinitely conflicts with this simple mechanism.

Specify the assumptions behind the trilemma

The comparison assumes similar credit risk, liquidity and tax treatment, negligible transaction costs and an exchange rate expected to hold over the investment horizon. A risk that the peg will break creates an expected currency gain or loss. Default risk on deposits can also matter independently.

An observed difference in interest rates alone therefore cannot prove capital controls or disprove the trilemma. Identify the denomination of each promise and who guarantees it. A premium for local bank risk is economically different from a free choice by the central bank over its risk-free short rate.

Locate the available policy adjustment

A country retaining a peg and open capital markets usually allows domestic monetary conditions to follow the anchor. With capital controls, some interest differences can persist because access to the foreign opportunity is restricted or costly. With a float, exchange-rate movements and expectations make the foreign payoff uncertain in domestic terms.

Actual regimes often lie between these extremes. A managed float can smooth some movements, and controls can be partial. On an exam specify which constraint is relaxed and explain the economic cost, such as lost autonomy, reserve exposure or restricted access to external finance.

Method

Write a comparable return in local money

  1. Start with one deposit

    State the local amount and the exchange rate in local units per anchor unit. Use division to buy the anchor currency.

  2. Earn and convert

    Apply the foreign gross return. Convert the final proceeds at the expected future rate, giving the local-currency payoff.

  3. Compare and qualify

    Compare with the local deposit and ask what risk, cost or restriction could offset the difference. State the implied pressure on reserves.

Worked case

A rate cut under a trusted peg

The rate is 5 local units per anchor unit today and is credibly fixed next year in this model. A resident can invest 500 locally at 2%, or transfer funds at zero cost and earn 5% in a deposit of the same credit quality.

Working

  1. Locally, the year-end payoff is 500 × 1.02 = 510. Abroad, 500/5 buys 100 anchor units, which grow to 105.
  2. At the unchanged peg the foreign route pays 105 × 5 = 525 local units. The advantage of 15 invites outward transfers.
  3. If policy makers continue to defend the peg while residents make those transfers, reserves fall. Raising the local rate, allowing depreciation or limiting transfers changes a different part of the calculation.

Interpretation

A one-off withdrawal tax of 15 local units on the foreign route would eliminate this example's payoff advantage. Enforcement, distortion and long-term effects lie outside the arithmetic.

A common mistake

Attributing any gap between a risky local deposit and a safe foreign bill to monetary independence.

How to correct it

Compare instruments with similar credit risk and maturity, then test for capital costs and expected currency movement.

Quick check
Which change gives the domestic bank more monetary independence while keeping capital freely mobile?

A floating exchange rate can adjust when returns differ. A stronger peg promise alone does not remove the incentive to move capital.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

At 8 local units per dollar, a resident has 800 local units. The local deposit offers 6%, the dollar deposit 3%. Ignore risk and fees except uncertainty about the future exchange rate.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    Calculate both future payoffs if the existing peg holds.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What future spot rate would make the dollar deposit deliver the same local payoff?

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Why could the local rate exceed the foreign rate under an announced peg?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

The local deposit pays 848. The foreign deposit yields 100 × 1.03 = $103, worth 824 local units at the peg.

Part B 3 marks

Solve 103 × future rate = 848. The break-even rate is about 8.2330 local units per dollar, a depreciation relative to the initial 8.

Part C 2 marks

Investors may expect devaluation or require compensation for local deposit or political risk. Compare those factors before concluding that the peg and capital mobility impose no constraint.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

In a small open economy with mobile capital and sticky prices, why can the same monetary expansion behave differently under a hard peg and a float?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would first ask about the exchange regime and access to international funds. With a trusted peg and open capital markets, a sustained home rate below the anchor encourages outflows. Defending the peg drains reserves and, without sterilization, domestic liquidity. A float lets the exchange rate adjust. In the sticky-price model depreciation can improve net exports and raise output, though import-cost or foreign-debt exposure may weaken that benefit.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A local one-year rate is two percentage points higher than the anchor despite the announced peg. What would you examine?

Compare with the follow-up answer

I would compare instrument credit risk and liquidity, ask whether investors expect devaluation before maturity and check taxes, controls or settlement frictions. An announced target by itself does not guarantee a riskless ability to move money and convert it back at that rate.
