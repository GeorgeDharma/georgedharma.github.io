Sudden stops

External funding can dry up before spending adjusts. Foreign-currency borrowing may worsen the domestic burden of debt after depreciation.

The starting point

A sudden stop occurs when private external funding falls sharply. An economy still has bills due for imports and existing foreign debt. New borrowing, reserve use, replacement financing or a reduction in spending must close the gap.

Currency mismatch adds pressure. A firm that earns local currency but owes dollars needs more of its own currency to repay after depreciation. An exporter earning dollars may have a natural offset, so the same exchange-rate move can affect two borrowers differently.

Theory and method

Sudden-stop framework and gross external financing needs

Calvo sudden stop · Rollover risk · Balance-sheet effect of depreciation

How can a country with a balanced current account face a foreign-currency funding crisis in the next quarter?

A utility's dollar bond falls due even if the country's current exports exactly cover its imports. It needs dollars to repay principal or a lender willing to renew the bond. A sudden stop in private financing can therefore create an urgent gap without a large current deficit. The stress intensifies when residents withdraw foreign deposits or depreciation raises the local value of foreign debt on firms with domestic income.

How the result is built

Gap = scheduled FX uses − confirmed nonreserve FX sources ; R₁ = R₀ − Gap

FX means foreign currency. R₀ and R₁ are freely usable opening and closing reserves in that currency. Uses include debt principal and commercial payments due. Sources include dated receipts, committed rollovers and other accessible funds.

  1. Produce a maturity calendar. If trade and interest receipts cover current payments, a $12m principal maturity still creates a gross financing need of $12m.
  2. Replace assumed refinancing by stressed committed amounts. A drop from $10m rollovers to $3m adds $7m to the required reserve draw.
  3. Check which sector owns the usable dollars. A sovereign reserve balance cannot automatically meet a private borrower's obligations without a transfer mechanism.

When the reasoning holds

  • Receipts and lines must be available by the actual deadline. Annual export estimates or undrawn lines with stress cancellation clauses can exaggerate liquidity.
  • A national net position hides gross and sector currency mismatch. Explicitly identify short foreign-currency borrowers and whether reserve access is authorized.
Terms in this lesson
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
First example

A bus company owes $2m and sells tickets only in local currency. With an exchange rate of 10 local units per dollar, the principal costs 20m local units. At 13 it costs 26m, an extra 6m before interest.

Distinguish net borrowing from gross refinancing

A country can have a modest current-account deficit yet face substantial financing pressure. Old foreign loans and bonds may mature this month. Even if new imports are close to exports, refinancing old principal requires lenders to advance new money or accept new securities.

A sudden stop is a sharp reduction in external financing. The loss can take the form of fewer new loans, lenders refusing to renew existing claims, or residents moving their own liquid funds abroad. A net annual figure can conceal a much larger short-term requirement.

Construct a currency and maturity ladder

Write foreign-currency obligations in order of due date. Separate contracted principal, interest, essential operating payments and contingent collateral calls. Beside each item record liquid reserves actually accessible by then, contracted foreign receipts and credit lines that remain available under the stress scenario.

A currency mismatch appears when debt is fixed in foreign currency and income arrives in local currency. Domestic depreciation then increases the local cost of the same dollar amount. A timing mismatch remains possible even when the currency matches, because an export receipt arriving next month cannot meet today's maturity.

Trace feedback between finance and output

If banks lose foreign funding, they may reduce lending to businesses. Firms can then cut production or imports, which can worsen income and tax receipts. Selling assets rapidly to meet deadlines can lower their prices and shrink the collateral supporting other loans.

There are several routes to closing an external gap, including spending adjustment, reserve use, voluntary refinancing and new official assistance. They have different effects on output and future obligations. A calculation of today's cash gap tells the size of the adjustment needed without identifying which route policy makers will choose.

Method

Build a stress-period sources-and-uses statement

  1. Uses

    Add contractually due foreign payments. Keep foreign-currency units throughout this first calculation and distinguish due from merely outstanding.

  2. Sources

    Add usable reserves, payments already committed to arrive and lending still available. Count only once any receipt already netted against a use.

  3. Gap and burden

    Subtract sources from uses. Then translate any unhedged obligations at the stressed exchange rate to identify which domestic borrowers face pressure.

Worked case

An airport cannot roll its dollar loan

An airport owes $30m principal and $2m interest this quarter. A lender previously expected to roll $24m now commits only $9m. Dollar landing fees of $14m and $5m of cash are available before maturity. There are no other immediate sources.

Working

  1. Contractual uses are $32m. Available sources under stress are $9m + $14m + $5m = $28m.
  2. The airport has a $4m funding gap. With the original $24m rollover its sources would have been $43m, leaving $11m after debt service.
  3. If it buys the missing dollars in the market at 12 local units per dollar, it needs 48m local units. The same gap would have cost 40m at the earlier rate of 10.

Interpretation

The bank's reduced renewal caused the immediate dollar shortage. Depreciation increased the local resources needed to fill it. These quantify distinct effects of the same stress episode.

A common mistake

Counting projected annual export revenue as cash available in the current week.

How to correct it

Allocate receipts to settlement dates and flag amounts exposed to cancellation or counterparty default. Verify committed bank facilities under their stress triggers.

Quick check
Which borrower is directly insulated from this conversion cost for a particular $2m payment?

A matched dollar receipt supplies the payment currency on time. A local asset or future revenue needs a sale, conversion or timing assumption.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A utility has $18m due this month and confirmed dollar receipts of $7m. Its reserves are $3m and a committed dollar line is $5m. A further $4m from exports arrives next month.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    What is the current funding gap?

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    At what local currency cost could this gap be filled if the rate moves from 6 to 7.5 local units per dollar?

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    Would a one-month $3m bridge loan resolve the underlying issue?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

Available funds are 7 + 3 + 5 = $15m. Uses are $18m, leaving $3m. The exports arriving next month cannot be counted yet.

Part B 2 marks

At the original rate it costs 3 × 6 = 18m local units. At the stressed rate it costs 22.5m, an extra 4.5m.

Part C 3 marks

It can meet this month's deadline. If next month's $4m arrives and covers bridge principal plus interest and any other payments due, repayment may be feasible. Its availability, cost and full next-month schedule must be checked.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a country with a balanced current account face a foreign-currency funding crisis in the next quarter?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would build a gross sources-and-uses statement. A balanced current account nets operating transactions over a period. It omits rollover of maturing principal and cannot ensure the right borrower has dollars at the deadline. Here principal of $12m against stressed rollover of $3m leaves $9m to find. I would then examine reserves, sector access and the domestic burden if the exchange rate moves.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A finance ministry says its reserves exceed the $9m shortfall by $1m. Would you clear the risk?

Compare with the follow-up answer

I would inspect pledged assets, required buffers, intraperiod payment timing and other entities relying on the same reserves. A net $1m margin on paper could still leave a bank or corporate borrower short on settlement day.
