Trade-weighted nominal and real effective exchange rates

Build a geometric nominal effective exchange-rate index using explicit trade weights.

Theory and terminology

Effective exchange-rate indices

NEER · REER · geometric trade weights

What you should be able to explain

  • Build a geometric nominal effective exchange-rate index using explicit trade weights.
  • Adjust the index for partner inflation and explain what a real appreciation can and cannot establish.

Make the unit and direction visible

An exchange rate against the dollar answers one trading-partner question. A country buying components in one currency and selling to several markets needs a measure of its overall currency position. An effective index weights bilateral moves by the relevance of the partners and combines percentage changes geometrically.

Use foreign currency per one home unit in this chapter. A rise means home appreciation. Many dealer screens show the reciprocal, home currency per foreign unit. Invert those series before combining them. A currency moving from two home units per dollar to 2.2 home units per dollar has lost purchasing power against the dollar.

Adjust for relative prices

Domestic goods can become relatively more expensive because the currency appreciates or because domestic prices grow faster than foreign prices. The real effective exchange rate (REER) in the appreciation-up convention includes both. Use growth factors from comparable price indices rebased to the same date.

The BIS methodology uses trade-based weights, geometric averages and relative consumer prices. Actual institutional weights can capture competition in third markets and vary across periods. For this exercise the weights are fixed and supplied, allowing the index to be calculated without a data download.

Interpret an index carefully

An index at 106 indicates 6% appreciation from its own base under this convention. It does not imply the currency is 6% overvalued. A base date is a normalization, while a misvaluation claim needs a model of sustainable fundamentals.

Sector experience can differ. Imported intermediate costs may fall for one exporter, while another's final goods become less competitive. Invoicing currency, margins, product quality and wage productivity also matter. A national CPI-based REER is an informative price comparison with those limits.

Method

Build the result

NEER₁ = 100 × ∏ⱼ(eⱼ₁/eⱼ₀)ʷʲ ; REER₁ = NEER₁ × h/∏ⱼ fⱼʷʲ

e is partner-currency units per home unit, j indexes partners, w is a fixed partner weight summing to one, h domestic price-growth factor and f each partner's price-growth factor. ∏ means multiply the weighted factors. Base indices equal 100.

  1. Convert bilateral changes into factors on the same appreciation-up basis, then raise each to its partner's weight.
  2. Multiply weighted currency factors to produce a nominal index. Separately combine partner price-growth factors using the same weights.
  3. Multiply the nominal index by home price growth divided by partner price growth. Interpret the result relative to the chosen base and quote convention.
Worked case

A two-partner competitiveness dashboard

Market A has weight 60% and B 40%. Home currency buys 10% more of A's currency and 10% less of B's. Domestic CPI rises 6%, A CPI 2% and B CPI 4%. All base indices are 100.

Working

  1. The A currency factor is 1.10 and B is 0.90. Both represent foreign units obtainable per home unit.
  2. The nominal effective index is 100 × 1.10^0.60 × 0.90^0.40 = 101.5156.
  3. The foreign inflation factor is 1.02^0.60 × 1.04^0.40 = 1.027953. Divide domestic 1.06 by this factor.
  4. REER is 101.5156 × 1.06/1.027953 = 104.6803. The real appreciation is about 4.68%, stronger than the nominal move because domestic prices rose faster.

Decision and interpretation

Flag a potential relative-price headwind and examine the largest markets by product. The 4.68% index change alone does not quantify lost exports.

Quick check
In an appreciation-up effective-rate index, domestic inflation exceeds weighted foreign inflation while the nominal effective rate is unchanged. What follows?

Domestic prices rise faster at the same exchange rates. The ratio of home to weighted foreign price factors therefore increases the real appreciation index.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Two partners have equal weights. Home currency buys 8% more of partner A and 2% less of B. Domestic prices rise 3%, A 1% and B 5%. Compute the nominal and real effective index from 100.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Nominal index is 100 × √(1.08 × 0.98) = 102.8786.
  2. Foreign price factor is √(1.01 × 1.05) ≈ 1.029806. Relative inflation is 1.03/1.029806 ≈ 1.000189.
  3. The real index is about 102.8980. Nominal appreciation explains almost all the change because weighted partner inflation is close to domestic inflation.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a nation's trade-weighted currency appreciate even when a major bilateral quote shows depreciation?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. A fall against one partner can be more than offset by appreciation against others, depending on the weights. I would put each currency into the same quotation direction, build the geometric nominal index, then adjust it by domestic inflation relative to weighted partner inflation. A rising appreciation-up index measures a change since the base date. I would look at volumes, costs and productivity before inferring an export loss.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Could two countries whose REER series both equal 110 be ranked as equally expensive?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. Each series is relative to its own base and trading partners. Comparing price levels or equilibrium values requires additional comparable price or fundamental data.
