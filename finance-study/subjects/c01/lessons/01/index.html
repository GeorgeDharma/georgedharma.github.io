National income

Output can be spent at home or abroad. Derive the connection between national spending, saving, investment and the external balance.

The starting point

A country’s factories can sell to people at home or abroad. Gross domestic product (GDP) measures production inside its borders. To count the same total from purchases, add household consumption, investment, government purchases and exports, then subtract imports already counted in those purchases.

National saving is the portion of national disposable income left after private and government consumption. The current account equals national saving minus domestic investment. Foreign income and transfers can make the current account differ from exports less imports.

Theory and method

National saving–investment identity

Sectoral balances · Twin deficits hypothesis

If a government cuts taxes by 4% of GDP, does the current-account deficit automatically increase by the same amount?

Consider a government that collects four fewer units and leaves spending unchanged. Its financial balance deteriorates. Households receiving the tax cut might save the full amount, consume it or buy imported machinery. The outcome depends on their response and on investment. Dividing the national balance into private and public components forces each explanation to account for the offset somewhere else. The equality is exact under the definitions, while a prediction about behaviour requires a separate model.

How the result is built

CA = (Sₚ − I) + (T − G)

CA is the current account, Sₚ private saving, I private investment in this simplified economy, T taxes net of transfers and G government purchases assumed entirely current. All values cover the same year in the same currency.

  1. Start from national saving less domestic investment. Here public saving is net taxes less government consumption and public investment is zero.
  2. Separate private saving less private investment from the public balance. A private surplus of 3 plus a government deficit of 5 gives a current account of −2.
  3. After a tax cut, hold behaviour fixed only if justified. If private saving rises by 4 while the government balance falls by 4, the two movements offset.

When the reasoning holds

  • A government building new infrastructure requires explicit public investment and consistent public saving or net borrowing measures.
  • Private saving and investment can respond to policy and income. Inferring a causal fiscal effect from the identity alone leaves these responses unspecified.

First example

An economy produces 200, spends 120 on household consumption, 30 on government consumption and 60 on investment. Assume zero foreign income and transfers. Saving is 200 − 120 − 30 = 50. Investment exceeds saving by 10, so the current account is −10.

Whose income are we measuring?

Imagine a local factory owned by an overseas family. The factory's output belongs in domestic production because the factory operates here. The profit paid to the family belongs to foreign residents' income. A country's residents might also receive wages, interest or dividends from their work and investments overseas.

Start with gross domestic product, the value added within the border. Add net primary income from abroad to obtain gross national income. Add net current transfers, such as incoming remittances less outgoing ones, to obtain national disposable income. Each step answers a different question about who can spend the resulting resources.

Why investment links saving to trade

Consumption uses resources now. Investment, including a new machine or goods added to inventory, sets resources aside for future production. National saving is disposable income left after private and government consumption. Investments in financial securities change ownership of claims and are recorded separately from producing new machines.

The current account records net exports plus net primary income and net current transfers. Substituting the expenditure expression for output shows that national saving minus domestic investment equals the current account. This is an accounting equality. To explain a deficit, investigate which decisions about consumption, investment or income changed.

Private and public balances

A government's deficit contributes to national borrowing when everything else stays fixed. Households can respond by saving more, or private investment can change. The national balance alone cannot tell us which response occurred.

Gross saving and gross investment include depreciation. Net measures subtract it from both sides. Match gross with gross and compare all entries over the same period. A flow for the year cannot be subtracted directly from the value of the existing capital stock.

Method

Reconcile two routes to the same balance

  1. Trade

    Subtract imports from exports and keep net income receipts and current transfers as signed additional lines.

  2. Saving

    Add foreign income and transfers to GDP. Deduct consumption by households and government to find national saving.

  3. Cross-check

    Subtract investment from saving. A difference from the current account signals a missing item, incompatible date or inconsistent definition.

Worked case

The port economy

Annual accounts in billions show private consumption 150, government consumption 40, investment 55, exports 70 and imports 75. Income paid abroad exceeds income received by 6. Residents receive net current transfers of 3.

Working

  1. GDP equals 150 + 40 + 55 + 70 − 75 = 240. Import subtraction removes foreign production embedded in spending.
  2. National disposable income is 240 − 6 + 3 = 237. National saving is 237 − 150 − 40 = 47.
  3. The current account is 47 − 55 = −8. The direct calculation, 70 − 75 − 6 + 3, independently gives −8.

Interpretation

The economy is a net borrower in the current account sense during this year. Its investment could still turn out to be productive. The accounts alone cannot evaluate the projects.

A common mistake

Calling all household savings invested in foreign shares domestic investment.

How to correct it

Trace what the money buys. Buying an existing financial claim changes the portfolio. Producing or acquiring new fixed capital for domestic use contributes to domestic investment.

Quick check
If saving stays at 50 and investment rises from 60 to 65, what balance follows?

Saving minus investment becomes 50 − 65 = −15. The identity describes the simultaneous balances. It does not identify why investment changed.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A second economy records GDP 300, household consumption 185, government consumption 50 and gross investment 70. It receives net primary income of 4 and sends net current transfers of 1.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate national saving and the current account.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Recover net exports from the spending identity.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Could a rise in government consumption of 3 leave the current account unchanged if income and investment stay fixed?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Disposable income is 300 + 4 − 1 = 303. Saving is 303 − 185 − 50 = 68. The current account is 68 − 70 = −2.

Part B 2 marks

Net exports equal 300 − 185 − 50 − 70 = −5. Adding net income of 4 and transfers of −1 reproduces −2.

Part C 2 marks

Yes, if private consumption falls by 3. Total consumption and national saving then stay constant. Without such an offset, saving and the current account each fall by 3.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

If a government cuts taxes by 4% of GDP, does the current-account deficit automatically increase by the same amount?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would report the fiscal effect and then estimate the private response. With initial private net saving of 3 and a fiscal deficit of 5, the external deficit is 2. If private saving rises by the full 4 tax cut, that external balance stays the same. If households consume the whole increase and investment holds still, the deficit grows to 6. Lending conditions and expected future taxes help distinguish the cases.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose private investment also rises by two and households save only one of the four. What happens?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Private net saving falls from 3 to 2 after adding one of saving and subtracting two of investment. With the public balance at −9, the current account becomes −7, five units weaker than initially.
