Balance of payments

Record current transactions and their financing counterparts. State the sign convention before reconciling financial flows and reserve changes.

The starting point

When a resident imports machinery on credit, two things happen. Equipment enters the country and the resident incurs a debt to a foreign supplier. The balance of payments records both the transaction and its financial counterpart.

Use the convention that the financial account equals residents’ purchases of foreign financial assets minus the new liabilities they incur to nonresidents. With a zero capital account and no measurement error, the current account equals the financial account. Official reserves count among the financial assets.

Theory and method

Double-entry balance-of-payments accounting

Net lending and borrowing · IMF financial-account convention

A donor forgives an existing foreign loan. Why can the capital and financial accounts move while the current account stays at zero?

A foreign creditor's formal agreement to forgive principal removes an obligation from the resident borrower's balance sheet. The donor has transferred wealth without receiving a good, service or ordinary income payment in return. Recording the debt reduction alone would make the country's external financial position change without a counterpart. A qualifying debt-forgiveness capital transfer supplies that counterpart. Separating forgiveness by mutual agreement from a lender's unilateral statistical write-off matters because the latter follows different recording rules.

How the result is built

CA + KA = FA = NAA − NIL when discrepancies are zero

CA is current-account balance, KA capital-account balance, FA financial-account balance, NAA residents' net acquisition of external financial assets and NIL net incurrence of external liabilities. Positive FA means net lending. All measures are transactions over a period.

  1. Identify the nature of the event before choosing an account. A negotiated gift of debt principal qualifies as a capital transfer in the example.
  2. The resident records a positive capital transfer of 8. Its liability falls by 8, so net incurrence is −8.
  3. With external assets unchanged, FA = 0 − (−8) = +8, equal to CA of zero plus KA of 8. Record no fictitious export or reserve inflow.

When the reasoning holds

  • The case assumes mutually agreed cancellation of recognized debt. A unilateral recognition of unrecoverability can instead be an other-volume change.
  • Use the same sign convention and no measurement discrepancy. A presentation calling financial inflows positive will label particular entries differently.
Terms in this lesson
Balance of payments
A country’s record of economic transactions between its residents and the rest of the world during a period.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
First example

A country imports 12 of goods. Its residents borrow 8 from abroad and its central bank uses 4 of reserves to settle the rest. The current account records −12. Financial assets change by −4 and liabilities by +8, giving −4 − 8 = −12.

Follow both sides of an international sale

A resident exporter can ship goods without immediately receiving cash. The shipment counts as an export when ownership changes. If the foreign buyer promises payment in ninety days, the exporter has acquired a financial asset, an enforceable claim on a nonresident.

When the buyer eventually pays into an existing foreign deposit account, the exporter exchanges its trade-credit asset for a deposit asset. That later exchange generally creates no second export. Double-entry bookkeeping records the two sides at the relevant time and keeps the transaction distinct from its settlement.

Identify assets, liabilities and the sign convention

External assets are claims residents have on nonresidents. External liabilities are claims nonresidents have on residents. Under the convention used here, the financial account equals net acquisition of external assets minus net incurrence of external liabilities. A positive difference represents net lending through financial transactions.

The current and capital accounts record different kinds of transfers. A worker's remittance ordinarily enters current transfers. A qualifying capital transfer enters the capital account. With no measurement discrepancy, current account plus capital account equals the financial account under this convention. The word “capital” in ordinary news may instead refer to financial flows.

Understand why a residual appears

Statisticians collect goods, services, financing and reserve data from different sources. Reporting dates, missing observations and price conversions can lead to a discrepancy. A residual helps reconcile published series while preserving the underlying accounting logic.

A central bank's sale of international reserves reduces an external asset. Buying its own currency in the same operation is easy to mislabel as a fresh inflow of foreign financing. Draw the balance sheets from the resident country's perspective before choosing a sign.

Method

Build a transaction ledger

  1. Name the resident

    Identify which person, company or public institution is the resident party. Residence follows the economic connection used by the accounts.

  2. Classify the first entry

    Distinguish a good, service, current income, capital transfer or change in a financial claim.

  3. Find the counterpart

    If the transaction is financed, ask who now owes whom. Add external assets and subtract external liabilities under the stated convention.

Worked case

New machinery with two sources of finance

A local factory imports 25 of equipment. The foreign seller leaves 6 on trade credit. A foreign bank lends 11. The central bank supplies the remaining foreign currency by drawing down reserves. Ignore other flows.

Working

  1. The equipment purchase gives a current-account debit of 25. Here there is no offsetting export or income receipt.
  2. New foreign liabilities total 6 + 11 = 17. Reserves fall by the unfinanced remainder of 25 − 17 = 8.
  3. Financial account = −8 − 17 = −25. The reserve loss and new liability both accommodate the import, though they enter different columns of the ledger.

Interpretation

The 25 is one purchase financed in three ways. The reserve loss is neither a further purchase of machinery nor an additional liability.

A common mistake

Subtracting imports and then subtracting the borrowing used to pay for them within the same current account.

How to correct it

Keep separate current-account and financial-account columns. The second entry explains the financing of the same transaction.

Quick check
An importer buys 9 of goods wholly on foreign supplier credit. With the same convention, what entries arise?

The goods purchase reduces the current account by 9. A new foreign liability enters the financial account with a minus sign under this convention.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

In a fresh year a country exports 14 entirely on trade credit and imports 20. Foreign lenders finance 13 of the import and residents use 7 from foreign deposits to pay the balance.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    Find the current-account balance if no other income or transfers occur.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the financial account using the resident-asset convention.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    The foreign customer later settles 14 into the exporter's foreign deposit. Explain the new entries.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

Exports 14 less imports 20 gives −6. Neither deferred settlement nor financing timing changes the recorded purchases and sales here.

Part B 3 marks

Foreign trade-credit assets rise 14 and foreign deposits fall 7, giving net asset acquisition of 7. New liabilities are 13. The financial account is 7 − 13 = −6.

Part C 2 marks

Trade-credit assets fall 14 and deposit assets rise 14. Net foreign asset acquisition in this exchange is zero and no new goods export is recorded.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A donor forgives an existing foreign loan. Why can the capital and financial accounts move while the current account stays at zero?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask first whether the lender and borrower agreed to forgive the debt. In that case I record a capital transfer received and a decrease in the country's external liabilities. Under the assets-minus-liabilities convention both sides of net lending rise by the forgiven amount. The country owes less, but that event alone supplies no cash with which to meet a different dollar payment tomorrow.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if residents instead repay the eight using an eight-unit deposit already held abroad?

Compare with the follow-up answer

External assets decline by 8 and external liabilities decline by 8. FA is −8 − (−8) = zero. It is a swap of two balance-sheet items with no capital transfer and no automatic current-account entry.
