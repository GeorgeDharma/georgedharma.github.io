Stocks and flows

A current-account balance is a flow over time. The international investment position is a stock that can also change through exchange rates and asset revaluations.

The starting point

A household’s monthly saving and the value of its portfolio today answer different questions. Countries also have a flow and a stock. The current account measures net transactions over a period. The net international investment position measures foreign financial assets less foreign financial liabilities on a date.

Price movements and currency conversions change the value of outstanding claims. To explain a change in a country’s position, distinguish new transactions from gains or losses on existing holdings. Use the same reporting currency at both dates.

Theory and method

International investment position reconciliation

Stock–flow adjustment · Currency valuation effect

Can a country's net external position improve after depreciation even when it runs a current-account deficit?

Imagine exporters own dollar bonds while many of the country's foreign investors hold shares denominated in local currency. When the local currency loses value, a dollar asset becomes worth more in local-currency accounts. Local-currency liabilities need not revalue on the same basis. New borrowing can therefore coincide with a larger valuation gain on old foreign assets. To interpret that result, identify the reporting currency, outstanding exposures and the timing of transactions.

How the result is built

N₁ = N₀ + FA + V + O

N₀ and N₁ are opening and closing net international investment positions in the reporting currency. FA is net financial transactions, V net price and exchange-rate gains on existing assets less liabilities, and O other volume changes.

  1. Convert opening gross positions at the opening rate. With dollar assets of 30 and dollar liabilities of 10 the net dollar exposure is +20.
  2. A move from 2 to 2.4 home units per dollar raises the home-currency value of that net exposure by 20 × 0.4 = 8.
  3. Add financial transactions at their transaction-date values. Net borrowing of 5 reduces NIIP by 5, while the earlier valuation gain adds 8.

When the reasoning holds

  • This arithmetic fixes the dollar prices of the existing instruments and other assets. Equity or bond price changes can reinforce or reverse the currency effect.
  • Transaction timing matters. Holdings acquired after depreciation are already booked at the new rate and should not receive the earlier revaluation.
Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
First example

Start with foreign assets of 90 and liabilities of 110, a net position of −20. The country runs a surplus of 5 and its existing foreign assets gain 8 in domestic-currency value. Ignore other changes. The ending net position is −20 + 5 + 8 = −7.

Construct an external balance sheet

Imagine photographing a country's financial claims at midnight on 31 December. On one side are residents' foreign shares, bonds, loans, property interests recorded as investment, deposits and reserves. On the other side are nonresidents' claims on residents. The difference is the net international investment position, abbreviated NIIP.

A negative NIIP indicates net external liabilities at that date. It can coexist with a current-account surplus this year because the surplus covers only the flow over the year. A large positive stock can likewise fall despite new net lending if existing foreign investments lose market value.

Three reasons a position moves

Acquiring assets or incurring liabilities changes the stock through transactions. A change in an asset's price changes the value of an existing holding. A change in the exchange rate changes its value expressed in the reporting currency. Other volume changes include particular reclassifications or write-offs.

Start the reconciliation with opening NIIP. Add the net financial transactions measured using assets minus liabilities, the net valuation effect and any other change. Only under additional assumptions, such as a zero capital account and no discrepancy, can you substitute the current account for the financial transactions line.

Read currency exposure on both sides

A stronger domestic currency reduces the domestic-currency value of a fixed foreign-currency asset. It also reduces the burden of a liability denominated in that currency. The net effect depends on the currency composition of both portfolios, even when gross assets and liabilities are similar.

Identify the order of price changes and currency conversion. If both move materially, multiply their factors. A rise of 10% in a foreign stock alongside a 10% fall in its domestic conversion rate gives 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99, a one-percent domestic loss.

Method

Reconcile a position change

  1. Opening snapshot

    Take opening asset and liability values in the same reporting currency. Label whether the question asks for the gross assets or the net position.

  2. Transactions

    Find new asset acquisitions less new liabilities. Treat purchases and sales using their signs and transaction-date values.

  3. Revaluation

    Reprice only the positions exposed over the stated interval and add any specified other changes. Reconcile the result with the ending balance sheet.

Worked case

An overseas portfolio changes price

A country begins with foreign equities worth 60 in domestic currency, other external assets of 50 and liabilities of 140. New transactions occur at year-end after market moves. It acquires net additional assets of 6 with no new liabilities.

Working

  1. The opening NIIP is 60 + 50 − 140 = −30. A negative starting balance means foreign residents hold more value in the country than its residents hold abroad.
  2. The old foreign equities gain 20% in their own currency while the home value of each foreign currency unit falls 10%. They end at 60 × 1.20 × 0.90 = 64.8.
  3. Only the 4.8 valuation gain and the 6 transaction gain change NIIP, giving −30 + 4.8 + 6 = −19.2. Other positions are assumed unchanged.

Interpretation

The country's external balance sheet improved by 10.8, while its net financial transactions amount to 6. The difference requires no extra capital inflow.

A common mistake

Treating a revaluation as income received or cash available for debt service.

How to correct it

Separate an unrealized change in market price from dividends, interest and actual sales. A better net position alone may provide little immediate liquidity.

Quick check
Starting at −7, what happens if the current account is zero and foreign assets fall in value by 3?

The valuation loss reduces the stock even though transactions over the period sum to zero.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

Another country opens with assets of 180 and liabilities of 220. It incurs 9 in new liabilities, buys 3 of foreign assets and has a 12 valuation loss on externally held domestic shares, measured from the foreign owners' perspective.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    Calculate net transactions.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What is the ending NIIP with no other changes?

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Why could residents still be worse off despite the NIIP improvement?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

New assets minus new liabilities equal 3 − 9 = −6. Transaction flows alone worsen the opening −40 position to −46.

Part B 3 marks

A fall in the value of the country's external equity liabilities improves its NIIP by 12. Ending NIIP is −40 − 6 + 12 = −34.

Part C 2 marks

A domestic stock-market collapse could reduce local wealth and investment while lowering the value of foreigners' holdings. NIIP measures cross-border financial claims, not welfare or domestic wealth in full.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a country's net external position improve after depreciation even when it runs a current-account deficit?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes, if outstanding foreign-currency assets exceed comparable liabilities in the chosen reporting currency. In this example depreciation adds 8 to the existing net dollar exposure and new borrowing uses 5, so the net position improves by 3. Before drawing a welfare conclusion I would identify who owns those assets and which borrowers need dollars immediately. A national gain can coexist with serious corporate cash shortfalls.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What happens if the dollar portfolio is hedged by a derivative that loses 8 in domestic value?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Include the derivative revaluation among external claims when the counterparty is nonresident. Its 8 loss cancels the earlier gain. Keeping the initial position and new borrowing unchanged, closing NIIP becomes −25.
