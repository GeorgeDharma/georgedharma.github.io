Dornbusch overshooting after a monetary surprise

Derive the spot quote required by a specified interest differential and future exchange-rate anchor.

Theory and terminology

The Dornbusch overshooting model

exchange-rate overshooting · sticky prices

What you should be able to explain

  • Derive the spot quote required by a specified interest differential and future exchange-rate anchor.
  • Distinguish an impact depreciation, a temporary overshoot and subsequent convergence using one quote convention.

Two prices adjust at different speeds

An exchange rate can change within seconds, while a restaurant price list or annual wage contract takes time to reset. In the sticky-price Dornbusch model an unanticipated increase in nominal money initially raises real money balances. Under the simple liquidity mechanism, domestic interest falls temporarily relative to the world rate.

In the longer run domestic prices adjust. A permanent change to nominal money can leave a permanently different nominal exchange rate even after the real effects unwind. Draw both the eventual exchange rate and the rate immediately after the surprise. They answer different questions.

What investors must expect

Use home-currency units per foreign unit. When the domestic rate is temporarily below the foreign rate and currency risk carries no premium, an investor lending at home must expect the home currency to appreciate over the holding period. Otherwise holding the foreign deposit dominates on expected return.

A domestic appreciation means the home-per-foreign quote falls from today's spot to the expected future spot. If the future quote has already moved upward to a new long-run anchor, today's quote has to start still higher. That extra distance is overshooting.

Where the mechanism can break

The argument compares expected returns on comparable assets. Default risk, liquidity differences or a time-varying currency premium can also produce a rate gap. A single market quote cannot identify the monetary shock or prove that prices are sticky.

The worked calculation uses an externally supplied future anchor and one-period rates. A full dynamic model determines the anchor and the path of domestic prices jointly. The calculation isolates the asset-market step so a student can diagnose a quoted path.

Method

Build the result

S₀ = E₀[S₁] × (1+iᶠ)/(1+iᴴ)

S₀ is today's home-per-foreign spot quote after the announcement. E₀[S₁] is its expected next-period value. iᴴ and iᶠ are matched home and foreign interest rates over that period. E denotes an expectation under the stated beliefs.

  1. One home unit invested at home becomes 1+iᴴ.
  2. Convert that unit into 1/S₀ foreign units, earn the foreign rate and translate using the expected later quote. Expected home proceeds are (1+iᶠ)E₀[S₁]/S₀.
  3. Set these equal under zero currency premium and rearrange. With iᴴ below iᶠ, S₀ exceeds its expected later value.
Worked case

A rate cut under a newly revised long-run anchor

Before the shock, spot is 1.00 home unit per foreign unit. After the announcement, the expected quote in a year is 1.20. Domestic and foreign one-year rates are now 2% and 5%. Assume convergence to the revised anchor by year end and matched risk.

Working

  1. The new year-end quote is 20% above the old level. The permanent component is a 0.20 rise in the quote.
  2. Spot consistent with the two returns is 1.20 × 1.05/1.02 = 1.23529.
  3. Impact depreciation measured as the rise in the home-per-foreign quote is 23.53%. Relative to the revised 1.20 anchor, spot is 2.94% higher.
  4. A subsequent decline in the quote from 1.23529 to 1.20 is 2.86% of its initial post-shock value. This expected home appreciation compensates for the lower home deposit rate.

Decision and interpretation

The currency overshoots on impact under these assumptions. A research note should separately report the old level, new anchor, immediate quote and expected reversal.

Quick check
With home interest temporarily below the foreign rate and zero currency premium, which spot path fits the model after a permanent home monetary expansion?

At the lower home interest rate, the domestic currency must be expected to appreciate. In home-per-foreign units that means the post-shock spot starts above the expected later quote.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A different economy starts at 2.00 home units per foreign unit. After a surprise its expected year-end spot is 2.10, domestic interest 3% and foreign interest 6%. Derive the impact quote and the expected move after impact.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Spot after the announcement is 2.10 × 1.06/1.03 = 2.16117.
  2. The quote rises about 8.06% from 2.00. It stands 2.91% above the 2.10 revised anchor.
  3. The expected change in the quote from impact is 2.10/2.16117 − 1 = −2.83%. That is an expected appreciation of the home currency in this quotation.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A permanent monetary expansion lowers the domestic interest rate today. Why might the domestic currency initially fall farther than its eventual depreciation?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would keep the quote in home currency per foreign unit. Sticky goods prices let a monetary expansion lower the domestic interest rate temporarily. Under zero currency premium, a domestic deposit paying less interest needs an expected currency appreciation to compete. Once the market expects 1.20 in a year, the current quote has to jump beyond 1.20 so it can come down afterward. At rates of 2% and 5%, that spot is 1.23529.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if goods prices adjust immediately and both rates remain 6%?

Compare with the follow-up answer

With the same future anchor and zero currency premium the impact spot is 2.10. Equal interest factors remove the need for an expected appreciation beyond the new equilibrium.
