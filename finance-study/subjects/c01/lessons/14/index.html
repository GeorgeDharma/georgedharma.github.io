Foreign direct investment, entry modes and the external accounts

Classify ordinary cross-border shareholdings and explain why a firm might build or buy a local business.

Theory and terminology

Foreign direct investment and the eclectic paradigm

FDI · Dunning OLI framework · greenfield · reinvested earnings · international investment position

What you should be able to explain

  • Classify ordinary cross-border shareholdings and explain why a firm might build or buy a local business.
  • Separate cash dividends, reinvested earnings, investment transactions and valuation changes in an outward or inward FDI position.

Determine who owns which claim

Foreign direct investment (FDI) is a lasting investment relationship between residents of different economies. For a single investor buying ordinary voting shares with no other group links, the statistical threshold is at least 10% of voting power. A holding from 10% through 50% represents significant influence under the convention. More than 50% gives control. A smaller holding of ordinary shares in an unrelated company is portfolio equity.

Use the investor's voting rights to classify the relationship and its economic ownership share to allocate profits. The two can differ when share classes have different rights. The exercises use one vote per share and equal income rights. Related-entity chains require a wider ownership analysis.

Why export, license, build or buy?

Dunning's OLI framework asks three questions. Ownership means the firm has an advantage it can use abroad, such as a process or brand. Location asks why the activity belongs in the host country, perhaps because customers need local service or inputs are nearby. Internalization asks why the firm should run it itself: a licensee may be hard to monitor or a secret hard to protect. A tight deadline or a high asking price can still decide the mode.

Greenfield investment establishes a new operation. Acquisition buys an existing business and can immediately deliver permits, employees or distribution, with integration costs and inherited risks. A market-seeking firm wants access to customers. Resource- or efficiency-seeking firms may want an input or a lower-cost production stage. A strategic-asset motive targets capabilities such as technology. Each motive can support more than one entry route.

Trace income, cash and the investment position

For an immediate direct investor, its share of eligible earnings includes cash dividends and the share retained in the enterprise. From the host's perspective, the investor earns income payable abroad. Under the IMF's BPM7 manual, this enters the current account's earned income account. The retained part has a matching financial-account entry that increases the foreign owner's equity claim, even if cash never crosses a border.

A flow measures transactions during the year. A stock, or position, is the value at a date. Market-price and exchange-rate movements can change a position without adding to the year's investment flow. Equally, buying shares from a resident seller changes foreign ownership, while the seller receives the purchase price. The acquired factory's physical capacity can remain identical.

Method

Build the result

R = s × (E − D). Closing FDI position = opening position + FDI transactions + valuation changes + other volume changes.

R is the investor's reinvested earnings, s its share of profit, E eligible enterprise earnings before distributions and D total ordinary dividends from those earnings. Money is measured in the reporting currency. Other volume changes include certain reclassifications between investment categories.

  1. Classify the relationship from residence and voting rights. Compute investor income s × E, cash dividend s × D and their difference R.
  2. Count cash equity investment and R once in FDI transactions. Eligible intercompany debt can also contribute in more general cases.
  3. Roll the opening position forward, keeping market revaluations and reclassifications outside transactions. State whether the figure refers to a host liability or a home asset.
Worked case

A train-servicing company enters a neighbouring market

A company with a proprietary fault-detection method needs a local depot to win a service contract starting in nine months. A new depot takes 18 months. Buying 60% of a licensed operator permits launch in four. On day one, it pays resident shareholders €48m for 60% of votes and profit rights. An unrelated foreign fund separately buys 4% for €3.2m. Annual eligible earnings are €10m, with €4m ordinary dividends. A separate market revaluation adds €5m to the buyer's stake. Both foreign investors start with zero. No other changes occur.

Working

  1. The diagnostic method supplies an ownership advantage. Response times favor a local depot, and keeping diagnostic work inside the group protects the method. Acquisition meets the deadline. Its 60% stake is direct investment. The fund's unrelated 4% is portfolio equity.
  2. The direct investor earns 0.60 × 10 = €6m, including 0.60 × 4 = €2.4m cash dividends. Its reinvested earnings are €3.6m. The host records €6m of income payable to this investor.
  3. FDI liability transactions attributable to this buyer are €48m + €3.6m = €51.6m. The fund's €3.2m purchase is a separate portfolio transaction.
  4. Closing host FDI liability to the buyer is 0 + 51.6 + 5 = €56.6m. Of the €51.6m flow, only €48m is a cash purchase, paid to previous shareholders.

Decision and interpretation

Recommend acquisition subject to verifying the permit survives the ownership change. For the host investment report, show the €51.6m financial flow separately from any evidence of new fixed investment.

Quick check
A foreign company owns 20% of the votes and profits of an unrelated operating company. The business earns €5m of eligible profit and pays no dividends. Which entries does the host record for this investor?

The 20% stake qualifies as direct investment. The investor earns 20% × €5m = €1m, all retained. Recording both entries attributes that income to its owner and records its reinvestment.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A foreign manufacturer wants local production of a proprietary component. Existing plants cannot accommodate its process, and delivery can wait for construction. It invests €30m on January 1 in 75% of a new host company. A local partner invests €10m for 25%. Annual eligible earnings are €8m and ordinary dividends €2m. Currency translation reduces the foreign investor's closing position by €1.5m. Separately an unrelated foreign pension fund buys 7% of another resident firm's voting shares for €4m. No other changes occur. Classify both investments and compute income, the FDI flow and the closing position.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The manufacturer chooses greenfield FDI with majority control. Building suits the process and timing. The unrelated pension fund's purchase is portfolio equity.
  2. The investor earns 75% × €8m = €6m, receives 75% × €2m = €1.5m in dividends, and reinvests €4.5m. The host records income payable of €6m to this investor.
  3. The inward FDI transaction flow is €30m + €4.5m = €34.5m. Deduct the €1.5m translation loss for a closing position of €33m. The local partner supplies domestic equity.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A foreign company buys an existing local business and leaves some earnings there. What attracted it, and how much foreign direct investment does the host economy record?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask what the company brings, why it needs a local operation and why it wants to control the work. An existing depot can meet a deadline that a new build would miss. Then I would classify the voting stake and trace what the investor earned. In the depot case, €48m changes hands, another €3.6m is reinvested, and a separate holding gain takes the investment position to €56.6m.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Later the pension fund's existing 7% is worth €4.2m. It buys another 3% for €1.8m and reaches exactly 10%. Is €6m a new FDI transaction?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The 10% threshold is inclusive. The €1.8m new purchase is an FDI transaction. The earlier €4.2m holding is reclassified from portfolio to direct investment as an other volume change. Together they give a €6m direct-investment position, with only €1.8m of new money that day.
