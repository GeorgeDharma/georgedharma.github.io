Money and inflation

Distinguish a one-time price-level change from persistent inflation. Explore how policy rates, expectations and supply shocks affect purchasing power.

The starting point

The price level measures the cost of a basket at a point in time. Inflation measures how quickly that level changes. If a supply disruption raises the price level once, purchasing power falls immediately even if the subsequent inflation rate returns to zero.

Higher policy rates can make borrowing and spending less attractive, which can relieve demand pressure with a delay. The expected real interest rate adjusts the nominal rate for expected inflation. A supply shortage can still raise prices while reducing output.

Theory and method

Fisher equation and quantity equation of money

Irving Fisher · Ex ante and ex post real interest · Income velocity

The money supply rose 12%, but consumer prices rose 4%. Does the quantity equation fail, and what happened to a saver earning 5%?

A euro can pay for several purchases during the year. The money stock and the rate at which money circulates together support nominal transactions or income, depending on how velocity is defined. More money can coincide with higher real output or slower turnover. Separately, a saver receives nominal euros and buys goods at the realized price level. Dividing nominal wealth growth by price growth reveals the change in purchasing power.

How the result is built

MV = PY ; 1+r = (1+i)/(1+π)

M is money stock, V income velocity, P the output price index and Y real output. i is a one-period nominal interest rate, π realized inflation relevant to the saver and r the realized real return.

  1. From the income identity divide nominal output PY by the chosen money measure to obtain velocity. Velocity is allowed to move.
  2. If money grows 12%, real output 3% and the output price index 4%, velocity's factor is 1.04 × 1.03/1.12 = 0.95643, a fall of about 4.36%.
  3. For a 5% deposit and 4% consumer inflation, purchasing power grows by 1.05/1.04 − 1 = 0.96%. Using expected inflation instead answers an opening-date question.

When the reasoning holds

  • A claim that money growth causes equal inflation needs assumptions about output and velocity. The identity itself does not fix either response.
  • The output deflator and household price index measure different baskets. The numerical example uses 4% for both only as an explicit case assumption.
Terms in this lesson
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
How to read the notation
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.

All notation examples

First example

A basket index moves from 100 to 108 and then stays at 108. Inflation is 8% for the first period and zero for the second. A worker whose pay stays at 100 can buy about 100/108 = 92.6% of the original basket.

Build a price index from a basket

A shop receipt contains prices and quantities. To isolate the change in prices, hold the quantities of a reference basket fixed and reprice it. Divide the cost in the comparison period by its cost in the base period, then multiply by 100. An index of 112 means the reference basket now costs 12% more than in its base period.

Successive inflation rates compound. If prices rise 10% and then fall 10%, the index moves from 100 to 110 to 99. A reduction in the inflation rate still allows the price level to rise. A sequence of falling price levels is called deflation.

Translate income and borrowing costs into purchasing power

A wage increase matters to living standards relative to the consumer's prices. To calculate real wage growth, divide the nominal wage growth factor by the inflation factor and subtract one. A fixed nominal repayment also loses purchasing power when the price level unexpectedly rises.

At the date a loan is made, lenders can anticipate expected inflation and adjust the nominal rate. The approximate expected real rate is nominal interest minus expected inflation. The exact one-period calculation divides the nominal gross return by the expected price-growth factor under the simple certain-inflation assumption.

Trace demand and supply separately

A restricted fuel supply can raise firms' costs while reducing their output. A demand expansion can increase both prices and output in the short run. The fact that inflation rose gives incomplete evidence about which shock occurred or which policy response suits it.

Raising the policy rate can slow borrowing and near-term demand and can influence expectations. It cannot immediately produce missing fuel. Transmission depends on debt contracts, exchange rates and expectations, so interpret a jump in inflation alongside wage data, output and the particular basket components that changed.

Method

Construct and interpret a price index

  1. Price the base basket

    Multiply each good's base quantity by its base price, then sum. Keep the quantity weights fixed in the comparison period.

  2. Compute inflation

    Divide the new basket cost by the immediately preceding cost and subtract one. State which period the percentage covers.

  3. Deflate a cash amount

    Divide nominal income or repayment by the price index relative to the starting date. Compare the result with the original purchasing power.

Worked case

A delivery driver's living costs

A monthly basket has 40 food units and 20 transport units. Base prices are 2 and 3. In the following year food costs 2.10 and transport 3.60. The driver's monthly pay rises from 1,400 to 1,470.

Working

  1. The base basket costs 40 × 2 + 20 × 3 = 140. The new basket costs 84 + 72 = 156. Inflation on that basket is 156/140 − 1 = 11.43%.
  2. Nominal earnings rise by 5%. Real earnings change by 1.05/(156/140) − 1 = −5.77%. The driver can afford fewer copies of the starting basket.
  3. If both prices then stay fixed, the next comparable inflation rate is zero. A zero rate does not restore the 140 basket cost or reverse the previous loss of purchasing power.

Interpretation

A household that uses fewer transport units could face a smaller personal price increase. The basket weights define whose spending pattern the index represents.

A common mistake

Subtracting the entire index level from a nominal growth percentage.

How to correct it

Convert both amounts into comparable growth factors. For an index moving from 110 to 121 use a factor of 121/110, not 121%.

Quick check
If the basket next moves from 108 to 110.16, which inflation rate applies to that period?

Divide the increase of 2.16 by the starting level of 108. Inflation is a change relative to the previous price level.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

An index rises from 125 to 130 in a year. A household's pay increases 3%. A one-year deposit contracted before the increase pays 6% and has no fees or tax.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    Find inflation for the period.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Calculate the change in the household's real wage.

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    Calculate the realized real deposit return and distinguish it from the expected real return.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

The increase is 5 index points on a base of 125. Inflation equals 130/125 − 1 = 4%.

Part B 2 marks

The real wage factor is 1.03/1.04 = 0.99038, giving about −0.96%. Nominal pay grew more slowly than prices.

Part C 3 marks

Purchasing power rises by 1.06/1.04 − 1 = 1.92%. This uses realized inflation. Expected real return at deposit opening depended on the inflation then anticipated.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

The money supply rose 12%, but consumer prices rose 4%. Does the quantity equation fail, and what happened to a saver earning 5%?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

No. Higher real output or lower income velocity absorbs part of a money increase in the quantity equation. I would check the money definition and use an output deflator for nominal GDP. For this saver, the relevant basket rose 4%, and 105/1.04 leaves about 100.96 in starting purchasing power. That realized return can differ materially from the return expected when the deposit was opened.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Could measured inflation fall while the price level rises again?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Yes. If this year's inflation falls from 4% to 2%, the price level still rises by a factor of 1.02. Lower inflation slows the loss of purchasing power on a fixed nominal balance; reversing the earlier price increase would require falling prices.
