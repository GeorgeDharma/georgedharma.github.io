Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A factory economy and its creditors

Annual GDP is 500. Private and government consumption are 325 and 85. Domestic investment is 108. Net primary income is −6 and net current transfers are +2. The capital account and statistical discrepancy are zero. Foreign liabilities rise through transactions by 30. Opening NIIP is −75 and net valuation gains are 9.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A harvest, a repayment and a currency squeeze

A cooperative normally exports 40 units at $2 and imports 20 tools at $4. A storm cuts exports to 30 units while export price rises to $2.40 and tool price to $4.40. Import quantity remains 20. A separate $10 loan and $1 interest are due today. Usable dollar reserves are $18. All trade payments settle today.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Inflation and pressure on a peg

A currency is pegged at 4 local units per dollar. Comparable risk-free one-year deposits pay 2% locally and 5% in dollars. Residents may transfer freely and believe the peg holds. Each month dollar demand exceeds private supply by $8m. Freely usable reserves start at $35m and the central bank keeps a $3m buffer. A domestic price index rose from 110 to 115.5.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Compare two external adjustments

Country R's current-account balances are −10 on GDP 250 before a shock and −2.5 on GDP 250 after. Country T's are +2 on GDP 200 before and −4 on GDP 200 after. R exports copper, T imports it, and copper prices rose. R's domestic investment also fell from 52 to 45 while GDP stays fixed.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A factory economy and its creditors

Part A 4 marks

Calculate disposable income, national saving and the current account. Verify it using net exports.

Disposable income is 500 − 6 + 2 = 496. National saving is 496 − 325 − 85 = 86. The current account is 86 − 108 = −22. Net exports are 500 − 325 − 85 − 108 = −18. Adding −6 + 2 again gives −22.

Part B 4 marks

Use assets minus liabilities to infer net external asset acquisitions and the ending NIIP.

If assets acquired minus 30 equals −22, residents acquired 8 in external assets. Ending NIIP is −75 − 22 + 9 = −88.

Part C 2 marks

Can this year's deficit establish that investment was unproductive? Explain.

No. Some investment could generate future export revenue sufficient to service its financing. Assess incremental future cash flows and payment timing. The accounts alone show the financing balance.

Case 2 · 10 marks

A harvest, a repayment and a currency squeeze

Part A 4 marks

Compute the change in terms of trade and in export purchasing power measured in tools.

The relative price goes from 2/4 = 0.50 to 2.40/4.40 = 0.54545, up 9.09%. Exports buy 80/4 = 20 tools initially and 72/4.40 = 16.36 later. Lower volume offsets the price improvement.

Part B 3 marks

Find the dollar funding gap after trade, debt service and reserves.

Exports supply $72, with $18 reserves bringing sources to $90. Imports require $88 and principal and interest $11, bringing uses to $99. The immediate gap is $9.

Part C 3 marks

A bridge lender offers $9 at 8% for one year. What income eventually needs to appear, and why does a bridge solve only part of the problem?

The bridge supplies today's $9. It requires $9 × 1.08 = $9.72 next year. The cooperative needs dated net dollar receipts or another financing source after other commitments. Future resources, rather than access to a single bridge, determine solvency.

Case 3 · 10 marks

Inflation and pressure on a peg

Part A 4 marks

Compare the end value in local currency of investing 400 locally or abroad and describe the pressure.

The local deposit yields 408. The dollar route buys $100, grows to $105 and converts to 420. Its 12-unit advantage encourages outward transfers and creates pressure for foreign currency at the peg.

Part B 3 marks

How long can the bank maintain the assumed sales above the buffer? Give one adjustment if the pressure persists.

Available reserves are 35 − 3 = $32m. Sales of $8m per month exhaust the amount above the buffer after four months. A local rate rise, a change in the exchange regime or a restriction on capital movement addresses a different margin.

Part C 3 marks

Calculate inflation and the realized real return on the local deposit if that price growth applies over the deposit year.

Inflation is 115.5/110 − 1 = 5%. The real return is 1.02/1.05 − 1 = −2.86%. The depositor earns more nominal units with lower purchasing power.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Compare two external adjustments

Part A 4 marks

Compute the level and change in each current-account share and the difference between their changes.

R moves from −4% to −1%, an improvement of 3 percentage points. T moves from +1% to −2%, a deterioration of 3. R's relative improvement is 3 − (−3) = 6 points.

Part B 3 marks

Use R's investment and current-account flows to infer saving in both years. Explain how the adjustment occurred arithmetically.

Saving equals investment plus the current account. R saves 52 − 10 = 42 before and 45 − 2.5 = 42.5 after. Saving rises 0.5 while investment falls 7, together improving the current account by 7.5.

Part C 3 marks

Propose evidence needed to connect these changes causally to the copper price.

Compare pre-shock patterns and net copper exposure. Break changes into export and import price and quantity effects and examine other investment or policy shocks. The two-country difference and accounting identity do not isolate the cause.

  1. C01.11 · Dornbusch overshooting after a monetary surprise

    A different economy starts at 2.00 home units per foreign unit. After a surprise its expected year-end spot is 2.10, domestic interest 3% and foreign interest 6%. Derive the impact quote and the expected move after impact.

  2. C01.12 · Optimum currency areas and adjustment to asymmetric shocks

    Two equal regions produce 72 and 96 after previously producing 90 each. A balanced arrangement insures 75% of each deviation from the new regional mean. Compute transfers and assess the remaining loss.

  3. C01.13 · Trade-weighted nominal and real effective exchange rates

    Two partners have equal weights. Home currency buys 8% more of partner A and 2% less of B. Domestic prices rise 3%, A 1% and B 5%. Compute the nominal and real effective index from 100.

  4. C01.14 · Foreign direct investment, entry modes and the external accounts

    A foreign manufacturer wants local production of a proprietary component. Existing plants cannot accommodate its process, and delivery can wait for construction. It invests €30m on January 1 in 75% of a new host company. A local partner invests €10m for 25%. Annual eligible earnings are €8m and ordinary dividends €2m. Currency translation reduces the foreign investor's closing position by €1.5m. Separately an unrelated foreign pension fund buys 7% of another resident firm's voting shares for €4m. No other changes occur. Classify both investments and compute income, the FDI flow and the closing position.
