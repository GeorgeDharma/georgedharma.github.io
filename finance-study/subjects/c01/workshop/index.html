Professional workshop · C01

Cordela's reserve floor before the bond maturity

On 31 December 2027 the governor of the fictional economy Cordela asks whether the published annual accounts support the claim that a quiet trade year leaves the currency peg adequately funded for the coming quarter. A foreign bond comes due in March. Reconcile the annual accounts with the external balance sheet, then produce a settlement-dated dollar cash assessment under a base case and a failed rollover. The governor must decide how much contingency liquidity to arrange before the payment run.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Country economist on a central-bank financial stability team
What to submit
A reserve-adequacy briefing for the monetary policy and financial stability committee, including a balance-of-payments reconciliation, quarterly foreign-currency cash ladder, intervention trigger and a distinct corporate currency-mismatch observation.

From inputs to decision

01

Reconstruct the national accounting story

The governor has been shown an export total in isolation. The external financing position also reflects imports, investment and income received or paid abroad. Begin by reproducing the result from two independent accounting routes.

  1. From expenditure GDP, net exports are 200 − 130 − 38 − 40 = −8. The merchandise and services data produce the same 57 − 65 = −8. Add net primary income of −3 and current transfers of +1 to derive a current account of −10.
  2. National disposable income is 200 − 3 + 1 = 198. National saving is 198 − 130 − 38 = 30. Subtract total investment of 40 to recover the same −10, or −5.0% of GDP.

Sign off the annual current-account flow of −$10bn. Neither national identity determines the maturity of any specific liability.

02

Explain financing of the annual deficit

An external deficit can be funded through several combinations of new foreign liabilities, reductions in assets or reserve use. The committee wants to distinguish the private financial transactions from official intervention.

  1. Use the asset-acquisition-minus-liabilities convention. Before reserves, financial account flow is +4 − 11 = −7. Let the annual change in reserve assets be x.
  2. The accounts require −10 = 4 + x − 11. Solve x = −3. Gross reserves fall from 24 to 21 with no reserve valuation effect in the case.

The financing partner is $7bn of net private inflow and a $3bn reduction in official assets. Do not add the full $11bn to the reserve stock because residents also acquire assets.

03

Reconcile the national stock and isolate usable cash

Net-worth gains on assets can coexist with settlement pressure. The governor should see why the end-of-year net external position and reserves convey different information.

  1. Opening NIIP of −46 plus financial transactions of −10 plus the supplied net valuation gain of 4 gives closing NIIP of −52, or −26% of GDP. The valuation gain is a change to the value of existing external claims.
  2. Gross reserve assets are 21, of which 2 are pledged. Freely usable opening reserves for Q1 are 19. Only 19 − 15 = 4 is available above the committee floor.

Carry 19 into the quarterly cash ladder. A valuation gain across the broader portfolio gives no authority to assume four additional dollars of usable reserves.

04

Separate gross dollar settlements from national saving

The March bond needs dollars on its due date regardless of the annual current account. Establish the quarterly uses before comparing uncertain receipts. The stated timing means a quarterly aggregation is permissible for this exercise.

  1. Uses are 12 of import settlements, 1 interest and 7 principal, a total of 20. The principal redemption is a financial outflow and belongs in the cash ladder even though it is outside the goods and current-income balance.
  2. In base, nonreserve sources are 9 + 1 + 5 = 15. The residual draw is 20 − 15 = 5. Ending freely usable reserves before new action are 19 − 5 = 14, one below the floor.

Base already requires at least a $1bn line draw or an equivalent verified change to receipts or payments.

05

Move the rollover and exports to stress

Rollover uncertainty and slower collections are separate risks. Neither can be repaired by quoting last year's reserve coverage ratio. Identify each source's contribution to the shortfall.

  1. Stress sources are 8 + 1 + 2 = 11. The reserve draw is 20 − 11 = 9. Closing freely usable reserves fall to 19 − 9 = 10, or 5 below the floor.
  2. The difference from base equals 1 of missed export collections plus 3 of lost refinancing. Securing a $5bn eligible line draw adds 5 to available sources and restores ending usable reserves to 15.

Reserve the ability to draw $5bn before settlement and plan an initial base draw of $1bn if realized sources follow the base schedule. Track the two triggers separately.

06

Explain the currency-policy and private burden

A committee member suggests floating the currency to remove the dollar gap. Address both the immediate contract and the longer-term policy choices with quantified balance-sheet effects.

  1. At the 5-unit peg, the $8bn unhedged corporate debt is equivalent to 40bn home units. At 6 home per dollar it becomes 48bn, an 8bn increase. The contract is still for $8bn in both cases.
  2. A float may eventually alter import demand, prices and private financing. It cannot retrospectively change March's contracted dollar principal or produce dollars before maturity. A permanent peg and independent domestic rate also face the open-capital-market trilemma.

Send the reserve decision to Treasury and request separate monitoring of corporate borrowers without matching export receipts. The national net position hides this specific distribution of risk.

07

Write a dated intervention and monitoring trigger

The committee needs a rule its dealers can apply as confirmations arrive. Set the threshold on usable dollars and specify what happens if a source misses its value date.

  1. Let S be confirmed nonreserve Q1 sources excluding the official line. Required line draw is max(0, 20 − S − 4), rounded up to the next $0.5bn. The 4 represents available reserves above the floor. Confirm the model reproduces 1 at S = 15 and 5 at S = 11.
  2. With line capacity capped at 6, the floor is achievable from the stated instruments only if S is at least 10. Below that, secure further funding, negotiate a dated rollover or reschedule eligible payments before the deadline.

Assign the reserve desk to verify settlement dates weekly and immediately alert the committee if forecast sources fall below 11 or a pledged collateral balance increases. Repeat the exercise daily near maturity.

Completed document

Governor's decision note · external financing through March

Decision

Secure a $5bn committed contingency line with value date ahead of the March settlement. Plan a $1bn base draw and scale the draw as confirmed sources change. The full $5bn stress draw meets the floor exactly.

Evidence

The 2027 current account was −$10bn or −5% of GDP. A $3bn reserve draw helped finance it. The end-2027 net external position is −$52bn, but freely usable reserves are $19bn after pledged assets. The quarterly assessment begins with only $4bn above the policy floor.

Downside and limits

A rollover shortfall of $3bn and a collection shortfall of $1bn reduce base coverage by $4bn. With the line drawn in stress, there is no residual headroom above the floor. If confirmed nonreserve sources fall below $10bn, the $6bn maximum line cannot maintain that floor alone.

Implementation

Treasury must confirm legal access, irrevocable value dates, costs and the future repayment schedule before counting a draw as usable. The reserve desk will track gross maturities and pledged assets. Supervision will separately scrutinize the sector's $8bn unhedged dollar debt.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

Stress collections fall a further $0.6bn with all other assumptions unchanged. In $0.5bn increments, what line draw preserves the floor?

See the full solution

Confirmed nonreserve sources fall from 11 to 10.4. Raw draw need is 20 − 10.4 − 4 = 5.6. Round up to $6bn. Ending usable reserves become 19 + 10.4 + 6 − 20 = 15.4.

Task 2

If another $1.5bn of reserves becomes pledged just before the original stress, how much below the $15bn floor would the country finish after drawing the full $6bn line?

See the full solution

Opening usable reserves fall from 19 to 17.5. Ending reserves are 17.5 + 11 + 6 − 20 = 14.5. The shortfall is 0.5.

Task 3

What is the local-currency increase on the $8bn of unhedged debt if the peg of 5 moves to 5.75 home units per dollar?

See the full solution

Opening local equivalent is 8 × 5 = 40bn. Revised amount is 8 × 5.75 = 46bn. The increase is 6bn home units, assuming fixed dollar principal and no matching hedge.

Task 4

Would you include a commercial bank's forecast $2bn overseas asset sale when advising the governor tomorrow?

See the full solution

Only when there is evidence of sale price, settlement by the required date, legal access and no competing collateral claim. Show it as a separate scenario before those conditions hold. Distinguish the bank's asset from sovereign reserves and identify how the dollars reach the paying entities.
