Populations and samples

Define the population that a claim concerns. A sample of current companies can omit failed firms and exaggerate historical performance.

The starting point

Suppose you want to estimate how a typical new company performs over its first five years. Contacting today’s successful companies is easy, but businesses that closed early belong to the original group too. Omitting them can make outcomes look unduly strong.

A population is the full group addressed by a question. A sample is the subset observed. Define the membership date, outcome and observation period before looking at results. Survivorship bias arises when the ability to appear in the data depends on survival.

Theory and method

Inverse probability weighting

Sampling weight · Horvitz–Thompson total

A regulator sampled half of large lenders and one tenth of small lenders. How can you estimate total lending without letting the heavily sampled firms dominate?

A survey is designed to observe every second large firm but only every tenth small one. A sampled small firm therefore represents a larger number of firms in the register. Giving both observations equal influence would describe the survey design rather than the actual lending market. Weight each observed amount by the inverse of its chance of selection. The resulting contributions estimate the total that a complete census would have measured.

How the result is built

Estimated population total T̂ = Σᵢ yᵢ/πᵢ; mean = T̂/N when population size N is known.

yᵢ is sampled lender i’s lending in euros, πᵢ its inclusion probability and N the number of lenders. Each selected lender contributes its observed amount times its design weight 1/πᵢ.

  1. There are 20 large and 100 small lenders. Observe 10 large at a mean €8m and 10 small at a mean €2m. The inclusion rates are 0.50 and 0.10.
  2. The observed €80m large-book total receives weight 2 and the €20m small-book total receives weight 10. The estimated market total is €160m + €200m = €360m.
  3. Divide by 120 firms to estimate €3m per lender. The simple 20-observation mean is €5m and overrepresents the large-lender group.

When the reasoning holds

  • Inclusion probabilities must be positive and known for all eligible units. A class of unregistered lenders with zero chance of selection cannot be recovered by these weights.
  • The observed firms must follow the stated selection and response design. Selective refusals within a class require response adjustments or sensitivity analysis.
Terms in this lesson
Standard error
An estimate of how much a statistic would vary across repeated samples under the assumed sampling process.
First example

A starting group contains eight funds. Six survive, each returning 10% over the period. Two close after losing 50% each. The equal-weight average for all eight is [6 × 10% + 2 × (−50%)] / 8 = −5%. The survivor-only average is +10%.

Define the quantity before collecting data

A fund manager asks about the average return earned by investors in start-up funds. That sentence leaves open whether each investor, euro invested or fund gets one vote. It also leaves out the entry date and what happens when a fund closes. The estimand is the precise population quantity the study tries to learn.

A sampling frame is the practical list from which observations can be selected. A register of currently open funds may be complete for that date yet unsuitable for estimating historical launch-cohort outcomes. Define eligibility as of the relevant start date and specify a terminal value for merged, liquidated or otherwise missing funds where evidence supports it.

Sampling design changes interpretation

A simple random sample gives every eligible unit the same chance of selection under the specified design. A stratified design samples separately within groups. If groups are represented in the sample at different rates from the population, use population shares to combine group means when estimating a population mean.

A large convenience sample can give a very precise estimate of the wrong population. Respondents who voluntarily report performance may differ systematically from nonrespondents. Random-sampling standard errors capture variation from the sampling process under assumptions. They do not fix an unrepresentative frame or outcome-dependent nonresponse.

Survival, missing endings and bounds

A failed business can disappear from a commercial database before the analyst downloads the list. Excluding it is defensible for a claim explicitly about surviving businesses but can distort a claim about all original businesses. Obtain closure records or verified terminal proceeds before defining what loss corresponds to closure.

When an outcome genuinely remains unknown, show a sensitivity range. If every missing unit has a plausible bounded outcome, calculate the target at the lower and upper bounds. This shows how strong a conclusion can be without inventing exact missing observations. A wide bound is information about what the sample cannot establish.

Method

Design the comparison

  1. Specify population and unit

    Name the target group, start date, outcome horizon and whether weights represent firms, owners or invested amounts.

  2. Inspect inclusion

    Compare the practical list with the target. Trace exclusions, closures and nonresponse and explain how these could affect outcomes.

  3. Estimate or bound

    Apply design weights where available and report sensitivity to genuinely missing outcomes. Keep selection uncertainty distinct from sampling error.

Worked case

A business register with two strata

A city has 800 small businesses and 200 large ones. A survey deliberately interviews 40 from each group. Within groups assume random sampling. Their estimated mean yearly investment is €4,000 and €30,000 respectively.

Working

  1. The unweighted survey mean is (€4,000 + €30,000)/2 = €17,000 because interviews are split equally.
  2. The population has shares 0.8 and 0.2. Estimated mean investment per business is 0.8 × €4,000 + 0.2 × €30,000 = €9,200.
  3. The implied estimate of total investment for 1,000 businesses is €9.2m. A survey targeting euros invested rather than businesses would address a different question.

Interpretation

If all 40 small firms came from a voluntary high-growth event, the 80% weight corrects representation between strata but cannot make those firms representative within their own stratum.

A common mistake

A large sample count is taken as proof that the estimate covers failed or unregistered businesses.

How to correct it

Read the sampling frame and selection rules. A thousand observations drawn exclusively from survivors still omit every other type of business.

Quick check
Which sampling rule best addresses this comparison?

An original-date list preserves membership independently of later success. You still need a defensible final return for each closing fund.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Of 100 funds existing on a launch date, 90 surviving funds return an average 8%. Ten closed funds have unknown returns between −100% and 0% for the same horizon. Use equal starting weights.

  1. Part A · 5 marks

    Find lower and upper bounds for the original cohort’s mean return.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Can the survivor mean establish that the original cohort had a positive return? Describe evidence that would narrow the conclusion.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 5 marks

At the lower bound use 0.90 × 8% + 0.10 × (−100%) = −2.8%. At the upper bound use 0.90 × 8% + 0.10 × 0% = 7.2%.

Part B 3 marks

No. The interval crosses zero. Verified liquidation proceeds and distributions for closed funds would narrow or identify their total returns. Return calculations should cover the same holding period and cash-flow basis.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A regulator sampled half of large lenders and one tenth of small lenders. How can you estimate total lending without letting the heavily sampled firms dominate?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would use each lender’s recorded inclusion probability. Large firms get weight two and small firms weight ten. The estimated market total is €360m, or €3m per lender on a known base of 120. I would check whether the selected lenders actually responded and whether the target includes institutions missing from the register because weighting can only represent units the design could reach.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The two smallest observed lenders now refuse to disclose their amounts. Is retaining weight ten automatically sufficient?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. The selection probability is still one tenth but the probability of providing a usable response has changed. I would examine a defensible response model or obtain the missing amounts. If smaller books systematically decline, applying the original weight to the larger responders can bias the market estimate upward.
