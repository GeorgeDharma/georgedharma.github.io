Use the observed cases to estimate sampling uncertainty

Build bootstrap replicates from units sampled with replacement and distinguish a replicate from the original estimate.

Theory and terminology

The nonparametric bootstrap

percentile interval · block bootstrap · resampling

What you should be able to explain

  • Build bootstrap replicates from units sampled with replacement and distinguish a replicate from the original estimate.
  • Read an empirical percentile interval and identify cases where resampling individual rows is inadequate.

Construct an empirical population

An analyst wants uncertainty for a statistic such as mean cost, a median or a ratio. A formula for its sampling variation may be inconvenient, even when the data were gathered by a defensible sampling design. A nonparametric bootstrap gives each observed unit its sample weight, then draws again from these empirical units.

Use the original sample size and allow replacement after each draw. Some observations appear repeatedly while others are absent. Drawing the original number without replacement merely rearranges the same records and gives the same mean every time. The variation between replicates is the desired signal.

Read uncertainty from the replicate distribution

Calculate the same statistic from each full replicate. Its standard deviation estimates a standard error. A percentile interval takes two empirical quantiles of the replicate statistics. The quantile convention matters for a tiny distribution; here the p quantile is the first value whose cumulative probability reaches p.

An interval describes the precision of an estimate across samples under the stated design. It is different from a prediction interval for the next individual cost. Percentile intervals can perform poorly for biased or highly skewed statistics, boundaries or very small samples. No number of simulated replicates compensates for a sample that omitted a rare catastrophe.

Resample the unit that could be sampled again

When accounts are sampled independently but each has six monthly records, resample whole accounts, carrying their months together. For paired before-and-after results, keep both observations of each pair together. For serially dependent returns, a time-series approach can sample blocks; the length must reflect dependence and the available history.

State the target when cluster sizes vary. Averaging every transaction estimates a transaction-weighted quantity, while averaging client means estimates a client-weighted quantity. Preserve or deliberately reconstruct those weights in every replicate. The bootstrap does not repair an unrepresentative sample, missing outcomes or a recording change.

Method

Build the result

SEboot = standard deviation of replicate estimates. Percentile interval = [q(α/2), q(1 − α/2)].

A replicate is one complete resampled dataset. q(p) is the p quantile of its statistic's empirical distribution. α is the intended total tail fraction. Standard error and interval endpoints use the statistic's units.

  1. Identify the independent sampling unit, the target statistic and how to recompute any weights or ratios.
  2. Sample that many units with replacement, recompute the statistic and repeat, or enumerate all ordered samples when tiny.
  3. Sort the replicate values, read the chosen percentiles and report the original estimate alongside the interval and its assumptions.
Worked case

An exact miniature bootstrap for service credits

To see every possibility, use three independently sampled case credits of €0, €3 and €6. Resample three values with replacement, each original value equally likely on each draw. Use a central 80% percentile interval for illustration only.

Working

  1. The observed mean is (0 + 3 + 6)/3 = €3. There are 3³ = 27 equally likely ordered resamples.
  2. A draw of (0,3,3) gives a €2 mean. Its different orderings also count, as does (0,0,6). Count all orderings for each possible mean.
  3. For means €0 through €6 the counts are 1, 3, 6, 7, 6, 3 and 1. The cumulative fraction reaches 0.10 at €1 and 0.90 at €5.
  4. The variance of a single empirical draw is [(0 − 3)² + (3 − 3)² + (6 − 3)²]/3 = 6 squared euros. The three independent draws in a replicate mean reduce this variance to 6/3 = 2 squared euros. Its bootstrap standard error is therefore √2 = €1.41.

Decision and interpretation

The miniature interval is €1 to €5. Its arithmetic illustrates the method. Three observations give little evidence about real-life tail behavior.

Quick check
A sample of 60 independent account IDs includes four transactions per account. For client-level uncertainty around the mean of account totals, what should one bootstrap replicate draw?

The independent sampling unit is the client account. Resampling that unit preserves dependence among its four transactions.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A study sampled 100 independent customers, each observed both before and after a billing change. Their average reduction in calls was 0.8. In 1,000 sorted paired-customer bootstrap replicates the 25th estimate was 0.15 and the 975th was 1.42. Use the stated cumulative-rank convention to report an interval and assess an analyst's plan to sample all 200 measurements separately.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Resample 100 customers with replacement, keeping the two readings together. Recalculate each customer's reduction and its mean in every replicate.
  2. The percentile 95% interval uses ranks 25 and 975 of 1,000, giving 0.15 to 1.42 fewer calls per customer, with original estimate 0.8.
  3. Independent sampling of 200 measurements breaks within-customer pairing and answers a different sampling question. An interval for a before-and-after change alone does not identify which other events caused it.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

What can one sample reveal about how much an estimate would change if the sampling were repeated?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Treat its observed independent units as a stand-in for the population. Repeatedly sample the same number of units with replacement and recompute the estimate. The spread approximates sampling variation when the sample and resampling design are appropriate.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If regulatory reporting requires an estimate of catastrophic claims absent from the dataset, would a million more bootstrap draws help?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Additional draws describe the observed empirical population more precisely. They cannot create an unseen severity level. The risk estimate needs additional relevant loss evidence or an explicit and defensible tail model.
