Expectation and variance

Expected value averages possible outcomes using probabilities. Variance measures squared departures from that average and penalizes large moves more heavily.

The starting point

Two investments can have the same average payoff while producing very different outcomes. Begin with the possible payoffs and how likely each is. Their probability-weighted average is the expected value.

Variance adds the squared distances from the expected value, weighted by probability. Squaring prevents gains and losses around the mean from cancelling each other. Standard deviation is the square root of variance, restoring the original units. Neither measure by itself identifies the worst possible outcome.

Theory and method

Law of total variance

Within-group variance · Between-group variance

Two merchant risk bands fluctuate very little internally. Why can the combined distribution still show large variability?

An insurer places merchants into quiet and volatile trading regions. Even if each region’s loss is fairly predictable around its own average, switching between a low-average and high-average region creates another source of uncertainty. First average the variability remaining within each group, then add the variability of the group means. This distinction helps decide whether collecting better data inside each region or improving classification across regions would reduce uncertainty most.

How the result is built

Var(L) = E[Var(L given G)] + Var(E[L given G]).

L is the euro loss and G identifies the group. E means probability-weighted average, Var is squared dispersion, and conditional means and variances are measured within the named group. Units of variance are squared euros.

  1. Two equally likely groups have mean losses €10 and €30, both with within-group variance 4 square euros. The portfolio-wide mean is €20.
  2. The average within-group variance remains 4. The two means deviate from €20 by −€10 and +€10, so their between-group variance is 100.
  3. Adding the two components gives 104 square euros and overall standard deviation about €10.20. The cross term vanishes because deviations around each group’s own mean average zero within that group.

When the reasoning holds

  • The groups form a complete, mutually exclusive partition with correct weights. Overlapping risk labels can double count exposures.
  • The within-band numbers must genuinely be variances. Averaging reported standard deviations and adding them to the spread of means mixes units.

How to read the notation
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

An investment pays €0 or €20 with equal probability. Expected payoff is €10. Variance is 0.5 × (0 − 10)² + 0.5 × (20 − 10)² = 100 square euros, giving a standard deviation of €10.

Build the distribution of money outcomes

An insurer can list which payouts might occur and attach probabilities. Expected value is the weighted total, a long-run average under repeated comparable risks. A payout equal to the expectation need never occur. A policy paying either zero or €1,000 can have a mean of €100 even though nobody actually receives €100.

Expectations add across exposures even when the exposures are dependent. If customer A’s expected loss is €8 and customer B’s €12, the total expected loss is €20. Dependence changes how losses cluster, and therefore changes variability and severe-tail outcomes, even when those individual expectations are fixed.

Calculate variance two ways

Variance is the expected squared difference between the outcome and its mean. A convenient equivalent calculation subtracts the square of the mean from the expected square of the outcome. Keeping both as separate lines helps avoid confusing the mean square with the squared mean. The standard deviation is the square root of the variance.

If every outcome increases by a fixed €5, both the mean and every outcome shift equally. Deviations from the mean remain the same, so variance does not change. Multiplying every outcome by three multiplies each deviation by three and its square by nine. Standard deviation triples while variance increases ninefold.

Risk measures depend on the use

Squared deviations treat unusually large gains and losses symmetrically. A firm with a hard minimum cash requirement may care specifically about the probability of falling below it and the size of shortfalls. Two projects with equal mean and variance can still have different downside profiles. Inspect the actual states when a covenant or funding deadline matters.

A theoretical probability variance uses the supplied probabilities. For an observed random sample used to estimate population variance, a common unbiased estimator of population variance under independent identically distributed sampling divides the sum of squared sample deviations by n−1. Be clear whether probabilities are fully specified or estimated from a sample.

Method

Compute and test an expectation

  1. List weights

    Assign each possible payoff its probability. Check all probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one.

  2. Center and square

    Find the mean, subtract it from each payoff, square each difference, and average with its probability. Check using the mean-square identity.

  3. Translate back

    Take the square root for units of money and compare with an outcome-specific measure such as shortfall probability.

Worked case

A producer’s variable royalty

A publisher pays €0, €20 or €60 according to sales, with probabilities 0.50, 0.30 and 0.20. The producer pays a fixed €5 administration fee regardless of sales.

Working

  1. Expected gross royalty = 0 + €6 + €12 = €18. Expected square = 0.30 × 400 + 0.20 × 3,600 = 840 square euros.
  2. Variance = 840 − 18² = 516 square euros. Standard deviation ≈ €22.72. After the fixed fee the mean net royalty is €13 and this standard deviation is unchanged.
  3. A negative net outcome occurs in the €0 gross state, with probability 50%. Its net amount is −€5. Neither the mean nor standard deviation alone displays this cash shortfall.

Interpretation

A fee charged as a percentage of royalties would instead scale both the mean and the standard deviation. Contract terms determine which transformation is appropriate.

A common mistake

An analyst claims a project with higher expected cash is automatically safe for a €10 payment due that day.

How to correct it

Calculate the probability and size of failing to meet the obligation directly from the state distribution. A large rare gain can raise a mean without helping in the most frequent state.

Quick check
A second claim pays €10 in every outcome. What are its expected payoff and standard deviation?

The second claim has the same expected payoff. All of its outcomes equal the mean, so every squared deviation is zero.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A claim pays €10 with probability 0.75 and €50 with probability 0.25. A contract instead offers twice this payoff less a fixed €8.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compute the original mean, variance and standard deviation.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Calculate the transformed mean and variance, and the probability its payoff is below €20.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Mean is €20. Variance is 0.75 × 10² + 0.25 × 30² = 300 square euros. Standard deviation is √300 ≈ €17.32.

Part B 4 marks

The new mean is 2 × €20 − €8 = €32. Variance is 2² × 300 = 1,200 square euros. New outcomes are €12 and €92, so probability below €20 is 0.75.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two merchant risk bands fluctuate very little internally. Why can the combined distribution still show large variability?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would separate uncertainty within a known risk band from uncertainty about which band a client belongs to. The model gives only four square euros of average within-band variance but 100 from different group averages. The total is 104. Observing the client’s true group could therefore eliminate most of the predictive uncertainty in this illustration, although €2 standard deviation persists within either band.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If both groups instead had the same €20 mean but their variances were 4 and 36 with equal weights, what would the total be?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The between-group component would be zero because both conditional means are €20. Average conditional variance would be (4 + 36)/2 = 20 square euros, giving a standard deviation of about €4.47. Group information would still identify different individual risk levels even though it would not change the expected loss.
