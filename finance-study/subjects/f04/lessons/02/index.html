Distributions

A distribution describes both possible outcomes and their probabilities. Mean, median and quantiles answer different questions about its centre and tails.

The starting point

A lender wants to know both the typical late fee and the size of an unusually bad month. One summary cannot answer both questions. A distribution identifies which outcomes are possible and how frequently or probably they occur.

The mean weights all outcome sizes by their frequency. The median is the middle ranked observation, and a quantile locates a chosen fraction of the distribution. An unusually large observation can pull the mean far above what most months experience.

Theory and method

Value at risk and expected shortfall in a discrete distribution

Loss quantile · Tail mean

Two portfolios have the same 95% loss threshold. How would you distinguish the severity of the worst five percent of their outcomes?

A risk manager needs both the border of a bad region and information about losses deeper inside it. Sort scenarios by the size of the loss and find the first loss whose cumulative probability reaches 95%. That threshold is value at risk. For expected shortfall, average the worst 5% of probability mass. When many scenarios tie at the threshold, include just enough of that mass to fill five percent. This keeps the interpretation consistent in a discrete distribution.

How the result is built

VaRₐ = smallest loss x with Pr(L≤x)≥a. ESₐ = average loss in the worst 1−a probability mass.

L is a positive loss in euros, Pr denotes probability and a is the confidence level. VaR is a quantile, ES expected shortfall and boundary mass refers to scenarios tied at the cutoff.

  1. Take losses €0m with 90%, €2m with 8%, and €10m with 2%. Cumulative probability reaches 98% at €2m, giving 95% VaR of €2m.
  2. The worst 5% comprises all 2% at €10m and 3 of the 8 percentage points tied at €2m.
  3. ES = (0.02 × €10m + 0.03 × €2m)/0.05 = €5.2m. Simply averaging all losses strictly above €2m would select only 2% of outcomes.

When the reasoning holds

  • Losses cover the same horizon and currency. Comparing a one-day gross P&L with a monthly net loss threshold gives a different risk measure.
  • Use probability mass to average the tail when tied observations straddle the cutoff. A routine that averages every observation above or at the cutoff can give a different quantity.
First example

Five monthly fees in euros are 0, 0, 10, 20 and 70. The sample mean is €100 / 5 = €20. The median is €10, the third observation after sorting. The €70 month affects the mean more strongly than it affects the middle rank.

Describe the random quantity

A record of late-payment days can be viewed as a list of observed values or as a model of future outcomes. An empirical distribution gives each observed item a weight, often 1/n. A theoretical probability distribution assigns weights according to a specified model. State which one you are using before interpreting frequency as a long-run probability.

For a discrete variable the probabilities at all possible values sum to one. A cumulative distribution gives the probability of an outcome at or below a threshold. It answers whether a loss limit is exceeded and supports quantiles. For an ordered value x, P(X > x) = 1 − P(X ≤ x). Including equality matters when outcomes cluster at the limit.

Center, dispersion and tail shape

The mean reflects every observation’s size and can be pulled by a long tail. The median identifies a central position and resists one extreme replacement better. Two portfolios can share a median and have completely different average losses if one occasionally suffers exceptionally severe damage.

The interquartile range is the difference between the 75th and 25th percentiles and emphasizes the central half of the distribution. A quantile locates an outcome threshold and says little about severity beyond it. Specify the sample quantile convention when the dataset is small because interpolation rules can differ across software.

Compare loss distributions sensibly

If losses are reported as positive numbers, a higher upper loss quantile means a more severe threshold. If returns are signed gains and losses, the bad tail is typically the lower return tail. Write down which convention the exercise uses before reading a chart. Otherwise conclusions can reverse simply because a minus sign was hidden.

One useful comparison asks for expected loss conditional on crossing a specified threshold. This averages only the relevant tail outcomes, using probabilities rescaled within the tail. At a quantile with many tied outcomes, say whether the condition is strictly above or at or above the cutoff. These definitions can yield quite different numbers.

Method

Construct a distribution table

  1. Sort and weight

    List distinct outcomes from smallest to largest with their probability or sample frequency. Check the total probability is one.

  2. Accumulate

    Compute cumulative probabilities in order and find the first outcome whose cumulative probability reaches the target probability when using this discrete quantile convention.

  3. Inspect severity

    Calculate the mean and a tail conditional mean if requested. Relate each statistic to the business question it can answer.

Worked case

Batch defects in a warehouse

The number of defective units in a shipment is 0 with probability 0.70, 1 with probability 0.20, 3 with probability 0.08 and 10 with probability 0.02.

Working

  1. Expected defects are 0 + 0.20 + 0.24 + 0.20 = 0.64. The discrete median is zero because cumulative probability already reaches 0.70 at zero.
  2. Cumulative probabilities at 1, 3 and 10 are 0.90, 0.98 and 1.00. The 95th percentile, first value reaching 95%, is 3.
  3. Given at least three defects, the probability mass is 0.10. The conditional mean is (3 × 0.08 + 10 × 0.02)/0.10 = 4.4 defects.

Interpretation

A manager choosing routine inspection staffing may care about the median. A manager planning emergency capacity should also consider the tail sizes and costs.

A common mistake

A 95th percentile is interpreted as the largest conceivable loss.

How to correct it

Check the cumulative distribution beyond the threshold. There can be much larger outcomes with smaller probabilities.

Quick check
If the largest fee changes from €70 to €170, what happens to these summaries?

The replacement adds €100 across five months, raising the mean by €20. The third observation remains €10.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A loan pool loses €0, €2m or €12m with probabilities 0.80, 0.15 and 0.05. Use the smallest value with cumulative probability at least q as the q-quantile.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate expected loss, the median and the 95th percentile.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Find the mean loss conditional on any positive loss and explain why it differs from the 95th percentile.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Mean loss = 0.15 × €2m + 0.05 × €12m = €0.9m. Median is zero. Cumulative probability reaches 0.95 at €2m, so the 95th percentile is €2m.

Part B 4 marks

The positive tail has probability 0.20. Its conditional mean is €0.9m/0.20 = €4.5m. This average includes rare €12m outcomes. A quantile identifies a boundary rather than averaging their size.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two portfolios have the same 95% loss threshold. How would you distinguish the severity of the worst five percent of their outcomes?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would report both numbers and the tail convention. In this example 95% loss VaR is €2m, but expected shortfall is €5.2m because a 2% group loses €10m. We take just enough of the €2m boundary scenarios to fill the worst five percent. The resulting mean tells the committee how costly the tail is on average at that probability level.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the rare loss rises to €20m while its probability stays 2%, which statistic changes?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The 95% threshold stays at €2m because its cumulative probability is still 98%. Expected shortfall rises to (0.02 × 20 + 0.03 × 2)/0.05 = €9.2m. The unchanged threshold alone would conceal the increased severity.
