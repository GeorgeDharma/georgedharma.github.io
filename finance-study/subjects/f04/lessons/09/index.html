Regression

Fit a line by minimizing squared errors. Interpret slope, intercept and residual in the units of the underlying data.

  1. Omitted variable bias in linear regression

The starting point

A shop wants to describe how weekly sales move with customer visits. Plot visits on the horizontal axis and sales on the vertical axis. A straight line summarizes how much the fitted sales figure changes for each additional visit.

Ordinary least squares chooses the intercept and slope that minimize the sum of squared vertical differences between observed and fitted outcomes. A residual equals observed minus fitted. The slope describes association in units of outcome per unit of predictor, subject to the range represented by the data.

Theory and method

Omitted variable bias in linear regression

Partial regression coefficient · Confounding

Sales rise with advertising in a one-variable regression. How can a holiday indicator change the estimated advertising effect?

A retailer advertises more during holiday weeks when customers would spend more anyway. Regression on advertising alone assigns part of the seasonal boost to the advertising variable because the two move together. Compare the fitted slope with the slope from a model that holds holiday conditions fixed. Algebra separates the genuine modeled advertising term from the holiday effect carried into the short regression by their correlation.

How the result is built

Short-regression slope = β + γδ under the stated linear model.

β is the advertising slope holding season fixed, γ the effect of a one-unit holiday indicator at fixed advertising and δ the slope from regressing that indicator on advertising. Units must make γδ an outcome change per advertising unit.

  1. Start with sales Y = a + βX + γZ + error, where X is advertising measured in €1k and Z is a holiday indicator. Assume the remaining error has zero covariance with X.
  2. The slope regressing Y on X alone equals Cov(X,Y)/Var(X). Substitute the long relation to obtain β + γCov(X,Z)/Var(X), where the ratio is δ.
  3. Suppose β=€2k sales per €1k advertising, γ=€10k holiday uplift and δ=0.10 indicator units per €1k advertising. The short slope becomes €3k per €1k.

When the reasoning holds

  • The identity uses a common sample and consistent variable units. If missing holiday observations change the sample, a slope difference can also reflect the changed people or dates.
  • A causal interpretation still requires suitable identification and no relevant remaining confounding. Including a holiday measure alone does not establish causal advertising returns.

Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Correlation
A measure of how closely two series move together in a linear pattern.
How to read the notation
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.

All notation examples

First example

A fitted line gives weekly sales in euros as 100 + 4 × visits. At 30 visits it predicts €220. If actual sales are €205, the residual is €205 − €220 = −€15. The fitted slope is €4 per additional visit.

Construct a line from observations

A line predicting revenue from transactions has form ŷ = a + bx. Here x is the recorded input, ŷ the fitted outcome, a the intercept and b the change in fitted outcome per unit increase in x. A residual for observation i is yᵢ − ŷᵢ. It describes the observed departure from the fitted line.

Ordinary least squares chooses a and b to minimize the sum of squared residuals. With an intercept and nonconstant x, the fitted line passes through the point given by the sample means. The slope equals the sum of cross-products of centered x and y divided by the sum of squared centered x. Thus unusually distant x values can strongly affect the slope.

What a slope tells an analyst

A slope of €3 per transaction says the fitted revenue difference for two observed transaction counts differing by one is €3, under the estimated linear relationship. It does not establish that forcing another transaction would generate exactly that amount. Transaction size, customer type or season can move both recorded variables.

The intercept is the fitted outcome at x = 0. It can be economically meaningful for a fee structure with a fixed charge, or pure extrapolation if no observation has x near zero. A fitted relationship estimated on small shops may not continue linearly for a nationwide chain. Express both numerical change and the range of data supporting it.

Look beyond a good fit

The coefficient of determination R² measures the proportion of sample variation around the outcome mean explained by the fitted model with an intercept. Compute it as 1 − residual sum of squares / total sum of squares. It is a descriptive measure of in-sample fit, not a causal test or guarantee of future forecasting accuracy.

Inspect residuals by fitted value, time and group. A curved pattern challenges the linear shape. A fan-shaped pattern signals changing variability. A single extreme observation can drive a high correlation. Examine whether its records are erroneous or structurally different before deciding whether exclusion is defensible.

Method

Fit a slope by hand

  1. Center the data

    Calculate the means of x and y, then list deviations for each matched pair of observations.

  2. Calculate the line

    Divide the sum of deviation cross-products by the sum of squared x deviations for b. Find a = mean(y) − b × mean(x).

  3. Inspect the fit

    Compute fitted values and residuals with signs and units. Recheck whether results would involve extrapolation or uncontrolled circumstances.

Worked case

Three service counters

Three counters record x = 1, 2, 3 hundred transactions and y = 3, 4, 8 thousand euros of fees. Fit an intercept and slope by ordinary least squares.

Working

  1. Means are x = 2 and y = 5. Cross-products of centered values are 2, 0 and 3, sum 5. Squared x deviations sum to 2.
  2. Slope b = 5/2 = 2.5 thousand euros per hundred transactions. Intercept a = 5 − 2.5 × 2 = 0.
  3. Fitted fees are 2.5, 5 and 7.5 thousand euros. Residuals are +0.5, −1 and +0.5. Their sum is zero with an intercept.

Interpretation

Squared residuals sum to 1.5. Total squared y deviations sum to 14, so R² ≈ 0.893. Three observations offer limited evidence about stability outside this range.

A common mistake

A coefficient reported per hundred transactions is interpreted as euros per transaction.

How to correct it

Keep both variable scales next to the coefficient. Here €2,500 per 100 transactions equals €25 per transaction, subject to the fitted model.

Quick check
What does a residual of +€12 mean in another week?

The residual subtracts the model’s fitted value from the observed result. A positive residual is an outcome above the line.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Four observations have x values 1, 2, 3, 4 and y values 2, 4, 4, 6, measured in consistent units. Mean x = 2.5 and mean y = 4.

  1. Part A · 5 marks

    Calculate the slope and intercept from centered values.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the residual at x = 3, and explain one limitation of using the model at x = 20.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 5 marks

Centered cross-products sum to 3 + 0 + 0 + 3 = 6. Squared x deviations sum to 2.25 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 2.25 = 5. Slope is 1.2 and intercept 4 − 1.2 × 2.5 = 1.

Part B 3 marks

Fitted y at 3 is 1 + 3.6 = 4.6, so residual is 4 − 4.6 = −0.6. An x of 20 lies far outside 1–4. Shape and operating conditions may differ beyond the observed range.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Sales rise with advertising in a one-variable regression. How can a holiday indicator change the estimated advertising effect?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask whether the higher advertising coincides with stronger demand for another reason. Here the long-model advertising slope is two, while holiday demand contributes another one to the simple regression because more ads run during holidays. The observed slope of three therefore overstates that conditional estimate by one. I would check the sample, timing and remaining confounders before interpreting the revised coefficient as causal.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if the retailer advertises more in normally weak weeks and the holiday indicator is negatively related to advertising, with δ = −0.10 instead?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The bias term becomes €10k × −0.10 = −€1k per advertising unit. The short slope falls from the conditional two to one. Omitted-variable bias can point downward when the omitted factor helps sales yet moves inversely with advertising.
