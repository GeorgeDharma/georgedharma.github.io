Test an intervention by replaying its assignment

Enumerate the assignments permitted by a randomized design under a sharp null.

Theory and terminology

Fisher’s randomization test

sharp null hypothesis · exact inference · permutation

What you should be able to explain

  • Enumerate the assignments permitted by a randomized design under a sharp null.
  • Choose a direction and calculate an exact tail probability, preserving pairing when appropriate.

Replay the assignment, keeping outcomes fixed

Imagine that an intervention changes no unit's outcome at all. This sharp null specifies what every unit would have shown under either assignment. Observed outcomes can therefore be held fixed while labels are reassigned according to the actual experiment. The uncertainty comes from who was selected for treatment.

If two of four branches were drawn uniformly, every two-branch subset is equally likely. If the company instead randomized one branch per region or allocated treatment with unequal probabilities, that simple list no longer describes the experiment. The design determines what counts as a possible comparison.

Choose the statistic and its tail in advance

Let the statistic be mean treated outcome minus mean untreated outcome. A positive value favors the treatment if larger outcomes are desirable. For a directional alternative count values at least as large as the one observed. For the two-sided statistic used here count absolute gaps at least as large, including ties.

With very few random assignments the distribution is discrete. A large observed effect can still have a relatively large exact p-value because the experiment could not have produced many different comparisons. Failing to reject a sharp null with a tiny design says little about how small a financially important effect might be.

Pairing and simulated alternatives

If treatment is randomized within matched pairs, change labels within each pair. Under the sharp null each signed pair difference can flip sign and its magnitude stays fixed. An unrestricted shuffle can destroy balance that was guaranteed by design and compare outcomes across unlike sites.

Exact enumeration works for a small number of assignments. For a larger design sample assignments using its actual probabilities. For B random draws with b as extreme as observed, a common Monte Carlo estimate including the observed assignment is (b + 1)/(B + 1). Record simulation size and seed, because repeating simulated draws need not give the same count.

Method

Build the result

T = mean(treated) − mean(control). For uniform assignments, p = count at least as extreme / number permitted.

T is measured in outcome units per branch. p is the randomization probability under the sharp null. In a two-sided comparison here, extremeness means |T| at least as large as |T observed|.

  1. Freeze outcomes under the sharp null and list each assignment the protocol could have generated.
  2. Calculate T under every assignment, including the one observed. Distinct assignments with equal T values still count separately.
  3. Apply the preselected tail rule and divide the count by the number of equally likely assignments. For unequal assignment probabilities add their probabilities instead.
Worked case

Four branches testing a faster document workflow

Exactly two of branches A, B, C and D were chosen uniformly for the pilot. Reduction in processing hours was 2, 4, 8 and 10 respectively. C and D received the new workflow. The team had prespecified that larger reductions support adoption.

Working

  1. Treated mean is (8 + 10)/2 = 9; untreated mean is (2 + 4)/2 = 3. Observed T = 6 hours.
  2. There are six possible treated pairs. Their gaps are −6, −2, 0, 0, 2 and 6 hours.
  3. Only C and D produce T at least 6. The exact one-sided p-value is 1/6, about 0.167.
  4. For a two-sided question both −6 and 6 count. The absolute-gap p-value would be 2/6, about 0.333.

Decision and interpretation

The trial suggests a six-hour difference but cannot reject the sharp null at 5%. A larger randomized rollout could distinguish the effect from branch-level variation more precisely.

Quick check
In a uniform assignment test, three of the twenty permitted assignments produce a statistic at least as extreme as observed. What is the exact p-value?

Count every qualifying assignment, including ties. Three divided by twenty is 0.15.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A new experiment pairs six offices into three regional pairs. One office in each pair is randomly treated. The observed treated-minus-control savings are +1, +3 and +5 days. Test for positive effects using their mean, then give the corresponding absolute-gap two-sided probability.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Each of three pairs has two possible label choices. There are 2³ = 8 equally likely assignments, not every three-office subset.
  2. The observed mean is (1 + 3 + 5)/3 = 3 days, the largest possible. Only the all-positive assignment reaches it, giving one-sided p = 1/8.
  3. Only the all-negative assignment reaches −3. The two-sided p-value is 2/8 = 0.25.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

With only four randomized branches, how unusual is it that the two treated branches have the highest observed outcomes?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

List the six equally likely assignments of two treated branches. Under no effect on any branch, only one puts both highest outcomes in treatment, giving a one-sided probability of one in six.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Could an analyst apply the same result if managers chose which office in each pair got treatment?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The random sign flips are justified by randomized assignment. If managers chose the office with the worse backlog, the signs need not be exchangeable. Describe selection and seek a defensible comparison before treating the exact probability as causal evidence.
