Professional workshop · F04

Does a collections reminder pay for itself?

A bank piloted a reminder before scheduled instalments. Within each of eight pre-matched pairs, a coin toss assigned one branch to the reminder and the other to usual practice. Before and after windows each contain exactly 1,000 eligible loans per branch. The risk committee wants to know whether missed payments fell and whether a full rollout to 40,000 comparable monthly loans clears its operating cost. Prepare the analysis as an evidence note. Use branch pairs as the independent units. The numbers and cases here are synthetic.

From inputs to decision

01

Define the outcome and target before calculating

A late payment count can mean many things. The causal and financial interpretations require one comparable endpoint and a clear target population.

  1. Use missed scheduled payments counted once per eligible loan during each window under the same recording rule. The common denominator of 1,000 makes differences directly comparable. A positive effect will mean fewer missed payments.
  2. For each pair subtract the comparison branch’s own before-minus-after improvement from the reminder branch’s improvement. The target is the mean additional reduction per thousand eligible loans in settings represented by these independent branch pairs.

Record the sign convention and analysis unit. The experiment randomized branches within pairs rather than assigning every loan independently.

02

Reconstruct the eight paired contrasts

Baseline customer risk differs across branches and some improvement may occur at usual-practice branches without the reminder.

  1. Pair A improves by 30−22 = 8 with reminders and 27−23 = 4 under usual practice, giving a net reduction of 4. Pair H improves by 11 versus 3, giving 8.
  2. The eight pair effects are 4, 6, 2, 8, 4, 6, 2 and 8. Their sum is 40, so their equal-weight mean is 5 per thousand. Each pair has the same eligible exposure under the stated design.

Save the eight original effects for uncertainty calculations. A single aggregate percentage loses the variation between pairs.

03

Check the descriptive result independently

The change comparison can also be reconstructed from the raw totals. Agreement catches flipped signs and inconsistent denominators.

  1. Reminder branches recorded 236 missed payments before and 175 after, a fall of 61 across eight sets of 1,000. This is an average improvement of 7.625 per thousand.
  2. Usual-practice branches recorded 226 before and 205 after, a fall of 21 or 2.625 per thousand. Subtracting 2.625 from 7.625 yields the same 5. The comparison group already improved independently of the reminder.

Only the additional five per thousand enters the incremental benefit forecast.

04

Estimate sampling uncertainty at the assignment level

Invoices within one branch share local staff and conditions. Eight independent pairs support a different precision calculation from 16,000 supposedly independent follow-up records.

  1. Subtract 5 from every pair effect to obtain −1, +1, −3, +3, −1, +1, −3 and +3. Their squared deviations total 40. Sample variance = 40/(8−1) = 5.7143 and standard deviation ≈ 2.3905.
  2. Standard error for the mean = 2.3905/√8 ≈ 0.8452 per thousand. Use the given t multiplier. The 95% interval is 5 ± 2.365 × 0.8452, approximately 3.00 to 7.00 avoided payments per thousand.

The interval excludes zero on the stated model, while the relatively small number of pairs warrants inspection of individual effects and measurement quality.

05

Translate uncertainty into the rule the committee uses

The operational decision needs a financial threshold. Scale both the point estimate and uncertain range onto the same rollout population before incorporating fixed costs.

  1. Each additional avoided event per 1,000 corresponds to 40 avoided events among 40,000 monthly loans. At €200 per event that is €8,000 monthly gross benefit for each unit of measured effect.
  2. The point estimate of 5 means 200 events avoided, gross benefit €40,000 and net benefit €12,000 after the €28,000 cost. Break-even requires €28,000/€8,000 = 3.5 avoided payments per thousand. The approximate interval translates to net results of about −€4,000 to +€28,000 per month.

A positive average reduction is supported more clearly than a claim that the financial hurdle is cleared throughout the uncertainty range.

06

Evaluate a specific data-quality challenge

The concentration of a reporting error in one treated branch could bias an experimental outcome even with genuine random assignment.

  1. If H’s reminder after count is 27, its improvement becomes 35−27 = 8 and its net paired effect is 8−3 = 5. The original H effect of 8 falls by 3.
  2. The total across pairs falls from 40 to 37 and the mean becomes 37/8 = 4.625. Applied to 40,000 loans this predicts 185 avoided events, €37,000 gross and €9,000 net monthly benefit. Recalculate the standard error on corrected records before publishing a revised interval.

Request a dated audit of H’s three disputed records. Show the €3,000 change in expected monthly net benefit separately from sampling uncertainty.

07

Recommend a decision with explicit evidence gates

A deployment decision also depends on how closely rollout borrowers resemble participants, whether effects persist and how much a loss state matters to the bank.

  1. Continue a controlled extension if management requires the lower two-sided bound to exceed the 3.5 threshold. The point estimate supports a favorable expected-case decision, but the current evidence leaves room for an unprofitable rollout.
  2. Audit delivery and outcome capture in both arms, look for cross-branch spillovers, and verify the €200 value reflects genuinely avoidable downstream costs. Keep the primary outcome and cost rule fixed for the next wave and retain all assigned pairs.

The committee can decide whether to accept expected-case risk or require stronger evidence. The memo makes that trade-off and the measured assumptions explicit.

Completed document

Evidence memorandum to the credit risk committee

Finding

The pilot reduced missed payments by an estimated five per thousand over the improvement in usual-practice branches. The effect was positive in all eight matched pairs. Under the stipulated paired t procedure, the approximate 95% range is three to seven per thousand.

Economics

At 40,000 eligible loans and €200 per genuinely avoided missed payment, the point estimate gives €12,000 net monthly benefit. The cost-recovery threshold is 3.5 per thousand. The estimated range spans that threshold, with a lower implied net result near minus €4,000.

Evidence limitations

Assignment within matched pairs supports a causal interpretation if outcome capture was comparable and branches did not materially affect each other. Eight pairs limit precision. Before extrapolating, confirm eligibility, baseline risk, intervention dose and the true marginal cost of an event in the rollout population.

Decision and controls

Approve a controlled extension and resolve the H reporting discrepancy before making a lower-bound profitability claim. If the committee chooses an expected-value rollout, document the downside and freeze the evaluation rule. Correcting H alone reduces estimated net benefit to €9,000 per month.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

If event value falls from €200 to €160 with 40,000 loans and the same €28,000 cost, what additional reduction per thousand is needed for break-even?

See the full solution

One unit of effect averts 40 events monthly, worth 40 × €160 = €6,400. Break-even is €28,000/€6,400 = 4.375 avoided events per thousand. This higher hurdle follows entirely from the lower economic value per event.

Task 2

If the H correction is confirmed and only 30,000 comparable loans are eligible, what net monthly benefit follows at €200 per avoided event and unchanged cost?

See the full solution

The corrected 4.625 per thousand applied to 30 groups of 1,000 gives 138.75 expected avoided events. Gross value is €27,750. Subtracting €28,000 leaves a €250 expected monthly loss. Expected event counts need not be whole numbers.

Task 3

Under the original 5-per-thousand estimate, how large can the monthly cost be before expected net value falls below zero at 40,000 loans and €200 per event?

See the full solution

Five times forty yields 200 expected avoided events. Their value is €40,000. A monthly cost of €40,000 exactly breaks even, and a higher cost produces a negative point-estimated net return.

Task 4

Would recording every day for another week in the same eight branch pairs automatically give an independent sample of 56 new pairs?

See the full solution

No. Days inside the same branch share staffing, customers and assignment. More days can make each measured branch outcome less noisy, but they do not create independent treatment assignments. Use additional independent matched pairs or an analysis that models within-branch and time dependence.
