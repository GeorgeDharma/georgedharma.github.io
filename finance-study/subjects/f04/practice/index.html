Theory midterm · 10 marks

When does a warning flag help?

After lessons 1–5, allow 20 minutes. Explain the reasoning behind your answer.

In a cohort of 1,000 firms, 40 default. A warning flag identified 30 of the defaults and appeared for 120 firms that repaid.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compare the default rate in flagged firms with the probability of a flag among firms that default.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What is the observed default rate without the flag? Interpret the two groups.

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    Would the 20% automatically transfer to a new population with less default?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the midterm marking guide

Part A · 4 marks

Of 150 flagged firms, 30 default, so the first probability is 20%. Of 40 defaults, 30 were flagged, so the second is 75%. Award one mark for each numerator-and-denominator setup and its result.

Part B · 3 marks

Ten defaults occur among the 850 unflagged firms, or about 1.18%. The flag separates groups with different observed risk. Most flagged firms still repay.

Part C · 3 marks

No. The base frequency matters. Even with unchanged flag sensitivity and false-positive rate, fewer defaults typically lower the default share among flagged firms. Population composition and how the flag was measured also matter.

Problem 1

In 100 independent observations the sample mean is 2 and standard deviation is 10. Approximate a 95% interval.

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

Standard error is 10/√100 = 1. A large-sample normal interval is 2 ± 1.96, or 0.04 to 3.96. Correlated observations require a different calculation.

Problem 2

Two regions have both higher lending rates and higher defaults. Does this establish that rates caused defaults?

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

No causal direction follows from that comparison. Borrower risk or the local business cycle can affect both measures. Specify a design that addresses those differences.

Suggested academic marking scheme

Use these weights when assessing the complete subject. The four problem sets collect the written problems from individual lectures. You can use the 10-mark midterm and the 40-mark subject paper with the weights below.

Problem sets 25%
Four sets per standard subject. Show reasoning, calculations, units and checks. The two practice problems in each subject are worked samples for these sets.
Theory midterm 20%
After the first half of the subject. Explain or derive a model, identify assumptions and state what would change if one failed.
Seminar paper or laboratory 25%
During the second half. Apply the theory to a case, dataset or implementation. Address an alternative explanation and document how to reproduce the result.
Individual final examination 30%
At the end of the subject. Solve an unfamiliar problem, interpret the result and critique a claim. M&A and lending each include a 30-mark examination question with a marking guide.
