Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A manufacturer chooses one production line

A small line costs 70 and produces 84 at year end after operating costs. A larger line costs 105 and produces 127 after operating costs before displacement. It displaces 3 of contribution from old products. An earlier study cost 4 and cannot be refunded. The firm can fund either and requires 10%. There are no further flows.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

Find the cost of financing a comparable division

A division targets market values of 150 equity and 50 debt. Risk-free rate is 3%, market premium 5%, equity beta 1.2 and debt costs 5% before tax. Deductions save 20%. A matching one-year project costs 101 and produces after-tax operating cash of 108 before financing.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Assess an amortizing loan and its borrowing incentives

An investment's unlevered NPV is 2. A loan has opening balances of 40 in year 1 and 20 in year 2, charging 5% annual interest. Full deductions save tax at 30%. Discount shields at 5%. Issue fees are 0.4 now and incremental expected distress costs have present value 0.8.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Distribute cash or buy managerial flexibility

An all-equity company is worth 150, includes 30 excess cash and has 15 shares. It considers distributing 15, or paying 15 today to reserve an expansion right. In one year expansion would cost 50 and the added project would be worth 90 in a strong state or 42 in a weak state. Risk-neutral probabilities are 40% and 60% and the discount rate is 5%. Assume fair share prices, no taxes or fees, no other cash effects.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A manufacturer chooses one production line

Part A 4 marks

Construct incremental cash flows and select the line by NPV.

For the small line use −70 and +84, giving NPV 84/1.10 − 70 = 6.36. For the large line use −105 and 127 − 3 = 124, giving NPV 124/1.10 − 105 = 7.73. Both exclude the sunk study. Choose the larger line.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate both individual IRRs and the crossover rate.

Small IRR is 84/70 − 1 = 20%. Large IRR is 124/105 − 1 = 18.10%. The incremental choice costs 35 and pays 40, implying a crossover of 40/35 − 1 = 14.29%. At 10% the extra investment adds value.

Part C 3 marks

If the firm could decide only on the extra capacity next year after seeing demand, why could the value differ? State the required inputs.

An enforceable right to expand can avoid the added cost in low-demand states. To value it, specify extra capacity cost and resulting state-specific business value at that date, the information then available, probabilities or risk adjustment and cost of reserving the right.

Case 2 · 10 marks

Find the cost of financing a comparable division

Part A 4 marks

Compute equity cost and WACC and justify the weights.

CAPM gives 3% + 1.2 × 5% = 9%. Market weights are 75% equity and 25% debt. WACC is 0.75 × 9% + 0.25 × 5% × 0.8 = 7.75%. These weights reflect current or target financing values.

Part B 3 marks

Determine project NPV and whether changing the discount rate could change the decision.

NPV is 108/1.0775 − 101 ≈ −0.77. Break-even return is 108/101 − 1 ≈ 6.93%, below 7.75%. Accepting requires justified changes to cash flows or required return.

Part C 3 marks

A manager proposes substituting more debt while holding equity return fixed. Explain the benchmark objection.

As equity becomes a smaller residual claim on the same business, its sensitivity to operating risk increases. In the tax-free irrelevance benchmark its required return rises and offsets the weight shift. With taxes, distress and refinancing risk, evaluate those effects explicitly.

Case 3 · 10 marks

Assess an amortizing loan and its borrowing incentives

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the present value of tax benefits.

Interest is 2 and 1. The shields are 0.6 and 0.3. Present value is 0.6/1.05 + 0.3/1.05² = 0.8435.

Part B 4 marks

Calculate APV, net financing benefit and economic interpretation.

Net financing benefit is 0.8435 − 0.4 − 0.8 = −0.3565. APV is 2 − 0.3565 = 1.6435. The project retains positive value, but the specified financing terms reduce value relative to the unlevered base.

Part C 3 marks

Separately, the debt covenant restricts a switch from a safe project to a gamble. Why might lenders request it? Name a cost.

With limited liability owners can benefit from an unusually good outcome while creditors absorb part of a bad outcome. Restrictions can prevent risk shifting. Broad restrictions may also block worthwhile new uses of funds, so the provision may need exceptions or consent rights.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Distribute cash or buy managerial flexibility

Part A 3 marks

Compare a dividend of 15 and a buyback at current fair value.

Dividend is 1 per share. The company would be worth 135 and each remaining share 9, so wealth per original share including cash is 10. A buyback at 10 retires 1.5 shares. The remaining 13.5 shares are worth 135/13.5 = 10 each.

Part B 4 marks

Value the expansion right gross and net of its price. Explain the state-contingent action.

Exercise only in the strong state for a gain of 90 − 50 = 40. In the weak state let the right expire for zero. Gross present value is 0.40 × 40/1.05 = 15.2381. Subtract the 15 premium to get NPV 0.2381.

Part C 3 marks

Should the company retain the 15 for this opportunity? Explain what could reverse the decision.

Under the stated assumptions the positive NPV marginally favours securing the right. A lower probability under the valuation measure, weaker strong-state payoff, higher reservation cost or an unenforceable right could remove the small advantage. Compare total shareholder wealth with the feasible payout alternative.

Additional exam cases

After the core paper, test the additional lectures below. Each case opens with an answer space and its own worked solution. Use the worked solutions to assess these cases separately from the 40-mark paper.

  1. C03.11 · Lease or buy an asset after tax

    A different firm buys for €60k or rents for €30k at each of two year ends. Purchase tax depreciation is €30k a year. Guaranteed resale at the second year end is €8k against zero tax basis. The tax rate is 20%, relief is immediate and sale proceeds are taxed at 20%. Use 5% after tax. Services and other costs are equal. Find the better choice and zero-advantage annual rent.

  2. C03.12 · Finding the economic age for replacing equipment

    A pump costs €60k new. Maintenance by year is €4k, €10k and €23k. Sale prices at ages one, two and three are €42k, €31k and €20k. Identical pumps can be replaced forever at 10%, with equal output and no taxes. Choose among the three replacement ages and decide what to do with a one-year-old pump.

  3. C03.13 · Forecasting the external funds needed for growth

    Sales rise from €300m to €330m. Existing plant supports €350m of sales, so there is no investment in plant. Operating current assets are 40% of sales and trade liabilities 20%. Net margin is 6%, and 40% of net profit is retained. Other accounts do not change. Determine the first-pass financing gap and the impact of a separate, mandatory €10m plant purchase.
