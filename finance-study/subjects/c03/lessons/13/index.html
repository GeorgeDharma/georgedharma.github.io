Forecasting the external funds needed for growth

Estimate additional funds needed from a forecast balance sheet and proposed payout policy.

In this lecture

  1. Turn sales growth into assets and claims
  2. Handle spare capacity explicitly
  3. Interpret the financing residual
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. What funds the increase in operating assets?
  6. A manufacturer targets 20% sales growth
  7. Independent exam problem
  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Additional funds needed

AFN · percentage-of-sales forecast · external financing

What you should be able to explain

  • Estimate additional funds needed from a forecast balance sheet and proposed payout policy.
  • Use existing plant capacity correctly and distinguish the implied financing gap from peak cash needs.

Turn sales growth into assets and claims

A seller often needs more inventory and trade receivables when sales rise. More sales can also bring more supplier credit or accrued operating costs. These spontaneous liabilities are funding created by normal operating terms. An arranged bank loan is a financing decision and must not be counted as spontaneous funding.

The projected balance sheet must balance. Additional asset investment is financed by additional spontaneous liabilities, profits kept after dividends, or another financing source. Forecast net income on the full projected sales level. Applying the profit margin only to the increase in sales omits contributions from the existing business.

Handle spare capacity explicitly

A receivables balance may grow with each extra sale. A plant may first absorb growth at little additional fixed investment. If today's sales represent 90% of installed capacity, full-capacity sales are today's sales divided by 0.90. Above that point the exercise assumes fixed assets can expand proportionally at the full-capacity productivity rate.

Real investments may be indivisible. If the only available production line costs 20, a smooth calculation that predicts 8 of investment cannot specify the cash purchase. Use the actual step cost and revised spare capacity. The model below ignores inflation, changes in asset age, and depreciation not already reflected in the assumed net balances.

Interpret the financing residual

A positive financing gap means the stated operating, asset and payout policies require new funding or an amended plan. A negative gap gives a projected balance-sheet surplus on those assumptions. It does not prove that cash is available on each day through the year.

Interest on new borrowing can reduce net income and the retained contribution. A first pass may use a given margin before that feedback. A full forecast should model interest based on when debt is outstanding, recalculate retained income and rebalance. Peak seasonal borrowing also requires a dated cash budget.

Method

Build the result

AFN = Δoperating assets − Δspontaneous liabilities − projected net income × retention fraction.

AFN is additional external funds needed in currency units at the forecast date. Δ means closing less opening balance. Retention is the share of net income left after dividends. Fixed-asset needs are estimated from usable capacity and any required expansion.

  1. Forecast the sales level, then link operating assets and trade liabilities to the applicable sales or physical driver.
  2. Check whether fixed assets can absorb the sales plan. Calculate extra fixed assets using full-capacity productivity or a genuine investment schedule.
  3. Deduct internally generated funding. Verify the increment in assets equals increments in spontaneous funding, retained profit and the external financing residual.
Worked case

A manufacturer targets 20% sales growth

All values are €m. Sales rise from 200 to 240. Operating current assets equal 30% of sales and spontaneous trade liabilities 15%. Net plant of 100 operates at 90% of capacity at current sales. Assume divisible expansion at full-capacity productivity only when needed. The forecast net profit margin is 5% before the interest effect of any new financing. Half the profit is retained. Other accounts do not change.

Working

  1. Current assets rise by 0.30 × (240 − 200) = 12. Spontaneous funding rises by 0.15 × 40 = 6.
  2. Current plant supports sales of 200/0.90 = 222.22. At full capacity the plant-to-sales ratio is 100/222.22 = 0.45. Plant required at 240 is 108, so the addition is 8.
  3. Net profit is 0.05 × 240 = 12. Distributing 6 leaves 6 in retained earnings. AFN is 12 + 8 − 6 − 6 = €8m.
  4. Check the balance: €20m more operating assets are covered by €6m additional trade credit, €6m additional retained profit and €8m new external financing. Assuming the current plant ratio stays at 100/200 would overstate the fixed investment by €12m.

Decision and interpretation

Begin planning €8m of external capital or revisit the payout or investment policy. A separate monthly cash forecast may reveal a larger temporary working-capital need before year-end sales turn into cash.

Quick check
Sales are forecast to rise by €10m to €110m. The net margin is 4% and the retention fraction is 50%. What retained profit belongs in the financing calculation?

The retained contribution is 110 × 0.04 × 0.50 = €2.20m. The remainder of net income is paid out.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Sales rise from €300m to €330m. Existing plant supports €350m of sales, so there is no investment in plant. Operating current assets are 40% of sales and trade liabilities 20%. Net margin is 6%, and 40% of net profit is retained. Other accounts do not change. Determine the first-pass financing gap and the impact of a separate, mandatory €10m plant purchase.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Additional current assets are 0.40 × 30 = €12m, and additional spontaneous funding is 0.20 × 30 = €6m.
  2. Projected net income is 0.06 × 330 = €19.80m. The retained contribution is 0.40 × 19.80 = €7.92m.
  3. With spare capacity the gap is 12 − 6 − 7.92 = −€1.92m, an estimated surplus. With the additional mandatory plant purchase it becomes €8.08m of outside financing.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a company expect positive profit and still need new capital to support its sales plan?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Profit and financing need answer different questions. Growth can tie up more cash in inventory, receivables and equipment than the company retains after dividends. I would forecast the change in operating assets, allow for supplier financing and check actual plant capacity. The residual after retained net income is the external capital needed on the plan. The timing of collections may require additional temporary borrowing.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If many sales are made in December but collected in February, is the negative first-pass figure enough to approve a January dividend?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. Reforecast receivables if collections differ from the assumed ratio and build the dated cash budget. The year-end financing residual does not establish January cash available for dividends.
