Adjusted present value

Value the project without leverage, then add financing effects separately. This is useful when the amount of debt changes markedly over time.

The starting point

Adjusted present value (APV) starts with the value of the project as if funded entirely by equity. Estimate operating cash flows before financing and discount them for the risk of the underlying business. Then add the present values of financing benefits and subtract financing costs.

This separation is especially helpful when debt runs down rapidly or the financing package is unusual. For each financing effect, identify its date, risk and availability. Avoid adding an interest tax shield a second time if it was already built into the starting value.

Theory and method

Adjusted present value

Myers APV · Financing side effects · Subsidized loan valuation

A development lender offers a €20m loan on preferential terms. How do you capture the subsidy in APV without claiming the €20m as free value?

A market-rate borrowing gives the company cash and an offsetting obligation. A concessional loan can add value because its required repayment is smaller than a market lender would demand for the same risk. Price the actual repayment using the comparable market rate and subtract that liability value from the cash received. Then add the resulting subsidy to the operating project's standalone value, with separate attention to taxes, fees and any operating conditions attached to the loan.

How the result is built

APV = NPVᵁ + L − PVₘ(payments) + PV(shields) − costs

NPVᵁ is unlevered operating net present value. L is loan cash advanced. PVₘ uses the market rate for comparable promised loan cash flows. Shields are incremental tax savings valued consistently. Costs are incremental financing and distress costs in present value.

  1. A loan advances €20m and requires €20.6m in one year. If comparable market borrowing costs 8%, the liability's market value is €20.6m/1.08 = €19.074m.
  2. The financing subsidy is €20m − €19.074m = €0.926m. A separate €0.20m upfront fee reduces the net benefit to €0.726m.
  3. Add the net subsidy to unlevered NPV. If a separate tax shield applies, use actual interest paid, and verify the reference market pricing and shield treatment avoid duplication.

When the reasoning holds

  • The market discount rate must match repayment risk, seniority and currency. Using the risk-free rate for a risky loan invents a different subsidy.
  • Grant conditions can affect operations. Costs of mandated hiring, procurement or restricted use must enter the relevant cash flows exactly once.

Terms in this lesson
Net present value (NPV)
Today’s value of incoming cash minus today’s value of outgoing cash.
Weighted average cost of capital (WACC)
A blend of the required returns on debt and equity, weighted by how much each supplies. It can be used to discount cash flows available to both.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
How to read the notation
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.

All notation examples

First example

A project costs €100m. Its unlevered operating cash flows are worth €106m today. Usable tax shields are worth €5m today and the issue fee is €2m paid now. APV is 106 − 100 + 5 − 2 = €9m.

Separate business value from financing arrangements

Adjusted present value asks two questions in order. What would the project be worth if financed entirely with equity? How do the actual financing arrangements alter that value? Keeping these layers separate helps when debt balances, tax benefits or issue fees change significantly through time.

The unlevered operating forecast includes taxes as if there were no interest deductions. Discount these operating cash flows at the required return on the underlying business assets. Subtract the initial operating investment to obtain unlevered NPV before adding financing side effects.

Classify financing cash flows correctly

Borrowing principal at fair market terms creates a matching repayment obligation. Loan proceeds alone are not incremental value for all investors. An issue fee uses real cash, a concessionary loan may confer a subsidy and interest deductions may reduce taxes. Each can create a separate adjustment.

A subsidy can be valued by comparing the cash actually received with the market present value of the promised payments on equivalent risk and terms. A financing guarantee may have its own value or cost. Keep separate a true subsidy and the mere availability of ordinary borrowed money.

Select a rate and date for each adjustment

A predetermined tax benefit that is as safe as the debt can be discounted at the stated debt rate in a simplified case. A shield conditional on operating profit or refinancing can carry a different risk. A fee paid immediately is already in present-value terms.

Compared with a stable-target WACC model, APV makes debt schedules easier to inspect. If the underlying risks, financing policy and tax assumptions are consistent, the two approaches should be reconcilable. Different numerical outputs warrant checking for a duplicated shield or a mismatched debt policy.

Method

Construct the APV bridge

  1. Unlevered layer

    Forecast project cash before interest and discount at the asset return. Deduct asset investment and operating working capital at their dates.

  2. Financing layer

    Calculate yearly interest tax benefits, subsidy value, issuance costs and any modeled distress effects. Use the supplied risk assumptions.

  3. Total and stress

    Add signed present values. Report which layer makes the investment attractive and whether that layer is vulnerable to a change in financing.

Worked case

A two-year project with shrinking debt

A project costs 100 now. Unlevered operating cash is 60 in each of two years and the asset discount rate is 10%. The loan pays interest of 4 in year 1 and 2 in year 2. Fully usable corporate tax is 25%. Discount the case's shield at 5%. Issue fees are 1 today.

Working

  1. Unlevered NPV is −100 + 60/1.10 + 60/1.10² = 4.13. The forecast is already after operating taxes before interest.
  2. Tax benefits of 1 and 0.5 are worth 1/1.05 + 0.5/1.05² = 1.41 today. Fees enter at −1.
  3. Total APV is about 4.54. Adding the loan's gross initial proceeds would be inconsistent because its market-rate repayment obligation has not disappeared.

Interpretation

The project is attractive even without the financing side effect under these assumptions. Writing the bridge distinguishes that from a project justified only by a subsidy.

A common mistake

Adding the interest shield after discounting operating cash at an after-tax WACC that already recognizes it.

How to correct it

Construct an explicit unlevered baseline with an asset discount rate. Follow one consistent methodology for each component.

Quick check
If the financing change also creates expected distress costs worth €3m in present value, what is the revised APV?

The €3m is an additional financing cost. Deduct it once from the €9m APV to get €6m.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A one-year project costs 80 and will generate unlevered operating cash of 84. Its required asset return is 8%. Separately, a guaranteed subsidy pays 4 next year, discounted at 3%, and a loan issue fee is 0.7 now.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    Calculate unlevered NPV.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find APV and show what changes the decision.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    If the subsidy were worth only 2 today, what would you conclude?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

84/1.08 − 80 = −2.22. The operating project alone does not earn the required return under these assumptions.

Part B 3 marks

The subsidy is worth 4/1.03 = 3.8835. APV is −2.2222 + 3.8835 − 0.7 = 0.96, so the financing benefit changes the sign.

Part C 2 marks

APV would be −2.22 + 2 − 0.7 = −0.92. Checking entitlement and timing is therefore material to the investment decision.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A development lender offers a €20m loan on preferential terms. How do you capture the subsidy in APV without claiming the €20m as free value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would first value a debt-free version using operating cash and an asset return. I would then price the loan as a liability at comparable market terms and subtract that value from the proceeds. Here the subsidy is about €0.93m. After a €0.20m fee a project with operating NPV of minus €0.50m has APV of roughly plus €0.23m, before any other financing or operating conditions.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A legally binding hiring requirement attached to the loan reduces operating value by €0.40m. Should the firm take the package?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Including the condition once gives APV of about €0.226m − €0.40m = minus €0.174m. Evaluate any independent option to undertake the project without the loan, whose stated operating NPV is also negative.
