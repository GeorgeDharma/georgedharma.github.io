Taxes and debt capacity

Interest deductions can add value when usable. Compare the benefit with expected distress costs and the loss of financial flexibility.

The starting point

If interest is deductible and the company has sufficient taxable income, borrowing can reduce taxes. The cash saved is called an interest tax shield. Its value depends on the tax rules in the case, whether deductions can be used, their timing and the risk of receiving the savings.

Debt also promises payments even in a bad year. Financial strain may raise supplier costs, deter customers or force valuable investments to be cancelled. A debt-capacity decision weighs the additional benefit from the next amount of borrowing against these additional expected costs.

Theory and method

Static trade-off theory of capital structure

Interest tax shield · Expected costs of financial distress · Debt capacity

Why might the debt level that maximizes a company's tax deductions exceed the debt level that maximizes its total value?

A railway adding debt can deduct interest when it has taxable profit. Its suppliers and customers may also react to a more fragile balance sheet, raising operating costs or discouraging long contracts. Compare two otherwise identical financing policies. Extra debt adds value while its next tax benefit exceeds its next expected economic cost. The right amount therefore depends on cash-flow volatility, recovery prospects and tax capacity, even for firms with identical asset values.

How the result is built

V(D) = Vᵁ + PV[usable shields(D)] − PV[distress costs(D)]

D is the chosen debt balance, V(D) total financing-inclusive business value, Vᵁ the unlevered base. PV is today's value of each policy's dated cash flows. All value figures use the same currency, date and business perimeter.

  1. Calculate deductions across plausible income states. A loss-making year may postpone or eliminate a current tax saving.
  2. Estimate incremental expected distress cost by considering probability and magnitude. Include economic disruptions such as lost customer contribution where not already in operating cash.
  3. Compare adjacent debt policies. If moving from 40 to 60 raises shield value by 4 and expected distress cost by 7, total value falls by 3.

When the reasoning holds

  • Operating forecasts must use the same underlying business. If distress losses are already built into scenarios, avoid deducting them a second time.
  • Discrete alternatives support the best of the listed policies. An interior continuous optimum requires a smooth feasible set and equality of marginal benefits and costs.

Terms in this lesson
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
How to read the notation
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.

All notation examples

First example

A firm owes €20m at 5%, pays €1m interest next year and can deduct it at 25%. The tax saving next year is €250,000. If this one saving is certain and discounted at 5%, it is worth about €238,095 today.

Calculate a tax shield on usable deductions

An interest tax shield is the reduction in taxes attributable to deductible interest. Start by comparing tax paid with and without the interest deduction in the same period. If the company has enough taxable profits and the full interest is deductible, tax saved equals interest times the marginal tax rate.

A business making losses may only use some deductions later, subject to the case rules. A tax saving used two years from now is worth less today than an otherwise equally safe saving received now. Label whether your figures are annual cash savings or present values.

Distinguish fixed debt from a target leverage policy

For a predictable debt schedule, calculate interest period by period from the opening debt. Calculate the usable tax benefit at each date and discount it for the modeled risk. The discount rate on the shield depends on the uncertainty of debt policy and the firm's ability to claim the deduction.

The expression tax rate times debt is a special result often obtained for permanent fixed debt with fully usable deductions and a particular risk assumption. It does not apply automatically to a two-year loan that amortizes or to deductions that may expire unused.

Evaluate costs of financial distress

Distress can reduce business value before formal insolvency. Customers may fear interrupted support, suppliers can shorten credit terms, and management may reject valuable projects to conserve cash. Expected distress cost weights possible economic losses by their probabilities and dates.

Compare the benefit and expected cost of an additional borrowing decision. Do not count a transfer of money from shareholders to existing lenders as lost total value unless the analysis is specifically from shareholders' perspective. Distinguish direct administrative costs and disruption of operations from a redistribution of claims.

Method

Evaluate additional debt

  1. Build the schedule

    List opening debt, interest rate, interest paid, deductible share and tax rate for each date. Identify deferred or unusable deductions.

  2. Discount

    Value expected tax savings with the case's risk and timing assumptions. Add fees or subsidy separately when relevant.

  3. Compare the increment

    Subtract added expected distress and lost flexibility using consistent present values. Clarify whether value is for all investors or one claimant.

Worked case

A two-year loan repays in stages

A firm borrows 50 at 6%, repays 20 principal at the end of year 1 and 30 at the end of year 2. It can use all interest deductions at 25%. Assume tax savings are as safe as debt and discount at 6%.

Working

  1. Interest is 3 in year 1 and 1.8 in year 2 because debt falls to 30 after the first repayment. Tax shields are 0.75 and 0.45.
  2. Their combined present value is 0.75/1.06 + 0.45/1.06² = 1.11. The principal repayments reorganize financing but generate no interest deduction.
  3. If incremental expected operating disruption is worth 0.80 today and fees are 0.15, the estimated net financing benefit is about 0.16. The marginal advantage can disappear under a modest stress.

Interpretation

Multiplying the tax rate by 50 gives 12.5, a very different result because that shortcut presumes a debt policy absent from the case.

A common mistake

Counting the entire interest payment as a tax saving.

How to correct it

Compare the tax bills. Interest reduces taxable profit by the usable amount. Only the tax rate applied to that amount becomes cash saved.

Quick check
If only €600,000 of the interest can generate a deduction this year, how much tax is saved this year at 25%?

Multiply the usable deduction by the tax rate. A possible future use of the rest would require separate timing and eligibility assumptions.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A one-year €10m loan charges 5%. Only €300,000 of interest can be used as a deduction this year, and the unused amount expires. The tax rate is 30%. Discount any certain saving at 5%.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find the nominal and present tax benefit.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Fees are €30,000 today and the loan raises expected distress cost by €70,000 in present value. Find the net financing benefit.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    What if the unused deduction can instead be used in two years?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Interest is €500,000 but only €300,000 is usable. The saving is €90,000 and its present value is €90,000/1.05 = €85,714.

Part B 2 marks

€85,714 − €30,000 − €70,000 = approximately −€14,286. These are additional financing effects, not the project's total operating NPV.

Part C 2 marks

The additional eligible €200,000 could save €60,000 then. Its present value depends on the probability and timing of use and an appropriate discount rate. It should not be added as €60,000 cash today.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why might the debt level that maximizes a company's tax deductions exceed the debt level that maximizes its total value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would look at the incremental borrowing. From €40m to €60m the present value of tax benefits rises by €4m but expected distress costs rise by €7m. The next €20m of borrowing therefore costs shareholders and lenders €3m in total economic value. I would test the assumed cash deterioration and customer behavior under stress, then check covenant headroom around the remaining alternatives.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Does a bond trading below face value itself measure the economic cost of distress?

Compare with the follow-up answer

It reflects investors' expected cash loss and pricing, which can include transfers between claimants. To estimate lost total business value, I would identify bankruptcy administration, disrupted sales, forced asset disposals and missed investments. I would track claim redistribution separately.
