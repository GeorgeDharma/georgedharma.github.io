Lease or buy an asset after tax

Build separate present-cost schedules for leasing and owning the same service capacity.

Theory and terminology

Net advantage to leasing

NAL · lease versus buy · after-tax cash flows

What you should be able to explain

  • Build separate present-cost schedules for leasing and owning the same service capacity.
  • Calculate the maximum rent worth paying and interpret the effect of changing payment dates.

Fix the service being bought

A lease-versus-buy comparison starts after the business has identified the required equipment and service period. The service must be economically equivalent on both sides. If a lease includes repairs, spare equipment or an upgrade right, assign the related costs or benefits to the relevant side. Treat any refundable deposit and return condition explicitly.

State who owns the asset for tax purposes and who can use each deduction. The simplified rules in this exercise give the owner depreciation deductions and the lessee deductions for rent when paid. Actual contracts and local tax law can assign benefits differently. A firm without enough taxable profit may also receive the relief later.

Put ownership cash and deductions on a timeline

The purchase price is paid today. Depreciation is an accounting allocation. It generates cash only through a reduction in actual tax. With a tax deduction of 40 and a 25% tax rate, the saving is 10 in that year. Selling an asset may create taxable proceeds relative to its remaining tax basis, its value for tax purposes.

For leasing, put the rent on its contractual payment date and the deduction on its tax date. Decide whether each flow is debt-like or carries a separate business risk. Here the rental, deductions and sale price are all assumed certain, and the case supplies one common after-tax discount rate. Uncertain resale or tax capacity may need different treatment.

Compare present costs on the same basis

The net advantage to leasing is the present cost of ownership less the present cost of leasing. A positive number favors the lease. Discounting debt-like payments at an after-tax borrowing rate already reflects the financing benchmark. Adding the full purchase loan principal and interest to this present-cost ownership schedule would mix methods.

A maximum acceptable rent is a negotiating number, not just a yes-or-no result. Solve for the rent that equalizes the present costs, then recompute for changed timing, mileage limits, end penalties or service promises. The same annual number paid today rather than at year end is more expensive.

Method

Build the result

NAL = P − PV(depreciation tax savings) − PV(after-tax sale proceeds) − PV(after-tax rentals).

NAL is the net advantage to leasing in today's money. P is today's purchase price. PV means present value. The formula assumes identical operating costs, no up-front lease payment and no other charges. A positive NAL favors the lease.

  1. Write separate annual costs for asset ownership and renting. Remove costs paid equally under both contracts.
  2. Discount available deductions and net sale proceeds at the rates stated for their risk, then deduct both from the purchase price.
  3. Discount rents after allowable tax relief. Subtract their present value from ownership cost. For the break-even rent, set the difference to zero.
Worked case

A laboratory needs three years of uninterrupted equipment use

Amounts are in thousands of euros. Purchase today costs 120, and straight-line tax depreciation to zero is 40 each year. Guaranteed resale after year three is 12. Tax is 25% and all relief is usable immediately. Assume sale proceeds above tax basis are taxed at 25%. Alternatively, rent is 42 at each year end and is fully deductible then. Maintenance and output coincide. The relevant after-tax borrowing rate is 6%.

Working

  1. The present value factor for three equal year-end payments is 1/1.06 + 1/1.06² + 1/1.06³ = 2.67301.
  2. Annual owner tax relief is 40 × 25% = 10. Net resale is 12 × (1 − 25%) = 9. Ownership present cost is 120 − 10 × 2.67301 − 9/1.06³ = 85.71.
  3. Each rent costs 42 × 0.75 = 31.50 after tax. Lease present cost is 31.50 × 2.67301 = 84.20. NAL is positive 1.51.
  4. Annual pretax rent that equalizes the two sides is 85.7133/(0.75 × 2.67301) = 42.75. Above that, ownership costs less on the supplied terms.

Decision and interpretation

Take the quoted lease, subject to confirming the timing and guaranteed return terms. A rent paid in advance has a different present cost, even if the undiscounted total rent is identical.

Quick check
In this lesson's tax regime, what belongs on the lessee's cash-flow schedule when the lessor owns the asset for tax purposes?

The tax owner claims depreciation. The lessee pays rent and receives the rental deduction stated in the case.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A different firm buys for €60k or rents for €30k at each of two year ends. Purchase tax depreciation is €30k a year. Guaranteed resale at the second year end is €8k against zero tax basis. The tax rate is 20%, relief is immediate and sale proceeds are taxed at 20%. Use 5% after tax. Services and other costs are equal. Find the better choice and zero-advantage annual rent.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Two-year annuity factor is 1/1.05 + 1/1.05² = 1.85941. Ownership costs 60 − 6 × 1.85941 − 6.4/1.05² = €43.04k.
  2. Leasing costs 30 × 0.80 × 1.85941 = €44.63k. NAL is −€1.59k, favoring ownership.
  3. The zero-advantage pretax rent is 43.03855/(0.80 × 1.85941) = €28.93k per year.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How much annual rent is a company willing to pay before owning the same equipment becomes cheaper?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would compare the present cost of the two ways to obtain the same service. For ownership I deduct the cash tax savings on depreciation and the after-tax resale proceeds from the purchase price. Then I find the rent whose after-tax payments have that same present value. For the laboratory the ceiling is about €42.75 thousand per year, assuming each rent and its tax relief fall at year end.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the original €42k three-year rents are payable at the beginning of each year, with tax relief on the same date. Does the choice survive?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Moving each net rent one year earlier multiplies lease PV by 1.06. It rises to €89.25k, above the unchanged €85.71k purchase cost. The company would buy.
