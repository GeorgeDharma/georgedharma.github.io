Flexibility and real options

The right to delay, expand or abandon changes an investment’s payoff. Identify when a fixed cash forecast understates the value of waiting.

The starting point

Some investment choices include a right to act later. A company can wait for information before opening a plant, expand it only if demand is strong, or close it if losses become large. These real options change which cash flows the company will choose in each outcome.

Build a decision tree from the final decision backwards. At each point, compare the value of exercising the right with the value of leaving it unused. Timing matters because the option can expire, delay can sacrifice sales, and competitors can act first.

Theory and method

Real options and backward induction

Option to delay · Option to abandon · Risk-neutral valuation

A director averages demand forecasts first and finds that waiting has no value. What did that averaging discard?

A mining company can inspect an assay before paying to reopen a pit. In a rich deposit it spends the money. In a poor deposit it walks away. Averaging the two deposit values before deciding forces a single action across both possible results. A right exercisable after the news lets the manager choose a different action in each state. The difference is economic value only if the decision really can wait and the company controls the opportunity.

How the result is built

C₀ = [q max(Vᵤ−K,0) + (1−q) max(V𝒹−K,0)]/(1+r)

C₀ is the gross present value of a one-period right. Vᵤ and V𝒹 are project values in the high and low states at exercise, K is investment due then, q the supplied risk-neutral high-state probability and r the matching risk-free annual rate.

  1. Compare exercise with expiration separately. Values of 95 and 45 against a cost of 65 give option payoffs of 30 and zero.
  2. With risk-neutral q = 0.4, the valuation-weighted next-year payoff is 12. At r = 5%, gross right value is 12/1.05 = 11.43.
  3. Compare gross value with the price of a reservation and any income lost while waiting. A right bought for 8 adds 3.43 on these terms.

When the reasoning holds

  • The state must be observed before the irrevocable choice. A binding construction commitment before assay results removes the ability to avoid the bad state.
  • The stated q must be risk-neutral or an equivalent valuation assumption must be supplied. Real-world probabilities with a risk-free discount can misprice unhedgeable business risk.
Terms in this lesson
Option
A contract giving the buyer a right to buy or sell on stated terms. The buyer pays a premium for the right.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
First example

Suppose next year a firm can pay 80 for a permit whose project will be worth 120 or 60, each with probability 0.5. It exercises only in the high state, earning 40 or zero. With no risk adjustment or discounting in this illustration, the right is worth 20 today.

Identify a decision that can actually be deferred

A real option is a right to change a business decision after new information becomes available. An empty site can have value if its owner can open a store after demand becomes clearer. If a competitor can take the site or the owner is already bound to build, the supposed right may be much less useful.

Distinguish uncertainty about cash from flexibility to act on it. A risky project with an irreversible spending commitment does not acquire a valuable option merely because outcomes are uncertain. Specify who controls the decision, when it can be exercised and what new information will be known.

Solve choices at the decision node

At the last decision date, compare the payoff from acting with the payoff from walking away. Choose the better permitted action separately in each state. Work backwards to the earlier decision using the case's valuation assumption. Averaging project values first and applying a single choice afterwards removes the flexibility.

For an abandonment option, compare the sale or salvage value with the value of continuing operations at that date. For expansion, compare incremental operating value with the expansion cost. Existing business already owned should enter both choice alternatives consistently.

Treat risk and opportunity cost explicitly

Actual outcome probabilities can be used with an appropriate risk adjustment when supplied. For training cases a risk-neutral decision maker, risk-neutral probabilities or a zero-risk-adjustment assumption may be specified. Avoid discounting a payoff with strong market risk at a risk-free rate merely because it is an option.

Waiting may forfeit early income, lead to a higher future construction cost or invite competition. Compare the full values of investing now, preserving the right and doing nothing. The value of flexibility is measured against an explicitly defined strategy without that flexibility.

Method

Work a small decision tree

  1. Final states

    Identify the operating value and exercise cost on the same date in each state. Calculate the better feasible action and its payoff.

  2. Move back

    Apply the stated probabilities and discount rate to those state-contingent payoffs. Retain whether they are current or future values.

  3. Choose today

    Subtract any cost to reserve the right. Compare with committing immediately or leaving the opportunity unused.

Worked case

A developer reserves a site

One year from now the finished venture will be worth 150 if a rail stop opens and 65 otherwise. Building then costs 100. The stated risk-neutral probability of the stop opening is 40% and the one-year risk-free rate is 5%. A reservation costs 4 now.

Working

  1. If the stop opens, pay 100 for a venture worth 150 and gain 50. Without the stop, allow the right to expire and earn zero.
  2. The gross option value today is (0.40 × 50 + 0.60 × 0)/1.05 = 19.05. Net of the reservation price the opportunity is worth 15.05.
  3. A binding future commitment instead yields 50 or −35. Its discounted value is (0.40 × 50 − 0.60 × 35)/1.05 = −0.95 before any fee.

Interpretation

The right changes the low-state outcome, which accounts for the difference between the optional and committed strategies. This calculation relies on the supplied risk-neutral probabilities.

A common mistake

Taking the maximum after averaging the high and low project values.

How to correct it

Evaluate the manager's choice after the state is known. Compute each state's optimized payoff, then value their distribution.

Quick check
Under the same simplified assumptions, what is the value if next year's exercise price rises to 100?

Exercise gives 20 in the high state and is declined in the low state. The expected payoff is 0.5 × 20 = 10.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A machine can be sold for 45 next year. At that same date the value of continuing is 70 in a strong state and 25 in a weak one. Risk-neutral probabilities are 60% and 40%, the discount rate 5%, and current purchase price is 55. Ignore interim cash.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    What is the machine worth today with a right to sell?

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Compare the value without a sale right and calculate the value of flexibility.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Would paying 55 be attractive in each case?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Continue for 70 in the strong state and sell for 45 in the weak state. Value is (0.60 × 70 + 0.40 × 45)/1.05 = 57.14.

Part B 3 marks

Without sale, value is (0.60 × 70 + 0.40 × 25)/1.05 = 49.52. The abandonment option adds approximately 7.62.

Part C 2 marks

With the right, NPV is about 2.14. Without it NPV is about −5.48. Verify the machine really can be sold for 45 in the weak market before relying on the result.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A director averages demand forecasts first and finds that waiting has no value. What did that averaging discard?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would preserve the decision timing. In the rich state I spend 65 for a project worth 95 and keep 30. In the poor state I can walk away. Using the supplied valuation probability and rate, that choice is worth 11.43 before reserving the site. A forecast that requires me to average demand first ignores the right to decline the loss-making investment after the assay.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Would delay still be attractive if a competitor could take the site for free before the assay?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The exclusive right is no longer assured. I would model the probability and timing of losing the site, any cost to secure exclusivity and cash sacrificed during delay. Without an enforceable right the earlier 11.43 can materially overstate attainable value.
