Agency and information

Managers, lenders and shareholders face different incentives. Information gaps can explain financing preferences and why an equity issue can move the share price.

The starting point

A loan can change what shareholders prefer after the money is lent. Their downside is limited, while they receive much of an unusually large gain. This creates an agency problem when they can replace a safe project with a gamble at the lender’s expense.

Information differences also matter when choosing financing. Investors who know less than managers may interpret an equity issue as a signal that shares are expensive. A preference for internal cash, then debt, then equity is one possible response. It is an explanation to test, since a business can issue equity for many reasons.

Theory and method

Pecking-order theory and debt overhang

Myers–Majluf asymmetric information model · Myers underinvestment problem · Agency cost of debt

A cash-poor firm rejects an investment with positive NPV. How would you distinguish adverse selection in equity issuance from debt overhang?

A founder knows her existing business is more valuable than outsiders believe. Selling shares cheaply can transfer enough wealth from old owners to new subscribers that she skips a good project. Using internal funds or relatively safe debt reduces that information problem. A second firm may face a different obstacle. Its existing debt is distressed and new investment would mostly improve the lenders' recovery. Here shareholder refusal can persist even when everyone agrees on the firm's value.

How the result is built

NPV = ΔV − I ; existing owners invest if ΔE − I ≥ 0 when they supply I

ΔV is the increase in total firm value, ΔE the increase received by existing equity before their contribution, and I the new cash provided by those owners. With two claimants, ΔV = ΔD + ΔE, where ΔD is the gain to existing debt.

  1. For debt overhang, trace the senior debt waterfall with and without the investment. If project value rises by 18 and lenders capture 15, shareholders gain only 3 before their contribution.
  2. If owners must contribute 10, the project adds 8 in total and costs them 7 personally. A negotiated priority right or write-down can unlock the gain.
  3. For pecking order, hold the asset waterfall fixed and compare issuing underpriced equity with internal cash or fairly priced safe debt. An issue transferring more than the project's NPV can deter old owners.

When the reasoning holds

  • The Myers–Majluf mechanism needs material private information and managers acting for existing owners. Informative disclosure or fairly priced issuance weakens the adverse-selection cost.
  • Debt overhang needs risky outstanding claims and inadequate capture of the marginal benefit by those funding the project. A safe existing claim with unchanged value cannot absorb the upside in this manner.

Terms in this lesson
Net present value (NPV)
Today’s value of incoming cash minus today’s value of outgoing cash.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Agency problem
People acting on behalf of someone else can have different goals and better information about their own decisions.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
Covenant
A promise or condition in a financing agreement, such as maintaining an agreed level of cash.
First example

A borrower owes 90. A safe project pays 100 for certain, giving owners 10. A gamble gives 160 or zero with equal probability. Its expected total payoff is only 80, but owners expect 0.5 × (160 − 90) = 35 because their downside is capped.

Use a payoff table to reveal conflicting incentives

A company's debt promises a fixed amount while shareholders receive the residual after creditors have been paid. In a bad state limited liability can reduce shareholder payoff to zero. This asymmetry can tempt owners of a distressed firm to take extra risk with creditors' money.

Compare total firm payoffs as well as outcomes for debt and equity. A decision that increases expected equity while reducing total expected firm cash is an agency conflict. Without that two-claimant comparison a risky project can look like an attractive investment even while it destroys economic value.

Recognize debt overhang as a different mechanism

With existing risky debt, a valuable new safe investment can increase creditor recovery. If shareholders must contribute all the new money but receive little of the gain, they can refuse a positive-NPV project. This is called debt overhang or underinvestment.

Creditors and owners can sometimes negotiate a partial debt write-down, give new money a priority claim or agree on covenants and control rights. The proposed remedy should change the particular incentive and remain credible in bad states.

Information also affects financing choices

Managers may know more about company performance than outside investors. If investors suspect a share offering occurs when shares are overpriced, they may demand a discount. Internal funds avoid selling a security whose quality is hard for outsiders to judge. Relatively safe debt is often less sensitive to this information than equity.

This logic helps explain a possible financing order, but companies also issue equity for acquisitions, regulation, risk reduction and growth options. Evidence of an issue alone cannot establish managerial intent. Consider other observable predictions, including how cash holdings or information quality affect financing.

Method

Map the conflict before proposing a remedy

  1. Compute available value

    Write the asset payoff under each strategy in each state and attach stated probabilities. Deduct real investment costs at their dates.

  2. Allocate the waterfall

    Debt receives the smaller of the amount promised and the amount available. Equity receives any positive residual.

  3. Compare incentives

    Compare the change in total value with the change for the decision maker. Explain how a contract modification could move them into alignment.

Worked case

An owner declines a safe improvement

A nearly insolvent business will pay 60 next year and owes existing creditors 80. Shareholders can contribute 10 today to a safe project that adds 18 to next year's assets. Ignore discounting and other costs.

Working

  1. Without investment lenders receive 60 and owners receive zero. With investment assets become 78, still below the amount owed.
  2. Lenders then receive 78, an increase of 18. Owners receive zero and would have spent 10. Their own net payoff falls by 10.
  3. Total value rises by 18 − 10 = 8. If existing lenders agree that 12 of the new proceeds goes first to the contributing owners, owners gain 2 net and lenders receive 66, up 6.

Interpretation

The renegotiated allocation enables the project while distributing its 8 of surplus. The lender's priority agreement is a real contractual assumption.

A common mistake

Calling every manager–shareholder disagreement adverse selection.

How to correct it

Identify when private information or hidden action occurs. Risk shifting after debt has been advanced is an incentive problem even if all parties know the available project payoffs.

Quick check
Which loan provision most directly addresses this incentive after funding?

A covenant makes the risk change a contractual issue. The original example shows why owners might otherwise choose an activity that lowers total expected value.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A firm owes lenders 70. A safe activity returns 82 for certain. A gamble pays 130 or 10 with equal probabilities. Both require the same investment and there is no discounting.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    Compare total expected payoffs.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Compute equity and debt payoffs under both strategies.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Propose one response and explain the trade-off.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

The safe activity yields 82. The gamble yields 0.5 × 130 + 0.5 × 10 = 70, a decline of 12 in total expected value.

Part B 3 marks

Safe gives equity 12 and debt 70. With gambling equity receives 60 or 0, expected 30. Debt receives 70 or 10, expected 40. Equity gains 18 while creditors lose 30.

Part C 2 marks

A covenant requiring lender consent before changing the funded activity can limit risk shifting. Overly broad restrictions can also block worthwhile opportunities, so define permitted substitutes and an approval process.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A cash-poor firm rejects an investment with positive NPV. How would you distinguish adverse selection in equity issuance from debt overhang?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would first model what the existing lenders receive if the project succeeds. If a large share of the gain repairs a distressed loan, that is debt overhang. I would then examine whether outside investors lack information the managers possess and require a low issue price. That is the Myers–Majluf channel behind the pecking order. The diagnoses imply different negotiations and can coexist.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose current lenders gain €15m and old owners gain €3m from a €10m investment. What minimum additional allocation gets the owners to break even?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Old owners need another €7m of project payoff to reach their €10m contribution in present value. Existing creditors could give up €7m of their €15m gain and still improve by €8m. Above €7m the owners would strictly prefer investing.
