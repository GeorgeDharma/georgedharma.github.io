Professional workshop · C03

Approve a sorting line and size its four-year loan

Orme Packaging can automate a French sorting operation on 31 December 2027. The investment committee wants a single recommendation on the project and the amount of amortizing debt. The operating team has quoted earnings before rent and the bank has offered two financing packages. Test a downside that lowers contribution in every operating year. Keep euros in millions, show all year-end cash flows, and identify exactly what could force the company back to the committee.

What to submit
A signed-style capital allocation memorandum and a four-year operating, financing and lender-coverage exhibit for the investment committee and treasurer. All case terms are fictional.

From inputs to decision

01

Establish the incremental perimeter

Operational forecasts include a warehouse bay that the company could rent out. Evaluating only invoice expenditure would overstate the benefit. Capital providers need the same economic perimeter for both financing packages.

  1. Record initial spending of €12.00m equipment plus €1.20m in working capital. The year-zero unlevered investment is €13.20m.
  2. Subtract €0.20m forgone rent from 5.20, 5.60, 6.00 and 6.40. Annual operating cash before depreciation and tax becomes 5.00, 5.40, 5.80 and 6.20. The comparison assumes the alternative rental income would be taxable at the same rate.

Carry the four adjusted contributions into operating tax and covenant calculations. Keep financing proceeds outside project NPV.

02

Reconcile operating tax and closure

The lender and business owner use different cash definitions. The operating valuation must include investment recovery while the annual covenant expressly excludes closure cash.

  1. Deduct €3.00m annual depreciation for tax, giving operating taxable profit of 2.00, 2.40, 2.80 and 3.20. Unlevered tax is 0.50, 0.60, 0.70 and 0.80.
  2. Operating cash after unlevered tax is 4.50, 4.80, 5.10 and 5.40. The sale at 1.00 over zero tax basis yields 0.75 after tax. Add this and 1.20 released working capital only in 2031.

Record four unlevered free cash flows of 4.50, 4.80, 5.10 and 7.35. There is no continuing terminal business beyond the stated exit.

03

Value operations independently of the bank quote

The project's timing and risk differ from the loan promise. Using the lower bank coupon as the hurdle would inflate the apparent operating gain.

  1. Use the asset beta with the stated market premium. The unlevered operating return is 3% + 1.60 × 5% = 11%. Asset beta already reflects operating risk before leverage.
  2. Discount each operating cash flow. The sum of 4.50/1.11, 4.80/1.11², 5.10/1.11³ and 7.35/1.11⁴ is 16.5206. Subtract 13.20 for an unlevered NPV of 3.3206.

The line clears the operating hurdle before considering financing benefits. The committee can now compare funding proposals without changing the machine's forecast.

04

Build each debt and shield schedule

Equal principal instalments mean interest and deductions fall each year. Treating the initial loan as perpetual would overstate the tax benefit.

  1. For €8m debt, opening balances are 8, 6, 4 and 2, with €2m principal repaid annually. Interest is 0.48, 0.36, 0.24 and 0.12. Tax shields are 0.12, 0.09, 0.06 and 0.03.
  2. For €6m, repay €1.50m annually with opening balances of 6, 4.5, 3 and 1.5. Interest is 0.36, 0.27, 0.18 and 0.09. Multiply by 25% for shields of 0.09, 0.0675, 0.045 and 0.0225.

At 6% the present shield values are 0.26745 and 0.20059. Subtract the identical 0.18 upfront fee in each proposal.

05

Price benefits and test cash available for debt service

A loan can add tax value while making payments too aggressive for the bank's annual coverage test. Both the economic and contractual tests matter for approval.

  1. Base APV at €8m debt is 3.32059 + 0.26745 − 0.18 = 3.40804. At €6m it is 3.34118. The extra €2m debt buys just 0.06686 of additional present value under the case assumptions.
  2. For the €6m loan in 2028, actual cash tax is 0.50 − 0.09 = 0.41. The bank's numerator is 5.00 − 0.41 = 4.59, and debt service is 1.50 + 0.36 = 1.86. Coverage is 2.468×. Apply the same calculation each year, excluding exit cash.

The €6m loan passes in all base years. The €8m loan also passes in base with minimum coverage of 1.863× in 2028.

06

Run the uniform contribution downside

The scenario reduces contribution by 1.40 each year. Both loan schedules remain fully tax-usable under the inputs, including the first stress year. Test the stress before choosing leverage.

  1. After tax the free-cash reduction is 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05 annually. Its total present value is 1.05 × the 11% four-year annuity factor of 3.10245 = 3.25757. Operating downside NPV is 0.06302.
  2. Downside APV is 0.15047 for €8m and 0.08361 for €6m. In 2028 the smaller loan gives (4.50 − 1.05 + 0.09)/1.86 = 1.903×. The bigger gives (4.50 − 1.05 + 0.12)/2.48 = 1.440×. Later debt service falls and operating earnings grow in this case.

The €8m case breaches the specified 1.50× stress limit in 2028. A positive project APV cannot cure that contractual funding constraint.

07

Issue approval terms and establish the re-review threshold

The director needs a funding amount and an observable escalation trigger. The treasurer needs the amount of internal equity cash that must be available on the closing date.

  1. For €6m debt the company puts up 13.20 + 0.18 − 6 = 7.38 at close. Require confirmation of this cash source and of rent and sale assumptions before committing.
  2. Let D be the initial debt in millions. Stressed 2028 coverage is (3.45 + 0.015D)/(0.25D + 0.06D). Setting this equal to 1.50 gives D = 7.6667. This is a first-year covenant ceiling under the scenario, independent of what bankers may actually offer.

Approve the €6m package, condition approval on maintaining internal cash and return to committee if the operating forecast deteriorates enough to eliminate the small positive downside APV.

Completed document

Investment committee decision memorandum · Orme Packaging

Decision requested

Approve the sorting line at €13.20m initial operating investment and select the €6m term loan. Authorize €7.38m of internal cash including the financing fee. Record the larger bank proposal as a tested alternative.

Economic case

Operating NPV is €3.321m at 11%. Financing adds €0.021m net under the selected structure, giving base APV of €3.341m. Value is predominantly generated by operating cash. The result does not depend on treating borrowed principal as a gain.

Bankability and downside

A €1.40m annual contribution loss reduces selected-case APV to €0.084m but leaves first-year coverage at 1.903×. The €8m loan offers €0.067m more base value and breaches the downside covenant at 1.440×. The committee's downside requirement favours the smaller facility.

Conditions and follow-up

Treasury will confirm the €7.38m cash allocation and written loan schedule before signing. Operations will compare monthly contribution after rent with the approved ramp. Reforecast cash, shield usability and coverage if contract volume slips or disposal and working-capital recovery change materially.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

At €6m debt, what is stressed first-year coverage if the contribution shortfall is €1.00m instead of €1.40m?

See the full solution

The after-tax cash reduction is 1.00 × 0.75 = 0.75. Starting from selected-case base available cash of 4.59, stressed cash is 3.84. Divide by 1.86 of service for 2.0645×, above 1.50×.

Task 2

What is the largest initial loan satisfying exactly 1.50× stressed first-year coverage in the stated €1.40m scenario?

See the full solution

At year 1 interest is 0.06D and principal 0.25D. The tax shield contributes 0.015D to unlevered stress cash of 3.45. Solve 3.45 + 0.015D = 1.50 × 0.31D. This gives D = 3.45/0.45 = 7.6667, before checking other limits.

Task 3

What maximum annual pre-tax contribution loss drives selected-case APV to zero, assuming the tax benefit on the loss continues to be usable?

See the full solution

Divide base APV of 3.34118 by the after-tax four-year factor, 0.75 × 3.10245. The answer is about €1.4359m per year. The €1.40m scenario lies close to this economic threshold.

Task 4

Why would an alternative policy of continually refinancing debt at constant leverage require a fresh APV analysis?

See the full solution

The tax shield timing and risk would differ from the scheduled 6, 4.5, 3 and 1.5 opening balances. Reforecast debt, interest, deduction use, refinancing costs and covenant service. The old shield PV and fixed first-year ceiling cannot be assumed to describe the new policy.
