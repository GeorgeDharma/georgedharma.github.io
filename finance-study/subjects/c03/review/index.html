Capital investment, tax and financing cash flows

The existing units construct incremental cash flows, discount mutually exclusive projects, isolate financing side effects, and compare two machines with fixed unequal lives. They supply the ingredients for a lease-versus-buy comparison and a decision about when to retire a single repeatable machine.

Business planning, retained earnings and funding

Payout policy already tracks the allocation of cash to owners, the operating budget translates sales into production needs, and treasury forecasts the timing of cash shortages. Planning the year-end balance sheet adds a distinct estimate of external capital needed to support a sales target.

New and expanded lessons

C03.11

Lease or buy an asset after tax

The course discounts incremental projects and explains interest tax shields in APV. It had no worked lease-versus-borrow-and-buy decision using mutually exclusive tax deductions, disposal proceeds and a break-even rental quotation.

  • Build separate present-cost schedules for leasing and owning the same service capacity.
  • Calculate the maximum rent worth paying and interpret the effect of changing payment dates.

C03.12

Finding the economic age for replacing equipment

The investment-criteria lesson compares two different machines with predetermined service lives. This chapter varies the discretionary disposal age of one machine and compares a defender's next-year keeping cost with the best replacement cycle.

  • Select a repeatable replacement age using incremental maintenance and age-dependent resale values.
  • Decide whether to keep an installed machine another year using its foregone sale price and marginal cost.

C03.13

Forecasting the external funds needed for growth

Corporate payout and budgeting lessons explain dividends, sales forecasts and cash timing. They did not reconcile a forecast increase in assets to trade credit and retained profits while adjusting explicitly for spare fixed capacity.

  • Estimate additional funds needed from a forecast balance sheet and proposed payout policy.
  • Use existing plant capacity correctly and distinguish the implied financing gap from peak cash needs.
