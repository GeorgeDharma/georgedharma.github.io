Priority and recovery

Apply the assumed payment order to distributable proceeds. A claim can recover only what remains after higher-priority claims have been satisfied.

The starting point

Priority is the assumed order in which claims receive scarce proceeds. A payment waterfall fills the senior claim first, then passes any remainder to the next rank. Equally ranked claims generally share in proportion to their claims when the case terms specify equal treatment.

Use cash available after any costs already taken out. For each rank, divide the amount distributed by the outstanding claim to calculate its recovery rate. Real priority depends on governing law, security and the relevant asset pool. Build the waterfall using the facts supplied.

Theory and method

Absolute-priority waterfall and the junior debt tranche

Pari passu distribution · Capped residual claim

How can junior debt gain value from more uncertain asset outcomes even if average proceeds stay fixed?

A junior lender is paid only after senior claims and receives at most its own face amount. As the estate grows, junior recovery is initially zero, then rises euro for euro over a middle range, then stops at face. Consequently expected junior recovery depends on the distribution of outcomes and where those thresholds sit. Computing a waterfall on the mean estate can hide the difference between a sure moderate recovery and a mixture of very poor and very strong recoveries.

How the result is built

Junior(V) = min[J, max(V − S, 0)] = max(V − S, 0) − max(V − S − J, 0)

V is distributable estate value after costs, S senior claims, J junior allowed face, and Junior(V) the euro cash paid to juniors. The max terms enforce the senior threshold and junior face cap.

  1. With €8m senior and €4m junior, at V = €6m all cash goes to seniors. At €10m the junior gets €2m. At €14m junior is capped at €4m.
  2. For evenly likely €6m and €12m estates, junior expected payout is half of zero plus half of €4m, or €2m. A certain €9m estate, the same mean, would pay junior only €1m.
  3. The example crosses the lower threshold. If outcomes straddle the upper cap instead, greater spread can reduce expected junior recovery. Always evaluate all states.

When the reasoning holds

  • The waterfall assumes the stated absolute priority and one common estate. Competing collateral pools, administrative costs or negotiated deviations alter thresholds.
  • The probabilities and valuation date need to be consistent. If distributions arrive at different dates, discount the class’s actual payout after applying each state waterfall.
Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
First example

The case offers €9m after costs. Senior claims total €6m, and two equal-ranking junior claims are €4m and €2m. Senior debt receives €6m. The remaining €3m pays juniors at 50%, so they receive €2m and €1m respectively.

A euro is paid once

A priority waterfall distributes a fixed pool among separate ranks. Start with proceeds available after the costs the problem says must be deducted. Pay the highest rank up to the amount of its allowed claim. Pass only the remaining pool to the next rank. Keep any unallocated residue for the last entitled party.

Within one rank, claims may share proportionally under the assumed rules. If two equally ranked claims of €6m and €4m share €5m, they recover €3m and €2m. Their euro recoveries differ while their recovery percentages match. A contractual cap can change the amount entering the class.

Collateral may create a separate pool

A secured loan has a claim against specified collateral under the case terms. Net collateral proceeds might not cover the full loan. The unpaid shortfall, called a deficiency, may or may not share in the unsecured pool depending on the stipulated law and agreement. State the assumed treatment before calculating.

A guarantee or cash held elsewhere can add a second recovery route, but cannot generally make a creditor recover more than its total allowed claim in the simple case. Apply previous payouts when computing the amount left outstanding and track which entity supplied each euro.

What is being quoted as recovery?

Recovery rate equals proceeds actually distributed to a class divided by its allowed face claim. A bond trading at 40 cents on the euro is a purchase price. If ultimate recovery is 60 cents, the buyer’s gross return on price is different from the 60% recovery rate on face.

Time matters when comparing alternatives. A nominal 70% recovery after several years may be economically worse than 65% today. Keep a nominal waterfall and then discount dated payouts separately. Ranks and legal remedies are jurisdiction-specific. The teaching case supplies its rules.

Method

Run and check the waterfall

  1. Open each pool

    Identify net proceeds, claims, security and the treatment of deficiencies. Deduct costs exactly once. Keep restricted collateral and general cash separate where required.

  2. Pay rank by rank

    Distribute the lesser of available pool and allowed claims. For equal ranks sharing a short pool, allocate in proportion to allowed claims. Carry only the residual onward.

  3. Check conservation

    Add creditor and owner receipts and compare with distributed proceeds. Confirm no claim exceeds its allowed amount and divide by the correct face value for percentage recovery.

Worked case

A secured shortfall competes with trade claims

A secured lender is owed €10m. Its collateral yields €7m net. The case permits its unpaid €3m to participate equally with €5m trade claims in a separate €4m general pool. There are no other costs, priorities or claims.

Working

  1. Pay €7m collateral to the secured lender. Its remaining €3m joins €5m trade debt, for €8m of equal-ranking unsecured claims.
  2. The €4m pool pays 50% of each claim. The lender receives an additional €1.5m and trades receive €2.5m. No residual reaches shareholders.
  3. The lender’s total recovery is €8.5m, 85% of its original €10m. Trade recovery is €2.5m/€5m = 50%. Total distributions of €11m match €7m + €4m available.

Interpretation

If the deficiency were excluded, the trade recovery would instead be €4m/€5m = 80%. The governing treatment changes distribution without adding economic value.

A common mistake

Placing the full €10m secured loan in the general pool after it has already received €7m from collateral.

How to correct it

Carry the actual remaining deficiency into the eligible pool. Reconcile all routes of recovery to the original allowed claim.

Quick check
A simple waterfall has €8m distributable, €5m senior debt and €6m junior debt, with senior paid first. What is the junior recovery rate?

After paying the senior €5m, €3m remains for €6m of junior claims. Junior recovery is €3m/€6m, not the junior share of the total proceeds.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A €12m secured lender receives €9m from collateral. Its deficiency joins €3m trade debt equally in a €4.2m unsecured pool. No other claim exists.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate total lender and trade recoveries.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    State the lender’s recovery rate and check the distribution.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The deficiency is €3m, making €6m equal claims sharing €4.2m at 70%. The lender receives €9m + €2.1m = €11.1m, while trade receives €2.1m.

Part B 3 marks

Lender recovery is €11.1m/€12m = 92.5%. Total receipts are €13.2m, exactly the €9m collateral plus €4.2m general pool. Trade recovers 70% of face.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can junior debt gain value from more uncertain asset outcomes even if average proceeds stay fixed?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would draw the individual claim before calculating its expectation. A junior lender with a €4m claim behind €8m senior receives nothing below eight and everything between eight and twelve, then no more. In the €6m/€12m example it expects €2m despite average estate of €9m implying only €1m in a certain outcome. The distribution matters because the claim has both a floor and a cap.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Keep expected assets at €12m. Compare a certain €12m with equally likely €10m and €14m. Does junior benefit from this spread?

Compare with the follow-up answer

At €12m for certain the junior class receives its full €4m. At €10m it gets €2m and at €14m it is still capped at €4m, averaging €3m. Here extra variability transfers expected value away from junior at its upper cap.
