Going-concern value

Value a reorganized business after operating changes. Distinguish sustainable operating value from the separate distribution of that value among creditors.

The starting point

A going concern is a business that continues operating. Its value rests on cash customers will generate after paying operating costs, tax and the investment needed to sustain operations. A rescue plan must explain why these cash flows will improve and who funds the transition.

Calculate operating value first. Distribution of that value among lenders, rescue investors and shareholders is a different question. Compare the continuation plan with liquidation on a common date, including necessary rescue costs that neither cash-flow forecast already contains.

Theory and method

Adjusted present value of a reorganized business

APV · Financing side effects

A turnaround plan changes the amount of debt every year. How would you keep operating value separate from the financing plan?

A chain of clinics can be worth keeping open because patients, leases and staff generate repeat operating cash together. Replacing expensive debt can also create tax benefits while introducing fees and failure risks. Adjusted present value values the operations as if financed independently of the proposed debt, then separately prices incremental financing effects. This is useful when leverage changes materially through a rescue and a single constant capital structure assumption would be difficult to defend.

How the result is built

APV = Vᵤ + PV(TS) − PV(FC) − I₀

Vᵤ is the present value of unlevered after-tax operating cash, TS incremental usable interest tax shields, FC incremental financing or expected distress costs omitted from Vᵤ, and I₀ immediate rescue investment outside those forecasts. PV converts cash on its actual dates to today.

  1. A reorganized company produces €1.2m sustained unlevered cash starting in one year. At a 12% unlevered required return and zero growth, Vᵤ is €10m.
  2. Tax shields already priced on a risk-appropriate basis add €0.7m present value. Distinct financing costs of €0.4m PV and an unmodeled upfront €1.4m rescue cost reduce total value.
  3. APV is €10m + €0.7m − €0.4m − €1.4m = €8.9m. Against €8.5m net liquidation, the plan creates €0.4m under the stated assumptions.

When the reasoning holds

  • Tax shields require taxable profits or another usable tax basis. A distressed borrower with persistent losses may defer or forfeit the assumed benefit.
  • Financing distress costs excluded from operating cash may be deducted separately. Lost customers already removed from the operating forecast cannot be deducted a second time.
Terms in this lesson
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Working capital
Money tied up in day-to-day operations. For a deal, agree which receivables, stock and payables count.
Perpetuity
A stream of payments assumed to continue indefinitely under the model.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
First example

A reorganized service business could generate stable cash of €1.2m a year forever, beginning one year from now. With no growth and a 12% discount rate, operating value is €10m. A required €1.5m upfront rescue cost leaves €8.5m versus €7m net liquidation today.

Value a business that survives

A reorganized company can keep relationships, staff, technology and systems that sell for less if scattered. Going-concern value measures the economic benefit of keeping that organization together. Start with cash the operations could sustainably generate after taxes, necessary investment and working capital under a credible turnaround.

A rescue has to pay for the path to those cash flows. If a new machine, severance payment or supplier catch-up is required today, include it unless the forecast already contains it. The cost of new money concerns economic resource use, how rescuers divide the resulting value is a separate financing question.

Build the future from operating drivers

Model what changes in units sold, realized prices, resource costs and capacity. A statement that margins will return to normal is inadequate if major customers left or skilled employees must be rehired. Compare the scale implied by the forecasts with supply constraints, working capital and the implementation timetable.

A constant perpetuity pays the same cash every year beginning one period from the valuation date. Its present value is annual cash divided by the required return under the stated perpetual, stable assumptions. If cash is delayed an extra year, discount the perpetuity one further period. Businesses with finite contracts require finite forecasts.

Compare continuation with disposition

Compare net values belonging to the same stakeholder group at the same date. Liquidation can release cash immediately, while operating value can take time to deliver. Include disposal costs in liquidation and rescue funding in continuation. Do not deduct existing debt on one alternative and omit it from the other.

Sensitivity matters because the decision can turn on a small long-run margin assumption. Compute the operating cash needed to break even relative to liquidation. Then ask if a credible plan can deliver and sustain it without hidden investment or additional risk.

Method

Value the rescue in three stages

  1. Stabilize cash

    Build a transition budget through the first sustainable year. Record implementation losses, asset sales and rescue investments once on their correct dates.

  2. Price continuing operations

    Use cash available to the relevant claimants. Apply a risk-consistent discount rate and realistic duration or terminal assumption. Explain capital replacement and working-capital needs.

  3. Compare net alternatives

    Subtract rescue costs not already in operating value. Compare with a similarly net liquidation figure and determine which uncertain operating input would reverse the decision.

Worked case

A printing business restructures

The plant can liquidate for €7m net today. A rescue costs €1m now. Beginning in one year it could generate €0.9m of stable annual operating cash after all necessary maintenance, indefinitely. Use a 10% required return, no growth and no other items.

Working

  1. Going-concern operating value is €0.9m/0.10 = €9m. Subtract the upfront rescue cost to obtain €8m of net continuation value.
  2. The rescue leads by €1m relative to €7m liquidation. Break-even annual cash is (€7m + €1m) × 10% = €0.8m with the same date and risk assumptions.
  3. If cash can start only in year two, first discount the €9m perpetuity one additional year to €8.18m, then subtract €1m. Continuation leads liquidation by only about €0.18m before any cost of waiting.

Interpretation

The business case does not tell which old creditor should fund the rescue or receive the upside. That requires a separate claim-by-claim analysis of bargaining and priority.

A common mistake

Capitalizing EBITDA as if it were perpetual distribution cash after tax, investment and working-capital needs.

How to correct it

Convert operating earnings into sustainable cash available to all capital providers. A printing business must eventually replace presses even if current depreciation is noncash.

Quick check
A rescue creates €9m of operating value but requires €3m of new cash today that is excluded from that valuation. Immediate liquidation yields €7m. Which has higher economic value under these assumptions?

The rescue delivers €9m − €3m = €6m net. Liquidation delivers €7m immediately. Comparing €9m with €7m would omit the rescue funding cost.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A rescue requires €2m today and would generate a constant €1.4m each year starting in one year. Use 10%. Net liquidation today is €11m.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compare both alternatives and find the break-even sustainable annual cash.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What if normalized annual cash is €1.2m because replacement investment was omitted?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Continuation net value is €1.4m/0.10 − €2m = €12m, €1m above liquidation. Matching €11m requires (€11m + €2m) × 10% = €1.3m annually.

Part B 3 marks

Revised net continuation is €12m − €2m = €10m. Liquidation now leads by €1m. Check equipment condition and maintenance needs rather than changing the discount rate to conceal missing expenditure.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A turnaround plan changes the amount of debt every year. How would you keep operating value separate from the financing plan?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would value the operating turnaround independently, then add the financing benefits the group can actually use and subtract the costs that have not yet entered the model. Here APV gives €8.9m versus €8.5m liquidation. I would examine the €0.7m tax shield first because the borrowers need sufficient usable taxable earnings. A new debt structure alone cannot make an unproductive operation profitable.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Only €0.2m of the €0.7m tax shield is usable. What changes for creditors considering the plan?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Rescue value falls by €0.5m to €8.4m, below the €8.5m liquidation benchmark. Distributing the rescue differently can change individual votes but does not recover that total €0.1m deficit. The plan needs better operating economics, lower costs or a revised credible liquidation benchmark.
