Liquidation value

Estimate proceeds, disposal costs and delay. Converting realizations into present value makes comparison with an immediate offer meaningful.

The starting point

Liquidation means selling assets and distributing what remains after costs. Begin with expected selling prices in the actual sale conditions. A machine’s book value may assume years of continued use. A forced auction, specialist buyer or costly removal can result in very different cash proceeds.

Map when each recovery will arrive and deduct costs at their expected dates. Compare alternatives on the same date and basis. A high amount expected after two years can be worth less than a smaller certain amount immediately.

Theory and method

Net orderly liquidation value and the hold-versus-sell threshold

Forced-sale valuation · Disposition carrying costs

How would you decide between an immediate distressed auction and an orderly sale six months later?

A specialist press can attract a higher price once technicians prepare it and buyers inspect it. Waiting also consumes rent, security, insurance and sale fees. Compare the immediate net cash with the present value of the later net receipts after paying carrying costs on their actual dates. A broker’s quoted orderly price is evidence about gross proceeds. The creditor needs the value that remains available for the estate and the cash to survive until then.

How the result is built

G* = [(N + H₀)(1 + r)ᵀ + Fₜ]/(1 − f)

G* is the future gross sale price needed for indifference, N immediate net offer, H₀ carrying expense paid now, r annual effective discount rate, T elapsed years, Fₜ fixed sale costs at exit and f proportional sale fee.

  1. Set immediate net value equal to the delayed choice. The latter equals future proceeds after percentage and fixed fees, discounted to today, less today’s carrying expense.
  2. Multiply both sides by the discount factor’s inverse and solve for gross price. The carrying cost raises the proceeds needed even if the equipment’s quoted selling price is higher.
  3. With €4m immediate net, €0.2m carrying cost now, €0.1m exit cost, 5% selling fee and a one-year 10% required return, the threshold is (€4.2m × 1.10 + €0.1m)/0.95 = €4.968m.

When the reasoning holds

  • The timing of carrying expenses and exit fees is specified. Monthly payments would need their own present-value schedule.
  • The sale-price forecast and carrying period must be jointly plausible. A high quote requiring a longer marketing period may also increase costs and execution risk.
Terms in this lesson
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
First example

A machine is expected to sell for €1.2m in one year, with €0.1m in sale costs paid then. At a 10% discount rate, net recovery today is (€1.2m − €0.1m)/1.10 = €1m. A €1.03m cash offer today beats it under those forecasts.

What could the assets actually fetch?

Liquidation replaces a continuing operation with proceeds from selling its resources and collecting claims. Start with saleable quantities and likely buyers. Specialized equipment may fetch less if removed from its site. Inventory sold in bulk might realise a lower price than if it were sold through an operating retail network.

A receivable’s book face value is a promise, not cash in hand. Estimate recoverable amounts by customer quality, disputes and collection time. Likewise, proceeds from a building may require environmental work and broker fees. State which costs are already netted so they do not appear twice.

Time and liquidity discounts are different

A rushed sale can command a lower expected selling price because few bidders can inspect or finance the asset in time. Separately, a given expected receipt next year is worth less today than an otherwise comparable immediate payment. Estimate the sale-price effect first and then discount the resulting timed cash.

A payment due to a professional adviser today and a percentage selling fee paid at closing belong on different dates. Discounting sale receipts but deducting all costs at their full amounts today can bias the comparison. Document whether returns are gross, net and risk-adjusted.

From asset values to creditor recoveries

The total liquidation value available to claimants is not a particular creditor’s recovery. Allocate net proceeds to the appropriate legal entity and collateral pool, then apply the agreed ranking and secured claim caps. A bank with security on the building may have no exclusive claim on inventory.

Compare liquidation with a going-concern sale or immediate cash offer on the same basis. If the immediate offer includes taking responsibility for environmental cleanup, avoid charging that cleanup to the seller again. Sensitivity to realisation rates, timing and disposal expenses reveals the fragile parts of the estimate.

Method

Construct a realization schedule

  1. Estimate units and prices

    List quantities, receivable exposure and expected recovery fractions. Document any market evidence and whether pricing assumes orderly or forced disposal.

  2. Place cash on dates

    Show gross sale proceeds, taxes or fees specified by the case, and carrying expenses. Subtract each item in its own period and discount net flows consistently.

  3. Apply priorities afterwards

    Keep net asset value distinct from the subsequent legal waterfall. Compare alternative realization plans before allocating them to creditors.

Worked case

A two-stage warehouse liquidation

A warehouse business has €2m receivables. It expects to collect 75% in one year and pay €0.1m collection costs then. Equipment can sell today for €0.8m net. Immediate wind-down expenses are €0.2m. Use a 10% one-year discount rate.

Working

  1. Receivables generate €1.5m gross in one year and €1.4m after collection expenses. Their present value is €1.4m/1.10 = €1.273m.
  2. Add €0.8m of net equipment proceeds and subtract €0.2m upfront to obtain €1.873m present liquidation value. Keep sufficient precision until the final comparison.
  3. An immediate buyer offering €1.9m cash net of all these same charges beats the estimated liquidation by approximately €0.027m. That is a thin margin relative to plausible collection uncertainty.

Interpretation

If only 60% of receivables are recovered, present liquidation value falls to (€1.2m − €0.1m)/1.10 + €0.6m = €1.6m. A tiny change in timing alone is not the only risk.

A common mistake

Comparing gross proceeds one year from now with an immediate offer already net of fees.

How to correct it

Build separate dated, net cash schedules for both options. Discount to today and disclose the realisation and cost assumptions that drive the difference.

Quick check
Liquidation should yield €5.5m in one year after all costs. At a 10% discount rate, which immediate net cash amount has equal present value?

Divide €5.5m by 1.10. An immediate €5.0m and a €5.5m net receipt in one year have equal value under the stated discount rate.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

Assets could generate €3.6m gross in one year with €0.3m selling costs then and €0.2m holding cost now. The discount rate is 10%. An immediate net offer is €2.9m.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Which has the higher present value and by how much?

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the gross year-end proceeds needed to match €2.9m.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Delayed sale is (€3.6m − €0.3m)/1.10 − €0.2m = €2.8m. The €2.9m net offer leads by €0.1m.

Part B 3 marks

Solve (gross − €0.3m)/1.10 − €0.2m = €2.9m. Required gross proceeds are €3.71m. Investigate evidence for reaching that price before accepting the delay.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How would you decide between an immediate distressed auction and an orderly sale six months later?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would turn the sale options into dated net cash. This one-year example needs just under €4.97m gross to beat €4m net today. A €5m orderly valuation leaves less than €30,000 of margin after costs and discounting. I would check closing evidence and the estate’s ability to fund the holding period before asking creditors to take that risk.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The buyer offers €4.85m gross in one year and the receivers can reduce today’s holding expense to €0.10m. Does waiting now win?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The delayed value is (€4.85m × 95% − €0.10m)/1.10 − €0.10m ≈ €4.00m, specifically €3.998m. It still falls slightly below €4m. A tiny unsupported change in price, fees or dates matters at this boundary.
