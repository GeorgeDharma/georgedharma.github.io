Corporate Distress and Restructuring

Creditor map

Trace legal borrowers, collateral, guarantees, ranking and maturities. Group-level value does not automatically flow to every creditor of a subsidiary.

The starting point

A group chart shows ownership. A creditor map shows who owes whom, which assets support each promise and which party guaranteed it. Start with each legal borrower. A parent can own a subsidiary’s shares without directly owning its machines or bank deposits.

Trace the loan and guarantee documents, collateral, maturity and claims that compete for the same assets. In a simple group without cross-guarantees, a parent lender may only reach the value left in subsidiary shares after subsidiary creditors are satisfied. Different agreements change this answer.

Theory and method

Structural subordination and guarantee deficiency

Legal-entity recovery map · Double-recovery cap

How can a guarantee make a subsidiary lender more valuable while leaving the group’s total asset value unchanged?

A lender to a subsidiary first looks to its borrower’s assets. An enforceable capped parent guarantee can give it access to parent assets for a shortfall. That added route can improve recovery at the expense of creditors who previously relied on the parent’s estate. Consolidated value need not change. The analysis must identify the direction of the guarantee, remaining deficiency and competing claims in each pool. A guarantee creates another route to the same underlying debt.

How the result is built

G = min(K, max(0, D − Rₛ)). Parent distribution to guaranty claim = G × min(1, Aₚ/(P + G))

D is original subsidiary debt, Rₛ its primary-borrower recovery, K the guarantee cap, G the allowed guarantee claim, Aₚ the parent's available estate, and P other equal-ranking parent claims. All inputs are euros.

  1. A subsidiary lender owed €10m recovers €7m, leaving €3m unpaid. A €2m parent guarantee admits at most a €2m additional claim in the parent estate.
  2. Parent has €3m to distribute against €4m other claims plus €2m guarantee claims. All share 50% under the stated equal-ranking rule.
  3. The subsidiary lender receives another €1m for €8m total. Other parent creditors recover €2m. Without the guarantee those creditors would have received the full €3m estate.

When the reasoning holds

  • The guarantee is valid and ranks equally with the stated parent claims. Security, priority or enforceability can give a different waterfall.
  • The example uses only the remaining deficiency and enforces a cap on total recovery. Intercompany reimbursement and subrogation rights are excluded in this simplified final allocation.
Terms in this lesson
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
First example

A subsidiary’s assets produce €9m cash after sale costs. Its own creditors receive €7m under the case terms. The parent owns 100% and has no other claim or guarantee. Its shares can contribute €2m toward the parent’s separate debts, subject to the assumed distribution terms.

Identify the legal borrower before adding values

A creditor lends to a legal person. An operating subsidiary might own plant and customer receivables, while a holding company owns shares in that subsidiary. Consolidated financial statements combine the group’s accounts for reporting. They do not automatically combine the groups of creditors entitled to each asset pool.

For each claim, identify borrower, guarantors, collateral, ranking, maturity and outstanding amount. A guarantee can create a second route to recovery subject to its terms. An intercompany loan can make a parent a creditor of a subsidiary as well as its shareholder. The applicable agreements and law govern reach and priority.

Why structural position matters

With no cross-guarantee and no parent-owned intercompany claim, a parent creditor can generally look to the parent’s own assets under the hypothetical terms. The subsidiary’s assets first support its own creditors. Only residual share value, if any, contributes to the parent’s pool.

Security can attach to particular property, while unsecured creditors share other value according to the assumed priorities. A guarantee adds a claim, not a second entitlement to recover more than the amount owed. Apply contractual caps and count prior recoveries before calculating any remaining deficiency.

Use a map, then a waterfall

A diagram helps locate legal relationships, but the numerical work requires a separate waterfall for each pool. Start where assets actually sit. Deduct costs as specified, pay claims by the stated order, then move any residual or guarantee recovery to the correct claimant.

Timing matters if several entities have different processes or cash restrictions. A parent might ultimately recover residual value from a subsidiary yet have no free cash this week. A recovery estimate and a dated cash forecast answer different questions, so a creditor should request both.

Method

Trace one monetary unit

  1. Locate each asset

    List cash and net realizations by entity. Mark shared names, common directors and consolidated figures as background, not evidence of legal ownership.

  2. Attach the claims

    Record security, guarantees and intercompany balances with their limits. Check whether creditor and debtor actually signed the same obligations being attributed to them.

  3. Distribute and reconcile

    Pay each pool according to the case rules, move residual equity or permitted guarantee proceeds onward, and verify total receipts do not exceed available value or the claim.

Worked case

A hotel group with a parent facility

A hotel subsidiary realizes €12m after sale costs. Its own senior lender is owed €8m and suppliers €2m, paid in that order. Parent owns all its shares, holds €1m cash and owes €5m under a separate unsecured facility. No guarantees, intercompany loans or other claims exist.

Working

  1. The subsidiary pays €8m senior and €2m suppliers, leaving €2m to its parent as shareholder. Subsidiary creditors are paid in full.
  2. Parent’s pool is €2m residual plus €1m cash = €3m. Its €5m lender recovers €3m, or 60%. Group realizations are €13m, but the parent creditor cannot reach all of them directly.
  3. If the hotel sells for €9m instead, the senior recovers €8m, suppliers €1m and no residual reaches parent. The parent lender now recovers only its €1m pool, or 20%.

Interpretation

A parent guarantee of subsidiary debt would be a different direction of support from a subsidiary guarantee of parent debt. Read the identity of the guarantor before redistributing any value.

A common mistake

Adding subsidiary assets to the parent pool while also including the parent’s interest in subsidiary shares.

How to correct it

Value the shares as a residual claim. The same hotel cannot be both distributed directly and counted again in the share value received by the parent.

Quick check
A parent owes €5m. A subsidiary owns a valuable building. What fact most changes whether the parent lender has a direct contractual claim on that building’s proceeds?

Consolidated reporting combines financial information. The borrower, guarantee and collateral documents determine direct claims against a particular entity or asset.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A subsidiary has €7m net assets available, €5m senior debt and €1m suppliers ahead of equity. Its parent owns 100%, has €0.4m other assets and €2m parent debt. There are no guarantees.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    What does the parent lender recover?

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What changes if net subsidiary proceeds rise €0.8m?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

After €6m subsidiary claims, €1m remains for the parent. Total parent assets are €1.4m, paying 70% of the €2m facility.

Part B 3 marks

Subsidiary residual grows to €1.8m, so parent assets reach €2.2m. Parent debt caps recovery at €2m and €0.2m remains for parent equity. No creditor receives more than its claim.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a guarantee make a subsidiary lender more valuable while leaving the group’s total asset value unchanged?

Compare with a spoken answer

I would work by entity. The subsidiary’s €7m recovery leaves €3m of the debt unpaid. Only €2m is covered by the parent guarantee, and that claim shares a short estate, so the lender receives €1m more. The arrangement transfers recoverable value from other parent lenders. I would never credit the whole guaranteed face as an additional asset without applying the parent waterfall.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the primary borrower now pays €9.5m. How much can the guarantee add under the same parent estate?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Deficiency shrinks to €0.5m, below the cap. Parent claims become €4.5m and the €3m estate pays two-thirds. The guarantee pays about €0.333m and the lender receives about €9.833m in total. The other parent creditors receive roughly €2.667m.
