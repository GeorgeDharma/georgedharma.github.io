Counting Chapter 11 votes and testing a dissenting creditor’s recovery

Apply the two voting thresholds to a class of US Chapter 11 claims.

Theory and terminology

Chapter 11 class voting and confirmation

best interests of creditors · US bankruptcy · cramdown

What you should be able to explain

  • Apply the two voting thresholds to a class of US Chapter 11 claims.
  • Calculate a dissenting holder’s present-value recovery under a plan and a hypothetical liquidation.

Build a voting register by class

An impaired claim has legal rights changed under a plan, subject to the governing legal definition. The class groups sufficiently similar claims under an approved classification. Begin a ballot schedule with each eligible allowed claim, its voting amount and its valid yes or no ballot. The examples use ordinary US corporate Chapter 11, one allowed claim per holder, established classes, and no special ballot rulings.

Under section 1126(c), class acceptance requires at least two-thirds of the amount voting and more than one-half in number of the claims voting. Abstentions do not enter either denominator. Exactly two-thirds of amount qualifies. Exactly half of count fails. Different rules apply to equity interests. A class with deemed treatment or a disputed ballot needs separate handling.

Give a dissenter a separate column

Under the usual section 1129(a)(7) comparison for an impaired class, a holder that has not accepted must receive at least the value it would receive under a hypothetical Chapter 7 liquidation on the effective date. A majority vote cannot replace that holder-level comparison. For simplicity the cases exclude special secured-creditor elections.

Build net liquidation proceeds after sale costs and claims ahead of the holder. Include the time and risk of collecting plan payments. A note promising 60 cents in two years is worth less today if an appropriate discount rate is positive. Compare like claims, currencies and dates.

Test execution as well as the ballots

A class’s yes vote establishes one input to confirmation. Feasibility generally requires that confirmation is unlikely to be followed by another reorganization or liquidation, except where the plan proposes one. A cash forecast must support the promised payments through their actual due dates.

A dissenting class can require consideration of section 1129(b), including fair-and-equitable treatment and absence of unfair discrimination, together with other applicable requirements. Where section 1129(a)(10) applies, at least one impaired class of claims must accept without counting insider acceptances. The court ultimately decides confirmation.

Method

Build the result

Acceptance requires Yₐ/Vₐ ≥ 2/3 and Yₙ/Vₙ > 1/2; plan PV = cash now + future cash/(1+r)ᵗ.

Yₐ and Vₐ are the yes and total valid voting amounts in one class; Yₙ and Vₙ are the corresponding counts. PV is present value in currency per claim or per dollar of claim, r the appropriate annual rate and t years to payment.

  1. Remove abstentions from voting amounts and counts, and mark any court-directed treatment.
  2. Calculate both ratios independently. Record class acceptance only when both thresholds pass.
  3. For each relevant nonaccepting holder, discount the proposed property and compare it with its liquidation distribution. Check feasibility separately.
Worked case

A large yes bloc falls one ballot short

Six allowed creditors vote on a typical corporate plan. Three yes claims are $50m, $15m and $5m. Three no claims are $20m, $6m and $4m. Another $10m claim abstains. All receive $0.24 now and $0.33 in a year per dollar owed. Use 10% to value the deferred amount. Liquidation pays $0.52 now.

Working

  1. Voting debt is $100m, of which $70m supports the plan. The 70% amount test passes.
  2. Three of six valid ballots are yes. The 50% count fails because a strict majority is required. The $10m abstention changes neither fraction.
  3. Plan value is 0.24 + 0.33/1.10 = $0.54 per dollar. A $4m dissenter would receive present value of $2.16m versus $2.08m in liquidation.
  4. If the $4m claimant switches to yes, the class reaches 74% by amount and four-sixths by count. The other nonaccepting impaired holders still need the applicable protection.

Decision and interpretation

Report initial rejection despite the positive recovery comparison. A proposed vote change addresses only the class result. Forecast liquidity on the deferred-payment date before making a feasibility argument.

Quick check
A voting class has 66⅔% by amount in favor and five of ten voting claims in favor. What is its ordinary section 1126(c) result?

Exactly two-thirds meets the value test. Five out of ten is exactly half, below the strict number requirement.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A class has four yes claims totaling $42m, two no claims totaling $21m and two abstentions worth $12m. The plan pays $0.46 now and $0.12 after two years per dollar. Use 10%. Chapter 7 pays $0.57 now. Test the class vote and the dissenters’ comparison.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Valid voting amount is $63m. Yes votes are 42/63 = exactly two-thirds and four of six in number. The class accepts.
  2. The plan is worth 0.46 + 0.12/1.10² = approximately $0.5592.
  3. A dissenter has a shortfall of about $0.0108 per dollar against the $0.57 comparator. Voting success alone cannot cure that shortfall.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can holders of 70% of the voting debt bind their class to a US Chapter 11 plan?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

That percentage satisfies the amount threshold for a class of claims. More than half the allowed claims actually voting must also vote yes. Even an accepting class leaves the plan subject to other confirmation requirements, including protection for an individual impaired holder who has not accepted.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Would paying the $0.12 immediately solve this specific comparison?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Yes. The effective-date value would be $0.58 versus $0.57. That would increase the cash needed on the effective date and warrants a new liquidity test.
