Theory and terminology

UK restructuring plans and the relevant alternative

Part 26A · cross-class cramdown · no worse off

What you should be able to explain

  • Apply the UK Part 26A voting and cross-class conditions to a two-class comparison.
  • Rebuild creditor outcomes when the most likely alternative to the plan changes.

Choose the relevant alternative before deriving a recovery

A UK Part 26A restructuring plan can alter creditor or member rights to address a company’s financial difficulty. To examine a dissenting class, identify what would most likely occur without sanction. A funded going-concern sale in administration could be more likely than a piecemeal liquidation. A preliminary bid with no financing and a distant completion date may carry less weight.

Prepare the evidence an independent examiner could challenge. Show available cash runway, financing commitments, bidder conditions, realistic sale costs and timing. Distribute proceeds through the actual priority and security arrangements. Compare net values on a common date including distributions from a guarantee if the holder can realistically enforce it.

Compute support and identify an in-the-money class

For the class vote considered here, the usual Part 26A threshold is 75% in value of those present and voting in the relevant class. Measure it class by class. There is no matching majority-by-number condition for this plan vote. The ordinary US claims test in the neighboring lesson has different denominators and thresholds.

When a class fails, the cross-class rule requires, among other things, a class that approved by the required value and would receive payment or have a genuine economic interest in the relevant alternative. A class with no economic stake in the alternative cannot supply that qualifying support by itself.

Test each dissenter and explain distributional choices

Compare what each member of the dissenting class gets in the alternative with the economic value of the plan package. A deferred note may be worth less than face. A class average can hide a worse outcome for a holder with separate security. If the evidence raises that possibility, show the holder-specific bridge and reconsider whether class composition is appropriate.

Meeting the two cross-class conditions permits the court to consider sanction. The court still evaluates matters including fairness and allocation of the benefits created by the plan. These examples isolate the numerical questions and assume a proper convening process and class composition.

Method

Build the result

Class support = supporting voting value / total value present and voting; headroom = plan distribution value − relevant-alternative value.

Class support is a percentage. Headroom is currency per claim or per currency unit of face on a common date. Test it for each member of any dissenting class. The relevant alternative is the outcome the court considers most likely without sanction.

  1. Identify and support the most likely alternative. Deduct execution costs and distribute its value by priority.
  2. Calculate each class vote. Identify which consenting class has an economic interest under the alternative.
  3. Value the plan consideration and compare each dissenting holder. Where headroom is small, test the precise assumption that changes the sign.
Worked case

A going-concern sale sets the junior floor

An English company has £60m senior and £40m junior claims. A financed sale in administration would produce £84m net immediately, senior first. All claims vote. Seniors vote £45m yes and £15m no; juniors vote £20m each way. The plan pays juniors £0.55 now plus £0.055 per pound in one year. Use 10%. All junior holders share identical rights.

Working

  1. The alternative pays seniors £60m and leaves £24m for juniors, or £0.60 per pound.
  2. Senior support is 45/60 = 75%. The class meets the threshold and has value in the alternative. Junior support is 20/40 = 50%, so the class dissents.
  3. The juniors’ plan package is worth 0.55 + 0.055/1.10 = £0.60 per pound. On these facts each junior equals its alternative.
  4. A credible revised bid raising net sale proceeds to £90m would leave £30m for juniors, or £0.75 per pound. At the same plan price the shortfall would be £6m across the class.

Decision and interpretation

At £84m of alternative proceeds the numerical conditions discussed are met with zero junior headroom. The company must defend valuation and fairness. If the £90m sale becomes the most likely alternative, improve the junior package or reassess the route.

Quick check
In a Part 26A meeting, 76% by value of the class members present and voting support the plan, although only 45% by number do. How did that class vote?

The class acceptance threshold for this restructuring-plan vote is 75% by value of those present and voting. Other conditions still govern sanction.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A company has £50m senior and £30m junior debt. Its most likely alternative realizes £62m net now. Seniors unanimously support the plan and juniors vote only 55% by value in favor. All junior claims have identical rights. The plan pays them 42p per pound now. Test the two stated conditions.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Alternative proceeds pay £50m senior and £12m junior. Junior alternative recovery is 12/30 = 40p per pound.
  2. The senior class supplies qualifying support and has an economic interest. The junior vote is below 75%.
  3. Every junior receives 42p against 40p in the alternative. Its 2p headroom permits consideration of cross-class sanction, subject to the court’s discretion and other requirements.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a UK restructuring plan proceed when a class of junior creditors votes against it?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The court can consider sanction despite the dissent if the statutory conditions are met. Each member of the dissenting class must be at least as well off as under the most likely alternative, and a qualifying class with an economic interest in that alternative must support the plan by the required value vote. The court retains discretion.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the existing sale becomes unavailable and the most likely replacement nets £65m, what plan amount would meet this comparison?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The new junior baseline is (65 − 50)/30 = 50p per pound. At least 50p of effective economic value is needed on these simplified facts, an increase of 8p from the proposal.
