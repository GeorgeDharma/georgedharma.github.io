Negotiation under uncertainty

Compare each class’s alternative if negotiations fail. Test the valuation assumption that determines whether a junior class receives anything.

The starting point

Each creditor compares a proposal with what it would receive if talks failed. Estimate those alternatives by class and date. A senior lender can earn full recovery in liquidation while a junior lender sees nearly all of its potential gain in a successful rescue.

Find the valuation point where a class first receives value. Around this boundary, a small forecast change can alter who supports the plan. Test low and high cases, the probability of each and the cost of waiting. Expected value is one input to negotiation.

Theory and method

Contingent value rights in restructuring negotiations

Contingent value right · Disagreement payoffs

Two groups disagree over a turnaround’s future value. Can a contingent payment create a feasible agreement even if they reject the same fixed split?

A senior creditor believes the business will barely recover while a junior creditor sees valuable upside. A contingent value right promises extra payment if an observable outcome crosses a threshold. It can allocate upside to the party willing to attach greater value to it while maintaining a fixed minimum acceptable to another party. It transfers risk rather than creating operating cash. Verify the trigger, measurement date, payment capacity and legal feasibility before relying on an economist’s model of agreement.

How the result is built

CVR(V) = min[K, a × max(V − H, 0)]

V is the specified exit-value measure in euros, H the attachment threshold, a the contractual sharing rate above it, K the maximum payment, and CVR the contingent amount actually payable before any credit shortfall.

  1. A right pays 50% of exit proceeds above €12m, capped at €2m. At €10m it pays zero, at €14m it pays €1m, and at €18m it pays €2m.
  2. If a junior expects a 60% chance of €18m and otherwise €10m, the right is worth €1.2m to it before discounting. A senior believing the upside probability is 30% attaches €0.6m cost.
  3. Agreeing to transfer the right can bridge a disagreement if the fixed cash split and each party’s fallback leave room. All actual payments must still fit within realized proceeds.

When the reasoning holds

  • The stated exit value must be objectively measurable and protected against value diversion. A disputed affiliate sale can make the right hard to enforce.
  • The subjective values use a common date and risk basis for illustration. Different beliefs alone cannot overcome a cash shortfall in the low state.
Terms in this lesson
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
How to read the notation
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.

All notation examples

First example

Senior claims are €8m and juniors are owed €4m. A rescue produces either €7m or €11m with equal probability, both net of costs. Under senior-first priority, junior receipts are €0 or €3m, averaging €1.5m. Applying the waterfall to the average €9m would give the wrong €1m.

Negotiating from the alternative

Each claimant enters talks with a fallback such as proceeds under liquidation, enforcement or another buyer’s proposal, adjusted for timing, costs and risk. State the legal process and valuation assumptions providing that fallback. A €10m face claim is a legal amount, not necessarily €10m of bargaining power.

A feasible rescue creates room for agreement when net value exceeds the participants’ combined alternatives and the agreement can distribute gains acceptably. Total value alone does not determine consent. A senior lender well protected in liquidation may require compensation to take additional business risk.

Priority introduces thresholds

A junior creditor starts recovering only after higher claims have been paid from its pool. Its payoff therefore changes nonlinearly with available value. In a poor outcome it can receive zero. In a strong outcome it can be paid in full while more upside goes to equity.

Apply the waterfall separately for each possible outcome and then calculate expected receipts. Distributing average asset value first usually gives the wrong answer near a priority threshold. A creditor accepting new equity may also prefer risky upside that a senior lender would avoid.

Use bargaining ranges transparently

A restructuring plan can mix immediate cash, new debt, equity and contingent payments. Compare their values on the same date. When beliefs differ about future performance, a contingent payment can direct some realized upside to the party that expects it, although it introduces verification and enforcement issues.

Prepare a sensitivity around the contested drivers. One stakeholder may forecast profit recovery while another expects liquidation losses after delay. Explain how each assumption affects the fallback or proposed value. This makes negotiations about evidence and risk allocation rather than the loudest percentage demand.

Method

Test a plan against a fallback

  1. Value the outside option

    Calculate each class’s own immediate or discounted recovery. Note collateral access and priority, costs and uncertainty. Do not substitute the group average.

  2. Apply state waterfalls

    For each credible rescue outcome, distribute value under the proposed terms. Probability-weight afterward and check the sum against expected total resources.

  3. Locate agreement

    Find a compensating transfer or contingent allocation that leaves each consenting group willing to participate. Verify funding, priority, timing and downside exposure.

Worked case

A contested restructuring threshold

Senior claims are €10m and juniors €4m. Liquidation today pays €9m to senior and zero to junior. A one-year rescue yields net proceeds of €8m or €14m with equal probability and the same senior-first rules. Discount both payouts at 10% for this simplified comparison.

Working

  1. In the weak state senior gets €8m and junior zero. In the strong state senior gets €10m and junior €4m. Expected year-end payments are €9m senior and €2m junior.
  2. Present values are €8.18m and €1.82m respectively. Combined present value €10m exceeds €9m liquidation, but senior prefers its €9m immediately by about €0.82m.
  3. If parties may reallocate €2m of the strong-state junior payment to senior, senior year-end expectation rises to €10m or €9.09m today. Junior still expects €1m, worth €0.91m today, above zero.

Interpretation

The example assumes a permitted contractual allocation giving senior an upside payment beyond its original €10m in the strong state. Consent or explicit new rights are required. The original waterfall alone cannot produce it.

A common mistake

Declaring everyone better off because continuation has a larger expected total pool.

How to correct it

Show each class’s state-dependent cash and its own fallback. A viable total rescue can need different terms for a protected class to support it.

Quick check
Senior claims are €10m, with junior debt next. There are no other costs. When does junior debt first receive a positive amount?

The first €10m goes entirely to the senior class. Above that point, each additional euro goes to junior claims until their full amount is reached.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

Senior debt is €6m and junior debt €4m. A rescue immediately pays either €4m or €10m in total with equal probability. Liquidation immediately pays €5.5m to senior and zero to junior.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate expected payouts under the rescue with senior-first priority.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Could a transfer within the high-state payment make each class better off, assuming consent?

Part A 3 marks

Senior gets €4m or €6m, expectation €5m. Junior gets zero or €4m, expectation €2m. Combined expected proceeds are €7m.

Part B 3 marks

Senior needs more than €0.5m expected improvement to exceed €5.5m. Giving it a consensual additional €1.2m in the high state raises expected recovery by €0.6m to €5.6m. Junior still expects €1.4m, above zero.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two groups disagree over a turnaround’s future value. Can a contingent payment create a feasible agreement even if they reject the same fixed split?

Compare with a spoken answer

I would suggest a right linked to independently measured sale proceeds, with an explicit threshold and cap. Juniors here value the €2m high-state payout at €1.2m while seniors estimate its cost at €0.6m under their own probabilities. That disagreement can make a transfer acceptable. I would still show the low-state cash available, define the measurement process and test each class’s realistic fallback.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The contract changes the metric from proceeds before fees to after a €2m disposal fee. What happens at €14m headline proceeds?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The original measured €14m gives 50% × (€14m − €12m) = €1m. After the €2m fee, measured proceeds are €12m, giving zero. I would specify permitted fees, related-party charges, independent review and dispute procedures because the change removes the entire payout in this state.
