Professional workshop · S04

Restructure a cold-storage operator before the second Friday

Prepare a liquidity and recovery memorandum for the secured bank, trade creditors and prospective rescue lender to North Quay Cold Storage. Show when emergency cash must be drawable, what each class receives from liquidation, whether continuation creates value, and a consensual allocation all three financing groups can accept. The operating and holding companies are distinct. All values in €m. The priority and consent rules below are assumptions for this fictional case.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Restructuring adviser to an ad hoc creditor committee
What to submit
A 13-week liquidity schedule, entity-by-entity recovery waterfalls and a term-sheet recommendation the creditor committee can put to a vote.

Case inputs

Illustrative case prepared for this workshop.

From inputs to decision

01

Determine the date by which support must arrive

A positive quarter-end balance does not prevent a payment failure during negotiations. Anchor the emergency mandate to the first week below the operational floor.

  1. Start at €0.70m. Unfinanced closing balances for weeks 1–4 are €0.35m, −€0.10m, €0.00m and €0.50m. The second Friday is €0.40m below the €0.30m floor.
  2. Continue through week 13. Weeks 5–13 close at €0.35m, €0.55m, €0.60m, €0.40m, €0.70m, €0.60m, €0.85m, €0.80m and €0.90m. Maximum unfinanced shortfall is therefore €0.40m.

At least €0.40m must be accessible by week two. It is part of the €4m rescue commitment. Do not add the peak draw again to the overall rescue investment.

02

Size the bank’s recovery against pledged property

The bank’s security attaches to the premises. The committee needs the unpaid balance before allocating general assets.

  1. Net property proceeds are €18m gross less €2m direct sale costs = €16m. Apply this to the €20m secured loan.
  2. The bank has a €4m remaining deficiency. Under the stated rules it shares equally by face with €8m trade debt in the unrestricted OpCo estate.

€16m bank recovery is known before the separate unsecured distribution.

03

Convert remaining assets into cash and rank the claims

Reported inventory and customer balances are not immediate proceeds. Disposal and wind-down charges reduce what any unsecured claimant can recover.

  1. Stock generates €6m × 65% − €0.40m = €3.50m. Receivables generate €8m × 70% − €0.60m = €5.00m. Add €0.70m bank cash and subtract €2.00m remaining wind-down costs to get €7.20m general proceeds.
  2. The €12m equal-ranking pool receives €7.20m/€12m = 60%. Bank receives €2.40m deficiency cash and trades €4.80m. Bank total is €18.40m, or 92% of original face. Trades recover 60%.

Total OpCo liquidation gives €23.20m to creditors. The subsidiary has no residual equity for the parent.

04

Keep parent creditors in the correct estate

A consolidated group figure can disguise which creditors are entitled to operating assets and whether any OpCo shareholder residual exists.

  1. Parent owns OpCo shares worth zero under liquidation. It also holds €1.50m cash. With no guarantee its separate €5m lender has no direct claim on subsidiary receipts.
  2. Parent lender receives €1.50m/€5m = 30%. Add this to OpCo distributions for a group conservation check of €24.70m, then keep it separate when comparing operating alternatives.

Parent lender’s €1.50m recovery is identical across the proposed plan and liquidation absent a value change to the parent’s own resources.

05

Value each continued operating state

The committee must see whether the rescue’s operating benefit pays for fresh capital before allocating proceeds among classes.

  1. On-time annual cash capitalizes to €3.60m/12% = €30m. Slow recovery produces €3.00m/12% = €25m. Before allocation, deduct the €4m rescue outlay for net continuation values of €26m and €21m.
  2. Their 70/30 weighted net continuation is €24.50m. Against €23.20m net OpCo liquidation the rescue creates €1.30m expected group surplus. The €4.60m new claim is an allocation of gross value. Do not deduct both that claim and its €4m principal when valuing the whole business.

Continuation wins on the weighted total but loses to liquidation by €2.20m in the slow state.

06

Test each claimant’s vote under the first term sheet

A majority of aggregate value does not guarantee consent from a class that fares worse under the allocation.

  1. Pay the new investor €4.60m first in both states, a €0.60m uplift over its €4m contribution. The old bank receives €20m in both states. This leaves €5.40m for trades in the on-time state and €0.40m in the slow state.
  2. Trade expected value is 70% × €5.40m + 30% × €0.40m = €3.90m, below €4.80m liquidation. The bank gains €1.60m over liquidation. Original terms fail the trade class despite aggregate expected surplus.

Find a consensual transfer sufficient to persuade suppliers while retaining a benefit for the old bank.

07

Construct a proposal that can preserve supply

Continuing trade suppliers can be essential to a cold-storage operation. Test concessions against each class’s own fallback and retain credible implementation conditions.

  1. Shift €1.00m of the bank’s allocation to trade in each state by agreement. Bank now receives €19m. Trade receives €6.40m on time or €1.40m in the slow state, giving €4.90m expected.
  2. Check all gains versus the relevant alternatives. Old bank gains €0.60m, trade gains €0.10m expected, and the new investor gains €0.60m net of its own contribution. These gains sum to €1.30m expected economic surplus.

Recommend the revised terms conditional on binding funding before week two and on trade support. The trade class still faces meaningful downside despite a small expected improvement.

08

Stress the funding deadline and class sensitivity

An operating merits test is insufficient if one invoice arrives earlier or the turnaround is less credible than expected.

  1. An additional €0.25m week-two payment raises the peak required line from €0.40m to €0.65m. Verify the rescue term sheet reserves that amount for drawable working capital after any upfront implementation spending.
  2. Under the original waterfall, expected trade is €0.4m + €5m × p, where p is probability of the on-time outcome. Matching €4.80m requires p = 88%. Under the €1m transfer, break-even falls to (4.8 − 1.4)/5 = 68%.

The revised offer works at the case’s 70% estimate but leaves only two percentage points of probability headroom for trade consent.

Completed document

Creditor committee term-sheet recommendation | North Quay Cold Storage

Immediate liquidity

Secure a binding draw available before the second Friday. The base flow schedule needs €0.40m to preserve the €0.30m operating floor. Ring-fence €0.65m inside the €4m rescue in case the identified €0.25m supplier payment accelerates.

Creditor alternatives

Liquidation returns €18.40m to the old bank and €4.80m to trade. Parent creditors have no OpCo security and recover €1.50m separately. The operating rescue produces €24.50m weighted economic value after new investment, €1.30m above liquidation.

Proposed allocation

Retain the €4.60m new-money package. Fix the old bank’s distribution at €19.00m in both specified states, with an agreed €1.00m reallocation to trade. Expected trade receipts reach €4.90m, €0.10m above liquidation. Document consent and the rights supporting the transfer.

Disagreement and monitoring

Trade continues to bear a €1.40m recovery in the slow state. Provide weekly cash variance reports, an independent update on sustainable free cash and a no-leakage covenant. Reopen terms if the committee’s supportable probability of on-time delivery falls below 68% or rescue cash cannot clear before week two.

Task 1

Stock instead realizes 55% of face in liquidation. Keep all costs and claims unchanged. Enter the new total old-bank liquidation recovery in €m.

Stock proceeds fall by €6m × (65% − 55%) = €0.60m. General pool becomes €6.60m, paying 55% across €12m face. Bank deficiency gets 55% × €4m = €2.20m, on top of €16m collateral, for €18.20m.

Task 2

Keep the revised €1m transfer and raise on-time probability to 80%. Enter trade creditors’ expected rescue receipts in €m.

See the full solution

Trade receives €6.40m or €1.40m. At 80% on time, expected receipts are 0.8 × 6.40 + 0.2 × 1.40 = €5.40m. That is €0.60m above the original liquidation fallback.

Task 3

At 70% on time, what minimum transfer from old bank to trade in each state makes trade indifferent to original liquidation, in €m?

See the full solution

The original expected trade recovery is €3.90m. A fixed transfer in both states increases expected recovery euro for euro. A €0.90m transfer gives €4.80m exactly. The proposed €1m leaves a small positive expected margin.
