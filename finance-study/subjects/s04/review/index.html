New and expanded lessons

S04.11

Counting Chapter 11 votes and testing a dissenting creditor’s recovery

The workout, recovery and negotiation units explain how creditor interests differ. The existing case does not tabulate actual ballots by number and value or distinguish class acceptance from the individual liquidation comparison.

  • Apply the two voting thresholds to a class of US Chapter 11 claims.
  • Calculate a dissenting holder’s present-value recovery under a plan and a hypothetical liquidation.

S04.12

Valuing a distressed rights offering and its backstop

The rescue-finance workshop reallocates cash to a new lender. A rights offering instead gives creditors a choice to invest fresh equity. This chapter isolates that investment from their recovery on old claims and values the effect of a discounted subscription price.

  • Calculate post-money equity value, dilution and the value of exercising a right.
  • Separate old-claim recovery, new cash and backstop compensation in a creditor comparison.

S04.13

Testing a UK restructuring plan against the relevant alternative

The workout case compares stakeholder outcomes under competing strategies. It does not apply the UK Part 26A class vote, test each dissenting holder against the most likely alternative or identify the qualifying consenting class.

  • Apply the UK Part 26A voting and cross-class conditions to a two-class comparison.
  • Rebuild creditor outcomes when the most likely alternative to the plan changes.

Public benchmarks

University of Michigan Law School

International Corporate Reorganization

The course uses US Chapter 11 plan requirements as a starting point for comparisons with other jurisdictions. It also studies financing during reorganization, creditor treatment and the use of present and expected value.

US Government Publishing Office

United States Code, Title 11, Chapter 11, sections 1126 and 1129

Section 1126(c) defines class acceptance for voting claims. Section 1129 specifies confirmation requirements including protection of individual impaired holders, an accepting impaired class in the circumstances specified, and feasibility.

INSEAD

Backstopped Rights Issue in Chapter 11: Should Backstop Parties Pay?

Pierre Hillion analyzes how committed buyers, subscription prices and fees distribute risk and upside in a Chapter 11 rights issue. The economic value of a backstop can depend on opportunities received alongside the commitment.

UK Parliament and The National Archives

Corporate Insolvency and Governance Act 2020, explanatory notes on restructuring plans

The notes explain the Part 26A class value vote and the court’s discretion when a class dissents. They define the most likely alternative, the no-worse-off condition and the need for a supporting class with an economic interest.
