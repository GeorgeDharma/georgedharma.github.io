Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A profitable borrower cannot make payroll

A service business starts week one with €0.45m and needs a €0.15m minimum. Weekly receipts are €0.30m, €0.20m and €0.85m. Weekly payments are €0.40m, €0.55m and €0.40m. No borrowing is included. Assets are worth €9m including all receivables and claims are €8m. Normal annual cash before debt service is €0.65m, compared with €0.80m of scheduled yearly debt service.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A parent and its secured subsidiary

A subsidiary’s pledged machine sells for €8.8m in one year with €0.55m sale costs in that year. Its secured lender is owed €10m. General cash of €2.6m is distributable at the same year-end. Under the case rules, the lender’s deficiency shares equally by face with €3.45m supplier claims in that general pool. The parent owns the subsidiary and has €1.1m cash at year-end with €2m of separate parent debt. There are no guarantees or further costs. Discount all year-end amounts at 10%.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A rescue whose funding benefits old creditors

Immediate liquidation gives old creditors €13m and owners zero. To continue, a new investor must supply €2m today. The operating business would then generate €1.8m annually forever beginning one year from now, after necessary investment, at a 10% required return. Old claims are €22m and rank first unless amended. Use today's equivalent values for all proposed rescue claims.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

The consent of a protected lender

A senior group is owed €9m, juniors €6m. Immediate liquidation pays senior €8m and junior zero. A rescue pays €6m or €15m in total one year later with equal probabilities. Under original senior-first priority, each class is capped at face. Discount both classes at 10% for this exercise. The agreement can create an extra senior contingent claim in the good state if juniors consent.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A profitable borrower cannot make payroll

Part A 4 marks

Calculate the weekly cash balances without financing, the first breach and the minimum draw needed then.

Week-end cash is €0.35m, €0.00m and €0.45m (one mark for each). Week two is the first breach and requires €0.15m to retain the floor (1).

Part B 3 marks

Can the bridge be repaid in week three if €0.25m of receipts move to week four?

Unfinanced week-three cash drops to €0.20m (1). With the €0.15m draw outstanding, cash is €0.35m (1). Repaying it leaves €0.20m, still above the €0.15m floor, assuming the other flows occur (1).

Part C 3 marks

Distinguish the structural issue from the immediate cash crisis.

Assets exceed claims by €1m under the valuation assumptions (1). Annual cash is €0.15m below scheduled service (1). A short bridge can fix week two while a viable recurring operating or debt-service change is separately needed (1).

Case 2 · 10 marks

A parent and its secured subsidiary

Part A 4 marks

Compute net collateral, deficiency and the sharing percentage.

Net collateral is €8.25m (1). The deficiency is €1.75m (1). Combined unsecured claims are €1.75m + €3.45m = €5.20m (1), which €2.6m pays at 50% (1).

Part B 3 marks

Compute year-end recovery and present value for the secured lender and suppliers.

The lender receives €8.25m + €0.875m = €9.125m (1), worth about €8.295m today (1). Suppliers receive €1.725m, worth about €1.568m today (1).

Part C 3 marks

What reaches parent lenders, and why is group cash a misleading shortcut?

No subsidiary equity remains (1). The parent lender gets €1.1m at year-end, 55% of face, worth €1m now (1). It has no direct claim on subsidiary assets under the assumed ownership and guarantee facts (1).

Case 3 · 10 marks

A rescue whose funding benefits old creditors

Part A 3 marks

Calculate operating value, net continuation value and total gain over liquidation.

The perpetuity is €1.8m/0.10 = €18m (1). After new funding cost, continuation is €16m (1), a €3m gain over liquidation (1).

Part B 3 marks

Why may an owner refuse to fund under present priority? Find break-even operating cash for continuation.

The €22m old claim absorbs all €18m so owners contributing €2m receive nothing (1). To match €13m liquidation plus €2m funding requires €15m operating value (1), implying €1.5m annual cash (1).

Part C 4 marks

Test a proposal granting the investor first economic value of €3m and leaving the rest to old creditors.

The investor gains €1m on its €2m contribution (1). Old creditors receive €15m versus €13m, gaining €2m (1). The gains sum to €3m (1). Feasibility requires valid consent/priority and credible operating cash and valuation, or the investor may not receive its assumed value (1).

Case 4 · 10 marks

The consent of a protected lender

Part A 4 marks

Find state-dependent and present expected payouts before changing the agreement.

Weak state pays €6m senior and nothing junior (1). Strong pays €9m senior and €6m junior (1). Senior expected value today is €7.5m/1.10 ≈ €6.818m (1). Junior is €3m/1.10 ≈ €2.727m (1).

Part B 3 marks

Does higher total rescue value guarantee approval? Find the minimum good-state transfer to make senior indifferent.

Total present value is €10.5m/1.10 ≈ €9.545m above €8m, but senior initially loses (1). Senior needs €8.8m expected at year-end (1). The €1.3m expected increment needs €2.6m in the 50% good state (1).

Part C 3 marks

Test an approved €3m good-state transfer to senior and state a practical condition.

Senior’s expected future amount rises to €9m or €8.182m today (1). Junior receives €3m in the good state, worth €1.5m/1.10 ≈ €1.364m today (1). A permitted new contingent right and measurable distribution trigger are needed. This is not the original face-capped waterfall (1).
