Interest-rate swaps

Exchange fixed for floating cash flows on a notional amount. The initial fair fixed rate makes the two legs equal in value under stated curves.

The starting point

In a simple interest-rate swap, one party pays a fixed rate and receives a variable rate on an agreed notional amount. Usually only the interest difference changes hands. The notional sets the scale. Payment timing and the fraction of a year in each interest period also affect the amount.

To find a fair fixed rate at inception, project the floating payments under the agreed reference convention and discount both sets of payments on the appropriate curve. The fixed rate equates their present values. A swap may reduce the uncertainty of a floating-rate loan while leaving the borrower’s credit spread or timing mismatch exposed.

Theory and method

Multi-curve swap valuation and the par swap annuity

Floating projection curve · Collateral discount curve

A new swap’s floating index has its own projection curve. Can you still value its floating coupons simply as one minus the final discount factor?

A company’s loan resets to one index while the collateralized swap is discounted at another reference. Projecting the floating payments answers which amounts are contracted through the relevant index. Discounting them answers their present value under the collateral convention. The compact one-curve identity relies on forward-rate factors canceling against their own discount factors. Once the two curves differ, price the explicit floating coupons and weight the fixed coupons with the collateral annuity.

How the result is built

Kpar = [ΣDᵢδᵢFᵢ]/[ΣDᵢδᵢ]; Vpayer = NΣDᵢδᵢ(Fᵢ − Kold)

Dᵢ is the collateral discount factor for date i, δᵢ the coupon year fraction, Fᵢ the projected annual index fixing for that coupon, N euro notional, Kpar the fair fixed rate and Kold the original fixed rate paid in an existing swap.

  1. For two annual payments take D₁ = 0.97, D₂ = 0.93, F₁ = 4% and F₂ = 5.2%. The projected floating-leg value per euro is 0.97 × 0.04 + 0.93 × 0.052 = 0.08716.
  2. The collateral-discounted fixed annuity is 1.90. Divide to obtain Kpar ≈ 4.5874%. The shortcut (1 − 0.93)/1.90 gives an inconsistent 3.6842%.
  3. For €10m notional and an existing 4.4% fixed payer, mark is €10m × (0.08716 − 1.90 × 0.044) = +€35,600.

When the reasoning holds

  • Fixed and floating schedules match in this example. Separate reset dates, day counts, accrued known fixings or a stub require separate leg schedules.
  • Projection rates and collateral discount factors share the valuation date and currency. A counterparty or funding adjustment must be modeled separately if applicable.
Terms in this lesson
Swap
A contract to exchange payment streams, such as fixed interest for floating interest.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Credit spread
Extra yield over a chosen reference yield. Credit risk, liquidity and market conditions can all affect it.
Discount factor
The price today of one unit of currency payable at a particular future date under specified risk assumptions.
Coupon
The interest payment specified in a bond’s terms.
How to read the notation
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

On a €2m notional for half a year, a company paying fixed 3% and receiving floating 4% collects €2m × (0.04 − 0.03) × 0.5 = €10,000. That receipt can offset part of the interest it owes on floating-rate debt.

Translate the swap into dated legs

A fixed-for-floating swap makes one side pay a fixed rate and receive a reference floating rate, typically on the same notional in a plain single-currency contract. Payment on a period equals rate × notional × accrual fraction. Netting the two interest payments does not remove the economic exposure to either leg.

The floating rate may reset in advance or accrue in arrears under the reference convention. A period that has already reset has a known contractual payment, while later periods require projected rates. Include any accrued amount in a mark-to-market measured between payment dates.

Derive a par fixed rate

Let A be the sum of each fixed-leg accrual fraction multiplied by its payment discount factor. A fixed coupon K on notional N has present value NKA. Project each floating cash flow under its appropriate forward convention and discount it. At inception the fixed rate that equates these leg values is the par swap rate.

In a simple single-curve swap beginning today on reset with no spread and matching conventions, floating-leg value excluding principal is N × [1 − d(T)]. In a multi-curve setting, project coupons and discount them with their respective curves. The compact identity may no longer apply.

Why an old swap moves in value

The fixed rate in an existing contract stays fixed. If comparable market fixed rates rise, a party paying the older lower fixed rate generally benefits, all else equal. A swap’s value is a signed difference in present leg values rather than the notional exchanged.

To hedge floating-rate borrowing, a company typically pays fixed and receives floating over matching dates. A loan margin over the reference rate remains. Day-count, payment floor, reset date, maturity, amount and counterparty terms can all prevent the two legs from matching exactly.

Method

Price and interpret a swap

  1. Calendar

    List notional, payer direction, reset dates and accrual factors. Distinguish known fixings from rates projected for future periods.

  2. Value

    Discount each leg and set them equal for a new par fixed rate. For an existing swap use its contracted coupon and subtract payments from receipts.

  3. Match

    Combine the loan and swap schedules and identify remaining spread, quantity or payment-timing risks.

Worked case

A two-payment fixed payer

A two-year swap begins today on reset with annual payments on €1m, matched single-curve assumptions and discount factors 0.96 and 0.91. The contract exchanges interest only and has no spread on the floating leg.

Working

  1. Fixed-leg annuity A is 0.96 + 0.91 = 1.87. Floating-leg value is €1m × (1 − 0.91) = €90,000. Par K is 0.09/1.87 ≈ 4.813%.
  2. A fixed payer with a contractual 5.50% at this valuation point pays a leg worth €1m × 0.055 × 1.87 = €102,850. Its net mark is €90,000 − €102,850 = −€12,850.
  3. An upfront payment of €12,850 to the fixed payer would offset that negative value for a new otherwise identical off-market swap. It does not change the contract’s annual fixed coupon.

Interpretation

At a later date, obtain updated discount factors, any rates already fixed and remaining dates. Reusing the inception curve would miss market revaluation.

A common mistake

Calculating a par swap rate by averaging two spot rates.

How to correct it

Price the floating interest payments and divide by the discounted fixed accrual sum. The dates and weights matter, especially on a steep curve.

Quick check
If the next half-year floating rate fixes at 2.5%, what does the same company pay or receive on the swap?

Floating receipts fall below the 3% fixed cost by 0.5 percentage point. €2m × 0.005 × 0.5 = €5,000 paid.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

A €5m company loan charges floating reference plus 1.2%. A matched swap pays fixed 4.1% and receives reference. The next half-year reference fixes at 3.6% and all accrual fractions equal 0.5.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute loan interest and net swap cash from the company’s view.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Find combined effective annual rate and one remaining risk.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Loan costs €5m × 4.8% × 0.5 = €120,000. Swap costs €5m × (4.1% − 3.6%) × 0.5 = €12,500. Both are payments here.

Part B 2 marks

Total €132,500 over half a year on €5m corresponds to 5.3% annually, fixed 4.1% plus loan margin 1.2%. A changed loan spread, notional mismatch or counterparty failure can alter the result.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A new swap’s floating index has its own projection curve. Can you still value its floating coupons simply as one minus the final discount factor?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would explicitly project the index coupons and discount them on the collateral curve. Here the float is worth 8.716 cents per euro versus a fixed annuity of 1.90, so par is 4.587%. An existing 4.4% payer has positive value. I would check known fixings, conventions, collateral currency, and the loan’s actual spread before claiming the swap fixes all borrowing costs.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If only the second projected fixing rises 40 basis points, with discount factors held fixed, what happens to the €10m payer mark?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The floating receipt on the second date increases €10m × 0.004 = €40,000 for a full-year accrual. Its present value is €40,000 × 0.93 = €37,200. The fixed payer’s mark rises from €35,600 to €72,800.
