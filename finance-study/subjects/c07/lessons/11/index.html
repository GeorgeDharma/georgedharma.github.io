Credit default swaps, premium legs and settlement

Calculate a fair CDS spread from dated survival and default probabilities.

Theory and terminology

Credit default swap valuation and settlement

CDS · premium leg · protection leg · recovery

What you should be able to explain

  • Calculate a fair CDS spread from dated survival and default probabilities.
  • Interpret cash settlement and the sign of an upfront payment on an off-market coupon.

Separate the people and the promise

A credit default swap, or CDS, names a reference entity or obligation. The protection buyer pays a premium and the seller promises a contingent payment if a credit event specified in the contract occurs. The reference company can be distinct from the protection seller. Their defaults create different losses for the buyer.

With cash settlement, a recovery price is used to value the loss on the protected amount. Where an auction provides the final price, use its result and the eligible obligation rules. A bank holding a different loan could recover a different amount. Protection can leave a basis between the loan loss and the CDS receipt.

Build two expected cash streams

Premium payments survive as long as the contract remains active. A protection payment occurs only in the default interval. If the cumulative probability of survival falls from 97% to 93%, the unconditional probability of default in that interval is four percentage points. It is not another 7% on top of earlier defaults.

Use pricing probabilities consistent with traded credit risk and the discounting convention. Realized default frequencies answer a separate forecasting question. A fair running spread equates the present value of contingent protection with the present value of paying that spread while the contract survives.

Convert value into a tradable quote

Standardized contracts may use fixed running coupons and an upfront amount. If agreed periodic premiums are below the fair cost of protection, the buyer receives a valuable contract and must pay upfront to bring its initial net value to zero.

Real transactions commonly include accrued premium on default, coupon day counts, specific credit-event definitions and settlement dates. The discrete contract below assumes payment at observation dates and explicitly waives accrued premium on default, so its numbers can be reproduced without a market convention sheet.

Method

Build the result

A = Σ DᵢαᵢQᵢ. H = (1 − R)Σ Dᵢ(Qᵢ₋₁ − Qᵢ). s = H/A. Buyer value before upfront = N(H − kA).

Dᵢ is the discount factor to date i, αᵢ the fraction of a year between coupons and Qᵢ the pricing probability of surviving through that date, with Q₀ = 1. R is recovery as a fraction of face, N notional currency, k the annual contractual spread, A the risky premium annuity, H protection value per unit notional and s the fair annual spread.

  1. For each date, multiply the elapsed year fraction by discounted survival to value one unit of running annual premium.
  2. Subtract consecutive survival probabilities to obtain unconditional interval default probabilities. Multiply each by its discounted loss after recovery.
  3. Set the two leg values equal for a par contract. A nonpar coupon leaves a currency amount the upfront transfer has to offset.
Worked case

Two semiannual periods on a €10m name

A one-year CDS has two equal half-year periods, discount factors 0.98 and 0.95, survival probabilities 0.97 and 0.93 and assumed recovery of 40%. Default settles at the interval end. No premium accrues on default. Compare a fair quote with a contractual 4% annual coupon.

Working

  1. Interval default probabilities are 3% and 4%. With a 60% loss, protection value per euro is 0.60 × (0.98 × 0.03 + 0.95 × 0.04) = 0.04044.
  2. The risky premium annuity is 0.5 × (0.98 × 0.97 + 0.95 × 0.93) = 0.91705 years.
  3. The fair running spread is 0.04044/0.91705 = 4.4098% annually, about 441 basis points. Each basis point is 0.01 percentage point.
  4. At 4%, premium value is €10m × 0.04 × 0.91705 = €366,820. Protection is worth €404,400, leaving €37,580 upfront payable by the buyer.

Decision and interpretation

If a qualifying event later settles at an auction price of 35 per 100, the contractual protection payment is €6.5m. The earlier 40% assumption priced possible future outcomes.

Quick check
A cash-settled €8m CDS has a qualifying event and final recovery price of 42 per 100. What protection does the seller pay?

The loss is 58% of €8m. Recovery value of €3.36m is the portion outside the contractual loss payment.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A €5m two-year contract pays annually with discount factors 0.96 and 0.92, survival probabilities 0.97 and 0.92, and 35% recovery. It uses the same interval-end convention. Calculate the par spread and upfront direction at a contractual 3% coupon.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Default probabilities are 3% and 5%. Protection per euro is 0.65 × (0.96 × 0.03 + 0.92 × 0.05) = 0.04862.
  2. The annuity is 0.96 × 0.97 + 0.92 × 0.92 = 1.7776. The par spread is 0.04862/1.7776 = 2.7351%.
  3. At 3% the buyer’s pre-upfront value is €5m × (0.04862 − 0.03 × 1.7776) = −€23,540. The seller would pay that upfront amount to the buyer to balance the contract.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

What spread pays fairly for credit protection, and who pays whom if the contract coupon is fixed below that spread?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would price the two default intervals separately and value premiums only while the reference name survives. The worked case gives a fair annual spread of about 441 basis points. A 4% coupon is cheaper for the protection buyer, so the buyer must pay €37,580 upfront to offset its advantage.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if the bank sells protection to hedge a loan it already owns?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Selling protection adds a contingent default payment to an existing credit loss. Buying matched protection reduces that exposure, subject to documentation, different recoveries and the protection seller’s ability to perform.
