Cross-currency swaps and the re-exchange of principal

Construct the signed two-currency cash schedule for a synthetic domestic borrowing.

In this lecture

  1. Specify which principal changes hands
  2. Mark each leg in compatible currency units
  3. Reconcile the hedge with the funding it serves

  5. Who supplies the dollars and when?
  6. Transform a US$10m funding issue
  7. Independent exam problem
  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Cross-currency swap cash flows

principal exchange · currency basis

What you should be able to explain

  • Construct the signed two-currency cash schedule for a synthetic domestic borrowing.
  • Value the remaining swap consistently and identify how basis, settlement and counterparty exposure enter.

Specify which principal changes hands

Suppose a euro company can issue dollars but needs euros to pay its suppliers. It borrows dollars, hands them to a swap dealer at inception and receives euros. Later the dealer sends dollar interest and ultimately dollar principal to service the bond. The firm pays agreed euro interest and returns euro principal.

In the fixed-notional structure here, both amounts are fixed on the original exchange date and exchanged back at maturity. There are also swaps with periodic notional resets. Print two currency columns and name the party’s perspective before adding any flows.

Mark each leg in compatible currency units

A cross-currency swap contains interest and often principal, so a modest exchange-rate move can change its value by much more than the near coupon. Price the remaining amounts in their own currencies with curves consistent with the collateral arrangement. Convert the foreign present value at current spot, then subtract the domestic present value paid.

Market basis is a spread that helps reconcile currency funding and hedging prices. It can be expressed on a specified floating leg and has a quote direction. Use consistent cash flows and market discount inputs. Adding a spread again when already included in the payable coupon or supplied valuation can double count it.

Reconcile the hedge with the funding it serves

When dollars strengthen, the euro value of the issuer’s dollar obligation ordinarily rises. A swap receiving those same dollars can become an offsetting asset. Report the derivative and the debt before examining their combined economic exposure.

Delivery timing matters. A positive mark today is not a pile of dollars ready to repay tomorrow’s bond. Counterparty failure, early termination, collateral calls and different payment dates can leave the company with funding exposure even if the original coupons matched exactly.

Method

Build the result

Vswap = X × Σ(Df,t × Cf,t) − Σ(Dh,t × Ch,t).

Vswap is the current value in home currency to the party receiving foreign and paying home. X is home currency per one foreign unit at valuation. Cf,t and Ch,t are positive amounts of remaining foreign receipts and domestic payments at time t. Df,t and Dh,t are discount factors compatible with the agreed collateral and the two cash streams.

  1. List all remaining interest and principal by currency. Include re-exchanged notionals whenever the signed confirmation specifies them.
  2. Discount each cash stream into its own units and convert the foreign present value at current spot. Reverse the sign for the opposite swap party.
  3. Combine with the debt separately. Matching foreign amounts, timing and valuation assumptions remove foreign promised cash flows from the combined schedule.
Worked case

Transform a US$10m funding issue

A euro issuer borrows US$10m for two years at a fixed 5% annual coupon. It gives the dealer US$10m at inception and receives €9m. The swap pays it 5% dollar interest and final dollar principal, against annual 4% euro interest and €9m final euro principal. Ignore fees, default and collateral transfers in cash-flow illustrations.

Working

  1. The bond generates US$10m and the inception swap uses exactly US$10m. The issuer has €9m available for its domestic project.
  2. Year one bond interest is US$0.5m. The swap supplies US$0.5m and requires €0.36m. Final combined service is €9.36m at year two.
  3. Just after year-one coupons, suppose spot is €0.94 per US dollar. Compatible remaining one-year discount factors are 0.965 for USD and 0.975 for EUR. Foreign leg is US$10.5m × 0.965 = US$10.1325m, worth €9.52455m.
  4. Domestic leg is €9.36m × 0.975 = €9.126m. Swap value is +€0.39855m. The dollar debt has €9.52455m liability value on the same simplified promised-cash basis, leaving €9.126m combined liability.

Decision and interpretation

The swap has gained value while the agreed euro payment stays fixed. A real issuer credit spread or hedge closeout charge can keep accounting or liquidation values from offsetting this exactly.

Quick check
The initial euro notional was €9m. A 25-basis-point annual spread is added only to the euro coupon on that fixed notional. How much extra is paid each full year?

Twenty-five basis points is 0.0025. Multiplying by €9m gives €22,500 per full coupon year.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A UK borrower receives US$10m on a bond and uses a fixed-notional swap to obtain £8m. With one year left the swap will receive US$10.6m and pay £8.4m. Current spot is £0.78 per dollar, and compatible one-year discount factors are 0.96 for USD and 0.97 for GBP. Compute the swap and combined liability value on the same promised-cash basis.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. US receipts have present value US$10.176m. At current spot that is £7.93728m.
  2. Sterling payments have present value £8.4m × 0.97 = £8.148m. The swap is worth −£0.21072m to the borrower.
  3. Dollar debt worth £7.93728m plus the £0.21072m swap liability gives £8.148m of combined promised liability. The dollar maturity receipt from the swap is already earmarked for the bond.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a swap turn a foreign-currency bond into a domestic liability even while the swap itself gains value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. In the example, the dollar bond needs US$10.5m at maturity, which matches the swap’s dollar receipt. Euro payments are fixed. As dollars strengthen the euro value of the hedged debt rises, and the swap can gain value. After the first coupon the derivative asset is €398,550 and the combined promised liability equals the €9.126m euro-leg value.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The dealer requests payment of the domestic principal one day before delivering dollars. Why does a matched maturity table no longer settle the problem?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The borrower must finance the early sterling payment and faces exposure to dealer failure before the dollars arrive. Compare actual calendars and cutoffs, seek payment-versus-payment settlement where available, and arrange sufficient liquidity for the dated gap.
