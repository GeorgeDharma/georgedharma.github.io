Clearing and counterparty risk

Collateral and clearing change loss and funding exposures. Assess the chosen hedge after fees, margin needs and possible failure of the counterparty.

The starting point

If a derivative is valuable to you, failure of your trading partner may force you to replace it at a loss. Netting combines eligible contracts under one agreement. Collateral can cover part of the resulting exposure, depending on its value and enforceability. Prices can still change between the last margin payment and the final closeout.

With central clearing, a clearinghouse stands between buyers and sellers for eligible trades. Variation margin settles current price changes and initial margin provides a buffer against possible changes during closeout. Clearing changes who bears exposure and when cash must be delivered. A firm still needs liquidity to meet margin calls.

Theory and method

Unilateral credit valuation adjustment

CVA · Expected positive exposure and wrong-way risk

A swap is fully collateralized today. Why can a counterparty credit valuation adjustment still be positive?

Today’s collateral may change before the customer defaults, and the portfolio can move again while it is being replaced. A trading desk subtracts the priced present value of expected losses on future positive replacement claims. Future exposure must allow for enforceable netting, usable collateral, timing and recovery. The most important exposure is the one present when default occurs. An unconditional average exposure can be misleading if the same shock raises the mark and weakens the counterparty.

How the result is built

CVA ≈ ΣDᵢ(1 − R)EPEᵢΔQᵢ

CVA is euro unilateral counterparty adjustment, Dᵢ the relevant date’s discount factor, R the recovery fraction, EPEᵢ the expected positive euro closeout exposure conditional on first default in interval i, and ΔQᵢ the pricing probability of first default in that interval. Σ sums dated losses.

  1. Partition first default by interval. Conditional on each default event, multiply the uncovered positive replacement claim by loss severity. A negative value owed to the customer produces no credit loss to this side in that branch.
  2. With discounted-date factors 0.98 and 0.95, conditional positive exposures €2m and €4m, default masses 1% and 2%, and recovery 40%, contributions are €11,760 and €45,600.
  3. Total unilateral CVA is €57,360. If the €4m number were merely an unconditional exposure in a wrong-way scenario, recompute the conditional profile before quoting the adjustment.

When the reasoning holds

  • The approximation uses given discount factors and conditional exposure estimates consistent with the default weights. With stochastic discounting their joint expectation may be needed.
  • This is a unilateral measure under the stated netting and collateral agreement. Own default, funding, capital and clearing charges are separate valuation components.
Terms in this lesson
Counterparty
The other party to a financial contract or trade.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Derivative
A contract whose value depends on something else, such as a currency, an interest rate or a share price.
Margin
In business accounts, profit expressed relative to sales. In derivatives trading, margin is collateral required to support a position.
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
First example

Under one enforceable netting agreement, contracts worth +€8m and −€3m give a net €5m claim. If €4m of usable collateral has already been received, the current uncovered claim is €1m, before closeout costs or future price moves.

Replacement cost and the netting set

A firm facing a defaulted counterparty may need to replace contracts at current market prices. A positive replacement value is a claim. An enforceable closeout netting agreement may allow assets and liabilities with the same counterparty and legal set to offset before calculating that claim. Separate agreements or entities may not be eligible to net.

Collateral received can reduce unsecured exposure to the extent it is legally available, correctly valued and liquid. Apply any contractual haircut to collateral value. Posted collateral has a different role from collateral received and can create a claim for its return under the arrangement.

Current and potential future exposure

Current exposure uses today’s positive replacement value after qualifying netting and collateral. Potential future exposure asks how large it might become over a future interval. During the margin period of risk, prices can change between the last margin call and completed closeout. A fully margined portfolio today can therefore produce a loss after a jump.

Wrong-way risk arises if exposure grows precisely when the counterparty is likely to fail or its collateral becomes less valuable. A hedge for oil purchases bought from an oil-dependent distressed counterparty can display this dependence. Scenario modeling should join default, trade value and collateral.

What central clearing changes

A clearinghouse interposes itself between counterparties for eligible trades. Clearing members post margin and contribute to loss-absorbing resources under a stated waterfall. Variation margin addresses accumulated price changes. Initial margin provides a buffer for potential changes during closeout. Default arrangements and contributions depend on the clearinghouse rules.

Netting and collateral can reduce exposures yet concentrate operational and liquidity needs. A firm may have to post cash on a cleared hedge before its underlying receipt arrives. A clearing member or clearinghouse failure is a different scenario from the default of a bilateral industrial customer.

Method

Measure a counterparty limit

  1. Partition

    Group trades by legally enforceable netting set and check closeout timing. A positive exposure on one set cannot automatically offset a liability on another.

  2. Secure

    Value eligible collateral after haircuts, currency moves and segregation terms. Floor each valid net exposure at zero when measuring claims.

  3. Stress

    Reprice during the margin period, reduce collateral under a joint shock and assess cash needed for replacement or variation payments.

Worked case

One client with two legal agreements

Two derivatives in set A have values +€9m and −€4m. Set B with another legal entity has a single value −€3m. A supplies securities worth €4m, subject to a 10% haircut. The bank cannot net A with B.

Working

  1. Set A nets to €5m. Usable collateral is €3.6m, leaving €1.4m unsecured. Set B contributes zero positive exposure even though the bank owes money under it.
  2. If A’s net value rises to €7m during closeout and collateral market value falls to €3m, usable collateral becomes €2.7m. Unsecured exposure becomes €4.3m.
  3. At an assumed 40% recovery on the unsecured amount, loss conditional on that stressed default would be €4.3m × 60% = €2.58m. Do not multiply by a default probability when describing this conditional loss.

Interpretation

Treating the negative B trade as a credit offset would understate exposure and ignore the separate entity that can still demand payment.

A common mistake

Subtracting initial margin posted to the counterparty from replacement exposure as though the bank had received it.

How to correct it

Track who posted, who holds, whether return is secured and which netting set can legally use each balance.

Quick check
If the same collateral falls by €0.5m while derivative values stay fixed, what is the uncovered claim?

Usable collateral falls to €3.5m. Subtracting it from the €5m net claim gives €1.5m of current uncovered exposure.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

Two trades are +€6m and −€2m in one enforceable set. The counterparty provides €3m eligible collateral with a 20% haircut. During closeout the net trade value increases €1m.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find current and stressed uncovered exposure if collateral market value holds.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    How would wrong-way collateral risk change this stress?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Current net claim is €4m, with eligible collateral €2.4m, leaving €1.6m. Stressed net claim €5m less €2.4m leaves €2.6m.

Part B 2 marks

The collateral itself could decline when the counterparty becomes troubled. Revalue it in the same default stress before applying the haircut, which would raise uncovered exposure above €2.6m.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A swap is fully collateralized today. Why can a counterparty credit valuation adjustment still be positive?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would calculate future positive replacement exposure after legally enforceable netting and collateral. A posted margin balance today covers current price movements under its agreement, but a gap remains through default and closeout. In the case, discounted expected losses total €57,360. I would check whether exposures were estimated conditional on default and whether collateral itself weakens in the same event.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Year-two conditional exposure rises from €4m to €6m under a commodity wrong-way scenario. What happens to the adjustment?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The incremental loss is 0.95 × 60% × €2m × 2% = €22,800. Total CVA rises to €80,160 with other inputs fixed. If default probability or recovery also changes under that scenario, re-estimate the inputs jointly.
