New and expanded lessons

C07.11

Credit default swaps, premium legs and settlement

The derivatives course calculates replacement exposure and counterparty valuation adjustment. Fixed income introduces default intensity. Neither treatment constructs a reference entity’s CDS premium and protection legs or reconciles an off-market coupon with an upfront payment.

  • Calculate a fair CDS spread from dated survival and default probabilities.
  • Interpret cash settlement and the sign of an upfront payment on an off-market coupon.

C07.12

Interest-rate caps, floors, collars and swaptions

Rate swaps and vanilla stock options were covered separately. Students had yet to calculate a caplet on the actual loan reset, value a caplet under stated pricing weights, or distinguish an optional future swap from protection on a borrowing already outstanding.

  • Separate a borrower’s benchmark-rate exposure, credit spread and premium cost.
  • Choose and calculate a caplet, collar or payer swaption for the relevant exposure.

C07.13

Cross-currency swaps and the re-exchange of principal

International funding lessons compare currencies and explain covered parity. They do not build a coupon-by-coupon cross-currency swap, mark its remaining principal and interest legs and reconcile that mark to a foreign bond on the same balance sheet.

  • Construct the signed two-currency cash schedule for a synthetic domestic borrowing.
  • Value the remaining swap consistently and identify how basis, settlement and counterparty exposure enter.

C07.14

Option strategies, premiums and expiration profit

The first derivatives lesson describes stock protection and introduces a vertical call spread as an approximation to a digital option. It does not compare the premium-inclusive breakevens, worst losses and best outcomes of stock overlays, a directional vertical and a long straddle from the same market quotes.

  • Build expiration profits for a covered call, protective put, bull call spread, bear put spread and long straddle.
  • Identify the market view, breakevens and downside exposure that distinguish those positions.

Public benchmarks

École polytechnique fédérale de Lausanne, Switzerland

FIN-416 Interest rate and credit risk models

Learning outcomes include valuing credit default swaps, working with rate derivatives and comparing caps, floors and swaptions.

Hong Kong University of Science and Technology

FINA 557 Fixed Income Derivatives

The syllabus progresses from rate forwards and futures to single-currency and cross-currency swaps, and then to caps, floors, collars and swaptions.

European Central Bank

Credit default swaps and counterparty risk

The report explains reference-entity risk, counterparty exposure and the use of auctions to establish recovery values for credit-event settlement.

European Central Bank

Role of cross currency swap markets in funding and investment decisions

The study follows interest and principal across currencies and connects the cross-currency basis to funding choices and hedging demand.

University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, United States

ASRM 410 Investments and Financial Markets, General Properties of Options, Part I: Option Contracts and Strategies

The chapter combines stock and options, considers the effect of initial premiums, and develops bull and bear spreads and same-strike straddles.

Cboe Global Markets

Options Market Structure: A Half Century of Innovation, section on common options trading strategies

The exchange’s overview distinguishes premium income from a covered call, downside protection from a put, and a two-option straddle on a common strike and expiry.

Options Industry Council

Long Straddle

The industry guide relates both paid premiums to the two expiration breakevens and maximum loss, and distinguishes interim volatility exposure from expiration profit.
