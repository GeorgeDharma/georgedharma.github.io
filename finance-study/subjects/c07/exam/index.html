Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A forward and a suspicious call

A dividend-free share costs €80. A one-year zero paying €1 costs €0.95. A call and put with identical European expiry and strike €80 cost €11 and €5. Ignore transaction costs and allow borrowing and short selling.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A cross-hedge and a funding call

A firm will buy 120,000 units locally. Standard deviation of the local change is 0.12, of futures change 0.10, and correlation 0.75. The futures contract covers 10,000 units. Use the nearest whole contract. Futures fall €0.10 immediately. No offsetting physical purchase has yet occurred.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A two-state valuation and a desk hedge

A €100 share becomes €120 or €90 in one year. Safe growth is 1.02. A European call struck at €100 is valued in this one-step model. A separate continuous-time book, after an initial delta hedge, has total gamma −18 and vega €240 per volatility percentage point.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Swap marks and replacement exposure

A bank receives floating and pays 5% fixed on a two-year €10m swap beginning on reset, with annual fractions of one and discount factors 0.97 and 0.92 under a single curve. In the same enforceable netting set it owns another derivative worth +€500,000. It holds €250,000 collateral with a 20% haircut.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A forward and a suspicious call

Part A 3 marks

Find the fair forward delivery price and value of an old agreement to buy at €85.

Fair delivery is €80/0.95≈€84.21. Old long value is €80−€85×0.95=−€0.75 per share. Award one mark for discounted setup and one for each result.

Part B 4 marks

Test put–call parity and identify the initial trade.

Call plus discounted strike costs €11+€76=€87. Share plus put costs €85. Sell the call and borrow €76, buy share and put for €85, retain €2. Award one mark per calculation and trading direction, up to four.

Part C 3 marks

Verify both terminal regions and identify one obstacle to the trade.

Above €80 deliver the share against the call and repay €80. Below €80 sell the share through the put and repay €80. Deposit and option execution costs or short/financing constraints can erase the €2. Award a mark per region and obstacle.

Case 2 · 10 marks

A cross-hedge and a funding call

Part A 3 marks

Compute theoretical hedge ratio, unrounded size and executable direction and size.

h=0.75×0.12/0.10=0.9. Desired contracts=0.9×120,000/10,000=10.8, so go long 11. Award one mark per result.

Part B 4 marks

Calculate daily variation and explain why a valuable hedge can create liquidity exposure.

The long position covers 110,000 units and loses €11,000 in cash on the €0.10 fall. Cheaper physical buying occurs later and may not exactly match the futures move. Funding is needed before that benefit. Award marks for size, cash amount/direction, timing and basis.

Part C 3 marks

If local cost later rises €0.20 and cumulative futures change from inception is +€0.12, find remaining increase in net purchase cost.

Physical cost rises €24,000. Futures gain €13,200. Net adverse increase is €10,800 before fees and financing, notwithstanding the initial margin outflow. Award one mark per result.

Case 3 · 10 marks

A two-state valuation and a desk hedge

Part A 4 marks

Derive the one-step shares and cash and price the call.

Terminal payoffs €20, €0. Delta=20/30=2/3. Borrow €60/1.02≈€58.824 to offset the down-state share value. Price ≈ €66.667−€58.824=€7.843. Award one mark per step.

Part B 3 marks

Independently use risk-neutral probabilities and distinguish a physical forecast of 60% up.

q=(1.02−0.90)/(1.20−0.90)=0.40. Price=0.4×€20/1.02≈€7.843. The 60% forecast describes actual outcome beliefs, not the replication weights. Award one mark per item.

Part C 3 marks

For the separate book, estimate residual profit following a €3 spot rise and a one-point volatility rise with time fixed.

Gamma effect is 0.5×(−18)×3²=−€81. Vega adds €240, so approximate net profit is €159 before cross-effects and higher orders. Award one mark per result.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Swap marks and replacement exposure

Part A 4 marks

Compute the par fixed rate and existing swap mark from the bank’s perspective.

Annuity=1.89. Floating value=€800,000. Par=0.08/1.89≈4.233%. Fixed leg is €945,000, giving bank mark −€145,000. Award marks for annuity/floating, rate, fixed and signed mark.

Part B 3 marks

Compute the current unsecured exposure on the set.

Net trade claim is €500,000−€145,000=€355,000. Eligible collateral is €200,000, leaving €155,000 unsecured. Award one mark per step.

Part C 3 marks

If during closeout net trade value rises €100,000 and collateral market value falls to €180,000, find stressed exposure and identify the timing risk.

Stressed net claim €455,000. Eligible collateral €144,000, leaving €311,000. Price changes from final margin to completed closeout produce gap exposure even when earlier margin was adequate. Award one mark for claims/collateral, one for result and one for mechanism.

Additional exam cases

After the core paper, test the additional lectures below. Each case opens with an answer space and its own worked solution. Use the worked solutions to assess these cases separately from the 40-mark paper.

  1. C07.11 · Credit default swaps, premium legs and settlement

    A €5m two-year contract pays annually with discount factors 0.96 and 0.92, survival probabilities 0.97 and 0.92, and 35% recovery. It uses the same interval-end convention. Calculate the par spread and upfront direction at a contractual 3% coupon.

  2. C07.12 · Interest-rate caps, floors, collars and swaptions

    A €3m borrower has a half-year loan at benchmark plus 1%. It buys a 5% caplet and sells a 2% floorlet on the full benchmark exposure. The quoted net premium is zero. Calculate net cash cost at a benchmark of 1% and at 7%.

  3. C07.13 · Cross-currency swaps and the re-exchange of principal

    A UK borrower receives US$10m on a bond and uses a fixed-notional swap to obtain £8m. With one year left the swap will receive US$10.6m and pay £8.4m. Current spot is £0.78 per dollar, and compatible one-year discount factors are 0.96 for USD and 0.97 for GBP. Compute the swap and combined liability value on the same promised-cash basis.

  4. C07.14 · Option strategies, premiums and expiration profit

    A share costs $50. The $50 call and put each cost $4, the $56 call costs $1.50, and the $46 put costs $2. All terms match the worked case. Compare profits at a final price of $57 for the covered $56 call, protective $46 put, $50/$56 bull call spread and $50 long straddle. Identify each position’s breakeven.
