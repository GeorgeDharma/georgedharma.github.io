Professional workshop · C07

A six-month dollar hedge with uncertain export volume and limited euro collateral

A fictional euro-reporting equipment exporter has US$6m of firm, credit-approved dollar invoices due in six months and a further US$2m renewal that may never be signed. Its euro production commitments require at least €5.35m of terminal proceeds from the firm invoices, after derivative premium financing. The treasurer requests an executable hedge with separate stress testing for interim collateral. The objective assumes the firm invoices are actually paid on time. All exchange rates are euros per US dollar.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Corporate treasury derivatives analyst
What to submit
Submit a hedge recommendation, dealing ticket and treasury liquidity escalation note to the CFO. Distinguish contracted cash from forecast sales, gross derivative payoffs from net proceeds, and terminal protection from the cash needed to keep the hedge open.

From inputs to decision

01

Establish which dollars can support an unconditional sale

Using an unsigned renewal to settle a short-dollar forward could force purchases at an adverse price. The hedge must remain feasible if only firm invoices arrive.

  1. Classify US$6m as firm and US$2m as contingent. Match both the settlement date and legal receiving entity to the proposed bank contracts. Record the euro liability threshold of €5.35m at month six.
  2. At a terminal spot of €0.80, the unhedged firm dollars produce €4.80m, €550,000 below budget. The additional expected order cannot enter the guaranteed floor because it may be absent.

Carry US$6m as the maximum unconditional delivery supported by the firm case. Preserve a separate volume scenario for the board.

02

Convert the credit and premium limits into feasible notionals

A hedge that meets the terminal budget can still fail because its margin call or initial option premium exceeds approved resources.

  1. For each US$1m sold forward at 0.900, the stated stress quote 1.000 creates a negative discounted mark of US$1m × (1.000 − 0.900) × 0.99 = €99,000. The €450,000 line therefore permits at most four whole forward lots in this stress. Five would need €495,000.
  2. Each US$1m option lot costs €12,000 today. The €30,000 budget permits up to two whole lots. Covering all US$6m strongly suggests four forward lots and two puts. Verify mathematically that this combination achieves the budget rather than stopping at the lot counts.

Book US$4m sold forward and US$2m bought dollar puts for the proposed order. Keep the US$2m contingent renewal uncommitted.

03

Write the entire euro cash-flow equation

A treasury recommendation should remain intelligible if the CFO changes an exchange rate or sales volume. A complete formula also exposes double counting of physical dollar proceeds.

  1. Let X be US dollars actually collected and s be euros per dollar at maturity. In millions, euro proceeds equal Xs + 4(0.900 − s) + 2 max(0.895 − s, 0) − 0.02424. The option’s €24,000 upfront premium costs €24,240 at maturity on the agreed financing basis.
  2. The first term converts all invoices at spot as an economic decomposition. The forward term adds the difference between contracted and spot on its covered dollars. Equivalently deliver four million dollars at 0.900 and convert the remaining cash at spot, then add any option receipt.

Maintain a single unit convention through all legs. A dollar put in this quotation pays when the dollar becomes cheaper in euro terms.

04

Prove the firm-volume floor across exchange rates

Testing three convenient scenarios alone cannot establish a guaranteed minimum for every nonnegative spot.

  1. With X = 6 and s below 0.895, two uncovered dollars per unit of notional are offset by two put units. Proceeds simplify to €3.60m + €1.79m − €0.02424m = €5.36576m. The slope below strike is zero.
  2. Above 0.895 the put expires, leaving €3.60m + 2s − €0.02424m in millions. This increases with s, so its minimum in that region occurs at the strike and equals the same €5.36576m.

The firm budget is protected by €15,760 under the payment, timing and bank-performance assumptions. The planned hedge covers the exchange rate on receipts collected. Customer default requires its own credit analysis.

05

Attribute terminal results across volume and rate states

The board also wants to understand the upside retained and the consequences of an unsigned customer renewal.

  1. At s = 0.800, the US$6m invoices convert to €4.80m. Forward gain €0.40m and put gain €0.19m bring gross cash to €5.39m. Subtract financed premium €0.02424m to obtain €5.36576m.
  2. At s = 1.000, firm receipts generate €6.00m, the forward loses €0.40m, and the puts pay zero. Net €5.57576m. If all US$8m arrives, add the extra US$2m times each scenario’s spot, because only the firm cash was hedged.

The contingent US$2m order can be separately hedged after signing and capacity review. It creates additional exchange exposure until then.

06

Compare discarded hedges on identical settlement terms

A cheaper-looking strike or a promise to hedge everything with forwards should be checked against both terminal and interim constraints.

  1. Selling all US$6m forward would lock €5.40m at maturity with no premium, but interim collateral under the specified mark is €594,000, above the €450,000 line by €144,000. Selling US$5m forward already fails the stress limit.
  2. Six US$1m puts would cost €72,000 today, above the €30,000 budget, and their financed net floor would be €5.37m − €0.07272m = €5.29728m. With only four forward lots and one put, at least US$1m remains exposed below strike. The floor fails for severe dollar weakness.

The proposed mix satisfies the two resource ceilings and the pointwise firm-volume floor. A more generous capital or pricing budget could make another hedge attractive.

07

Issue executable instructions and stress escalation triggers

The firm can protect terminal euros and still be forced to close the forward if collateral arrives before export receipts.

  1. At the stated interim stress, the four forwards require €396,000 cash, leaving €54,000 of the approved line. The projected negative value must be measured off the live forward quote for the same remaining delivery date, rather than using spot automatically.
  2. Monitor conditional sales, invoice credit, forward market value, collateral timing and counterparty limits weekly. Escalate if projected collateral exceeds €450,000 or confirmed firm invoices fall below US$6m. Ask the bank to confirm both options’ expiry instructions, euro cash settlement and all derivative dates.

The controller should identify the hedge relationship and preserve trade and exposure evidence under the organization’s own accounting policy. This decision memo concerns economic protection and cash management.

Completed document

Treasury risk authorization · export-receivable hedge

Execution request

Sell US$4m six months forward at €0.900 per US dollar and buy two US$1m European dollar puts struck at €0.895. Pay €24,000 now, €24,240 at terminal-equivalent cost. Use the bank counterparty and settlement account matched to the firm invoices. The target is euro cash available for production commitments.

Budget outcome

Provided the firm invoices settle and the bank performs, minimum net proceeds are €5,365,760. The amount exceeds the €5.35m budget by €15,760 even if the optional customer renewal is lost. Above the put strike the firm retains dollar upside on US$2m of invoices. Additional receipts from a signed renewal would increase euro proceeds at the prevailing exchange rate.

Funding authorization

Reserve €396,000 of the €450,000 margin facility under the stated €1.000 interim forward stress. The remaining €54,000 forms a narrow buffer. Treasury must escalate a higher projected call before an unplanned derivative closeout or use of money reserved for operations.

Exceptions and owner

The sales director must report credit deterioration, delivery delays or partial cancellations immediately. Treasury will resize or roll only after reconciling new cash exposure, live replacement values and the CFO’s limits. Premiums and conditional renewal exposures must remain separate in performance reports.

The interim same-maturity forward quote rises to €1.030 per dollar with the same 0.99 discount. How far does the four-lot collateral requirement exceed the €450,000 line?

The negative mark is US$4m × (€1.030 − €0.900) × 0.99 = €514,800. Subtract the €450,000 line to obtain €64,800 additional cash required under the agreement.

Task 3

At terminal spot €0.950, suppose US$7m rather than six or eight million is actually collected. What are net euro proceeds after financing?

Unhedged conversion is €6.65m and the €0.05-per-dollar forward loss is €0.20m. Both puts expire. Deduct €0.02424m financed premium for €6.42576m.
