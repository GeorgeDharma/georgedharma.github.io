F01.11

Why tax expense can exceed the current tax bill

F01 links profit with cash and shows how depreciation changes carrying amount. The acquisition material also encounters deferred tax. A standalone asset schedule showing both the first tax saving and its later reversal connects those treatments.

  • Calculate a taxable or deductible temporary difference from two independently stated bases.
  • Reconcile current tax and deferred tax through profit across two reporting dates.

F01.12

When a warranty or lawsuit belongs on the balance sheet

F01 teaches estimated bad debts and accrued expenses. The existing cases do not work through the obligation, probability and measurement decisions that distinguish a recorded provision from a disclosed contingency under IAS 37.

  • Classify a fact pattern as a provision, disclosure or no contingent-liability disclosure under the stated rule.
  • Measure and roll forward a portfolio warranty provision without double-counting repair costs.

F01.13

Is a factory worth less than its book value?

F01 covers inventory write-downs, depreciation, goodwill and group accounts. The bridge from those measurements to a recoverable-amount test, a cash-generating-unit allocation and an asset-specific floor needs its own worked case.

  • Choose the relevant cash-generating unit and the higher recovery route.
  • Allocate an impairment through goodwill and other assets subject to asset-specific floors.

F01.14

Follow one loan through all three IFRS 9 impairment stages

F01.06 already teaches probability-weighted discounted shortfalls and a receivables allowance. The loan case here connects those tools to significant deterioration since origination, lifetime versus 12-month measurement, the net interest basis and the actual accounting entries.

  • Classify an ordinary amortised-cost loan using evidence about default risk since it was first recorded.
  • Discount and weight cash shortfalls across forward-looking scenarios, then reconcile the allowance, impairment expense and interest basis.

Public benchmarks

Swinburne University of Technology, Australia

Financial Reporting (ACC80014)

The modules teach obligations with uncertain outcomes, accounting for income taxes including deferred balances, and impairment at asset and cash-generating-unit level.

Massachusetts Institute of Technology, United States

Introduction to Financial and Managerial Accounting, lecture notes

Lectures connect inventory and operating cash with long-lived asset depreciation and deferred taxes.

IFRS Foundation

IAS 12 Income Taxes

The standard relates tax bases to carrying amounts and distinguishes current tax from future tax effects.

IFRS Foundation

IAS 37 Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets

A present obligation, probable settlement and a reliable estimate govern recognition. Possible outflows and uncertainty have distinct disclosure consequences.

IFRS Foundation

IAS 36 Impairment of Assets

Recoverable amount can be tested for an asset or a cash-generating unit, with rules for allocation and later reversal.

IFRS Foundation / International Accounting Standards Board

IFRS 9 Financial Instruments, project summary, impairment chapter

The Board explains the three-stage general model, default-event horizons and the change from gross to net interest after an asset becomes credit-impaired.

Australian Accounting Standards Board

AASB 9 Financial Instruments, compiled February 2025

The operative text in sections 5.4–5.5 requires assessment of increased default risk against initial recognition, forward-looking probability-weighted loss measurement and a profit-or-loss adjustment to the required allowance.

Association of Chartered Certified Accountants

Diploma in International Financial Reporting, IFRS 9 Financial Instruments

Exam teaching distinguishes impairment approaches and explains how an allowance reduces the amount shown for an amortised-cost asset, with movements in profit or loss.

Basel Committee on Banking Supervision

Guidance on credit risk and accounting for expected credit losses

Supervisory guidance explains how accounting estimates use bank risk information and distinguishes that work from setting regulatory capital requirements.
