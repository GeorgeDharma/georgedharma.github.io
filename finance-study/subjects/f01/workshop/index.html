Professional workshop · F01

Harbour Instruments’ first-quarter close

On 31 March, Harbour Instruments’ finance director asks why a profitable quarter raised cash by only €1,000. Treasury requires at least €40,000 available cash. The board also wants to know whether a €12,000 customer delay would have made the quarter’s dividend imprudent. Prepare a three-statement close from the ledger facts, reconcile cash in two ways and write the conclusion the finance director can send to the board. Use €000 throughout. Assume no tax effects beyond the stated tax charge, no foreign currency movements and no other transactions.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Group reporting analyst
What to submit
A quarterly close workpaper with an income statement, opening-to-closing balance sheet, operating, investing and financing cash flows, a working-capital bridge, and a signed-off liquidity note for the finance director.

From inputs to decision

01

Agree the opening balance sheet

A close built from an unbalanced opening statement cannot establish which period generated the difference.

  1. Net customer receivables equal 60 gross less 3 allowance = 57. Opening total assets are 45 + 57 + 48 + 90 = 240.
  2. Opening liabilities are 42 suppliers plus 70 bank debt = 112. Adding owners’ equity 128 gives 240. Confirm that opening cash and closing last-quarter cash are both 45.

The opening control difference is zero and all movements can be traced to the quarter.

02

Separate earnings from cash collected

The cash receipt for future services has a different accounting date from earned sales, while collecting a customer invoice is a second cash event on a sale already recognized.

  1. Recognize revenue of 180 made up of 12 cash sales and 168 credit sales. Keep the separate 6 advance in liabilities because the service will be supplied after March.
  2. Gross receivables roll from 60 + 168 − 154 − 2 = 72. The 2 write-off reduces both gross receivables and the allowance, with no cash receipt or new expense just from that write-off.

Revenue is 180, closing gross receivables 72 and closing customer advances 6.

03

Close the allowance, stock and supplier subledgers

Operating balances explain how much of the sales margin translated into cash. The allowance entry must be kept distinct from an actual payment.

  1. The allowance before new expense is 3 − 2 = 1. A charge of 3 takes it to the required 4. Net receivables close at 72 − 4 = 68, an increase of 11 from 57.
  2. Inventory closes at 48 + 110 − 105 = 53. Trade payables close at 42 + 110 − 106 = 46. The difference between credit purchases and supplier payments is visible in the latter balance.

Record net trade receivables 68, inventory 53, trade payables 46 and a credit loss charge of 3.

04

Prepare the quarterly income statement

Start from delivered customer sales and recognize the consumption of stock, equipment services and credit value during the period.

  1. Revenue 180 less cost of goods sold 105 leaves gross profit 75. Payroll 30, depreciation 9 and credit loss expense 3 reduce operating profit to 33.
  2. Interest 4 leaves pretax income 29. Tax 5 leaves net income 24. Equipment purchases, loan principal movements, the dividend and the advance each belong elsewhere.

The board’s reported quarterly profit is 24, with an operating margin of 33/180 = 18.33%.

05

Reconstruct operating cash directly

A direct cash bridge independently checks whether the accounting adjustments explain actual trading receipts and payments.

  1. Customer receipts are 12 current cash sales, 154 credit collections and 6 advances, totaling 172. Cash paid for suppliers, payroll, interest and tax is 106 + 30 + 4 + 5 = 145.
  2. Subtract 145 from 172 for operating cash 27. The future-service advance increased cash without increasing profit. A signed bank movement listing should support all four outgoing categories and the three receipt categories.

Operating cash is 27 on the stated interest and tax classification.

06

Prove cash using the indirect bridge

Starting from profit and arriving at the same 27 is a separate cross-check on stock, payables and noncash estimates.

  1. Begin with net income 24. Add depreciation 9, subtract the increase in net receivables 11 and inventory 5, then add increases in supplier balances 4 and advances 6. This gives 24 + 9 − 11 − 5 + 4 + 6 = 27.
  2. The receivable line uses the net balance, so adding the 3 allowance expense again would double count its effect. Another route adds that charge and uses the credit-sales less actual credit-collections timing difference of 14 before write-offs. Both give the same cash adjustment.

Cash from operations exceeds profit by 3 in total, while the individual working-capital movements are larger.

07

Finish investing, financing and ownership

The quarter’s equipment and financing choices explain the small net cash change and the balances on the closing statement.

  1. Investing cash is −24. Financing is +15 bank draw −10 principal repayment −7 cash dividend = −2. Net cash movement is 27 − 24 − 2 = +1; opening 45 therefore closes at 46.
  2. Equipment is 90 + 24 − 9 = 105. Bank debt closes at 70 + 15 − 10 = 75. Owners’ equity closes at 128 + 24 profit − 7 dividend = 145. The dividend reduces owners’ claims and never reduces net income.

Closing assets of 46 + 68 + 53 + 105 = 272 equal claims of 46 + 6 + 75 + 145 = 272.

08

Challenge the dividend with a collection delay

A liquidity minimum concerns cash on the required date. Keeping all other facts fixed isolates exposure to a late customer payment.

  1. With €12k less collected before close, net receivables rise from 68 to 80, operating cash falls from 27 to 15, and closing cash falls from 46 to 34. The shortfall against 40 is 6.
  2. If the board had retained the 7 dividend under this downside, closing cash would be 41. That covers the minimum by 1, but the cushion is narrow. Ask treasury for daily dates before releasing a future distribution.

The base case complied by 6; the specified stress with the dividend fails by 6.

Completed document

Quarter-end reporting memorandum to the finance director

Reported result

For the quarter ended 31 March, sales of 180 produced gross profit of 75, operating profit of 33 and net profit of 24. The advance of 6 is held against future service. The allowance of 4 is supported by the stated closing loss estimate.

Cash reconciliation

Operating activities brought in 27. We spent 24 on production equipment and 2 net on financing activities. Cash rose from 45 to 46. Gross receipts from customers were 172 and trading, interest and tax payments were 145, agreeing with the independent working-capital bridge.

Funding and distribution

The base close exceeded the 40 treasury minimum by 6 after paying the dividend of 7. An otherwise identical collection delay of 12 would have left 34, below policy. Retaining the dividend in that downside would have left 41. For the next proposed distribution I would require dated collection confirmations or committed alternate liquidity.

Close evidence and accountability

The opening and closing balance sheets both reconcile. Obtain the bank balance, receivable ageing and subsequent collections, vendor cutoff, equipment invoices, debt confirmation and dividend authorization for the close file. A post-close payment can support collectibility while still leaving the 31 March cash balance exactly as reported.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

If gross credit collections were €9k lower with the dividend paid and no other change, what would unrestricted closing cash be?

See the full solution

Operating cash would fall from 27 to 18, while investing and financing stay −24 and −2. Starting at 45, closing cash would be 37, which is 3 below the 40 minimum.

Task 2

If the required closing allowance rose from 4 to 6, with no other change and fixed tax, how much net income would remain?

See the full solution

The impairment expense must rise from 3 to 5 so the extra two reaches the allowance. Net income falls from 24 to 22. Net receivables fall by 2 and cash remains 46 because no collection or payment changes.

Task 3

If the board wanted to preserve exactly €40k under the €12k collection delay, what maximum dividend could it have paid that quarter?

See the full solution

Under the delay closing cash before any distribution is 34 + 7 = 41. The maximum dividend consistent with 40 is 41 − 40 = 1. This assumes payment and collection dates produce the same position through the minimum test date.

Task 4

Why would adding back credit loss expense after using the change in net receivables create an error?

Saved on this device as you type.
See the full solution

Net receivables already incorporate the allowance. The net balance rose by 11 even though current credit sales exceeded collections by 14. The difference of 3 is the period’s allowance expense under the stated write-off treatment. Adding it again to the net-receivables bridge would overstate operating cash by 3.
