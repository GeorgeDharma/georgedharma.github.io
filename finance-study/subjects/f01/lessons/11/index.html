Why tax expense can exceed the current tax bill

Theory and terminology

Deferred tax on temporary differences

IAS 12 · tax base · deferred tax liability · book and tax basis

What you should be able to explain

  • Calculate a taxable or deductible temporary difference from two independently stated bases.
  • Reconcile current tax and deferred tax through profit across two reporting dates.

What the two ledgers measure

The accounts spread a machine's cost over the periods it helps earn revenue. A tax authority may allow the same cost to be deducted faster. The carrying amount is the value still recorded in the accounts. In this example, the tax base is the machine cost still available to deduct against future taxable income. Both start at purchase price and eventually reach zero.

Current tax is the amount due on this period's taxable profit. Deferred tax records the future tax consequence of recovering an asset or settling a liability already on the balance sheet. A permanently nondeductible fine affects the tax rate calculated on accounting profit, but it creates no future deduction to reverse.

Decide which balance and which rate

Compare the book amount and tax base at the reporting date. For an asset recovered through use in this illustration, book above tax base creates a deferred tax liability. The business expects to recover more book value than its remaining tax deductions. For a liability such as deductible-on-payment warranty costs, an expense booked before its tax deduction can create a deferred tax asset.

Under IAS 12 use the enacted or substantively enacted rate expected when the difference reverses. A deferred tax asset requires probable future taxable profit against which the deduction can be used, subject to the standard's recognition rules. For these ordinary operating examples all tax effects go through profit. An item originally reported outside profit can require a different presentation.

Use movements to explain the income statement

A closing liability tells you about future periods. Its change tells you how much deferred tax expense the current period records here. An increase debits tax expense and credits the deferred liability. When deductions run below book depreciation, the liability can shrink.

Check the whole life of the asset. If both systems eventually deduct the same cost and the rate stays fixed, the cumulative temporary difference goes back to zero. The business still benefited from keeping cash earlier. A stable total tax expense and a changing current payment can coexist.

Method

Build the result

Closing DTL = (B − T) × r for this taxable asset difference. Total tax expense = current tax + change in DTL.

B and T are closing carrying amount and tax base in thousands of euros. DTL is the deferred tax liability in the same units. r is the applicable tax rate as a decimal. There are no other tax items here.

  1. Subtract accounting depreciation from initial cost to get B. Independently subtract allowable tax deductions to get T.
  2. Measure the closing DTL at the applicable rate, then subtract its opening balance to find the deferred tax expense or credit.
  3. Calculate current tax from taxable profit and add the deferred amount. Compare the result with tax at the stated rate on accounting profit.
Worked case

The kiln's first two years

A kiln costs €120,000. Accounting depreciation is €30,000 each year. Tax deductions are €60,000 in year one and €20,000 in each of years two to four. Profit before either depreciation charge is €100,000 in each year. Tax is 25%, with no exemptions or other differences.

Working

  1. In €000, year-one book profit is 100 − 30 = 70. Taxable profit is 100 − 60 = 40, so current tax is 10.
  2. Closing bases are 90 and 60. The difference of 30 produces a liability of 7.5. Total year-one tax expense is 10 + 7.5 = 17.5.
  3. Year two ends with book 60 and tax base 40. The liability falls to 5, a deferred credit of 2.5.
  4. Year-two current tax is (100 − 20) × 25% = 20. Tax expense is 20 − 2.5 = 17.5, again 25% of 70.

Decision and interpretation

A cash forecaster uses the current-tax schedule and the payment dates. A reviewer can ask for a revised rate or deduction schedule before projecting the liability's reversal.

Quick check
A machine's carrying amount falls to €64,000 and its tax base to €50,000. At 25% its opening deferred tax liability was €5,000. What is this period's deferred tax entry?

The closing liability is €14,000 × 25% = €3,500. Reducing the previous €5,000 liability by €1,500 credits tax expense.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A retailer accrues a £24,000 warranty charge. Tax relief arises only on payment. Its accounting profit after the charge is £96,000. Tax is 20% and sufficient future taxable profits are probable. Find both tax components now and explain next year's settlement.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Taxable profit now is £96,000 + £24,000 = £120,000. Current tax is £24,000.
  2. The liability's tax base is zero because its full amount is deductible later. Record a deferred tax asset of £4,800 and a deferred tax credit. Total tax expense is £19,200.
  3. On payment next year the tax deduction reduces current tax by £4,800 relative to no deduction. Release the £4,800 asset as a deferred tax expense. Settlement uses cash but adds no second warranty expense.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A machine has already saved its owner tax. Why might that saving create a liability today?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Tax deductions used early leave fewer deductions for later. If the machine's book amount exceeds the amount still deductible, recovering it creates a future taxable difference.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What evidence would you need if the retailer were loss-making?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Review future taxable profits of the relevant taxable entity, timing of reversals and any expiry of relief. Recognize only the part for which use is probable under the applicable rules.
