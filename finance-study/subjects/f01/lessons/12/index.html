When a warranty or lawsuit belongs on the balance sheet

Classify a fact pattern as a provision, disclosure or no contingent-liability disclosure under the stated rule.

Theory and terminology

Recognition and measurement of provisions

IAS 37 · contingent liabilities · expected value

What you should be able to explain

  • Classify a fact pattern as a provision, disclosure or no contingent-liability disclosure under the stated rule.
  • Measure and roll forward a portfolio warranty provision without double-counting repair costs.

First identify an obligating event

A provision is a liability with uncertain amount or timing. A contract, law or established public practice can leave a company with no realistic alternative to settlement. An intention to improve next year's service generally leaves the company a choice. The reporting date matters because the triggering event must already have happened.

Under IAS 37 recognize a provision if a past event created a present obligation, an outflow is probable and a reliable estimate can be made. A possible obligation, or a present obligation with an outflow that is possible but not probable, normally goes in a contingent-liability note unless outflow is remote. An inability to estimate a present obligation reliably is unusual and generally also leads to disclosure.

Distinguish a population from one dispute

For thousands of comparable warranties, consider the whole population when deciding whether outflow is probable. Multiply the cost of each outcome by its probability and add the results. A small probability per customer can coexist with near certainty of some repairs overall.

A single claim with two possible court outcomes needs judgment. The most likely outcome can be a starting point; consider other possible amounts, their likelihood and legal advice. Multiplying every individual lawsuit by its loss probability mechanically can produce a misleading settlement estimate. Discount future expenditure when the time value of money is material.

Keep estimate changes separate from spending

A new provision creates an expense and a liability in these cases. Later, when the workshop makes repairs, charge the cost against the liability. Charging the same work again to warranty expense would count one estimate and one payment as two losses.

At each reporting date reassess open claims, failure rates and unit costs. A safety recall affecting newer products can make a historical average obsolete. Reconcile opening to closing liability, record a top-up or release and explain a significant change in estimation assumptions to a reviewer.

Method

Build the result

Closing provision = opening provision + additions and estimate increases − amounts used − unused amounts released. Portfolio estimate = N × sum of (p × c).

N is the number of covered products. For each mutually exclusive outcome p is its probability and c its expected cost in currency per product. The roll-forward excludes discount effects here.

  1. Identify the sales still covered and define outcomes that include a no-repair category.
  2. Weight each cost by its frequency, then multiply the expected cost per sale by covered units.
  3. Reconcile claims paid and claims still expected. Book the difference needed to reach the closing estimate for the remaining obligation.
Worked case

A pump maker closes its warranty book

A maker sells 1,000 pumps with standard one-year assurance warranties. It expects no fault on 92%, a €40 repair on 6%, and a €140 repair on 2%. During the year it pays €3,600 on this cohort. The updated estimate for all still-open claims is €2,000. Ignore discounting.

Working

  1. Cost per pump is 0.92 × 0 + 0.06 × 40 + 0.02 × 140 = €5.20.
  2. At sale record €5,200 expense and liability for all pumps, although most individual units will never need work.
  3. Repairs use €3,600 of provision, leaving €1,600 before reassessment. They do not create another €3,600 warranty expense.
  4. Open claims now need €2,000. Increase expense and liability by €400. Total expense is €5,600, split between €3,600 settled and €2,000 outstanding.

Decision and interpretation

Coverage and claims records reconcile to the ledger. Reassess the repair assumptions before using this cohort's rate on a redesigned pump.

Quick check
Which event uses an already recognized assurance-warranty provision?

Settlement reduces the provision and cash or a payable. The new batch creates its own obligation.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

At its reporting date a builder has a probable present legal obligation estimated reliably at £80,000. A second, unrelated claim is merely possible, with £35,000 at stake. Management also plans £12,000 of voluntary staff retraining next year. Classify all three.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Record an £80,000 provision and expense for the first claim under the assumptions given.
  2. Disclose the nature and estimated financial effect of the possible £35,000 exposure unless the chance of an outflow becomes remote.
  3. The retraining plan leaves management able to avoid the spending and creates no present obligation on these facts. Recognize no provision for the £12,000.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a company owe warranty costs before it knows which customers will claim?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Selling goods with a repair promise creates the obligation. Across many sales some claims are probable, and experience can support a reliable estimate of their total cost.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A post-year-end judgment confirms facts that already existed at year-end and fixes the first award at £73,000 before the accounts are authorized. What should the preparer investigate?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Consider the subsequent-event rule for evidence about conditions already existing. On these facts revise the year-end liability to £73,000, verify any other costs separately and document the evidence.
