Follow one loan through all three IFRS 9 impairment stages

Theory and terminology

IFRS 9 expected credit loss staging

12-month ECL · lifetime ECL · significant increase in credit risk

What you should be able to explain

  • Classify an ordinary amortised-cost loan using evidence about default risk since it was first recorded.
  • Discount and weight cash shortfalls across forward-looking scenarios, then reconcile the allowance, impairment expense and interest basis.

Choose the stage before calculating the allowance

For a performing loan originated at amortised cost, Stage 1 normally begins with 12-month expected credit losses (ECL). This includes all discounted cash shortfalls caused by a default that could start within the next 12 months. Recoveries or losses from that default can occur years later.

In Stage 2 the risk of default over the remaining life has increased significantly since initial recognition. Compare current credit risk with the risk expected at origination, considering the loan’s changing remaining term and reasonable, supportable forward-looking information. Lost contracts, refinancing trouble or a sustained downgrade can give evidence before arrears. A drop in collateral value alone may increase the loss on default without showing that default itself has become more likely. Use lifetime ECL, while interest for a loan still accrues on its gross carrying amount.

Recognize credit impairment separately from deterioration

Stage 3 requires evidence of credit impairment affecting expected future cash flows. Examples include significant financial difficulty, a default, or a concession the lender grants because of financial distress. Both Stages 2 and 3 use lifetime ECL. For this originally performing loan, interest in subsequent periods is calculated using the original effective interest rate on amortised cost after deducting the allowance.

Review staging at each reporting date. If the credit impairment ceases and the improvement can be related to a subsequent event, the normal gross interest basis can resume under the applicable rule. A return to 12-month ECL requires evidence that the significant increase in credit risk since recognition no longer exists. Purchased or originated credit-impaired assets and the simplified trade-receivables approach have different rules.

Build a financial-reporting estimate

For each plausible economic scenario, estimate the contractual cash the lender will fail to receive, when the shortfall will arise and what it can recover. For the ordinary fixed-rate loan below, discount using the original effective interest rate. Weight scenarios using their reporting-date probabilities. Within each scenario, weight default events only once and include the possibility of full payment.

A market price can move when benchmark rates or liquidity change even if collections are unchanged. A lender’s stress loss or regulatory Basel estimate can use a different purpose, calibration and horizon. Those models may supply evidence, but the accounting entry must reconcile to the IFRS 9 cash shortfall measure. A recognized allowance is a deduction from the asset, not a separate transfer of cash.

Method

Build the result

ECL = Σₛ wₛ Σₖ pₛₖ Lₛₖ. Lₛₖ = Σₜ (contractual cashₜ − expected received cashₛₖₜ)/(1 + i)ᵗ.

s labels economic scenarios. wₛ is a scenario probability. k is a mutually exclusive default time band. pₛₖ is the probability of first default in that band, measured from the reporting date within scenario s. Lₛₖ is the present value of shortfalls conditional on that event. t is time in years from reporting, and i is the original annual effective rate. ECL and L use currency.

  1. Select default bands within the next 12 months for Stage 1 or throughout the remaining life for Stage 2. Use probability-weighted expected collections for a credit-impaired asset.
  2. Within each band discount every conditional shortfall to reporting date, including later recovery. If given a later default rate conditional on survival, first multiply it by survival to the start of the band.
  3. Sum across bands and scenarios. For a static loan without write-offs or transfers, closing allowance less opening allowance gives the impairment charge or release.
Worked case

A manufacturer's three-year €100,000 bullet loan

The bank lends €100,000 at par. It receives 5% annual coupons and principal at the end of year three. The original effective rate is 5%. The first two coupons arrive in full. Ignore tax, write-offs, other portfolio movements and fees. In the first two estimates the only conditional cash shortfall occurs at final maturity.

Working

  1. At origination the normal and downturn scenarios have weights 80% and 20%. Twelve-month first-default probabilities are 1% and 4%. Either early default causes a €23,152.50 maturity shortfall, worth €23,152.50/1.05³ = €20,000 now. Stage 1 ECL is (0.80 × 0.01 + 0.20 × 0.04) × €20,000 = €320. Debit impairment expense and credit loan loss allowance €320.
  2. After year one's coupon, the borrower loses its principal customer and forecasts show sustained refinancing trouble. The bank documents a significant increase in default risk since origination, with no evidence yet of credit impairment. Stage 2 includes both remaining years. Normal/downturn weights are 70%/30%. From this reporting date, first-default probabilities in the first and second remaining years are 3% and 6% in normal conditions, 10% and 16% in downturn. The year-two bands exclude borrowers defaulting earlier.
  3. Conditional maturity shortfalls are now €22,050 normal and €33,075 downturn, each discounted two years to €20,000 and €30,000. Lifetime ECL is 0.70 × 0.09 × €20,000 + 0.30 × 0.26 × €30,000 = €1,260 + €2,340 = €3,600. Using only next year's defaults would give €1,320. Increase the €320 allowance by €3,280 with a debit to impairment expense and a credit to the allowance.
  4. After the year-two coupon, the company enters insolvency proceedings that reduce expected collections. Stage 3 applies. In one year €105,000 is due. Alternative final receipts of €84,000 and €63,000 have weights 60% and 40%, giving expected receipts of €75,600. Value them at €75,600/1.05 = €72,000. Against €100,000 gross, the required allowance is €28,000. Debit impairment expense €24,400 and credit the allowance €24,400. Net carrying value is €72,000; the subsequent year's interest at this point is €3,600.

Decision and interpretation

The allowance now reconciles as €320 + €3,280 + €24,400 = €28,000. Stage 2 and Stage 3 each used lifetime losses; the later deterioration changed recovery estimates and the interest base. The weights and judgments in the case are illustrative.

Quick check
A Stage 1 loan might default in month 8 and leave an unpaid amount in month 19. It might instead first default in month 15. Which outcomes enter today's 12-month ECL?

The 12 months limit when the possible default begins. They do not limit when the cash shortfall caused by that default can occur. A first default in month 15 falls outside this horizon.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A lender has a £40,000 fixed-rate loan with two years remaining and a £400 opening allowance. Reliable new evidence establishes a significant increase in lifetime default risk, with no credit impairment yet. Normal/downturn weights are 75%/25%. Within each scenario, first defaults in the next year or the year after have respective probabilities 2%/4% and 6%/10%. Conditional cash shortfalls at maturity are £10,816 normal and £16,224 downturn. The original effective rate is 4%. Determine stage, allowance and entry. One year later the borrower is credit-impaired, gross is still £40,000, and the only expected final collection is £29,120 a year later.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. At the first date classify the loan as Stage 2. Conditional present values are £10,816/1.04² = £10,000 and £16,224/1.04² = £15,000. Lifetime first-default probabilities are 6% and 16%.
  2. The lifetime allowance is 0.75 × 0.06 × £10,000 + 0.25 × 0.16 × £15,000 = £450 + £600 = £1,050. Debit impairment expense and credit the allowance £650, leaving £38,950 net. Stage 2 interest still uses £40,000 gross.
  3. On credit impairment, expected collections are worth £29,120/1.04 = £28,000. Stage 3 requires a £12,000 allowance, an additional £10,950 charge assuming no intervening allowance movements. Subsequent annual interest at that point is £28,000 × 4% = £1,120.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a bank recognize more impairment on a loan before its borrower has missed a payment?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A meaningful increase in default risk since lending can move the loan from 12-month to lifetime expected credit losses. Later possible defaults then enter the estimate, even when payments remain current. Recognize the increase needed to bring the allowance to the revised estimate.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If collateral prices fall but the borrower’s ability and incentive to pay are unchanged, must the loan move from Stage 1 to Stage 2?

Compare with the follow-up answer

A larger cash shortfall on default can increase Stage 1 ECL. Assess whether the collateral change also indicates a significant increase in the chance of default since origination. With no such evidence the change in loss severity alone does not trigger Stage 2.
