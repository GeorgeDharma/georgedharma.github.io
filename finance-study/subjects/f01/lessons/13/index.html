Is a factory worth less than its book value?

Choose the relevant cash-generating unit and the higher recovery route.

Theory and terminology

Recoverable amount and asset impairment

IAS 36 · value in use · fair value less costs of disposal

What you should be able to explain

  • Choose the relevant cash-generating unit and the higher recovery route.
  • Allocate an impairment through goodwill and other assets subject to asset-specific floors.

Start with an economic unit

A stone crusher feeding an internal processing line may have no customers of its own. Its usefulness depends on the line's sales. IAS 36 groups it with the smallest collection of assets whose cash inflows are largely independent when the machine's own recoverable amount cannot be determined. Expanding the group to the whole company may let profitable unrelated plants conceal a loss.

A damaged plant or a lost contract can trigger a test. Goodwill and particular intangibles require annual tests even without such an indicator. Inventory and financial assets use their own measurement rules. Start by checking which assets fall within scope and whether the carrying amount compared includes the same operations as the recovery measure.

Compare two routes to recovery

Fair value less costs of disposal is a market-based exit amount after eligible disposal costs. Value in use is the present value of the future cash flows the business expects from the asset in its current condition, including eventual disposal where appropriate. The choice of cash-flow scope, risk treatment and discount rate must be consistent.

The recoverable amount is the higher of the two routes. An owner that can sell for more than continued use should not be written below that sale route solely because management's use forecast is weak. If the higher amount still lies below book value, record the shortfall.

Allocate with boundaries

Apply a unit's loss to its allocated goodwill first. Allocate any remaining loss to the other eligible assets in proportion to carrying amounts. An individual asset cannot be reduced below the highest of its determinable fair value less costs of disposal, its determinable value in use, and zero. Reallocate any blocked loss to other eligible assets within their floors.

A later recovery can reverse a qualifying non-goodwill impairment, limited by the carrying amount that would have remained after depreciation without the impairment. IAS 36 prohibits reversing a recognized goodwill impairment. Future depreciation uses revised asset amounts and remaining useful lives.

Method

Build the result

R = max(U, S). Loss = max(0, B − R).

R is recoverable amount, U is value in use, S is fair value less costs of disposal, and B is the comparable carrying amount. All are measured on the same date in the same currency.

  1. Establish the unit and total its relevant carrying amounts, including the goodwill allocated to it.
  2. Choose the higher recovery route and calculate the unit loss. Allocate up to the carrying value of goodwill first.
  3. Divide the residual in proportion to eligible non-goodwill balances. Enforce individual floors, redistribute any excess and reconcile the total.
Worked case

A division with a separately saleable press

In €000, a division carries goodwill 20, press 90 and ovens 60. Its value in use is 115 and its fair value less disposal costs is 120. The press has a known individual floor of 80. The ovens have no determinable higher floor than zero. Assume ordinary cost accounting.

Working

  1. The unit carries 170. Recovery is max(115,120) = 120. The impairment is 50.
  2. Write off the full 20 of goodwill first. There are 30 left for the press and ovens.
  3. A provisional 90:60 split assigns 18 to the press and 12 to the ovens. The press can lose only 90 − 80 = 10.
  4. Assign the blocked 8 to the ovens. Final assets are goodwill 0, press 80, ovens 40. Together they equal 120.

Decision and interpretation

Recognize a €50,000 noncash impairment on the assumptions given. A separately observed market value may change the asset allocation even when the unit's total impairment stays fixed.

Quick check
A unit carries €90m, with value in use of €77m and fair value less disposal costs of €82m. What loss does the recovery test give?

The recoverable amount is the higher route, €82m. Book value exceeds it by €8m.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A different unit carries £12m of goodwill and £68m of eligible equipment. Use value is £69m and net sale value is £65m. Find the loss and closing balances. A year later recovery rises to £84m. Can goodwill be restored?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Choose £69m because it exceeds the net sale route. Relative to £80m carrying amount the loss is £11m.
  2. Allocate all £11m to goodwill. The closing balances are £1m goodwill and £68m equipment.
  3. The later improvement cannot restore the £11m goodwill write-down under IAS 36. No equipment loss was recorded to reverse.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

One factory machine has no customers of its own. At what level can you test whether its book value is recoverable?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Use the smallest group of assets that generates cash inflows largely independently of other groups. Compare that unit's carrying amount with the higher of continued-use value and net sale value.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Would a single aggressively optimistic growth rate be enough evidence to avoid the loss?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Test the forecast against contracts, capacity and externally supported assumptions, and check consistency with the discount rate. Show how a plausible change alters the recoverable amount before accepting the estimate.
