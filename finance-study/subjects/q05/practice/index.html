Theory midterm · 10 marks

A forecast wins by looking into the future

After lessons 1–5, allow 20 minutes. Explain the reasoning behind your answer.

A researcher predicts next month’s return using monthly fundamentals. It uses a year-end revised database, standardizes every column with the full-sample mean, and reports the best result out of 100 models on the same test period.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Identify two independent forms of information leakage and repair each.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    How does choosing the best of 100 models change interpretation of the test?

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    Specify a simple benchmark and a comparison that reflects trading.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the midterm marking guide

Part A · 4 marks

The revised values may have been unavailable at prediction time. Use point-in-time releases with publication lags. Global standardization uses future values. Fit scales on the training window and apply those fixed values forward. Give a mark for each diagnosis and repair.

Part B · 3 marks

The test period has become part of model selection, creating optimistic performance. Select with a past validation period and reserve a later untouched period. Record the number of alternatives searched.

Part C · 3 marks

Use a forecast available at each decision date, such as the historical training mean or zero. Compare out-of-time loss and a feasible portfolio after costs, using identical dates and universe. Also explain the uncertainty in the estimated improvement.

Problem 1

Errors of a forecast are 2, −1 and 3. Compare its mean absolute error with a naive forecast whose mean absolute error on the same dates is 2.4.

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

Mean absolute error is (2 + 1 + 3)/3 = 2. The model is better on these three points, but the sample is too small to support a strong generalization.

Problem 2

A researcher chooses the best of 200 rules using the final test period. What happens to the interpretation?

The period has served in model selection. Report that search, then evaluate a frozen rule on fresh data with the same baseline and trading assumptions.

Suggested academic marking scheme

Use these weights when assessing the complete subject. The four problem sets collect the written problems from individual lectures. You can use the 10-mark midterm and the 40-mark subject paper with the weights below.

Problem sets 25%
Four sets per standard subject. Show reasoning, calculations, units and checks. The two practice problems in each subject are worked samples for these sets.
Theory midterm 20%
After the first half of the subject. Explain or derive a model, identify assumptions and state what would change if one failed.
Seminar paper or laboratory 25%
During the second half. Apply the theory to a case, dataset or implementation. Address an alternative explanation and document how to reproduce the result.
Individual final examination 30%
At the end of the subject. Solve an unfamiliar problem, interpret the result and critique a claim. M&A and lending each include a 30-mark examination question with a marking guide.
