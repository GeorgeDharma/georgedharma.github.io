Professional workshop · Q05

Rebuild an earnings-signal rebalance from dated inputs to net active return

An investment committee has been shown strong returns from a historical two-stock earnings strategy. Recalculate a single auditable monthly rebalance using the versions of scores actually available before the order, a liquidity cap and a realistic per-euro execution charge. Compare the result with the portfolio that would have made no trade. Trace the impact to individual active holdings and explain whether the evidence supports live use or further testing. All names and prices in the case are synthetic.

From inputs to decision

01

Freeze the information set and benchmark

A reproducible return starts with an order the desk could actually have placed. The no-trade benchmark asks whether this rebalance improved on retaining the positions the desk already had.

  1. From the archived May 2 snapshot, score ordering is B (0.90), D (0.80), A (0.60), C (0.50). The top two are B and D. The June 12 restatement was unavailable at the May decision.
  2. Record the fixed €1m base, consistent currency, start and end marks, zero external cash flows and total-return outcomes. Freeze the no-trade benchmark weights (0.40, 0.30, 0.20, 0.10).

Select the executable May top two and preserve all four rows, including the later strong performer C that the live ranking would not select.

02

Translate unconstrained targets into planned trades

Portfolio weights must become currency orders against opening holdings. An analyst who writes the target weights directly into a return calculation skips the liquidity decision and turnover costs.

  1. Equal weights on B and D imply target amounts (€0, €500k, €0, €500k). Against opening amounts, planned trades are sells of €400k A and €200k C, buys of €200k B and €400k D.
  2. Planned purchases and sales each total €600k, so the target is self-funded before trading costs. Gross planned turnover is €1.2m and half-turnover 60% of the opening portfolio.

Keep the target and actual filled portfolio distinct in the ledger so infeasible positions do not receive investable returns.

03

Apply the capacity rule and finance the buys

B can take its planned €200k purchase. D can take only €250k of the planned €400k. The policy specifies that sells are scaled in their original ratio to meet the feasible buy amount.

  1. Total allowed purchases are €450k. Planned sales total €600k, giving a sales scale of 450/600=0.75. Sell €300k A and €150k C.
  2. Buy €200k B and €250k D. Ending opening-day invested amounts are (€100k, €500k, €50k, €350k), which sum to €1m. The holding weights for the return period are therefore (0.10,0.50,0.05,0.35).

The achieved portfolio retains C because liquidity prevents a complete transition. There is no unexplained cash inflow required by the trade list.

04

Calculate execution expenses on actual orders

The waiting investor cares about the net result and the policy quotes cost per euro traded. Once-only booking prevents confusing a one-way fee with a half-turnover convention.

  1. Gross actual traded notional is €300k + €150k + €200k + €250k = €900k. The half-turnover summary is 45%, while gross traded notional is 90% of the portfolio.
  2. Apply 0.001 × €900k = €900, equivalent to nine basis points of portfolio value. The fee is settled at the interval end under the stated convention, so beginning invested weights can be used directly.

Record a single €900 charge and leave the no-trade benchmark at zero fees. A model using 45% × 10 bp would halve the actual cost.

05

Reconcile wealth and per-security attribution

Security-level active P&L reveals whether the forecast, position cap or retained names drove the result. Every contribution must reconcile to an actual exposure and a realized return.

  1. Rebalanced gross P&L is −€2,000 on A, +€15,000 on B, +€4,000 on C and +€3,500 on D. The total is +€20,500. The no-trade portfolio earns −€8,000, +€9,000, +€16,000 and +€1,000, totaling +€18,000.
  2. Gross active contributions are +€6,000 on the A underweight, +€6,000 on B, −€12,000 on C and +€2,500 on D. They sum to +€2,500. Subtracting the €900 fee gives +€1,600 net active P&L.

Final strategy value is €1,019,600 against €1,018,000 for no trade. Net active return measured as the arithmetic difference relative to common starting wealth is +0.16 percentage point.

06

Test the tempting revised-data story

The strongest subsequent security is also the name with the favorable post-decision revision. A modern database can therefore produce a deceptively persuasive historical argument.

  1. With C restated to 0.95 the ranked top two would incorrectly become C and B. Equal-weighting their realized returns gives gross return (8%+3%)/2=5.5% on an unconstrained paper book.
  2. The genuine unconstrained May ranking B and D has gross return (3%+1%)/2=2.0%; the actual cap-constrained portfolio earns 2.05% gross. Timing leakage and feasibility are distinct differences, so show both counterfactual calculations explicitly.

The 5.5% figure is a diagnostic of the error and cannot be reported as the strategy’s tradable return. Preserve the immutable first-vintage data to prevent recurrence.

07

Set the release criterion and a genuine forward test

A one-month result can establish whether the implementation is internally consistent. It provides very little evidence about a persistent premium, residual risk or the capacity at a larger notional.

  1. At these realized returns, gross active value of €2,500 is exhausted at a per-side cost of 2,500/900,000 = 0.0027778, or 27.78 basis points per euro traded. The supplied 10 bp charge leaves positive net active value in this one case.
  2. Before a live release, fix score vintage, formation delay, liquidity participation, cost model, benchmark and selection rules. Freeze transformations inside dated training windows, keep an untouched test period and compare net outcomes including delistings, turnover and stress months.

Sign off the replay as implementable under its stipulated fills. Recommend continued paper or shadow deployment pending multi-period out-of-sample performance, trade-capacity evidence and reconciliation to executable prices.

Completed document

Quantitative research replay and release note

Recommendation

Accept this month as a passed implementation replay and retain the strategy in monitored evaluation. The point-in-time ranking and funding reconciliation are reproducible. A single positive month does not justify production capital or establish a persistent earnings premium.

Financial result

The executable portfolio returned 2.05% gross and 1.96% net. The existing portfolio would have returned 1.80% with no trades. The rebalance therefore added €1,600 after fees on €1m, or 16 basis points of starting value.

Model and data findings

The C revision on June 12 cannot enter a May 2 feature. A retroactive top-two selection produces a 5.5% gross paper return and gives a materially different story about performance. The €250k purchase limit on D also changes actual holdings. Log both vintage and order constraints in each monthly report.

Controls before deployment

Freeze the full universe and feature versions, archive target and filled quantities, reconcile total-return data and realized implementation cost, and compare every live paper order with no-trade and another defensible strategic benchmark. Escalate missing historical vintages or systematic fill-cost deviations before tuning the signal.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

Suppose D’s purchase limit falls to €100k while B’s remains €200k. Apply the same sales-scaling rule. What is net active P&L against the unchanged no-trade benchmark?

Feasible buys total €300k, so sell half each planned sale, €200k A and €100k C. Invested values become (€200k, €500k, €100k, €200k). Gross P&L is −4k +15k +8k +2k = €21,000. Gross trades are €600k and fees €600. Net €20,400 minus no-trade €18,000 gives +€2,400.

Task 2

With original fills, what per-euro gross traded cost exactly consumes the €2,500 gross active advantage? Enter basis points per euro traded.

See the full solution

€2,500 divided by €900,000 gross traded equals 0.00277778. Multiply by 10,000 to convert to 27.7778 basis points, with fees incurred on both sides.

Task 3

If C earns only 4% rather than 8% while original fills and all other outcomes hold, what is net active P&L?

See the full solution

The active C position is −€150,000 relative to the benchmark. Its active loss shrinks from €12,000 to €6,000, improving gross and net active P&L by €6,000. Add that to the initial €1,600 net advantage to obtain €7,600.
