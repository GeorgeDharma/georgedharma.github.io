Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A signal that could actually be traded

A signal uses earnings filings after market close and trades next-session opens. One share closes at 80 on release day and opens at 88 next session. It exits at 92.4. A training return spans five trading days and is finalized two days after its end. The forecasting pipeline is frozen just after all releases at close 100.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A penalized forecast and a branching alternative

A centered ridge model has Σx² = 16 and Σxy = 24 using sum-of-squares loss. Consider penalties 0 and 8. A later validation case has x = 2, y = 2.2. Separately a tree’s four ordered training outcomes are 0, 0, 4 and 4 and it can split after the second observation.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A capacity-constrained rebalance

At current prices a €5m fund holds €2m in A and €3m in B. Its unconstrained target is 60% A and 40% B. Only €0.5m of A can be bought today. Ignore market moves and use pre-cost wealth for target weights. Per-euro traded cost is 12 basis points on purchases and sales. Return variances are 0.04 for A and 0.09 for B with covariance 0.01.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

From a strategy search to an attributable result

A research team tries 10 prespecified independent null signals at 5% significance, showing only the best p-value of 0.012. On a later illustrative month its chosen portfolio earns 1.6% gross against a benchmark’s 1.1%. Portfolio and benchmark costs are 0.25% and 0.05%. Active factor exposure 0.4 multiplies a factor return of +1.0%. All figures refer to matching periods.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A signal that could actually be traded

Part A 3 marks

Calculate an implementable return and a leaked close-entry return on this share.

Implementable return is 92.4/88 − 1 = 5% (1). Leaked return is 92.4/80 − 1 = 15.5% (1). The gap reflects a price jump before the modeled trade could occur (1).

Part B 3 marks

Find the latest eligible training label start t and specify the availability condition. Explain how to scale predictors in that fold.

Require t + 5 + 2 ≤ 100 (1), so t ≤ 93 (1). Estimate scaling parameters using eligible training observations under the stated cutoff and apply them unchanged to validation (1).

Part C 4 marks

A static sample includes survivors and excludes two insolvent firms. Design an investable correction and two audit checks.

Reconstruct the eligible membership as known at each date (1), follow delisting or liquidation proceeds for held firms (1), verify release versus execution timestamps (1), and reconcile cash, corporate actions, unresolved missing data or universe exclusions with dated records (1).

Case 2 · 10 marks

A penalized forecast and a branching alternative

Part A 3 marks

Find both ridge coefficients and their squared validation errors.

Coefficients are 24/16 = 1.5 and 24/(16 + 8) = 1 (2). Predictions are 3 and 2. Squared losses are 0.64 and 0.04 (1).

Part B 3 marks

Calculate the tree’s parent and two-child losses and explain why the reduction can overstate future performance.

Parent mean 2 gives total squared loss 16 (1). Both leaves are constant, so split loss is zero (1). Choosing among splits on this tiny sample can fit selection noise and leaves weakly estimated groups (1).

Part C 4 marks

Design an evaluation to choose among the linear models, tree and a simple benchmark for a future live period.

Score all methods on identical chronological validation origins with an objective declared beforehand (1). Fit transformations and choose penalties or tree depth using permitted training/inner validation (1). Include an executable simple benchmark under matching costs where trading is evaluated (1). Freeze the procedure and evaluate a final untouched period (1).

Case 3 · 10 marks

A capacity-constrained rebalance

Part A 3 marks

Calculate desired and feasible trade amounts and achieved weights.

The desired A position is €3m, implying €1m requested purchase financed by €1m sale of B (1). Buy and sell €0.5m each under the cap (1). Ending pre-cost weights are 50% and 50% (1).

Part B 4 marks

Calculate gross traded notional, half-turnover, euro cost and cost in portfolio basis points.

Gross traded is €1m (1). Half-turnover is €0.5m/€5m = 10% (1). Cost is €1m × 0.0012 = €1,200 (1), equal to 2.4 portfolio basis points (1).

Part C 3 marks

Calculate variance at feasible weights and state why evaluating the desired portfolio alone would mislead.

Variance = 0.5² × 0.04 + 0.5² × 0.09 + 2 × 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.01 = 0.0375 (2). The target risk or simulated return assumes an unavailable trade and omits the exposure the portfolio must retain (1).

Case 4 · 10 marks

From a strategy search to an attributable result

Part A 3 marks

Find the probability of at least one false positive in the search under the stated nulls. Test the best p-value using a 5% Bonferroni family criterion.

Probability is 1 − 0.95¹⁰ ≈ 40.13% (1). Bonferroni threshold is 0.05/10 = 0.005 (1). 0.012 exceeds it (1).

Part B 4 marks

Bridge gross active return to net active return, factor contribution and pre-cost residual.

Gross active is 1.6 − 1.1 = 0.5 point (1). Differential cost is 0.20, so net active is 0.30 point (1). Factor contribution = 0.4 × 1 = 0.40 point (1), leaving gross residual 0.10 point (1).

Part C 3 marks

The team tuned its rule after inspecting this month. Explain what its result can support and identify two records needed for an independent assessment.

The month has become development evidence, so use a fresh untouched or prospective period for the frozen procedure (1). Retain the search specification and dated data/feature/model versions (1), and the feasible universe, actual orders, fills or cost reconciliation (1).

Borrowed shares cost 5% per year on their initial short notional for exactly one quarter. Ignore dividends, interest, commissions and margin changes. Size the hedge, compute both net P&Ls, and assess a 90% closing estimate.
