Factor tests and mean–variance inputs

New and expanded lessons

Q05.11

Build relative and time-series momentum portfolios

Q05.01 works through size and value sorts and Q05.08 accounts for turnover. This chapter builds two trend signals from the very same price history and shows how their different definitions create distinct market exposure and net results.

  • Construct a lagged-return ranking and an own-return sign signal without leaking future observations.
  • Explain the net exposure and cost-adjusted realized return of each rule.

Q05.12

Translate portfolio views into Black–Litterman expected returns

Q05.04 shrinks estimated slopes and Q05.07 shrinks covariance. The new exercise infers a prior expected return from benchmark weights and combines it with an uncertain relative-return view before running a constrained optimizer.

  • Reverse a mean–variance first-order condition to obtain benchmark-implied excess returns.
  • Update those expectations with a relative view and quantify the effect of confidence.

Q05.13

Test a factor premium with Fama–MacBeth regressions

Q05.01 constructs factor portfolios and Q05.10 attributes realized returns. This chapter estimates a cross-sectional premium for pre-estimated exposures and assesses uncertainty using the time series of period-specific prices of risk.

  • Distinguish a time-series factor loading from a cross-sectional price of exposure.
  • Calculate period-by-period slopes, an average premium and an illustrative standard error, and diagnose limitations.

Q05.14

Merger arbitrage in cash and stock deals

S01.06 values cash and share consideration from the transaction parties’ perspectives. Q05.08 prices implementation costs. Here a third-party investor buys the target after announcement, hedges the announced terms, and estimates gains and losses if the deal closes or fails.

  • Infer a discounted two-outcome closing weight and stress its dependence on the break price and timetable.
  • Size a fixed-ratio stock hedge and calculate both outcomes after share-borrow fees.
  • Distinguish a conditional annualized spread from a strategy's expected return.

Public benchmarks

University of Milano-Bicocca, Italy

Strategie di Investimento (Investment Strategies), 2022–23

The assessed investment course combines Black–Litterman allocation and implementation, Fama–French and other equity models, portfolio screening, performance attribution and practical model-building.

Dartmouth College, Tuck School of Business, United States

Kenneth R. French Data Library, Detail for Monthly Momentum Factor (Mom)

The data definition gives the prior-month return window, monthly rebalance, size and momentum sorting, and the average winner-minus-loser return for its specific US equity momentum factor.

Tobias Moskowitz, Yao Hua Ooi and Lasse Heje Pedersen, NYU Stern-hosted research, United States

Time Series Momentum

The research studies whether each instrument's own past excess return predicts its future return across futures and forward markets, and distinguishes this from ranking assets against one another.

University of Houston, Bauer College of Business, United States

Raul Susmel, Financial Econometrics, 2023 Lecture Notes, cross-sectional asset-pricing tests

The lectures specify separate time-series beta estimates, cross-sectional regressions and extensions to multiple risk factors in tests of expected excess returns.

Eugene F. Fama, University of Chicago, Nobel Prize lecture

Two Pillars of Asset Pricing

The lecture explains testing pricing relations with repeated monthly cross-sections, and how time variation in their slopes informs the precision of the average slope.

UPF Barcelona School of Management, Pompeu Fabra University, Spain

MSc in Finance and Banking, Mergers & Acquisitions

The master's syllabus pairs cash and stock deal valuation with hedge funds' role in transactions and identifies merger arbitrage as part of deal design.

Columbia Business School, United States

Legal Financial Arbitrage: Merger Arbitrage and Beyond

The course examines how investors assess legal and regulatory events in special situations and incorporate those judgments into an investment analysis.

The Journal of Finance, Mark Mitchell and Todd Pulvino

Characteristics of Risk and Return in Risk Arbitrage (2001)

The empirical study uses 4,750 announced mergers from 1963–1998 to examine transaction costs, asymmetry of deal outcomes and the changing association between strategy returns and broad market returns.

Federal Reserve Bank of New York, United States

Merger Options and Risk Arbitrage, Staff Report 761

The paper models how prices of target shares and options jointly inform forecasts of pending deal outcomes and evaluates the predictions empirically.
