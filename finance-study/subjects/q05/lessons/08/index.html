Execution and costs

Turn changes in holdings into trades, define the turnover convention and deduct spreads, fees, impact or borrow costs on the matching basis.

The starting point

A trading strategy incurs costs when holdings change. The bid–ask spread compensates liquidity providers, fees are charged by brokers or venues, and price impact can grow with order size. Short positions may also require borrowing securities for a fee over the holding period. Match each cost to the trades or balances that generate it.

Turnover has several definitions. State whether it is purchases plus sales divided by portfolio value, or half that amount for a balanced rebalance. Costs quoted per euro traded apply to gross traded notional. Applying a per-side fee to half-turnover would understate the charge.

Theory and method

Perold implementation shortfall

Decision-price benchmark · Opportunity cost of unfilled orders

A trader shows a small fee bill on a partially filled buy order. How would you measure the cost of the shares never acquired?

At the decision time a manager wanted a specific number of shares at an observable reference price. A paper portfolio obtains the whole order at that price, while the live portfolio pays actual executions and fees and may leave part unfilled. Comparing their wealth at the same final valuation captures both execution slippage and the opportunity cost of missing a rising stock. When the stock falls, the unfilled component can be a gain relative to the paper plan.

How the result is built

ISbuy = Σⱼqⱼ(pⱼ−p₀) + fees + (Q−Σⱼqⱼ)(pT−p₀)

Q is intended shares, qⱼ and pⱼ each fill’s shares and euros per share, p₀ the decision reference price, pT final reference price, and ISbuy the positive euro cost relative to the full paper buy. Assume zero cash interest during the measurement interval.

  1. For purchased shares, compare their actual cash cost with the intended shares times decision price. Summing the fill differences isolates execution slippage.
  2. For unfilled shares, compare the end value the paper position would have earned with the cash left uninvested. Add explicit fees once.
  3. For Q=1,000, p₀=50, 600 at 50.10, 200 at 50.30, pT=50.40 and fees 16, cost is 60+60+80+16=€216, or 43.2 basis points on intended €50,000.

When the reasoning holds

  • Define the decision timestamp, final marking horizon and treatment of cash in advance. Changing the reference after execution changes the counterfactual.
  • The displayed signs apply to a buy. A sell order requires the opposite direction for price slippage and opportunity cost.
Terms in this lesson
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Basis point
One hundredth of a percentage point. A rise from 4.00% to 4.01% is one basis point.
How to read the notation
Basis points and percentage points
A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises by one percentage point, or 100 basis points. Its increase relative to the original rate is (5 − 4) ÷ 4 = 25%.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

A €100,000 portfolio buys €10,000 and sells €10,000. Gross traded notional is €20,000 and half-turnover is 10%. At 10 basis points per euro traded, cost is €20,000 × 0.001 = €20, or 0.02% of initial portfolio value.

Measure trading from pre-trade positions

A target weight change should be computed from the portfolio’s weights immediately before the rebalance. Even without trading, previous returns cause holdings to drift. If a winning stock grows from a 50% share to 55%, resetting it to 50% requires a sale even though the target percentages did not change.

Total traded notional is the sum of absolute currency amounts bought and sold. One-way or half-turnover is half the sum of absolute weight changes under common definitions. When flows or leverage change, buying and selling can be unbalanced, so publish the convention and actual monetary trades.

Break down implementation costs

A marketable purchase crossing a spread typically pays an ask above the midpoint, and a sale receives a bid below it. If the quoted spread is full bid–ask width, a one-way midpoint-to-execution estimate often uses half that width for a small trade. Explicit fees and modeled impact are separate components.

Price impact can grow faster than trade size and depends on liquidity and execution horizon. Short borrowing costs accrue over the duration and market value of borrowed holdings. Apply each cost on its intended basis to avoid double counting or omitting an expensive exposure.

Reconcile predicted and executed performance

Paper returns calculated at a midpoint or decision price differ from completed trading results. Implementation shortfall compares actual outcomes with a defined paper portfolio, incorporating price moves during execution, costs and any unfilled opportunity under its convention.

Use matching currency, corporate actions and dates when reconciling holdings and cash. Record trades skipped for capacity or unavailable borrowing. A strategy can show positive gross signal performance while producing a net loss under plausible execution assumptions.

Method

Cost a rebalance

  1. Revalue existing holdings

    Calculate current cash and market values after returns but before trading. Compute target amounts on the proper portfolio base.

  2. Translate changes into trades

    Take the difference asset by asset, retain buy and sell signs and sum absolute traded amounts for per-side fees.

  3. Accrue separate charges

    Add spread crossing, fees, estimated impact and time-based borrowing on appropriate quantities. Recompute net performance consistently.

Worked case

Restoring equal weights

A €1m portfolio starts equally invested in two shares. A rises 20% and B is flat. The manager rebalances to equal weights at current prices. Assume a one-way all-in cost estimate of 15 basis points per euro traded and use pre-cost wealth for targets.

Working

  1. Before trading the holdings are €600,000 and €500,000. Portfolio value is €1.1m, so targets are €550,000 each.
  2. Sell €50,000 A and buy €50,000 B. Gross traded notional is €100,000 and half-turnover is €50,000/€1.1m ≈ 4.55%.
  3. Estimated trading cost is €100,000 × 0.0015 = €150, or about 1.36 basis points of pre-trade portfolio value. Exact post-cost target weights would require a small additional cash adjustment.

Interpretation

Comparing yesterday’s 50/50 targets with today’s 50/50 targets would incorrectly predict no trades at all.

A common mistake

Deduct a rate quoted per euro traded from outstanding portfolio value every day regardless of trading.

How to correct it

Apply execution charges to actual trades. Separate holding-period fees such as borrow or management charges and apply their accrual convention to relevant outstanding amounts.

Quick check
For the same rebalance, suppose the all-in fee doubles to 20 basis points per euro traded. What is the cost?

Twenty basis points is 0.002. Applying it to €20,000 of purchases plus sales yields €40. The turnover convention does not change the actual traded amount.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A fund buys €300,000 and sells €200,000. One-way fee plus spread estimate is 8 basis points. It also holds a €100,000 short for 30 days at a 3.6% annual simple borrow fee on actual/360.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compute trading and borrowing costs separately.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Why would half-turnover multiplied by the quoted trading rate understate cost here?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Gross notional is €500,000, giving €500,000 × 0.0008 = €400. Borrow cost is €100,000 × 0.036 × 30/360 = €300. Total for these stated components is €700.

Part B 3 marks

The 8-basis-point estimate applies to each euro purchased or sold. Halving notional discards half of the actual fee base. Unbalanced flows make assuming equal purchases and sales particularly inappropriate.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A trader shows a small fee bill on a partially filled buy order. How would you measure the cost of the shares never acquired?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would mark both the intended paper order and the actual executed order at the same final price. The two fills cost €120 versus the decision price. The missing 200 shares would have earned €80, and fees add €16. The full €216 shortfall therefore captures both trading prices and the cost of not getting all the desired exposure.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What happens if the final reference price is €49.50 instead?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The opportunity component becomes 200 × (49.50−50)=−€100. Filled-share slippage stays €120 and fees €16, so total shortfall becomes €36. Missing the price decline helped relative to the intended paper order in this particular measurement window.
