Nonlinear learning

Trees can capture interactions and thresholds. Compare their additional flexibility with overfitting risk and inspect where predictions are unstable.

The starting point

A decision tree divides observations using threshold questions. A branch may ask whether leverage is high and then whether refinancing is near. This represents an interaction, meaning the predictive effect of one feature depends on the other. A single straight-line slope without interaction terms can miss such a pattern.

Tree fitting searches over many splits. Tiny terminal groups can fit chance movements, and small data changes can alter the chosen threshold. Limit depth, require enough observations in each leaf and evaluate the finished rule on later dates. Averaging suitably varied trees can reduce prediction variance while preserving nonlinear behavior.

Theory and method

Bootstrap aggregation and random forests

Bagging · Tree correlation

A team already averages 100 decision trees. Why might 900 more trees barely reduce prediction variance?

Trees fitted to different resamples can make the same mistakes because they observe similar market history and favor the same dominating feature. Averaging cancels the independent parts of their errors but retains the shared component. Random forests introduce feature subsampling to decorrelate trees as well as fitting varied samples. Lower correlation can improve ensemble stability even if an individual tree becomes somewhat weaker. The trade-off must be assessed on appropriate later dates.

How the result is built

Var(B⁻¹ΣⱼTⱼ) = σ²[ρ + (1−ρ)/B]

B is the number of trees, Tⱼ a random tree prediction at the same feature point across training/randomization repetitions, σ² their common variance and ρ the assumed common pairwise correlation. Var has squared prediction units.

  1. Expand the variance of the sum into B individual variances and B(B−1) ordered covariance terms.
  2. Substitute σ² and ρσ², then divide by B² because the ensemble uses an average. The common component tends to ρσ² as B grows.
  3. At σ²=25 and ρ=0.2, ensemble variance equals 5+20/B. It falls to 5.2 at B=100 and only to 5.02 at B=1,000.

When the reasoning holds

  • Equal variances and common correlations simplify the formula. With different trees inspect the full covariance structure and validation outcomes.
  • Randomized iid bootstrap sampling of dated finance rows can violate chronology. Fit the ensemble within time-valid training folds and preserve market or issuer dependence where relevant.
Terms in this lesson
Overfitting
Learning patterns peculiar to one sample that fail to hold up on new data.
Regression
A way of estimating how an outcome varies with one or more observed inputs.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.

All notation examples

First example

One fitted tree forecasts a 6% default probability for firms with high leverage and large debt due within a year, but 2% for highly leveraged firms with distant maturities. This conditional difference is a model prediction. It does not establish that moving one debt maturity will cause a four-point risk reduction.

A tree partitions the observations

A regression tree repeatedly divides observations by conditions such as “leverage exceeds three times operating profit.” At a terminal leaf the squared-error-optimal prediction is the average training outcome among observations in that leaf. Different branches can split on different features, creating interactions.

For classification the leaf may predict a category or estimated event probability. A tiny leaf containing two defaults and zero nondefaults predicts 100% under the raw frequency rule, yet its uncertainty is large. Minimum leaf-size and validation rules protect against such extremes.

Compute a split improvement

For a candidate split, calculate squared errors using the unsplit mean and subtract the sum of squared errors using the two child means. A large reduction indicates the threshold divides outcomes in the training set. Growing many potential splits allows a tree to capitalize on chance patterns.

A split threshold between observed values may be unstable. If future companies cluster near the chosen boundary, a tiny input change can jump the prediction from one leaf mean to another. Stress values around the boundary and check their economic plausibility.

Control flexibility and inspect interactions

Limit maximum depth or minimum leaf size, or prune weak branches using a complexity penalty chosen on earlier validation dates. Ensembles can reduce the variance of a single tree. Averaging predictions across trees trained on varied samples is one such approach. It changes interpretability and computational needs.

A fitted threshold is predictive under the observed data and training procedure. Calling it a causal tipping point would require a research design addressing differences between firms above and below it. Compare with a simple linear or grouped baseline.

Method

Evaluate a proposed split

  1. Calculate the parent loss

    Compute a common mean for all observations and the sum of squared deviations about that mean.

  2. Compare child nodes

    Calculate separate means and losses on each side of the proposed threshold. Store counts as well as improvement.

  3. Challenge stability

    Test nearby thresholds and new dates, constrain small leaves and examine whether a simpler economic rule performs similarly.

Worked case

A threshold in funding pressure

Four firms have ordered refinancing-pressure scores 1, 2, 3 and 4 and subsequent losses 1, 1, 5 and 7 in common units. A one-level tree splits between scores 2 and 3.

Working

  1. The parent mean is 3.5 and parent squared loss is 6.25 + 6.25 + 2.25 + 12.25 = 27.
  2. The lower leaf mean is 1 with zero squared loss. The upper leaf mean is 6 with squared loss 1 + 1 = 2.
  3. The training gain from this split is 25. If a later firm with score 3 has a loss of zero, the upper-leaf prediction 6 incurs squared error 36. Historical gain alone cannot show forecast success.

Interpretation

With two observations per leaf the threshold deserves particular scrutiny. A change in one recorded loss could materially alter both predictions and selected cut.

A common mistake

A deep tree with zero training error must be the best predictor available for live trading.

How to correct it

Check untouched later data, loss after costs where relevant, and sensitivity to small feature changes. A tree can isolate historical observations without capturing repeatable patterns.

Quick check
A leaf forecasts 80% default from four defaults among only five firms. Which next step directly tests its reliability?

A five-firm estimate has substantial sampling uncertainty. Out-of-sample evaluation tests whether the pattern persists after the tree’s search over historical splits.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Returns for four observations with increasing signal scores are 0, 2, 4 and 6. Consider splitting after the second observation.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate unsplit and split squared-error losses.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    A proposed extra split isolates the single observation with return 6. Explain what should govern acceptance.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

The parent mean 3 gives 9 + 1 + 1 + 9 = 20. Child means 1 and 5 yield losses 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4. Training improvement is 16.

Part B 4 marks

The extra split lowers training loss but leaves tiny leaves. Use minimum sample-size constraints, dated validation, stability of the threshold and the economic cost of errors to decide whether added flexibility generalizes.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A team already averages 100 decision trees. Why might 900 more trees barely reduce prediction variance?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Increasing the tree count primarily attacks randomness specific to individual trees. At 100 trees the variance is already 5.2 against a theoretical floor of five in this model. I would investigate shared mistakes across regimes and features, check whether decorrelation changes validation performance, and compare the added computational cost with any measurable economic improvement.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Would making tree predictions less correlated always improve the portfolio?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The formula holds individual variance and prediction quality fixed. Aggressive feature randomization may introduce bias or remove information important to trading. Compare full forecast loss, risk exposures, turnover and net portfolio outcomes on the same later dates.
