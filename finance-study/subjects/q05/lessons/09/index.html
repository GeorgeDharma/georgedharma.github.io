Multiple testing and robustness

Repeatedly trying signals can select luck. Report the search process, uncertain estimates, regime results and a genuinely untouched period.

The starting point

Searching many signals increases the chance of finding a persuasive historical result by luck. A single-test p-value assumes a particular test, while selecting the winning result after repeatedly changing inputs changes the statistical question. Keep a record of attempted signals, parameter choices and discarded portfolios.

Report results across plausible settings and economic regimes. Consider multiple-testing adjustments appropriate to the collection of tests, and evaluate a frozen process on an untouched future period. Repeatedly checking that period while redesigning the strategy turns it into another development sample.

Theory and method

Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate control

Step-up multiple testing · False discovery rate

Your team screens five factors and wants to limit the expected proportion of false discoveries among selected signals. Which thresholding procedure addresses that objective?

Searching many factors produces some small p-values by chance. A researcher may care about the expected fraction of false discoveries among reported selections, rather than the chance of making even one false discovery. Benjamini–Hochberg ranks p-values and compares them with increasingly permissive thresholds. It can retain more promising signals than a family-wise rule, provided its dependence and p-value assumptions hold and the actual search family is honestly recorded.

How the result is built

k=max{i:p₍ᵢ₎≤iq/m}; reject hypotheses with ranks 1 through k

m is the number of tested hypotheses, p₍ᵢ₎ the i-th smallest p-value, i its rank, q the chosen false-discovery-rate target, and k the largest passing rank. If no rank passes, reject none.

  1. Sort the five valid p-values 0.004, 0.018, 0.032, 0.15 and 0.40. At q=0.05 their thresholds are 0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04 and 0.05.
  2. The largest passing rank is 2. Select the first two factors and retain the other three among the documented tested family.
  3. Formally the controlled criterion is E[V/max(R,1)] where V is the number of false rejections and R the total rejections; it is a repeated-sample expectation, not an observed fraction guaranteed in one backtest.

When the reasoning holds

  • The usual BH guarantee assumes valid null p-values with independence or specified positive dependence. Arbitrary dependence can require another procedure.
  • Count discarded variants, tuned windows and other searched hypotheses where they belong to the family. Hidden trials render the nominal correction misleading.
Terms in this lesson
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
How to read the notation
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

Suppose 20 genuinely independent signals have no effect, and each test has a 5% chance of a false positive. The chance of at least one false positive is 1 − 0.95²⁰ ≈ 64%. Correlated tests change this calculation, but trying more variants can still influence the winner.

Selection changes the null question

For one specified test, a 5% threshold roughly limits false discoveries of significance under its null and assumptions to 5% across repeated samples. A researcher who publishes the strongest result from many attempted signals asks a different question about the maximum statistic.

For m independent null tests with individual false-positive probability α, probability of at least one false positive is 1 − (1 − α)ᵐ. Dependence changes the exact probability. Shared data, similar signals and repeated parameter choices can make independence implausible.

Control or disclose the search

The Bonferroni procedure controls the probability of any false rejection by testing each of m hypotheses at α/m. The union bound gives an overall false-rejection probability no greater than the sum of individual probabilities, without requiring independence. This comes at a power cost.

A different aim is controlling the expected proportion of false discoveries among selected results. The suitable procedure depends on the decision and dependence assumptions. Neither adjustment fixes lookahead data, impossible trades or a badly specified return benchmark.

Stress economically meaningful alternatives

Track the full number of searches, including window lengths, rebalancing dates, universe choices and sign reversals. Re-run a frozen rule across markets, periods and input definitions for which the mechanism should apply. Report failing settings alongside successful ones.

Keep a genuinely untouched time period for the final assessment. Once its results influence any choices, it is development data. Robustness checks can illuminate which conditions matter. Repeatedly changing the hypothesis to pass them introduces further selection.

Method

Design an honest search

  1. Define a family

    List candidate hypotheses and variations before evaluation where possible. Preserve a log of data-guided additions.

  2. Set the evidence criterion

    Choose individual or family-level error control and declare the intended economic as well as statistical threshold.

  3. Separate discovery from evaluation

    Lock the complete investable decision process, then assess untouched observations and report uncertainty and regime behavior.

Worked case

Twelve return signals

Suppose 12 prespecified signals are all null, with independent valid tests at the 5% level. A researcher plans to present any signal that achieves significance. The smallest observed p-value is 0.008.

Working

  1. The chance of one or more false positives is 1 − 0.95¹² ≈ 45.96% under independence. The expected number of false rejections is 12 × 0.05 = 0.60.
  2. A Bonferroni threshold for family-level 5% control is 0.05/12 ≈ 0.00417. The observed minimum 0.008 is below 0.05 but above this threshold.
  3. This result does not establish the absence of all effects. It says that the smallest observed p-value misses this particular family-wise evidential criterion.

Interpretation

If the researcher actually tried many more hidden variants, treating twelve as the whole family would understate the selection problem.

A common mistake

A winning backtest becomes independent evidence once its final report uses a clean statistical specification.

How to correct it

Independence concerns the information used throughout development. Retain an untouched sample or prospectively collect outcomes after freezing the rule and documenting all earlier choices.

Quick check
A researcher checks the final test data six times and changes the signal after each poor result. How should that period now be described?

Information from each check guided the design. The resulting performance incorporates choices tailored to those dates, regardless of how many lines of code changed.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A team tries 25 investment signals and asserts family-level 5% control using a 0.01 threshold on each test. All individual p-values are valid.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find the Bonferroni threshold for the claimed family-level control. Does a p-value of 0.003 pass?

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    What would the family false-positive probability be at 0.01 under mutual independence, and why name that assumption?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

The required threshold is 0.05/25 = 0.002. A p-value of 0.003 does not pass. The proposed 0.01 threshold only yields a much looser union-bound guarantee, at most 0.25.

Part B 4 marks

It is 1 − 0.99²⁵ ≈ 22.22%. Correlated trading signals can give a different joint probability. The Bonferroni upper bound does not require independence.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Your team screens five factors and wants to limit the expected proportion of false discoveries among selected signals. Which thresholding procedure addresses that objective?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would first define the full signal family and verify that the p-values are meaningful for the dependent trading data. Then I would apply the BH step-up rule at the chosen false-discovery-rate target. Here it selects the first two signals. Statistical selection still needs economically plausible costs, coherent mechanisms and tests of a frozen procedure on untouched dates.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If ranks two and three instead have p-values 0.021 and 0.029, which signals are selected?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Rank three passes its 0.03 threshold. The step-up rule therefore selects ranks one through three, including rank two even though 0.021 exceeds that rank’s own 0.02 threshold. The procedure uses the largest passing rank rather than stopping at the first failure.
