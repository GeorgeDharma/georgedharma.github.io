Features and labels

Separate information available at decision time from the outcome to predict. Track filing release dates, revisions and the delay before a trade can execute.

The starting point

A feature is an input observed before making a prediction, such as a published leverage ratio. A label is the later outcome being predicted, such as the return from the next executable price over five trading days. Timestamping both is necessary to establish what a trader could actually know and earn.

Accounting periods end before companies publish reports. Economic series may be revised, and datasets often overwrite the first release with a later vintage. Use the release version available at decision time. Allow for processing, market hours and execution delays between observing the feature and opening the simulated position.

Theory and method

Point-in-time as-of join and data vintage

Publication lag · Bitemporal data

A filing is dated 31 March, originally released in May and amended in July. Which number should a strategy use for a June order?

A financial statement has at least two clocks. Its accounting period tells what activity it describes. Its publication or delivery time tells when the strategy could observe a particular version. An as-of join attaches to a decision only the latest eligible record available at that instant. A second date restriction may identify the desired accounting period. This prevents later revisions from silently altering past trading decisions and enables exact reconstruction of a model run.

How the result is built

v*(i,t)=arg maxᵥ aᵢᵥ subject to aᵢᵥ ≤ t and the record meeting the feature’s period rule

i denotes issuer, t decision timestamp, v a version of a filing, aᵢᵥ its actual availability timestamp, v* selected version, and arg max the eligible record with the latest arrival. A deterministic tie rule resolves simultaneous releases.

  1. For each issuer preserve the period end and each filing or correction as separate records with release and pipeline-arrival timestamps.
  2. Filter to the specified period and versions accessible no later than the decision. Sort by true availability, then apply a stable tie rule.
  3. If profit was €4m on 12 May and €6m in a 7 July revision, the 15 June feature is €4m. A July decision after the revision may use €6m.

When the reasoning holds

  • The availability timestamp must reflect what this feed and strategy could actually access, including delivery and processing lag. Publication alone may be insufficient.
  • Using a revised value to measure a later ground-truth outcome can be justified for some objectives, but past predictor construction must still use its historical vintage.
Terms in this lesson
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Backtest
A historical simulation of decisions made under an explicit rule, using information available at each date.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.

All notation examples

First example

A company’s March-quarter profit is published on 15 May after the exchange closes. A strategy that trades only in regular hours can first use the figure when the exchange next opens. Booking a return earned from 31 March through 15 May leaks future information into the signal.

Define a decision timestamp

A feature is an observable predictor at the instant a forecast is issued. A label is the future outcome the strategy seeks to predict. Record at least the underlying economic date, public release time, vendor delivery time and first feasible execution. They can be days or months apart.

A quarterly balance sheet belongs to a quarter end for accounting purposes but becomes a usable signal only when published and available to the strategy. Applying a generic lag can help when release records are missing, but it creates an explicit timing assumption that needs sensitivity checks.

Define the label from the execution price

Suppose a signal is computed after Friday’s close and orders execute at Monday’s open. A five-trading-day forward return should generally start at Monday’s execution price under that trading rule. A Friday close-to-close label includes a weekend opening move the strategy could not earn.

A label may be a raw return, benchmark-relative return, default event or cross-sectional rank. Choose one that corresponds to the proposed decision. If a total-return label includes dividends, include the same economic treatment when calculating realized trading results.

Prevent leakage in transformations

Information can leak even when each raw column is appropriately timestamped. Normalizing a predictor by its mean and variance across the entire backtest uses future observations. An imputer or feature selector fitted on all dates creates a similar problem.

Cross-sectional ranks for one date can be computed using all eligible securities known at that moment. They cannot legitimately use future index members or later corrected prices. Distinguish information across assets at one known date from information arriving at later dates.

Method

Audit one feature-label row

  1. Record feature vintage

    Identify the version and arrival time used to build the feature, including restatements and corrections.

  2. Locate the feasible trade

    Use the first feasible order and execution rule after observation, taking market hours and delay into account.

  3. Define label endpoints

    Record entry, exit and any corporate actions during the holding period. Check all derived preprocessing against the same information cutoff.

Worked case

An earnings release after the auction

A company closes at €50 Friday, then releases a profit surprise after the exchange closes. Its next tradable opening price is €55 and the selected exit price is €57.20. Ignore costs and assume execution at those prices.

Working

  1. A Friday-close label gives 57.20/50 − 1 = 14.4%, incorporating the jump from €50 to €55.
  2. The implementable trade begins at €55, giving 57.20/55 − 1 = 4.0%. The opening jump was already embedded in the price when the order could execute.
  3. To compute a training row, store the release time, entry/exit timestamps and the 4% forward outcome. A signal that predicts the earlier opening jump is a different decision problem.

Interpretation

Costs, auction participation constraints and partial fills could further change the return achievable at the opening price.

A common mistake

Use the latest financial statement vintage throughout because it contains the company’s most accurate accounting information.

How to correct it

Use the initially available version for past decisions. A restatement can become a new signal on its release date, and revised figures can be used later for measurement where justified.

Quick check
If the firm restates that March profit in August, which value belongs in a June backtest decision?

A historical decision can use the information actually available then. The August restatement becomes eligible only for decisions made after its release.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A model trades next-day opens. A firm files Tuesday at 18:00 after the market closes at 17:30. Tuesday close is 40, Wednesday open 42 and Friday exit 41.16.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate the implementable return and the stale-close return.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    If a missing feature is filled using the median over all years, describe the leakage and a repair.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Actual rule return is 41.16/42 − 1 = −2%. A Tuesday-close label would show 41.16/40 − 1 = +2.9% and includes an unavailable price move.

Part B 4 marks

Future feature distributions influence past values. Estimate and save the median from the relevant training history or other prespecified contemporaneous eligible information, then apply that fixed transformation to later observations.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A filing is dated 31 March, originally released in May and amended in July. Which number should a strategy use for a June order?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

For the June order I would use €4m, the version actually available then. I would keep both timestamps and the version identifier in the feature table so a reviewer can reproduce that decision. A later correction can become a July signal. It cannot retroactively inform a June order even when the vendor’s modern history shows only the corrected value.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if the May filing reached the vendor a day after its public release?

Compare with the follow-up answer

For a strategy using only that vendor, use the later arrival plus any required processing window. A claim of faster access needs an independently documented source and executable trading rule. Reconcile each simulated order with the market session after availability.
