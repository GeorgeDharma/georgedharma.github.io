Hypothesis and investable universe

Specify why a signal might earn a return and which securities could actually have been held at each date, including delisted names where relevant.

The starting point

An investment signal is a rule that turns observed information into a forecast or ranking. Start with an economic hypothesis. For example, investors may underreact to a sequence of improving earnings reports, allowing prices to adjust gradually. State what data would undermine the proposed mechanism before testing many variations.

Define the investable universe at each historical date. Include only names actually eligible then and follow what happened to holdings that were suspended, acquired or delisted. Using today’s membership throughout history selects survivors. Currency, liquidity and execution rules also determine whether a proposed historical portfolio could have existed.

Theory and method

Fama–French size and value portfolio sorts

HML factor · Independent double sorting

A value strategy outperforms growth in a backtest. How could a two-dimensional sort help you decide whether the result is mostly a small-company effect?

A stock screen selecting inexpensive shares can concentrate in small companies, which have other distinctive risks and trading costs. Partitioning the universe by size as well as valuation lets a researcher compare value with growth within the same size group before averaging those spreads. The Fama–French HML construction provides a concrete example. Its realized payoff is a factor-return series, and its interpretation still depends on eligibility, breakpoints, weighting and dated accounting inputs.

How the result is built

HML = ½(RSH+RBH) − ½(RSL+RBL)

HML is high-minus-low book-to-market portfolio return for one period. RSH and RBH are returns of small/high and big/high portfolios, and RSL and RBL small/low and big/low. Returns use one common currency and convention.

  1. Build size and book-to-market group assignments using information and breakpoints available on the formation date. Tradable returns accrue after formation.
  2. Construct high-minus-low returns separately within the small and big groups, then give each size group equal weight in this HML calculation.
  3. For small low −1% and high +3%, the spread is four points. For big low +2% and high +4%, it is two. HML is (4 + 2)/2 = 3 percentage points.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use observations, accounting publication lags and membership available at formation. A later book value or surviving-only universe can manufacture a factor return.
  • This formula describes a particular factor portfolio. Applying equal versus value weights within cells, changing market or switching currencies changes the empirical series.
Terms in this lesson
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Risk factor
A common source of variation in returns, such as market movements or a change in interest rates.
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
First example

A test buys four eligible firms equally. Three gain 5% and one goes bankrupt, paying shareholders zero. The portfolio return is (5 + 5 + 5 − 100)/4 = −21.25%. A file containing only the three surviving tickers would misleadingly report +5%.

From an observation to a testable signal

A researcher notices that firms reporting unexpectedly strong earnings sometimes keep rising after the announcement. A testable hypothesis identifies why adjustment might be delayed, which firms should show it most strongly and the holding period over which it should disappear. Each prediction can fail.

A competing explanation is compensation for bearing a risk factor. The signal might select small, illiquid or cyclically exposed firms whose returns differ for other reasons. State beforehand which comparisons would distinguish delayed adjustment from these alternative stories.

Define the universe through time

At each decision date, build the list of securities the mandate could actually hold then, using contemporaneous listing, membership and liquidity information. Acquisitions, bankruptcies and new listings change membership over time. A static list of present-day constituents gives historical data the benefit of future survival.

Track how discontinued holdings are liquidated. A takeover may pay cash or shares, while a distressed delisting can involve a severe loss. Removing a ticker when its price history ends silently discards an outcome. Determine the treatment from corporate-action details and report unresolved gaps.

Define the currency and funding

A foreign share’s local return and its return to an unhedged euro investor differ through the currency conversion. If the foreign share grows by factor Gₛ and euros per foreign currency grow by Gₑ, the euro wealth factor is GₛGₑ. Whether to hedge is part of the investment rule.

Manage cash from corporate actions and cash left by restricted or unavailable trades under stated conventions. A theoretical ranking can exist even when an investor cannot trade the asset at the modeled time, quantity or price.

Method

Write an investable rule

  1. Specify the signal

    Define input fields, sign, ranking date and holding horizon, and articulate a mechanism with a potentially falsifying result.

  2. Reconstruct eligibility

    Use period-specific listings and constraints. Store the dropped names and reasons at each decision date.

  3. Track ending wealth

    Include distributions, currencies, delistings and the stated cash return to obtain a realizable portfolio result.

Worked case

A disappearing holding

An equal-weight fund buys three eligible names for €100 each. One grows 10%, another pays a final takeover cash amount of €120 and the third liquidates for €30. Assume no costs or later reinvestment.

Working

  1. Ending wealth is €110 + €120 + €30 = €260. On €300 invested the actual portfolio return is 260/300 − 1 = −13.33%.
  2. Using only the first two histories gives +15% from their average +10% and +20% returns. The 28.33-point difference is caused by the omitted loss.
  3. Treating the third weight as cash at its original value instead would give €330 and +10%, another incorrect result. The liquidation value is an economic outcome.

Interpretation

If liquidation cash arrived much later than the measurement date, the treatment of a tradable or marked claim on those proceeds would need to be specified.

A common mistake

Backfill a newly launched index with its current constituents and describe it as a historical investable universe.

How to correct it

Recover dated membership or use another demonstrably point-in-time rule with known limitations. Document announcement and effective dates where they differ.

Quick check
A new company enters an index in July. When can an index-members-only strategy first include it?

A historical price shows the stock existed, but eligibility depends on the mandate and date. Point-in-time membership prevents future index decisions from rewriting earlier portfolios.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A euro investor buys a foreign share for 100 local currency units when €1 buys 2 local units. The share finishes at 110 local units when €1 buys 2.2 local units. No dividend is paid.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate the local and euro returns.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Explain one way currency exposure could mislead a cross-country signal test.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Local return is +10%. Initial euro cost is 100/2 = €50 and ending value is 110/2.2 = €50, giving zero euro return. The foreign-currency depreciation offsets local growth.

Part B 4 marks

A value or earnings signal can overweight countries whose currency moves drive returns. Compare local and common-currency portfolios, state hedging assumptions, and test performance within countries or with controlled currency exposure.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A value strategy outperforms growth in a backtest. How could a two-dimensional sort help you decide whether the result is mostly a small-company effect?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would reconstruct investable size and valuation buckets at the date of formation and compare the richer versus cheaper stocks within each size band. These data show spreads in both bands, with a larger small-stock spread. The three-point factor return is descriptive. It neither proves mispricing nor says what an investor could earn after shorting, financing and trading costs.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can I substitute today’s index membership and retain the same historical factor interpretation?

Compare with the follow-up answer

That would condition eligibility on later survival and index selection. Restore point-in-time membership and corporate actions for the intended universe, then calculate returns under the stated weighting and trading rules. The revised result may have different exposures and costs.
