Build relative and time-series momentum portfolios

Construct a lagged-return ranking and an own-return sign signal without leaking future observations.

Theory and terminology

Cross-sectional and time-series momentum

relative momentum · trend following

What you should be able to explain

  • Construct a lagged-return ranking and an own-return sign signal without leaking future observations.
  • Explain the net exposure and cost-adjusted realized return of each rule.

Define the information clock

A return signal requires a precise formation date, data convention and holding period. One common equity signal for holding month t compounds the monthly returns from t−12 through t−2 inclusive. It omits the most recent completed month t−1. A total-return history includes distributions and corporate actions. At formation, retain delisted companies and use only prices known by that date.

A published factor is a precisely defined portfolio. The French US monthly momentum factor combines size groups and prior-return groups, using value-weighted portfolios within those groups. The simple four-asset exercise is a teaching strategy. Matching its ranking window alone would not replicate the published factor. Time-series studies can also use a different lookback and volatility scaling.

Choose which comparison defines strength

Cross-sectional momentum ranks assets against each other. Going long the strongest and short the weakest creates a spread even if all have lost money. Equal dollars on each side give zero net dollar exposure. Different industry, beta or volatility exposures can leave market and sector risk even at zero net dollars.

Time-series momentum assigns direction from each asset's own past return or excess return against a stated threshold, often zero. When every signal is negative it may short every market. If risk targeted, specify each volatility estimate, portfolio scaling and the information available when estimates were made. These are distinct construction decisions from the definition of direction.

Measure a trade rather than a chart

Choose an executable formation price and next-period return for each instrument. A negative weight profits from a falling price. Report gross exposure as the sum of absolute weights and net exposure as their signed sum. State the financing assumption and account for borrow availability, funding and potential recall on a short book.

For a transaction-cost assumption stated per dollar traded, sum the absolute changes in weights and multiply by that rate. Some reports define turnover as half that sum. Reconcile the convention before subtracting costs. Assess several market regimes using a frozen rule and preserve a final untouched evaluation period. A single realization illustrates how the trades work. Evaluating expected profitability requires a sample across regimes.

Method

Build the result

mᵢ,ₜ=∏ₛ(1+rᵢ,ₛ)−1; Rnet=Σᵢ wᵢRᵢ−cΣᵢ|Δwᵢ|

m is the lagged compounded signal for asset i at formation month t. s runs from t−12 through t−2 in this example and rᵢ,ₛ is that month's historical decimal return. wᵢ is the signed current weight, Rᵢ the subsequent realized return, Δwᵢ the traded weight and c the one-way cost per dollar traded.

  1. Compound the permitted monthly returns, then subtract one. Use a declared excess-return convention if the time-series threshold is meant to include the cost of cash.
  2. For a relative rule assign long and short weights by rank. For the own-trend rule assign signs from each asset's signal. Normalize both to the intended gross exposure.
  3. Multiply signed weights by subsequent returns, sum, then subtract one-way costs. Both weights and cost assumptions must be known before the holding period.
Worked case

Four falling stocks give two different books

Assume zero cash rate, freely available shorts and identical financing. Four stocks A–D have already computed eligible lookback returns of −2%, −5%, −12% and −20%. Next-month returns are +4%, −1%, +2% and −6%. Start flat. Relative weights are +0.5 best, −0.5 worst. Own-trend weights are 0.25 times the sign of each signal. Entry costs 8 bp per dollar traded.

Working

  1. Relative ranking chooses A and D. Weights (+0.5,0,0,−0.5) have gross 1 and net 0. A is long despite a negative historical return.
  2. All four own-trend signals are negative. Weights (−0.25,−0.25,−0.25,−0.25) have gross 1 and net −1.
  3. Relative gross return is 0.5×4%−0.5×(−6%)=5.00%. Own-trend gross is −0.25×(4%−1%+2%−6%)=0.25%.
  4. Each strategy trades 100% of starting capital in absolute notional and pays 0.08%. The month's returns after entry cost are 4.92% and 0.17%, respectively.

Decision and interpretation

Attribute the difference to the chosen signal and exposures on these particular returns. A full performance comparison needs many independent periods, realistic borrow and subsequent rebalancing costs.

Quick check
Every eligible asset has a positive own-trend signal. Which construction can still contain short positions by design?

Relative ranking shorts the weakest eligible assets even when their individual historical returns are positive.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Eligible lookback returns for four assets are +12%, +8%, +4%, +2%. Their following returns are −3%, +1%, −2%, +4%. Apply the same weighting rules from flat but pay 10 bp per dollar on entry. Compute net performance.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Relative weights are (+0.5,0,0,−0.5), while all own-trend weights are +0.25. Their net dollar exposures are zero and +1, respectively.
  2. Relative gross return is 0.5×(−3%)−0.5×4%=−3.50%. Own-trend gross is one-quarter of (−3%+1%−2%+4%)=0%.
  3. Both trade one unit of notional per unit capital and pay 0.10%. Net returns are −3.60% and −0.10%. The top signal need not outperform in the next month.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a stock that lost money over the lookback period still be a momentum buy?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes, under relative momentum it can be the strongest stock in a falling universe. An own-trend rule compares its past excess return with zero and could short that same name. I state the signal, lookback, weighting rule, tradable universe and turnover cost before comparing performance.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Would an equal-dollar long–short spread necessarily be protected from a market rally?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. If the long side has a higher beta than the short side the spread retains positive market exposure. Estimate and document beta, sectors and other exposures separately from net dollars.
