Portfolio construction

Translate scores into weights subject to exposure, leverage and liquidity limits. Shrink noisy covariance estimates and compare with simple weights.

The starting point

A return score ranks opportunities, but investors hold quantities. Turning scores into weights requires a budget and a method for balancing predicted gain against risk. A portfolio can face limits on position size, total gross exposure, currency, industry and how much can be traded relative to market liquidity.

Historical covariances are noisy and optimization can amplify their errors. Shrinkage blends the sample matrix with a more stable target. For a blend weight between zero and one, combining valid covariance matrices preserves nonnegative portfolio variance. Compare the resulting allocations with simple feasible rules under the same trading limits.

Theory and method

Ledoit–Wolf covariance shrinkage

Linear shrinkage estimator · Minimum-variance portfolio

A portfolio optimizer sees two almost interchangeable stocks and demands huge opposite positions. How can covariance shrinkage change the risk it assigns to that trade?

A sample covariance matrix can make two securities appear almost perfectly correlated. Their long-short combination then has a tiny estimated variance, which an optimizer may exploit aggressively. A shrinkage estimator blends a noisy sample with a more structured target. The Ledoit–Wolf approach estimates a shrinkage intensity under a specified estimation-loss objective. A fixed classroom weight illustrates the mechanism while leaving the empirical estimation of that weight as a separate task.

How the result is built

Σ̃=(1−δ)S+δF

S is the sample covariance matrix of decimal returns, F a structured covariance target on the same scale, δ between zero and one the shrinkage intensity, and Σ̃ the combined estimate. Portfolio variance is wᵀΣ̃w for exposures w.

  1. Take two assets with variances 0.04 and sample covariance 0.039. The small eigenvalue for their difference direction is 0.001.
  2. Choose a diagonal target with the same variances and zero covariance and δ=0.25 for illustration. Off-diagonal covariance becomes 0.75 × 0.039=0.02925.
  3. The smaller eigenvalue rises to 0.01075. The estimated variance of the half-long, half-short holding rises from 0.0005 to 0.005375, reducing its apparent free diversification.

When the reasoning holds

  • The target and sample should share units, observations and return horizon and be valid covariance matrices. A convex blend then preserves nonnegative variance.
  • The chosen 25% here is illustrative. A Ledoit–Wolf data-driven estimate depends on the target, estimator assumptions and loss convention, and does not guarantee profitable portfolios.
Terms in this lesson
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Diversification
Combining exposures that do not always move together to reduce dependence on a single outcome.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
Transpose and multiplication · wᵀr
A transpose turns a column into a row. Multiplying that row by a column pairs their entries and adds the products. Weights (0.75, 0.25) multiplied this way by returns (4%, 8%) produce a portfolio return of 5%.
Summation · ∑
The symbol asks you to add the terms for the listed items. For weights of 0.6 and 0.4 and returns of 5% and 2%, ∑ wᵢrᵢ means 0.6 × 5% + 0.4 × 2% = 3.8%. A standalone capital sigma (Σ) can also name a covariance matrix in portfolio equations.
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.

All notation examples

First example

A sample estimate gives a covariance of 0.018 between two equity returns. A diagonal target assigns zero covariance to different assets. Giving the target 40% weight yields 0.60 × 0.018 + 0.40 × 0 = 0.0108. That value is an estimation choice, even though market exposure has not changed.

A forecast needs a position-size rule

An investment signal can rank companies without specifying how much money to allocate. Mean-variance construction balances forecast portfolio return μᵀw against a risk penalty proportional to wᵀΣw. The vector w contains portfolio weights and Σ is the covariance matrix of asset returns over a matching horizon.

A budget constraint may require Σwᵢ = 1. Shorting permissions, gross exposure, sector neutrality and tradability create further constraints. A signed sum can hide large offsetting positions. Gross exposure sums absolute weights and measures a different property.

Why estimation error gets amplified

In an unconstrained quadratic problem, optimal weights involve the inverse of the covariance matrix. A direction with tiny estimated variance receives large weight unless expected return along it is also tiny or limits bind. Slight changes in noisy covariances can therefore cause large portfolio changes.

Shrinkage blends the sample covariance with a stable target. For valid matrices S and T, λT + (1 − λ)S is positive semidefinite for λ between zero and one because each portfolio variance is a weighted average of two nonnegative variances. Shrinkage trades responsiveness for stability.

Translate liquidity into capacity

A liquidity rule can limit daily trade size to a fraction of an asset’s typical volume. A position limit restricts total holding, whereas a participation limit restricts the change achievable today. Starting holdings therefore affect the feasible set in a daily rebalance.

Compare a model portfolio with equal weights under the same limits. If scores are close but the optimizer demands large turnover, test lower risk forecasts, shrinkage strength and stricter trading budgets. Record the economic cost of enforcing each binding condition.

Method

Construct feasible weights

  1. Check feasibility first

    Compare the total permitted long weight with the budget and inspect what shorting, cash or borrowing is actually permitted.

  2. Compute risk and exposure

    Align return horizons, calculate variance and verify units, positions, gross exposure and each constraint.

  3. Stress the inputs

    Perturb return scores and correlations, compare simple alternatives and measure both position stability and implementation cost.

Worked case

Two assets with independent risk

A fully invested long-only portfolio invests fraction w in A and 1 − w in B. Assume zero covariance, equal expected returns, variances 0.04 for A and 0.09 for B. First choose the minimum-variance weight without a concentration cap.

Working

  1. Portfolio variance is 0.04w² + 0.09(1 − w)². Differentiating gives 0.08w − 0.18(1 − w) = 0.
  2. Solving 0.26w = 0.18 gives w ≈ 0.6923. The lower-variance asset receives more weight, but diversification still favors some B.
  3. If A is capped at 60%, the best feasible weight is 0.60. Variance becomes 0.04 × 0.36 + 0.09 × 0.16 = 0.0288, giving volatility about 16.97%.

Interpretation

The cap raises model variance relative to the unconstrained optimum while protecting against concentration and uncertain risk estimates. That trade-off can be assessed explicitly.

A common mistake

An optimal weight is actionable even if the market cannot absorb the required trade at the modeled price.

How to correct it

Compare desired weight changes with capacity and execution assumptions. Scale or stage trades, recalculate exposure, and distinguish today’s feasible portfolio from its long-run target.

Quick check
A fully invested long-only portfolio has only two eligible assets and caps each at 40%. What should an optimizer report?

With full investment the weights must total 100%. Satisfying position caps while leaving 20% unallocated violates that equation unless cash is explicitly permitted as a third asset.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A fully invested portfolio wants weights (0.65, 0.35), starting at (0.45, 0.55) on €10m. Only €0.8m may be bought in A today. Ignore price changes and costs.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find the maximum achievable weight in A today and the corresponding B weight.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    If the portfolio has variances 0.04 in each asset and covariance 0.02, calculate variance at the achieved weights.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Increasing A by €0.8m raises its weight by 0.08 to 0.53. Selling the same amount of B preserves full investment, so B becomes 0.47.

Part B 4 marks

Variance is 0.04(0.53² + 0.47²) + 2 × 0.02 × 0.53 × 0.47 = 0.030036. Include both diagonal risks and the covariance term.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A portfolio optimizer sees two almost interchangeable stocks and demands huge opposite positions. How can covariance shrinkage change the risk it assigns to that trade?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would identify the low-variance direction before changing the matrix. In this example it is the long-short spread. Shrinking the correlation toward zero raises the estimated risk of that spread and improves the condition number. I would compare realized risk and trading behavior under the sample, an estimated shrinkage intensity, and a feasible simple portfolio on held-out dates.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Does shrinking toward a diagonal target always lower the risk of every portfolio?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. It can lower estimated common-movement risk for a long-long position while raising estimated spread risk for a long-short position. At the portfolio level the answer is (1−δ)wᵀSw + δwᵀFw and depends on the chosen holdings.
