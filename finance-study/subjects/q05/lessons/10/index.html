Attribution and reproducibility

Explain performance using factor exposures and actual trades. Rebuild results from dated inputs and state the conditions under which the strategy would be suspended.

The starting point

Attribution asks which measured exposures or trades account for past returns. A factor attribution multiplies each estimated exposure by its factor’s realized return, on matching time and currency bases. What remains is a residual under that model. It may include omitted risks, noise, pricing differences, fees or implementation effects.

For reproducibility, preserve dated market and accounting inputs, corporate actions, signal versions, execution assumptions and the code or formulas that generated trades. Reconcile simulated holdings to returns and cash. Define in advance which data failures, trading constraints or abnormal hedge errors would pause the rule or trigger review.

Theory and method

Brinson–Fachler performance attribution

Sector allocation effect · Selection and interaction effects

A portfolio outperforms by one percentage point. How can you distinguish its sector-weight decision from its within-sector stock choices?

A portfolio can gain by overweighting a sector that performs well or by choosing stronger securities inside a sector. It may also combine an overweight with especially good selection in that same sector. Brinson–Fachler attribution uses a stated benchmark’s sector weights and returns to allocate a single period’s arithmetic active return into these three effects. It is an accounting decomposition of realized decisions. A repeated, risk-adjusted assessment is still needed before inferring skill.

How the result is built

Aᵢ=(wPᵢ−wBᵢ)(rBᵢ−RB), Sᵢ=wBᵢ(rPᵢ−rBᵢ), Iᵢ=(wPᵢ−wBᵢ)(rPᵢ−rBᵢ)

i indexes sectors, wP and wB are beginning portfolio and benchmark weights, rP and rB their sector returns, RB the total benchmark return, and A,S,I allocation, selection and interaction effects in return units.

  1. Write total active return as ΣwPᵢrPᵢ−ΣwBᵢrBᵢ. Add and subtract intermediate terms using benchmark weights and sector returns.
  2. Because both sets of weights sum to one, Σ(wPᵢ−wBᵢ)RB=0. This permits centering the sector allocation effect on overall benchmark return.
  3. For portfolio weights (0.6,0.4), benchmark (0.5,0.5), portfolio sector returns (6%,1%) and benchmark returns (4%,2%), active return is 4%−3%=1 point, split into 0.2 allocation, 0.5 selection and 0.3 interaction.

When the reasoning holds

  • The one-period arithmetic identity assumes aligned exhaustive sectors, consistent beginning weights and return conventions. Include cash or residual sectors where necessary.
  • Multiple periods and intraperiod trades require suitable subperiod attribution and linking. Directly summing monthly contribution points need not recover compounded active return.
Terms in this lesson
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Percentage point
The difference between two percentages. A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises one percentage point.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Regression
A way of estimating how an outcome varies with one or more observed inputs.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
First example

A fund outperforms its benchmark by 0.7 percentage point in a month. A factor model attributes 0.5 × 1.0 point = +0.5 to market exposure and 0.2 × (−0.5 point) = −0.1 to size exposure. Explained return is +0.4 point, leaving +0.3 under this model.

Explain active return with exposures and positions

Attribution decomposes portfolio outcomes using a declared comparison. In a linear factor description, active return equals active factor exposure multiplied by realized factor returns, summed across factors, plus a residual. Active means the difference between the portfolio and the chosen benchmark.

Factor definitions, currency, horizon and gross-versus-net convention must line up. A return residual can arise from omitted factors, estimation error, security selection, nonlinear payoffs or accounting differences. Calling it skill requires additional evidence about persistence, costs and alternatives.

Reconcile holdings and trades first

For a period without external capital flows, ending wealth equals beginning wealth plus investment profit and loss net of charges. Position-level price changes, income, currency translation and trade-related cash movements should reconcile to the published total. A dimensionally correct factor regression does not repair a missing dividend or duplicated trade.

Distinguish economic active performance from execution effects. Comparing live fills with decision-time reference prices can reveal implementation shortfall. Comparing a gross paper series with a net real portfolio mixes execution, fees and other effects unless each component is documented.

Reproduce and govern the decision

Archive the exact input vintage, feature transformations, parameter and code versions, universe, calendar and execution assumptions. An audit should reconstruct both successful and rejected trade orders from data available at the time. A random seed alone does not reproduce a revised price feed.

Define review triggers in advance, such as withheld critical inputs, breach of position limits, unexplained holdings discrepancies or persistent slippage above tolerance. Separate an operational pause from a hypothesis losing economic support. Each calls for a different investigation.

Method

Build an attribution bridge

  1. Reconcile portfolio value

    Track opening holdings, realized trades, corporate actions, FX and costs, ending at the reported wealth figure.

  2. Apply the chosen factor model

    Use consistently scaled active exposures and realized factor returns. Identify whether exposure estimates were known before the period.

  3. Investigate the residual

    Trace its main dates and names, test omitted exposures and rebuild trades from archived inputs before interpreting manager skill.

Worked case

Three contributors to net active return

A fund earns 1.40% gross while its benchmark earns 1.00% gross. Fund and benchmark pay trading costs of 0.15% and 0.03%, respectively. Active market exposure is 0.20 to a factor returning 1.5% and active value exposure −0.10 to a factor returning −0.5%.

Working

  1. Gross active return is 1.40% − 1.00% = 0.40 point. The active factor contribution is 0.20 × 1.5 + (−0.10) × (−0.5) = 0.35 point.
  2. Gross residual is 0.40 − 0.35 = +0.05 point. Differential trading cost is 0.15 − 0.03 = 0.12 point.
  3. Net active return is 0.40 − 0.12 = +0.28 point, reconciling as 0.35 factor contribution + 0.05 gross residual − 0.12 extra cost.

Interpretation

Using net active return directly against gross factor contributions without presenting costs would change the residual to −0.07 point and obscure why it changed.

A common mistake

Use realized end-of-month weights to attribute the whole month’s return when the strategy traded substantially during the month.

How to correct it

Use beginning-of-subperiod or actual trade-aware exposures and suitable linking across periods. End weights can reflect the returns being attributed and create misleading timing effects.

Quick check
The portfolio file cannot reproduce a large trade from the dated inputs and code. Which investigation takes priority before attributing its profit to skill?

An unexplained calculation cannot establish skill. Reconstructing how the trade entered the portfolio can distinguish a legitimate decision from a data revision, coding issue or accounting mismatch.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A strategy earns 0.9% net active. Its pre-cost active factor contributions are +0.4 and −0.1 point. Its implementation costs exceed the benchmark’s by 0.2 point.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Reconstruct gross active return and gross residual.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    A position responsible for half the residual cannot be reproduced. What evidence should be recovered before any interpretation?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Gross active return is 0.9 + 0.2 = 1.1 points. Total active factor contribution is 0.3 point, so the gross residual is 1.1 − 0.3 = 0.8 point.

Part B 4 marks

Find the dated source data and releases, algorithm and parameters, requested orders, actual fills, position and cash histories, and corporate actions. Reconcile their dates and monetary effect. Report the remaining unexplained amount.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A portfolio outperforms by one percentage point. How can you distinguish its sector-weight decision from its within-sector stock choices?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would calculate the sector-weight allocation against the benchmark’s sector return relative to the whole benchmark, then selection under benchmark weights, and finally their interaction. Here stock selection contributes half a point and sector allocation two tenths. Calling the whole one-point gain stock-picking skill would ignore both the overweight and interaction decisions.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If portfolio and benchmark use identical sector weights, what remains?

Compare with the follow-up answer

All weight differences are zero, so allocation and interaction vanish. Remaining active return comes from within-sector return differences weighted by the common sector weights. Costs, currencies and cash must still be handled consistently before drawing an economic conclusion.
