Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A product line competes for machine time

A workshop has 100 machine-hours of unused capacity. A customer offers €4,900 for 60 units requiring 150 hours in total. The order adds €2,400 material, €300 setup and no new ordinary rent. Additional machine-hours displace regular work contributing €35 per hour.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

When a larger month uses costlier inputs

A paint shop planned 800 drums. Standard inputs are 3 kg per drum at €4 per kg and fixed overhead €5,000. Actual output was 900 drums. The shop consumed 2,800 kg at €4.20, and actual fixed overhead was €5,100.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A divisional bonus and an expansion

A division earns €480,000 after-tax operating profit on €3m capital. An equally risky expansion adds €1m and €130,000 after-tax operating profit. Its capital charge is 9%. A forecast also shows €700,000 of year-one sales, of which only 60% is collected that year.

A lecture venue with a capacity step

Each basic enrolment contributes €20 and each advanced enrolment €35. Expected mix is two basic per advanced. The venue costs €6,000 for up to 300 participants. Above 300, another room costs €2,250 for the whole event. Calculate with whole three-person bundles.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A product line competes for machine time

Part A 4 marks

Calculate the lost contribution, minimum total price and decision at the quoted price.

Only 50 hours displace ordinary work, costing €1,750. Minimum total price is €2,400 + €300 + €1,750 = €4,450. Acceptance adds €450.

Part B 3 marks

What is the minimum price per unit if spare capacity falls to 40 hours? State the decision.

Now 110 hours displace €3,850. Minimum total is €6,550, or €109.17 per unit after rounding upward to cents. The €4,900 offer should be declined.

Part C 3 marks

A supervisor charges 150 hours of allocated fixed rent to the decision. Evaluate this and name one extra fact that could matter.

Rent unchanged under both schedules is irrelevant. A new shift charge, lost higher-margin orders, special inspections or an avoidable lease commitment would matter if it changes between alternatives.

Case 2 · 10 marks

When a larger month uses costlier inputs

Part A 3 marks

Calculate static and flexible input-plus-overhead budgets and the effect of volume.

Static cost is €14,600. Flexible cost at 900 is €15,800. The €1,200 increase represents 100 extra drums at €12 standard materials each.

Part B 4 marks

Split the remaining excess into material price, material usage and fixed spending, then reconcile actual cost.

Price adds 2,800 × €0.20 = €560. Usage adds (2,800 − 2,700) × €4 = €400. Fixed spending adds €100. Actual cost is €16,860, exceeding flex by €1,060.

Part C 3 marks

Can the bridge establish who caused the excess? Specify evidence for two explanations.

It reveals differences, not causation. Check material grade and invoices for a price change and production logs, scrap or calibration for consumption. A higher grade could affect both price and yield.

Case 3 · 10 marks

A divisional bonus and an expansion

Part A 3 marks

Compute the division's return before and after expansion and the expansion's own return.

Existing return is 16%. Combined return is €610,000/€4m = 15.25%. The expansion's own return is 13%.

Part B 4 marks

Compute both annual economic profits and diagnose a bonus based on increasing divisional return.

Existing economic profit is €480,000 − €270,000 = €210,000. Combined economic profit is €610,000 − €360,000 = €250,000. A ratio-growth bonus can discourage an added €40,000 of annual economic profit.

Part C 3 marks

How much of the stated sales converts into cash that year, assuming no opening receivables? Why does it matter for approval?

Collections are €420,000, leaving €280,000 receivable. Capital expenditure and operating payments must be financed during the collection delay, so review cash flows and lifetime NPV before approval.

Case 4 · 10 marks

A lecture venue with a capacity step

Part A 3 marks

Find break-even before expansion and profit at 100 bundles.

Each bundle contributes €75. Break-even is 80 bundles or 240 people. At 100 bundles contribution is €7,500 and profit €1,500.

Part B 4 marks

Find profit at 105 bundles and the first expanded volume that restores the €1,500 profit at 100 bundles.

105 bundles require another room. Profit is €7,875 − €8,250 = minus €375. For €1,500 profit, 130 bundles are needed because (€8,250 + €1,500)/€75 = 130.

Part C 3 marks

At 105 bundles, one advanced enrolment becomes a basic enrolment. Quantify the change and identify what an operating decision should verify.

Headcount and fixed costs stay the same. Contribution falls €15 and loss becomes €390. Verify the mix, binding room limit, ability to cap sales and contractual commitment before opening the extra room.

    A second plant has no opening work, starts 600 units and completes 480. Closing goods have all their material and are 25% converted. Material costs €7,200 and conversion costs €7,650. Find both assigned balances.

    A meter is expected to sell for €80 with a required 30% margin. Sales are 15,000, design costs €90,000 and the manufacturing, support and disposal costs are €42, €5 and €2 per unit. A new disposal rule in the scenario would add €3 per unit. Assess both designs without discounting.
