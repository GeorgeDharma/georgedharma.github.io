Business planning under uncertainty

Build upside and downside cases around operational drivers. Locate the demand, cost or exchange-rate threshold that changes the decision.

The starting point

A useful downside forecast changes things that a business can observe, such as customer orders, wage rates or exchange rates. Follow the effect through sales, costs, collections and funding. Cutting revenue by an arbitrary percentage can hide which assumption actually matters.

One way to challenge a decision is to solve for the threshold that changes it. Then compare that threshold with a credible range of outcomes. Combine related changes in a scenario when, for example, a loss of volume would also remove a supplier discount.

Theory and method

Expected value of sample information

Bayesian decision analysis · Decision-tree rollback

How much can a manufacturer pay for a demand survey if it will launch only after a favourable result?

A manufacturer can launch now, decline now, or buy information before choosing. A survey has value when its result can change the action in at least one state. Compare the best unconditional expected payoff with the probability-weighted payoff of choosing the best action after the survey. The improved choice is the source of the value. The price of the information, delay to market and costs incurred while waiting must then be weighed against that improvement.

How the result is built

EVSI = ΣₛP(s) maxₐ E[πₐ given s] − maxₐ E[πₐ]

s is a survey outcome, a a feasible action, P its probability, π project profit in euros, E a probability-weighted mean and max the best available action. EVSI is expected value of sample information before its cost.

  1. Launching pays €100k in strong demand and loses €60k in weak demand, each initially equally likely. Launch now has expected profit €20k versus zero for declining.
  2. The survey is favourable half the time. Strong demand has conditional probability 80% after favourable information and 20% after unfavourable information. Launch values become €68k and minus €28k.
  3. Launch only after favourable results. The expected informed payoff is 0.5 × €68k + 0.5 × zero = €34k. The survey is worth at most €14k before other waiting costs.

When the reasoning holds

  • The conditional probabilities must be calibrated and consistent with the prior. An optimistic survey supplied by the promoter may overstate value.
  • Management must be able to act differently after the result. A binding lease signed before the survey may remove the option to decline.
Terms in this lesson
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Covenant
A promise or condition in a financing agreement, such as maintaining an agreed level of cash.
First example

An expansion commits €60,000 in annual fixed costs and earns €15 per extra unit after variable costs. It needs 4,000 extra units annually. A forecast of 4,200 leaves a cushion of only 200 units even though the base case shows a profit.

Operational drivers and their connections

A scenario should describe a coherent economic story. Selling fewer units may trigger lower utilization and higher unit costs, but it can also release inventory and delay capacity spending. Forecasting every variable as an independent downward percentage can double-count hardships or miss cash relief.

Specify what is held constant in a sensitivity and what moves jointly in a scenario. A sensitivity isolates one mathematical relationship. A scenario tests decisions under a plausible set of related changes and may produce a very different threshold.

Switching values and the value of flexibility

A switching value is the input at which the preferred decision changes. Set the incremental outcome to zero and solve, then ask whether the resulting number lies inside the range in which your cost assumptions apply. Crossing a capacity threshold can introduce more than one relevant crossing.

Some commitments are reversible. A short trial or staged lease may preserve the ability to stop after poor demand becomes visible. Compare the cash flows after an actual exercise decision with those of a business forced to continue under every scenario.

Do probabilities answer the management question?

A probability-weighted forecast averages outcomes and can be informative for repeated decisions. It can conceal a cash shortfall in one outcome that makes continuation impossible. A positive expected profit and an unacceptable chance of violating a funding covenant can therefore coexist.

Identify evidence that would change the probabilities, such as binding customer orders. Set a decision date and action rule before the information arrives. Run a reverse stress by asking which combination would exhaust available cash and how much warning the business would have.

Method

Testing an uncertain launch

  1. Identify thresholds

    Separate committed payments, optional investments and per-unit cash effects. Determine where the choice to launch or expand becomes worthwhile.

  2. Link operations to cash

    Place receipts, inventory purchases and staffing on their contractual dates before comparing the worst cash balance with available funding.

  3. Define a response

    Tie monitoring to a specific intervention, such as delaying a shift if signed orders fall below the required volume.

Worked case

A seasonal distribution contract

A retailer can sign a one-season lease with €36,000 fixed cost and €12 contribution per order below 4,000 orders. Above 4,000 orders it must hire extra supervisors for €18,000, with the same unit contribution throughout.

Working

  1. At low capacity the switching point is 3,000 orders. At 3,800 orders profit is €45,600 − €36,000 = €9,600.
  2. At 4,100 orders the extra commitment applies, so profit is €49,200 − €54,000 = minus €4,800. The new break-even is 4,500 orders within that capacity range.
  3. More orders are not always better across a step. If partial acceptance is feasible, the retailer can compare capping volume at 4,000 with expanding once expected orders justify the commitment.

Interpretation

If a customer contract requires accepting every order, capping sales is infeasible. A useful model makes that contractual constraint visible.

A common mistake

Applying a single slope across a new shift gives a break-even number that may fall outside its own operating range.

How to correct it

Solve separately in each range and test quantities just below and above the step as well as each calculated crossing.

Quick check
A plan adds €48,000 in fixed costs. Unit contribution falls from €12 to €8 in the downside case. What annual volume pays the fixed bill in that case?

Use the downside contribution. €48,000 divided by €8 gives 6,000 units. The 4,000-unit threshold belongs to the original price and cost assumptions.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

An entry programme costs €30,000 and yields €10 per participant. It has a 60% chance of 4,500 and a 40% chance of 1,500 participants. A grant of €8,000 is paid only if at least 4,000 participate.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate both outcomes and expected profit.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    The organization can absorb at most a €10,000 loss. Does positive expected profit settle the decision? Give an operational response.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

High volume yields €45,000 − €30,000 + €8,000 = €23,000. Low volume yields minus €15,000. Expected profit is 0.60 × €23,000 + 0.40 × (−€15,000) = €7,800.

Part B 3 marks

No. The lower-demand outcome loses €15,000 with 40% probability. A commitment conditional on advance registrations, a lower fixed fee, or separate secured funding could bring the downside within capacity.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How much can a manufacturer pay for a demand survey if it will launch only after a favourable result?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would pay at most €14,000 before allowing for delay and cost of capital. Launching immediately is worth €20,000 on average. The survey lets us launch the favourable half and decline the other half, lifting the expected payoff to €34,000. Its value depends on actually retaining the right to decline and on the signal having the stated predictive power.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The survey costs €8,000 and waiting cuts the strong-demand payoff from €100,000 to €80,000. Is the survey still worth buying?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Recompute post-survey choices. Favourable launch value becomes 0.8 × €80k − 0.2 × €60k = €52k. The unfavourable launch remains negative. The delayed informed payoff is €26k before the survey fee and €18k after it, below the immediate €20k launch.
