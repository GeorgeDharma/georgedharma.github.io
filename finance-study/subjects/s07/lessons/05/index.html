Operating budgets

Translate activity into units, prices, staff and cash. Distinguish a target used for accountability from a forecast used to anticipate outcomes.

The starting point

A bicycle maker must schedule assembly before customers pay. Its operating budget starts with expected sales and converts them into a production schedule, material purchases and staffing. Inventory carried into the period already meets some of the demand.

The cash budget follows payment dates. A credit sale can count as revenue this month yet fund wages only next month. Keep a management target visible alongside the latest forecast so a change in expected demand reaches the operations team.

Theory and method

Cash collections schedule

Receivables roll-forward · Cash budget

How can the sales team hit its quarterly target while treasury has to borrow more?

A wholesaler books a March sale when it delivers, but the customer pays in instalments. Treasury needs the timing of several overlapping invoice cohorts. Cash in March can come from January, February and March sales, while some March invoices remain outstanding. Applying a collection pattern to each cohort makes the cash calendar explicit. A check against opening and closing receivables catches accidental double counting and shows whether a shortfall comes from slower payment or lower sales.

How the result is built

Rₜ = f₀Sₜ + f₁Sₜ₋₁ + f₂Sₜ₋₂; Aₜ = Aₜ₋₁ + Sₜ − Rₜ

S is credit sales in euros, R customer cash receipts, A closing trade receivables, t the month and f₀, f₁, f₂ the fractions collected in the sale month and the following two months.

  1. Suppose every invoice pays 30%, 50% and 20% at monthly intervals. January, February and March sales are €60k, €80k and €100k.
  2. March receives €12k from January, €40k from February and €30k from March. Cash receipts total €82k against €100k newly billed.
  3. February-end receivables are €12k January plus €56k February, or €68k. March-end receivables are €68k + €100k − €82k = €86k.

When the reasoning holds

  • Cohorts are fully collectible on the stated pattern. Returns or bad debts need their own reductions in the receivables bridge.
  • The monthly model assumes cash arrives in time for monthly uses. Large payroll or debt due early in the month requires a dated daily schedule.
Terms in this lesson
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Payable
Money a business owes a supplier for goods or services already received.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
First example

Suppose planned sales are 800 bikes. With 100 bikes available now and a desired closing inventory of 140, production must be 800 + 140 − 100 = 840 bikes. Supplier payment terms determine when that production uses cash.

How the schedules fit together

A master budget starts with expected customer demand, then derives production, resource needs and financing. Opening stock and desired ending stock connect adjacent periods. Purchases made just before year-end can fund next year's production, so the final period may require assumptions beyond the forecast horizon.

The production equation is sales plus desired closing inventory minus opening inventory. The same structure applies one stage upstream to raw materials, using material consumption in place of sales. Keep units of finished goods separate from kilograms, litres or staff-hours.

Cash is a calendar question

Sales, production cost and cash collection follow different recognition dates. Credit terms can leave a growing company short of cash even as it reports profit. A cash budget starts with opening cash, adds actual collection schedules and financing, then deducts payments when due.

Include opening receivables and payables, payroll and asset purchases. A budgeted depreciation charge reduces accounting profit but does not itself use cash this month. Trace the underlying machinery purchase to the period when suppliers receive payment.

Why the target and forecast can differ

A target sets a performance ambition while a forecast is management's best current estimate. If missing a target is punished by changing the information reported, managers may build in slack or withhold bad news. A practical budget can show both numbers side by side.

Rolling forecasts add a new future period when the latest actuals arrive. They help operations reorder stock or renegotiate funding early. Keep the original approved budget so reviewers can understand what management believed when it authorized spending.

Method

From demand to a cash request

  1. Schedule units

    Balance opening inventory, required production, sales and closing inventory for each period.

  2. Time receipts

    Apply each customer collection fraction to its own sales period, including outstanding opening invoices.

  3. Calculate the funding gap

    Compare cash after all dated payments with the minimum operating balance and identify when financing must be available.

Worked case

A furniture maker's busy month

A shop expects to sell 200 desks at €300 each in April, begins with 30 desks and targets 50 at month-end. It collects half of current sales immediately and has €12,000 of prior customer invoices due in April.

Working

  1. April production is 200 + 50 − 30 = 220 desks. Reported April sales are €60,000. Collection terms and opening invoices give April receipts of €30,000 + €12,000 = €42,000.
  2. With €8,000 opening cash and €55,000 of planned cash payments, ending cash before financing would be minus €5,000. A €6,000 operating minimum requires €11,000 of financing by the relevant payment dates.
  3. An earlier collection from a customer can reduce the need for credit without changing April sales. Giving up an early-payment discount to encourage that collection has a real cost that the budget should show.

Interpretation

Monthly totals can hide an intra-month shortfall if payroll is due before collections. Treasury may need a weekly or daily calendar around the tightest date.

A common mistake

Treating all production as a cash sale confuses factory activity, customer billing and collection.

How to correct it

Use separate inventory and receivables schedules and reconcile their closing balances to the budgeted balance sheet.

Quick check
A shop begins with 90 units, expects to sell 300 and wants to finish with 70. How many units must arrive during the period?

The shop needs 370 units available over the period. The opening 90 reduce the required receipts to 280. Adding those receipts to 90 and subtracting 300 leaves 70.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

May sales are 500 units at €40. Opening finished stock is 80, desired closing stock 100. Sixty percent of May sales is collected in May, plus €3,000 of opening receivables.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find production, recorded sales and cash receipts.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Opening cash is €2,000 and payments are €19,500. How much new funding preserves a €1,500 minimum?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Production is 520 units. Sales equal €20,000. Cash receipts are €12,000 from May customers plus €3,000 from earlier customers, or €15,000.

Part B 3 marks

Before funding, cash is €2,000 + €15,000 − €19,500 = minus €2,500. Raising €4,000 brings it to €1,500.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can the sales team hit its quarterly target while treasury has to borrow more?

Compare with a spoken answer

I would reconcile the cash shortfall before concluding sales missed. Here March sales are €100,000 and receipts €82,000 because three invoice cohorts collect at different rates. Receivables increase €18,000, tying the two views. I would check due-date performance cohort by cohort, then use actual settlement dates to decide whether to draw a facility before payroll.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Half the expected February receipt slips from March into April. What changes at March close?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Half of the €40,000 February instalment is €20,000. March receipts fall from €82,000 to €62,000, and closing receivables rise from €86,000 to €106,000. March revenue stays €100,000 if the delivery and recognition facts stay the same.
