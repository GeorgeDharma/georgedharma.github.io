Flexible budgets

Recalculate the budget at actual activity before analysing performance. This separates an output change from changes in unit cost or selling price.

The starting point

A restaurant serving more dinners than expected will usually spend more on ingredients. Comparing its total bill with the original budget mixes extra meals with changes in prices, portions or waste. A flexible budget asks what spending should have been at the actual activity level.

Keep the planned fixed cost and the planned variable cost per unit, and substitute the actual number of meals. The difference between this revised budget and actual spending isolates a unit-cost or efficiency issue for investigation.

Theory and method

Flexible-budget performance report

Activity variance · Spending variance

Why would you flex a support centre's cost budget to standard minutes instead of the actual staff hours worked?

A helpdesk can spend more because more customers called, because the calls were harder, or because staff took too long. A flexible budget should allow the resources normally required for the work delivered. Basing the allowance on actual staff time gives inefficient processing an automatic increase in budget. Weighting delivered call types by their pre-agreed standard minutes holds the treatment of the work constant and leaves time overruns visible for investigation.

How the result is built

B* = F + kΣⱼqⱼtⱼ; spending variance = A − B*

B* is flexible cost in euros, F fixed cost, k standard euros per working minute, qⱼ actual count of call type j, tⱼ its standard minutes, A actual euro cost, and Σⱼ adds across call types.

  1. The standard permits two minutes per simple call and six per complex call. One thousand simple plus 200 complex calls allow 3,200 minutes.
  2. At €0.50 per standard minute and €4,000 fixed, the allowance is €5,600. Actual spend of €5,900 exceeds it by €300.
  3. If staff used 3,600 minutes, flexing to those hours would allow €5,800 and conceal €200 of the excess. Separate the time gap and the remaining spending difference.

When the reasoning holds

  • Work classification and time standards must be set independently of the reporting manager. Recoding calls can manufacture a favourable result.
  • Fixed capacity must cover the observed call mix. A predetermined staffing step should be included when the threshold is genuinely crossed.
Terms in this lesson
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.

All notation examples

First example

A kitchen planned €4,000 fixed costs and €6 per dinner for 1,000 dinners. At 1,200 dinners its flexible budget is €11,200. Actual spending of €11,500 is €300 above the flexible budget, even though it is €1,500 above the original plan.

Separating volume from operating performance

A static budget answers what managers expected at the planned volume. A flexible budget keeps the original standard prices and resource requirements but substitutes the actual output. The comparison with the static budget quantifies how much spending or profit should change purely because output differs.

Comparing actual spending with the flexible budget then asks whether operations performed as anticipated for the output actually delivered. A flexible budget cannot by itself assign fault. A genuine change in input prices and inefficient consumption can both produce the same spending excess.

The correct activity to flex

For direct materials, the relevant activity is often finished output adjusted for normal yield. For a call centre, different call types can consume different standard minutes. If the mix changes materially, flexing only to total call count gives a distorted performance measure.

Fixed costs stay fixed across the relevant range, even though their cost per unit changes. When volume crosses a shift boundary, use the capacity step specified in the original cost plan. A spending variance should not punish an unavoidable capacity addition already built into the standard.

When a favourable number hides a problem

Spending below the flexed allowance can result from better purchasing, but it may also reflect skipped maintenance or lower product quality. Compare physical outputs, customer outcomes and subsequent rework before calling a favourable expense variance an economic gain.

For profit, use a favourable sign when actual profit exceeds the comparison figure. For expenses measured as positive amounts, an excess is unfavourable. State the convention explicitly when reconciling expense and profit movements in the same report.

Method

Building a two-stage bridge

  1. Calculate planned cost

    Apply the original fixed and variable assumptions to the planned activity. Record the result as the reference point.

  2. Flex only activity

    Price the actual quantity of output and its relevant mix at the original resource standards, preserving applicable capacity steps.

  3. Compare execution

    Reconcile the two differences to the total actual-versus-plan gap. Investigate prices, waste and quality before attributing responsibility.

Worked case

A repair centre takes harder jobs

A centre planned 80 simple and 20 complex repairs. Parts standards are €10 and €40 respectively, with €2,000 fixed costs. Actual work comprises 60 simple and 40 complex jobs and costs €4,420 in total.

Working

  1. Planned cost is €2,000 + €800 + €800 = €3,600. Both periods have 100 jobs, but the simple-count comparison would overlook their different needs.
  2. The flexible cost is €2,000 + €600 + €1,600 = €4,200. A switch toward complex repairs explains €600 of additional cost. Actual cost exceeds the flexible allowance by €220.
  3. The full €820 excess reconciles exactly to €600 of mix and €220 remaining. The remaining excess could reflect parts prices, work quality or inefficient use, requiring more data.

Interpretation

The centre should also compare revenue by repair type before concluding that the more expensive mix reduced overall profit.

A common mistake

Multiplying fixed cost per planned unit by actual output silently converts fixed cost into variable cost.

How to correct it

Flex physical inputs and genuine variable costs, keeping fixed commitments at the level required by the relevant capacity range.

Quick check
A depot budgets €2,000 fixed plus €4 per parcel. It handles 600 parcels and spends €4,250. How does it perform against the flexible budget?

At actual activity the allowance is €2,000 + 600 × €4 = €4,400. Spending is €150 lower. The comparison holds the number of parcels constant.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A small manufacturer planned 1,000 units, €5 materials per unit and €8,000 fixed cost. It produced 1,200 units and spent €14,400. Capacity did not change.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Build the static, flexible and actual cost bridge.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    A buyer says higher supplier prices caused the €400. What records would distinguish that explanation from excess usage?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Static cost is €13,000. Flexible cost is €14,000. Volume explains €1,000 and spending another €400, summing to a €1,400 excess.

Part B 3 marks

Compare actual price per input and quantities purchased or consumed against standard price and standard input allowed for 1,200 units. An invoice total without quantities cannot separate them.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why would you flex a support centre's cost budget to standard minutes instead of the actual staff hours worked?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would flex using the standard time for the jobs completed. Those jobs justify 3,200 minutes and €5,600 total cost. Actual spend is €300 above that allowance. If I flexed to the 3,600 minutes used, €200 would disappear from the gap before anyone asked why the calls took longer. I would check call quality and whether the standard still describes real cases.

Interviewer’s follow-up

An independently reviewed rule makes complex calls eight minutes each for this period. How does the conclusion change?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Two extra minutes on 200 complex calls add 400 allowed minutes. The flexible allowance rises €200 to €5,800 and the unaccounted excess falls to €100. I would document why the revision applies to this period and preserve the original budget for comparison.
