Variance analysis

Reconcile budget and actual performance through price, volume, mix and currency. Specify how interaction effects are allocated so the bridge adds up.

The starting point

A sales manager explaining a revenue miss needs to separate changes in prices from units sold. A variance bridge changes one assumption at a time. The sum of the changes should take the reader from the budget all the way to actual revenue.

State the order of the calculation. If volume changes first at the planned price, then the effect of the price change is measured on actual units. Applying both changes to the original figures misses the interaction. The same logic extends to product mix and currency conversion.

Theory and method

Sales mix and sales quantity variances

Contribution-margin bridge · Standard sales mix

Sales volume grew and prices stayed fixed. Can an unfavourable product mix erase the gain?

A music venue sells more tickets than planned, but many customers switch from full-price to lower-contribution tickets. Split total volume change from the pattern of tickets sold. First imagine selling the actual total in the original proportions. Then replace that hypothetical mix with the actual mix, keeping standard contribution per ticket fixed throughout. This sequence attributes the lost value of the substitution while ensuring that all reported contributions add back to the actual volume-driven result.

How the result is built

Quantity effect = (Q₁ − Q₀) Σs₀ⱼcⱼ; mix effect = Σ(q₁ⱼ − Q₁s₀ⱼ)cⱼ

Q₀ and Q₁ are planned and actual total tickets, q₁ⱼ actual tickets of type j, s₀ⱼ planned fraction of type j, cⱼ standard euros contributed per ticket, and Σ adds ticket types.

  1. Plan 100 tickets, half premium contributing €30 and half basic contributing €10. Planned contribution is €2,000 and average planned contribution €20.
  2. At 120 actual tickets the old mix would produce 60 premium and 60 basic. The total-volume gain is 20 × €20 = €400.
  3. Actual mix is 40 premium and 80 basic. Relative to the intermediate mix, losing 20 premium and gaining 20 basic costs €400, cancelling the quantity gain.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use standard contribution per item to isolate a volume-and-mix bridge. Actual price or variable-cost differences belong in separate effects.
  • The original mix must be a meaningful planning comparison. A discontinued product may require an explicitly restated baseline.
Terms in this lesson
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Calibration
Choosing model parameters so that the model reproduces specified market observations as closely as possible.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.

All notation examples

First example

A budget of 100 units at €20 gives €2,000. Selling 120 units adds €400 at the old price. Charging €18 on 120 units takes away €240. Actual revenue is €2,160, a net increase of €160.

From a variance to a causal question

Price and quantity variances are a decomposition of an arithmetic difference. They do not establish why prices or quantities changed. The procurement team may have accepted costlier materials that cut waste, while cheap materials can require more labour or create later warranty claims.

For input spending, a convenient convention measures the price difference on actual quantity consumed and the usage difference at the standard price. With purchases and usage in different periods, a purchase-price variance measured on purchased quantity must be reconciled through inventory.

Material price and usage

Standard quantity allowed means the material that actual output should have consumed at normal yield. This is different from the standard input for planned output. Comparing actual consumption to the latter would wrongly assign output growth to factory inefficiency.

With expense increases treated as unfavourable, price variance is actual quantity times actual minus standard price. Usage variance is standard price times actual minus allowed quantity. Their sum takes standard allowed cost exactly to actual cost.

Volume, mix and currency

An exporter's revenue can change because of quantities, local-currency prices, product mix and exchange rates. Build a bridge by replacing one input at a time and stating the order. Interactions are assigned to whichever step changes later.

A different order changes the named effects without changing the total difference. For accountability, constant-currency sales at a common rate can be useful alongside actual consolidated revenue. Cash gains on hedges should be shown in the appropriate location so they are not inadvertently counted twice.

Method

Reconciling a production variance

  1. Fix output

    Calculate standard physical inputs allowed for the actual good output, including any normal loss explicitly built into the standard.

  2. Change inputs one at a time

    First substitute actual usage at the standard price, then actual price on that same usage.

  3. Verify and investigate

    Add the movements and compare the result with invoice and inventory data. Pair financial variances with physical quality or yield evidence.

Worked case

A pigment supplier changes grade

A paint maker's standard is 2 kg of pigment per drum at €5 per kg. It produced 500 drums, used 1,120 kg and paid €4.70 per kg on the amount consumed.

Working

  1. Standard allowed cost is 1,000 kg × €5 = €5,000. At the standard price, excess usage of 120 kg adds €600 unfavourable.
  2. A lower price saves 1,120 × €0.30 = €336 favourable. Net excess spending is €264, reconciling €5,000 to actual €5,264.
  3. One hypothesis is that lower-strength pigment required more input. Another is a calibration failure unrelated to procurement. Compare production batches, grade specifications and equipment records to distinguish the explanations.

Interpretation

Rewarding the buyer for €336 alone could encourage a contract change that made the company worse off.

At the board

Reconcile a change before explaining it

A unit sells 100 items at €10 in the budget and 120 at €9 in the actual period. Calculate the price and volume explanations relative to an explicit baseline. A manager can be rewarded differently depending on where the interaction is assigned.

TermCalculationRevenue effect
Volume at old price(120 − 100) × €10+€200
Price on old volume(€9 − €10) × 100−€100
Interaction(120 − 100) × (€9 − €10)−€20

The effects add to +€80, the difference between actual revenue of €1,080 and budgeted revenue of €1,000. A two-term bridge instead prices the 120 actual units at the €1 shortfall, giving a −€120 price effect and +€200 volume. Both reconcile. As in portfolio performance attribution, identify which convention allocates the interaction before interpreting who contributed what.

A common mistake

Calling an unfavourable usage variance proof that workers wasted material treats an accounting description as a causal test.

How to correct it

Inspect input quality, production mix, equipment settings and whether the standard still describes feasible work.

Quick check
Plan was 200 items at €10. Actual sales were 180 at €11. After measuring volume at the old price, what price effect completes the bridge?

Apply the €1 increase to all 180 actual units. Volume contributed minus €200, and price added €180. The two changes reconcile to the €20 revenue decline.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

An exporter budgeted 100 units at $10 each and €0.90 per dollar. It sold 120 units at $9 each and translated sales at €0.95 per dollar. Change volume first, then price, then currency.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Show all three effects and reconcile euro sales.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Which revenue should evaluate local pricing and volumes at a common exchange rate? Explain the comparison.

Part A 4 marks

Budget is €900. Volume adds €180, price removes €108 and currency adds $1,080 × €0.05 = €54. Actual sales are €1,026, up €126.

Part B 3 marks

Actual dollar sales at the budget rate equal €972. Comparing €972 with €900 isolates their combined €72 contribution before the €54 translation effect.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Sales volume grew and prices stayed fixed. Can an unfavourable product mix erase the gain?

Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. In this case total contribution remains €2,000 despite sales volume rising from 100 to 120. At the original mix the extra tickets would have added €400, but the actual mix displaced 20 premium sales relative to that intermediate benchmark. Each switch costs €20 of contribution. I would investigate availability, discounts and channel promotions before attributing the change to the sales team.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Premium tickets also earned €2 less contribution each than their standard. Where does it enter the bridge?

Compare with the follow-up answer

On 40 actual premium tickets that is a further €80 unfavourable unit-margin effect. The quantity and mix amounts stay at plus and minus €400 under the stated convention. Actual contribution becomes €1,920.
