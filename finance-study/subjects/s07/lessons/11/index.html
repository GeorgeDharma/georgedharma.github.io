Absorption costing, inventory and reported profit

Reconcile absorption and variable-cost profit to inventory movement.

Theory and terminology

Absorption versus variable costing

fixed overhead · inventory profit reconciliation

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile absorption and variable-cost profit to inventory movement.
  • Calculate and expense underabsorbed fixed manufacturing overhead under a stated policy.

Two ways to treat the same factory rent

Under variable or marginal costing, each unit carries variable production cost. Fixed production overhead, such as a factory lease, enters this period’s income statement in full. Under absorption costing, completed units also carry a production share of fixed factory overhead. The expense follows those units into cost of sales when they are sold.

A sales commission is a selling cost, even if it varies with volume. A fixed head-office sales expense stays separate from production overhead under both versions here. Classification depends on the activity that causes the cost as well as whether it varies.

Set the production rate before comparing departments

Dividing budgeted fixed factory costs by a normal production level gives a standard amount to attach to each unit. A low-output month then leaves overhead underabsorbed. With actual fixed spending equal to budget, it is the portion of expected capacity that was unused.

The accounts in this exercise expense the underabsorbed balance immediately. Using actual output to divide every euro of rent among fewer units would give a different inventory number. State the allocation and variance treatment before comparing two reported profits.

What reverses on sale

When stock grows, some current fixed overhead remains in closing stock under absorption costing. When older stock sells, previously deferred amounts enter cost of sales. A controller can identify this timing effect even if all cash receipts and wages are identical under both reports.

Overproduction can support a short-term profit target while tying up working capital. Track units made, units sold, storage expense and obsolete inventory alongside the profit reconciliation. A production bonus based on absorption profit alone creates an avoidable incentive.

Method

Build the result

Absorption profit − variable-cost profit = fixed production overhead in closing stock − fixed production overhead in opening stock

Profit and inventory overhead are in the same currency and period. The identity assumes identical sales, variable costs and other expenses, and consistent treatment of production overhead variances.

  1. Calculate the fixed rate per unit as budgeted fixed production overhead divided by normal production.
  2. Apply the rate to output. Reconcile applied overhead to actual fixed spending and expense the difference under the stated policy.
  3. Identify fixed overhead in opening and closing stock. Its net increase is the amount deferred from this period’s absorption expense.
Worked case

A water-filter plant adds 2,000 finished filters to inventory

The plant starts empty, makes 10,000 filters and sells 8,000 at €46. Variable factory cost is €18 per filter. Normal output is 10,000 and budgeted and actual fixed factory costs are both €120,000. Ignore other expenses and taxes.

Working

  1. Calculate the fixed overhead rate as €120,000/10,000 = €12 per filter. Absorption unit cost is €18 + €12 = €30.
  2. Under variable costing, sales of €368,000 less €144,000 variable cost and €120,000 fixed overhead gives €104,000 profit.
  3. Under absorption costing, cost of sales is 8,000 × €30 = €240,000. All overhead was applied, leaving profit of €128,000.
  4. Closing inventory is €60,000 under absorption and €36,000 under variable costing. Its €24,000 difference equals the €24,000 profit difference. Sales and fixed spending were identical.

Decision and interpretation

Explain the €24,000 as fixed costs deferred on 2,000 units. Realizing the value attributed to those units still depends on their condition and eventual sale.

Quick check
A factory’s stock falls from 800 to 500 units, each carrying €8 of fixed production overhead. Under the stated equal-rate convention, how does absorption profit compare with variable-cost profit?

Selling 300 units of inventory releases 300 × €8 = €2,400 of previously deferred overhead into absorption cost of sales. Variable costing already expensed that amount.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A separate plant opens with 1,000 units, makes 4,000 and sells 4,500 at €32. Variable factory cost stays €14 per unit and fixed overhead stays €9 per unit of normal output of 5,000. Actual fixed overhead is €45,000. Opening inventory uses those same rates. Expense underabsorbed overhead. Reconcile both profits.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Variable-cost profit is 4,500 × (€32 − €14) − €45,000 = €36,000.
  2. Applied overhead is 4,000 × €9 = €36,000. Expense €9,000 underabsorption. Absorption profit is 4,500 × (€32 − €23) − €9,000 = €31,500.
  3. Units fall from 1,000 to 500, releasing €4,500 of fixed overhead from inventory. That exactly explains €31,500 − €36,000.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a plant earn more reported profit by producing units it has not sold?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Producing beyond sales places a share of fixed factory cost in closing inventory under absorption costing. In the filter case, 2,000 unsold units each carry €12, so reported profit exceeds variable-cost profit by €24,000. When those units sell, the deferred overhead enters cost of sales. I would reconcile both inventories and check whether the extra production served demand.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If old inventory carries €7 of fixed overhead per unit and 500 older and 4,000 current units are sold, can you multiply the 500-unit rundown by €9?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. Opening stock holds €7,000 and the 500 old units still on hand hold €3,500. The decrease is €3,500. The bridge uses the actual overhead in each inventory layer when rates differ.
