Process costing and equivalent units of production

Prepare a weighted-average process cost report from physical flows and completion estimates.

In this lecture

  1. Count physical output before attaching money
  2. Build one rate for each input
  3. Verify the estimate
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. Physical litres, equivalent work and assigned cost
  6. A finish-coating department closes its month
  7. Independent exam problem
  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Equivalent units in process costing

work in progress · conversion costs · weighted average

What you should be able to explain

  • Prepare a weighted-average process cost report from physical flows and completion estimates.
  • Reconcile both cost pools to completed units and closing work in progress.

Count physical output before attaching money

A plant making indistinguishable litres of coating can pool costs for the mixing department. Units in opening work in progress plus units started must equal units transferred out plus closing work in progress, subject to measured waste. This balance is a count of physical litres.

An equivalent unit represents the work required for one fully completed litre, separately for each type of input. Eighty half-converted litres embody forty complete litres of conversion work. If all the pigment has been added, they already represent eighty material-equivalent litres.

Build one rate for each input

Under weighted-average process costing, pool opening work-in-progress costs and costs added this period. Divide each pool by completed units plus the closing equivalent units for that input. A transferred unit has completed the whole department, so it receives a full share of both rates.

First-in, first-out process costing answers a different current-period efficiency question. It identifies work needed to finish opening units and divides current-period costs by current-period work. Its result can differ when input prices or the opening degree of completion have changed.

Verify the estimate

Estimating 60% instead of 40% completion gives closing work a greater share of conversion spending and reduces the amount charged to transferred goods. Obtain evidence from production stages or measured machine time and check that the estimate relates to the particular department.

A single overall percentage is often inappropriate. Materials can enter at the start, inspection can occur at the end, and conversion may proceed continuously. If spoilage occurs, establish when it was discovered and who bears it before applying the no-loss convention used here.

Method

Build the result

Equivalent units = transferred units + closing units × completion. Cost per equivalent unit = (opening cost + current cost)/equivalent units.

For each separate pool, completion is a fraction from zero to one. Physical units are litres in the example. Costs are euros and the rate is euros per equivalent litre.

  1. Reconcile opening, started, completed and ending physical quantities so that missing stock cannot be disguised in a unit cost.
  2. For each pool, calculate equivalent units and divide total cost to account for by that quantity.
  3. Multiply the rates by the appropriate equivalent units for each destination. Their combined values must equal all available costs.
Worked case

A finish-coating department closes its month

The department opens with 200 litres and starts 800. It transfers 700 finished litres. The remaining stock has all its material and is 40% converted. Opening material and conversion costs are €3,000 and €2,000. The month adds €12,000 material and €14,400 conversion. No units are lost.

Working

  1. One thousand physical litres are available. Transferring 700 leaves 300 litres to count on the floor.
  2. Materials have 700 + 300 = 1,000 equivalent litres. Conversion has 700 + 0.40 × 300 = 820 equivalent litres.
  3. Material costs (€3,000 + €12,000)/1,000 = €15 per equivalent litre. Conversion costs (€2,000 + €14,400)/820 = €20.
  4. Transferred goods cost 700 × (€15 + €20) = €24,500. Ending work costs 300 × €15 + 120 × €20 = €6,900. Together €31,400 matches the total available costs.

Decision and interpretation

Book €6,900 as department work in progress and transfer €24,500 onward. A physical count confirms the 300 litres, while process evidence must substantiate their 40% conversion.

Quick check
A process transfers 900 units and retains 200 with all materials and 30% conversion. How many equivalent units should be used to divide conversion cost under weighted-average process costing?

All 900 transferred units completed conversion. The 200 unfinished units add 200 × 0.30 = 60 equivalent units, giving 960.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A second plant has no opening work, starts 600 units and completes 480. Closing goods have all their material and are 25% converted. Material costs €7,200 and conversion costs €7,650. Find both assigned balances.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Closing work is 120 physical units. Material equivalent units are 600. Conversion equivalent units are 480 + 120 × 0.25 = 510.
  2. Rates are €7,200/600 = €12 for materials and €7,650/510 = €15 for conversion.
  3. Transferred cost is 480 × €27 = €12,960. Closing cost is 120 × €12 + 30 × €15 = €1,890. The two sum to €14,850.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

What share of a mixing department’s costs belongs to finished batches when material enters immediately and conversion work occurs gradually?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would count material and conversion separately. In the coating case, 300 closing litres contain 300 material-equivalent litres and 120 conversion-equivalent litres. At €15 of material and €20 of conversion per equivalent litre, unfinished output carries €6,900. The completed 700 litres receive both rates in full. Together the two destinations account for €31,400.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can the department use weighted-average rates to prove its labour productivity improved this month?

Compare with the follow-up answer

It should isolate hours, output mix and work completed during this month. Weighted-average rates mingle opening costs and current work. A FIFO process report or direct standard-hours comparison can separate current-period performance, provided the opening completion estimates are credible.
