Incentives and responsibility

A manager evaluated on an uncontrollable exchange-rate movement may make poor decisions. Consider controllability, short horizons and incentives to game measures.

The starting point

Giving a manager a single annual target changes behaviour. A division paid solely for this year's profit can raise its bonus by postponing maintenance even when the delay leaves expensive failures for next year. The principal–agent problem arises when the decision maker and the owner benefit from different outcomes.

A responsibility centre should be judged on decisions it can influence. Pair a controllable financial measure with quality, service or longer-term measures that reveal side effects. Keep a separate record of economic outcomes caused by decisions taken elsewhere.

Theory and method

Controllability principle in responsibility accounting

Transfer pricing · Goal congruence

How could a divisional return target make the group buy a part externally even when its own factory could make it more cheaply?

Two division heads are judged on their separate profit statements. An internal price becomes revenue for the supplier and a cost for the buyer even though the group as a whole receives no cash from the transfer. A policy price can encourage a division to refuse a transaction that creates group profit. A negotiated interval compares the supplier's extra cost plus sacrificed external contribution with the buyer's genuinely obtainable outside price. Its existence depends on the alternative available to each manager.

How the result is built

v + o ≤ p ≤ b; group gain per item = b − v − o

v means supplier's incremental euro cash cost, o euro opportunity cost of displaced work, p internal price and b comparable outside delivered price in euros per item.

  1. An internal part costs €14 cash and displaces work contributing €4. The supplying division needs €18 to make its economic result whole.
  2. The buyer can obtain an equivalent part for €25 delivered. Paying more than €25 would worsen the buyer's controllable result.
  3. Any internal price from €18 to €25 shares the €7 group advantage. At a policy price of €27, the buyer can improve its own score by giving up that group gain.

When the reasoning holds

  • The external offer must cover comparable quality, delivery and reliability. An unreliable quote overstates the outside alternative.
  • The supplier must control or have reliable evidence on the displaced work. Allocated fixed overhead cannot substitute for an actual sacrifice.
Terms in this lesson
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
First example

A French division invoices in dollars, but headquarters decides all currency hedges. Its euro profit falls €40,000 after the dollar weakens. Report the translation effect to the group and use a constant exchange rate when examining the local manager's selling performance.

Who holds the decision rights?

A cost-centre manager controls inputs, a profit-centre manager usually controls selected revenues and expenses, and an investment-centre manager also controls assets committed. Classifying a unit by its name alone can assign accountability for prices or assets decided centrally.

A good measure should reflect decisions the manager can influence without hiding losses borne by the organization. Report both the full economic result and an evaluation adjusted for uncontrollable items. The adjustment needs a rule agreed before results arrive.

When a measure becomes a target

A principal delegates work to an agent whose effort is hard to observe. Basing pay on an observable result can encourage effort, but it can also shift attention toward the easiest measured task. A sales-volume bonus can encourage deep discounts, long payment terms or risky customer selection.

A balanced set of measures should address specific trade-offs rather than simply collecting a longer list. Measure contribution after discounts, track cash collection and monitor subsequent returns. A delayed review or clawback can align rewards with outcomes that arrive later.

Transfers between divisions

An internal transfer price changes reported divisional margins while cancelling out for the group. A selling division with spare capacity gives up little by transferring internally. A fully occupied division may have to forgo profitable external orders.

A negotiated range exists when the supplying unit's incremental cost plus its opportunity cost is no higher than the buying unit's outside alternative. If accounting rules prevent a beneficial deal, review the incentive contract as well as the transfer formula.

Method

Evaluating a performance measure

  1. Trace authority

    Identify who controlled prices, volumes, staffing, credit terms and exchange hedges during the period actually assessed.

  2. Predict gaming

    State one decision that could improve the score while reducing group value, then identify evidence of that behaviour.

  3. Align information and reward

    Keep controllable items visible, monitor downstream effects and reconcile adjusted measures back to the full group result.

Worked case

Internal components or an external supply?

Division A can supply B with a component for €18 incremental cash. B can buy it outside for €27. Group policy charges €30 on internal transfers. Assume the outside component is identical and A has spare capacity.

Working

  1. The external purchase costs the group €27 while internal supply costs €18. The group would save €9 per unit internally even though B's score makes external purchasing look €3 cheaper.
  2. A transfer price between €18 and €27 inclusive can support a mutually acceptable internal exchange under these assumptions. The exact transfer redistributes reported profit between units.
  3. If A is full and would sacrifice €12 contribution per component transferred, its economic minimum rises to €30. An outside purchase at €27 now saves the group €3.

Interpretation

A stable transfer rule should explicitly describe what happens when the supplying division's capacity becomes scarce.

A common mistake

Giving corporate profit and the manager's controllable result the same meaning can encourage managers to avoid worthwhile risk or shift losses elsewhere.

How to correct it

Keep a reconciliation and test what decision a rational manager would choose under the proposed score.

Quick check
A service manager can choose staffing but cannot set rent. Which measure best rewards efficient staffing while protecting service?

The combined measures reflect choices staff can influence and reveal whether apparent savings shift costs to customers or create extra work. Report rent separately in the division's total result.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A sales team can increase billed revenue by €100,000 through unusually long credit terms. Expected extra bad debt is €35,000 and extra variable production costs are €75,000.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate the expected incremental contribution and identify the incentive conflict.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Design two measures and explain the time at which each should be checked.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Expected contribution is €100,000 − €35,000 − €75,000 = minus €10,000. A gross-billings bonus can reward the team despite the expected loss.

Part B 3 marks

Measure contribution after expected credit losses when orders are approved. Later compare actual collections and returns with the assumptions, retaining an adjustment to the reward if results materially diverge.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How could a divisional return target make the group buy a part externally even when its own factory could make it more cheaply?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would separate the group decision from the allocation of divisional credit. Internal supply costs €18 including displaced contribution and saves a €25 outside purchase. A €27 transfer price can cause the buyer to reject a €7 group gain. An agreed price within €18 to €25 or an explicit performance adjustment would let the managers act in the group's interest.

Interviewer’s follow-up

An outside customer offers the supplying unit work contributing €13 rather than €4 per part displaced. Should headquarters still insist on internal sourcing?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Now the supplier's economic minimum is €14 + €13 = €27, above the buyer's €25 outside offer. The group saves €2 by sourcing externally and using its capacity for the new customer, if the displaced work is genuinely available.
