Capital allocation

Compare incremental cash, return on invested capital and economic profit. Positive accounting earnings can coexist with a return below the capital charge.

The starting point

An extra warehouse can produce a positive profit and still use capital that would earn more elsewhere. A capital charge expresses the required return on the funds committed. Compare after-tax operating profit with that charge over the same period and on a consistent capital base.

Economic profit equals after-tax operating profit less invested capital times the required return. For a new investment decision, also discount its future incremental cash flows. A first-year comparison can be misleading for a project that takes time to reach normal output.

Theory and method

Residual income valuation identity

Economic profit valuation · Clean-surplus relation

Can you reconcile a positive two-year project NPV to its annual economic profits when accounting depreciation is not the cash outlay?

A firm pays for equipment at installation but recognizes its cost over the equipment's life. Discounted cash flow records the purchase up front. Residual income instead deducts depreciation and a charge on the remaining opening investment each year. The two approaches agree when the capital roll-forward links earnings, cash and closing capital, the required return is the same, and terminal capital is handled explicitly. This provides an independent valuation check for a controller reviewing divisional returns.

How the result is built

RIₜ = Eₜ − rBₜ₋₁; FCFₜ = Eₜ + Bₜ₋₁ − Bₜ; NPV = Σ RIₜ/(1+r)ᵗ when Bₙ = 0

RI is annual residual income, E operating earnings after tax, r annual cost of capital, B opening or closing operating capital as dated, FCF free cash flow, n final year, t year index and Σ sums years. All money amounts use euros.

  1. A €100 investment depreciates €50 each year to zero and delivers €65 annual cash for two years. Earnings are €15 yearly before charging for invested capital.
  2. At r = 10%, residual incomes are €15 − €10 = €5 and €15 − €5 = €10. Their present values sum to €5/1.1 + €10/1.1² = €12.81.
  3. The cash-flow check gives −€100 + €65/1.1 + €65/1.1² = €12.81. Discounted capital recoveries cancel the opening investment when the balance finishes at zero.

When the reasoning holds

  • Operating earnings, capital and cash must be defined consistently and exclude financing costs already reflected in the capital charge.
  • Residual terminal capital requires an explicit terminal valuation or recovery. Assuming zero while productive assets remain understates value.
Terms in this lesson
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Discounted cash flow (DCF)
A valuation method that brings a series of future cash flows back to a common date.
Working capital
Money tied up in day-to-day operations. For a deal, agree which receivables, stock and payables count.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
First example

With €2m invested, €140,000 annual operating profit after tax gives a return of 7%. At a required return of 9%, the annual capital charge is €180,000 and economic profit is minus €40,000.

Who provided the capital?

Return on invested capital compares after-tax operating profit with the operating capital tied up in the business. It is useful when financing structures differ because the numerator is measured before interest. Compare it with a required return reflecting the risk and financing of those same operating assets.

Economic profit is invested capital times the gap between the operating return and its required return. A positive result means that period's operating profit exceeded the capital charge. Specify average or beginning capital and use the convention consistently.

How ratio targets can reject good work

A division returning 20% can decline an additional project earning 15% because it dilutes its average. If the project has comparable risk and the required return is 10%, accepting it can add economic profit despite lowering the headline percentage.

Residual income rewards the gain above the capital charge in money terms. It can align marginal investment decisions better than a minimum divisional ratio. It is still sensitive to asset age, depreciation policy, and when the project begins generating cash.

Project valuation over time

Discounted cash flow compares the initial investment with future incremental after-tax cash adjusted for reinvestment and changes in working capital. Use cash flows and a discount rate consistent with the same capital providers. Financing interest already priced into the rate should not also reduce unlevered operating cash.

For mutually exclusive projects, a higher annual return percentage need not deliver more total value. Forecast ramp-up, useful life, sale or closure costs and alternative uses of constrained capital. A one-year accounting profit cannot replace the full timeline.

Method

Reconciling two decisions

  1. Set the capital charge

    Choose the invested capital and an appropriate required return for the activity and risk being judged.

  2. Compare incremental outcomes

    Calculate current annual economic profit both before and after acceptance. Separately calculate the incremental project's own contribution.

  3. Check the horizon

    For a temporary or growing investment, value the dated cash flows and explain why the annual accounting comparison agrees or differs.

Worked case

A division decides whether to expand

A division earns €1m annual operating profit after tax on €5m capital. A comparable-risk expansion adds €1m capital and €140,000 annual after-tax operating profit. The required return is 10%.

Working

  1. Existing return is 20%. After investment the division shows €1.14m/€6m = 19%. A bonus linked solely to increasing the ratio discourages the investment.
  2. Existing economic profit is €1m − €500,000 = €500,000. After expansion it is €1.14m − €600,000 = €540,000, an increase of €40,000.
  3. The expansion earns 14% on its own capital, exceeding its 10% charge. For a long-lived investment, confirm that the timeline, asset replacement and cash conversion support the annual figures.

Interpretation

A manager can present both average performance and marginal value so the board sees why the two signals point in different directions.

A common mistake

Interpreting one positive year of economic profit as a proven positive project valuation overlooks later capital and cash needs.

How to correct it

Construct the entire cash-flow timeline and test recovery values and reinvestment, even when the first-year return looks attractive.

Quick check
A steady-state operation earns €90,000 after tax on €1m of operating capital. At an 8% required return, what is annual economic profit?

The capital charge is €80,000. Deduct it from €90,000 to find €10,000 of annual earnings above the required return. This comparison uses operating profit before financing charges.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A one-year project costs €500,000 now and pays €556,500 in cash one year later, with no other flows. Another proposal earns €52,000 annual after-tax operating profit on €400,000 of capital. Both require 10%.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find the first project's net present value.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the second proposal's annual economic profit. Can the two numbers by themselves rank the projects?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Discount the final payment to €556,500/1.10 = €505,909.09. Subtract the €500,000 investment for NPV of €5,909.09.

Part B 3 marks

The charge is €40,000, leaving €12,000 annually. A one-period residual profit and a lifetime NPV have different meanings. The second project's duration and cash flows are necessary to compare present values.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can you reconcile a positive two-year project NPV to its annual economic profits when accounting depreciation is not the cash outlay?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. I would use the €100 opening capital in year one and €50 in year two. The 10% charges are €10 and €5, which leave €5 and €10 residual income from the €15 annual operating earnings. Their present value is €12.81. A direct cash forecast gives the same figure, so the different expense timing is fully reconciled.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the model assumes €20 of assets still on the books after year two and no third-year operation. What must be added?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Determine the actual sale or settlement proceeds and include the remaining capital recovery. A sale different from book value also affects terminal earnings or the valuation adjustment. An identity derived for zero terminal capital cannot simply ignore the €20 balance.
