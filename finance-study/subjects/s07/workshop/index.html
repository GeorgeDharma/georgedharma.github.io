Professional workshop · S07

The quarter that grew revenue and missed profit

It is the June quarter close at Cordage Systems, a fictional equipment supplier. Finance approved a two-product plan in March. Revenue finished above plan, but the operating director needs a reconciled explanation of contribution and profit before tomorrow's performance meeting. Sales also wants an immediate answer on a one-off order for the next quarter. Provide the price floor, quantify the trade-off at the bottleneck and test whether the business can fund the order before the customer's payment.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Regional FP&A analyst supporting the operating director and group controller
What to submit
Submit the management-accounting close bridge and a priced special-order recommendation. The group controller uses the reconciliation to release the pack. The sales director uses the quote floor and treasury conditions when negotiating. All euro amounts exclude taxes and financing charges unless stated.

From inputs to decision

01

Reconstruct both income statements

A complete control total prevents a favourable sales story from being mistaken for actual value creation.

  1. Planned revenue is 6,000 × €40 + 2,000 × €80, or €400,000. Planned variable spending is €144,000 + €88,000 = €232,000. Contribution is €168,000, leaving €48,000 operating profit after €120,000 of fixed costs.
  2. Actual revenue totals €241,800 + €180,400 = €422,200. Actual variable cost is 6,200 × €25 + 2,200 × €46 = €256,200. Contribution is €166,000, leaving €42,000 after the €124,000 fixed bill.

Revenue increased €22,200 or 5.55%. Operating profit fell €6,000 or 12.5%. Both figures will appear in the meeting pack with their different denominators.

02

Isolate the gain from additional units

The board wants to understand the effect of size before product composition, price or unit cost. Fix an order for the bridge so the components can be reproduced.

  1. Planned contribution is €16 per standard unit and €36 per enterprise unit. The budgeted three-to-one mix therefore contributes €21 per unit on average.
  2. Aggregate units rose from 8,000 to 8,400. At the planned mix the 400 additional units contribute €8,400, taking profit from €48,000 to €56,400 with planned prices, costs and fixed expenses.

The intermediate comparison uses 6,300 standard and 2,100 enterprise units. It is a calculated baseline used only to isolate mix.

03

Separate mix, invoiced pricing and variable spend

The extra enterprise orders can improve contribution even if purchasing costs subsequently erode it. Three independent calculations identify the decision owners.

  1. Against the intermediate mix, actual sales comprise 100 fewer standard and 100 more enterprise units. The mix adds 100 × (€36 − €16) = €2,000. Actual quantities at planned margins now imply €58,400 profit.
  2. Apply actual prices to actual units. Standard discounting costs €6,200 and enterprise pricing adds €4,400, a net €1,800 loss. Then apply actual unit costs. Standard cost adds €6,200 of spending and enterprise adds €4,400, reducing profit by another €10,600.

The resulting profit at planned fixed spending is €46,000. Inspect invoice discounts by customer and purchase or yield data by product. The arithmetic alone cannot identify why actual unit costs rose.

04

Close the fixed-cost bridge and challenge allocation

Finance can reconcile the income statement without assigning an unsupported operational cause to the extra €4,000.

  1. Actual overhead is €124,000 versus €120,000 planned, reducing profit from €46,000 to €42,000. The full check is €48,000 + €8,400 + €2,000 − €1,800 − €10,600 − €4,000 = €42,000.
  2. The unit-cost effect already contains variable operating spending. A nightly maintenance invoice should enter fixed spending only if the period accounts classify it there. Obtain the ledger and purchasing reconciliation before reassigning charges.

The controller can sign off the €6,000 difference. The cause of higher variable unit spend remains under investigation and should not be presented as a proven supplier-price increase.

05

Price the scarce machine time

The one-off bid uses capacity that may be idle for some units but valuable for the rest. Convert the entire requested batch into finishing hours before pricing.

  1. One thousand special units need 600 hours. Three hundred are freely available, leaving 300 hours that would displace saleable enterprise work. That corresponds to 200 enterprise units at 1.5 hours each and €7,200 lost contribution.
  2. Special units cost €25,000 in total and the batch adds €1,500 setup. The economic minimum is €33,700, or €33.70 per unit. The offered €35,000 creates €1,300 of incremental operating contribution after displacement.

The €33.70 floor assumes the €36 enterprise contribution and all 300 available hours are verified. Existing fixed factory overhead is already committed for the period.

06

Stress the bargain and confirm liquidity

A profitable batch can still be an unacceptable commitment if finishing time vanishes or cash falls below the operating floor before payment.

  1. If spare finishing hours fall to 150, 450 hours displace €24 contribution per hour, a €10,800 cost. Total economic cost rises to €37,300 and the existing bid loses €2,300. Obtain a written production booking before accepting.
  2. Before collection the incremental cash outlay is the €18,000 supplier deposit plus the €1,500 setup. Cash drops from €36,000 to €16,500, €1,500 below the €18,000 floor. The supplier deposit is part of the €25,000 variable cost and must not be charged again in the profit bridge.

A client deposit or available facility of at least €1,500 before supplier payment fixes the stated liquidity gap. Any facility fee or customer concession must be compared with the thin €1,300 contribution.

07

Release the pack with action owners

A finance close and a quote approval are two decisions. Give each owner explicit evidence and conditions.

  1. In the close meeting report quantity and mix gains of €10,400 against losses of €16,400 from price, unit cost and fixed spend. Procurement and operations will reconcile bill-of-material prices, scrap and labour standards before the next forecast.
  2. For the bid ask production to release a binding 300-hour slot and treasury to document a dated liquidity solution. Sales should avoid promising the €35 price if a concession or fee would use the residual contribution.

Decision record is conditional acceptance. Without capacity confirmation or timely minimum funding, renegotiate price or delivery date and rerun the displacement schedule.

Completed document

Operating review note and exception quote approval

June result

Operating profit was €42,000, €6,000 below the approved plan, on €422,200 revenue. Scale and mix added €10,400. Unit spending was €10,600 adverse and is the largest single adverse component.

Management action

Sales will reconcile the €6,200 standard-product discount loss against enterprise price improvement. Operations and procurement will split the €10,600 unit-cost movement into price and physical usage using actual purchase and production records.

Quote recommendation

Accept 1,000 units at €35 only when 300 spare finishing hours are secured. Our all-in economic floor is €33.70 per unit. Allocating more hours to the batch would replace enterprise contribution of €24 per hour.

Cash release

Treasury must document at least €1,500 of immediately available funding or customer deposit. A funding fee or concession reducing the projected €1,300 contribution to zero requires fresh commercial approval.

Escalation

If capacity falls to 150 spare hours, the floor becomes €37.30 and the submitted price loses €2,300. Bring the revised amount and execution schedule back to the operating director before making a commitment.
