Theory midterm · 10 marks

Contribution per hour under a bottleneck

After lessons 1–5, allow 20 minutes. Explain the reasoning behind your answer.

Product A sells for 45 with 25 variable cost and uses two machine hours. B sells for 32 with 18 variable cost and uses one hour. There are 100 free machine hours and sufficient demand for either item. Fixed costs are unchanged.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Choose the mix and quantify the difference from using all time for A.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    If demand for B is capped at 60 units, recalculate.

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    Why could an allocated cost per product lead to a different, mistaken decision?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the midterm marking guide

Part A · 4 marks

A contributes 20 per unit or 10 per hour. B contributes 14 per hour. Make 100 B for contribution 1,400 versus 50 A for 1,000. The improvement is 400. Credit both per-hour figures, the mix and the difference.

Part B · 3 marks

Make 60 B using 60 hours. The other 40 hours make 20 A. Total contribution is 60 × 14 + 20 × 20 = 1,240.

Part C · 3 marks

An allocation can assign fixed overhead that stays the same under either mix. The binding resource and incremental contribution determine this short-run result. Capacity changes or avoidable fixed costs would require a fresh comparison.

Problem 1

Budget is 1,000 units at €10. Actual sales are 1,100 at €9. Decompose the revenue change using budget price for volume.

Show the worked solution

Volume contributes +€1,000. Price contributes 1,100 × (9 − 10) = −€1,100. Net revenue change is −€100.

Problem 2

A fully costed product loses €2 each but contributes €5 after its variable costs. Should production stop?

Assessment criteria
  • Apply the right theory and state the assumptions.
  • Show each step with consistent units, dates and currency.
  • Interpret the result, test an alternative and explain what could change the decision.
Suggested academic marking scheme

Use these weights when assessing the complete subject. The four problem sets collect the written problems from individual lectures. You can use the 10-mark midterm and the 40-mark subject paper with the weights below.

Problem sets 25%
Four sets per standard subject. Show reasoning, calculations, units and checks. The two practice problems in each subject are worked samples for these sets.
Theory midterm 20%
After the first half of the subject. Explain or derive a model, identify assumptions and state what would change if one failed.
Seminar paper or laboratory 25%
During the second half. Apply the theory to a case, dataset or implementation. Address an alternative explanation and document how to reproduce the result.
Individual final examination 30%
At the end of the subject. Solve an unfamiliar problem, interpret the result and critique a claim. M&A and lending each include a 30-mark examination question with a marking guide.
