Recover barrier crossings hidden between simulation dates

Derive the conditional probability that a Brownian bridge crosses a fixed lower barrier.

In this lecture

  2. Reflect the paths that have touched

  4. Rebuild the calculation

Theory and terminology

Brownian-bridge barrier correction

continuous monitoring · barrier options · crossing probability

What you should be able to explain

  • Derive the conditional probability that a Brownian bridge crosses a fixed lower barrier.
  • Decide when to apply a survival correction and calculate a discounted path contribution.

Read the monitoring clause

A down-and-out contract ceases to pay once the underlying touches a lower barrier. A continuously monitored contract observes every instant. A close-only contract observes specified closing prices. Equal prices at the supplied sample dates can consequently imply different payoffs. Monitoring frequency is contractual information, separate from the computational choice of a simulation grid.

Assume the log price follows Brownian motion with constant volatility and constant drift over one interval, and the log barrier stays fixed. Once its two endpoint values are given, the random path joining them is a Brownian bridge. Conditional on the endpoints, its distribution inside the interval does not depend on the constant drift. The endpoints still depend on drift before conditioning.

Reflect the paths that have touched

Measure each endpoint by its distance above the barrier. Start at positive distance a and end at positive distance c. For driftless Brownian motion, reflect the portion after the first barrier touch. The mapping pairs a touching path ending at c with a path ending at −c. Brownian symmetry permits computing the density of the former from the density for the reflected endpoint.

Divide the reflected endpoint density by the ordinary endpoint density. The normalizing constants of the two Gaussian densities cancel. Their squared distances from the initial position are (c+a)² and (c−a)², whose difference is 4ac. This gives a crossing probability even when both observations safely exceed the barrier.

Turn a conditional probability into a price contribution

For a no-rebate knock-out with terminal payoff determined by the simulated state, multiply the path payoff by one minus the conditional hitting probability. On consecutive intervals with constant model coefficients, conditioned on the grid endpoints the interiors are independent Brownian bridges. Multiply their survival probabilities. Average these path contributions over the simulated grid states to obtain the price.

If a monitored endpoint is already at or below the barrier, survival is zero. If the contract checks only the stated closes, a bridge correction between those closes would add a monitoring obligation that was never sold. State-dependent volatility, a moving barrier or jumps also change the crossing law. A rebate paid immediately upon touching needs the time of touch to discount it correctly.

Method

Build the result

p_hit=exp[−2ac/(σ²h)]; path contribution=Dg(1−p_hit)

a=log(S_start/B) and c=log(S_end/B) are distances above price barrier B. h is the interval in years, σ is annual log volatility, D is the discount factor and g is the terminal cash payoff conditional on survival. All inputs in the exponent are dimensionless after time scaling.

  1. Endpoint transition variance is σ²h. The ratio of reflected to ordinary Gaussian endpoint densities equals exp[−((a+c)²−(a−c)²)/(2σ²h)].
  2. Expand and cancel the two squares to obtain exp[−2ac/(σ²h)]. Values approach one when a positive endpoint moves towards the barrier.
  3. Use one minus this probability as the conditional survival weight. The one-interval discounted path contribution still requires averaging across endpoint simulations.
Worked case

A survival-linked €10 coupon

A no-rebate contract pays €10 if the stock stays above 100 throughout one quarter. The simulated endpoint prices are 100 exp(0.10) and 100 exp(0.05), about 110.52 and 105.13. Annual log volatility is 20%, h=0.25 and the discount factor to payment is 0.99.

Working

  1. The log distances to the barrier are a=0.10 and c=0.05. Squared volatility times the interval equals 0.20²×0.25=0.0100.
  2. The conditional crossing exponent is −2×0.10×0.05/0.0100=−1. The hitting probability is exp(−1)=0.36788.
  3. Conditional survival is 0.63212. The discounted contribution for these endpoints is €10×0.99×0.63212=€6.258.
  4. A contract monitored only at these two endpoints has discounted path contribution €9.90. The €3.642 difference comes from unobserved touches under continuous monitoring.

Decision and interpretation

Use €6.258 as this grid path's contribution to the continuous-monitor estimate. A full price still averages over possible endpoint values under the pricing measure.

Quick check
What happens to conditional hit probability if volatility increases and the same two positive log distances are held fixed?

A larger σ²h brings the negative exponent closer to zero. Its exponential approaches one.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A coupon pays 8 after a quarter only if a lower barrier was never touched. Both endpoints exceed it by log distances 0.12 and 0.08. Annual σ=0.40, h=0.25 and D=0.98. Compute the conditional hit probability and discounted path contribution.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Interval variance is 0.40²×0.25=0.0400, giving an exponent of −2×0.12×0.08/0.0400=−0.48.
  2. Conditional hit probability is exp(−0.48)=0.61878 and survival probability is 0.38122.
  3. Discounted contribution is 8×0.98×0.38122=2.989. If monitoring instead took place only at the endpoints it would be 7.840 on this path.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Both simulated closing prices were above the knock-out level. How much of this path's apparent payoff can a pricing analyst count?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

For a contract monitored only at those closes, the two endpoints settle survival over that interval. For continuous monitoring, I calculate how often paths connecting those endpoints would have touched the barrier and weight the payoff by the conditional survival probability.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the contract pays an immediate rebate when the barrier is first touched. Is the hitting probability enough?

Compare with the follow-up answer

It determines the probability of receiving the rebate. With a nonzero short rate its present value also depends on when it is received. Use the first-passage time distribution or an appropriate simulation for the touch time.
