Itô’s formula

For a smooth function of a diffusion, combine time change, state sensitivity and half the variance times curvature. Compare with the ordinary chain rule.

The starting point

A curved payoff responds differently to a rise and a fall. Ordinary first-order changes can cancel on average while curvature still adds value. Itô’s formula explains how to transform a continuous random process. Write its small change as dX = a dt + b dW, with drift a and shock size b.

For a sufficiently smooth function f(t, X), the time contribution is fₜ dt, the slope contribution is fₓ dX, and the additional curvature contribution is ½b²fₓₓ dt. The second derivative survives because the accumulated squared Brownian increments have nonzero quadratic variation.

Theory and method

Multidimensional Itô’s formula

Itô product rule · Quanto adjustment

A foreign share and its exchange rate both have zero instantaneous drift under the assumed measure. Must the translated share value also have zero drift?

A euro analyst carries a share priced in dollars. Euro value multiplies its dollar price by euros per dollar. Even when the average separate change in each price is zero, days when both rise can produce an extra euro gain. Days when the two tend to move in opposite directions can produce an extra loss. Multidimensional Itô calculus quantifies this mechanism using the mixed derivative and the local covariance of the drivers.

How the result is built

d(SX)/(SX) = (μS + μX + ρσSσX)dt + σS dW₁ + σX dW₂

S is dollars per share, X euros per dollar, SX euros per share, μS and μX are annual proportional drifts under one measure, σS and σX are annual proportional volatilities, ρ is instantaneous correlation, and W₁, W₂ are correlated Brownian motions in time t measured in years.

  1. Use d(SX) = XdS + SdX + d[S,X]. The first two terms transmit the separate shocks and drifts.
  2. The covariation is SXρσSσXdt under the proportional models. Divide by SX to express all terms per euro of holding.
  3. At volatilities 25% and 12% and correlation −0.5, the covariance drift is −0.5 × 0.25 × 0.12 = −0.015 per year, an annual rate of −1.5%.

When the reasoning holds

  • All drifts and the covariance must be expressed under the same probability measure. Mixing a dollar pricing-measure drift and euro forecasting drift lacks a joint interpretation.
  • The quote is euros per dollar. Inverting it changes the transformed drift and correlation sign convention through Itô’s rule.

Terms in this lesson
Derivative
A contract whose value depends on something else, such as a currency, an interest rate or a share price.
Option
A contract giving the buyer a right to buy or sell on stated terms. The buyer pays a premium for the right.
Correlation
A measure of how closely two series move together in a linear pattern.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
Time and random increments · dt, dW
Over a short interval Δt, a standard Brownian increment has mean zero and standard deviation √Δt. If a year is one time unit, a quarter-year increment has standard deviation 0.5. A drift term scales with elapsed time itself.
Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.

All notation examples

First example

Let X start at 3 with zero drift and constant shock size 2. For f(X) = X², the slope is 2X and the second derivative is 2. The drift of X² is ½ × 2² × 2 = 4 per unit time. After a quarter year, its expected value is 9 + 4 × 0.25 = 10.

Recover the term that ordinary calculus loses

For a small interval, Taylor expansion of f(t,X) includes fₜΔt + fₓΔX + ½fₓₓ(ΔX)². In a diffusion ΔX contains bΔW, of size √Δt. The square of that term is consequently large enough to survive aggregation, unlike an ordinary differentiable path’s squared changes.

Terms involving Δt² or ΔtΔW vanish in the limit under standard regularity. Quadratic variation replaces accumulated (ΔW)² by elapsed time. This gives the extra drift term ½b²fₓₓdt, with derivatives evaluated at the current time and state.

Compute the full transformation

For dX = a dt + b dW, collect time terms to obtain df = (fₜ + afₓ + ½b²fₓₓ)dt + bfₓdW. The first coefficient controls conditional expected local change under the specified measure. The second controls exposure to the driving shock.

The formula ordinarily requires a function differentiable once in time and twice in state along the region of interest. The payoff max(X − K,0) has a kink at K, so blindly differentiating it there misses additional mathematics. In option pricing the value function before expiry can be smoother than the terminal payoff.

A product of two random quantities

Applying the logic to a product gives d(XY) = XdY + YdX + d[X,Y], where [X,Y] is covariation. The final term can influence the drift even when both individual processes have zero drift. Simultaneously changing foreign prices and currency exposures can therefore affect expected translated value.

For a single Brownian driver, products of its coefficients determine covariation. Independent Brownian drivers contribute zero cross-variation. Keep the stochastic drivers’ correlation and the units of volatility explicit before applying a multivariate result.

Method

Transform a model

  1. Differentiate the payoff

    Write the time derivative, state slope and state curvature. Check smoothness over the region used.

  2. Collect the drift

    Combine explicit time, the underlying’s drift times the slope, and half the local variance times curvature.

  3. Calculate the shock exposure

    Multiply the original shock coefficient by the slope and retain the originating Brownian driver.

Worked case

A convex balance measure

An analyst models X in arbitrary index units as dX = 1dt + 0.5dW and monitors f(t,X) = t + X³. Evaluate the local dynamics at X = 2.

Working

  1. The time derivative is 1, the state slope 3X² = 12 and the state curvature 6X = 12.
  2. The instantaneous drift at the chosen state is 1 + 1 × 12 + ½ × 0.5² × 12 = 14.5 index-function units per year.
  3. The shock coefficient is 0.5 × 12 = 6. Over a sufficiently short interval h the approximate mean change is 14.5h and its standard deviation is 6√h.

Interpretation

These coefficients change with X. Multiplying the initial local drift by an entire year need not give the exact one-year expected change.

A common mistake

Apply an ordinary chain rule to compute the expected change in a curved payoff under random shocks.

How to correct it

Calculate quadratic variation and include the second-derivative contribution. A zero conditional mean for the price shock can coexist with a nonzero average change in a nonlinear function.

Quick check
If dX = 3 dW and f(X) = X², what is the drift contributed by curvature?

The curvature term is ½ × 3² × 2 = 9. The mean of X can remain constant even as its increasing dispersion raises the mean of X².

Independent exam problem

Take dX = 2dt + 3dW and f(t,X) = tX + X². Evaluate at t = 1, X = 2.

  1. Part A · 5 marks

    Compute the transformed drift and diffusion coefficient.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    An analyst omits curvature. Quantify the resulting local mean-change error over h = 0.01.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 5 marks

fₜ = X = 2, fₓ = t + 2X = 5 and fₓₓ = 2. Drift is 2 + 2 × 5 + ½ × 9 × 2 = 21. Diffusion coefficient is 3 × 5 = 15.

Part B 3 marks

The omitted drift is 9 per unit time, so the first-order error over the short interval is about 0.09 function units. The calculation is a local approximation since slope and time derivatives evolve.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A foreign share and its exchange rate both have zero instantaneous drift under the assumed measure. Must the translated share value also have zero drift?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The translated position has an extra local drift equal to its proportional covariance. In this example opposite movements subtract 1.5 percentage points per year from the sum of the two component drifts. I would establish the exchange-rate quotation, measure and dividend terms before interpreting this as a valuation adjustment for an actual book.

Interviewer’s follow-up

How does this enter the more general multivariate theorem?

Compare with the follow-up answer

For a smooth function of several diffusions the additional drift is half the sum of each Hessian entry times its matching instantaneous covariance. For a product the two symmetric mixed derivatives equal one, and their contributions together leave exactly the single covariance term.
