Martingales and numeraires

A martingale has its current value as its conditional expected future value under a chosen measure. Discounted traded prices have this property under a suitable pricing measure.

The starting point

A numeraire is a positive traded asset used as a measuring unit, such as a bank account earning interest. Dividing a future price by the account balance expresses both current and future amounts in units of that account. This removes the account’s growth from the comparison.

A martingale has conditional expected future value equal to its current value under a specified probability measure. In an arbitrage-free model with a suitable pricing measure for the account numeraire, discounted traded gains are martingales under the usual integrability conditions. For a non-dividend-paying stock this applies directly to its discounted price.

Theory and method

Change-of-numeraire theorem

Forward measure · Numeraire pricing

Why can an interest-rate desk use a maturity bond as numeraire when the eventual payment is already in euros?

A payment due on one future date can be measured in units of a traded bond paying one euro on that date. Under the bond’s associated probability measure, its current price supplies the discounting and the expectation describes the future euro payoff. Changing the measuring unit also changes the relative probabilities assigned to states. A cash-measure expectation can therefore differ from the bond-measure expectation when interest rates are uncertain.

How the result is built

V₀ = P(0,T) Eᵀ[H], and qⱼᴺ = πⱼNⱼ/N₀

H is a euro payoff at maturity T, V₀ its current euro value, P(0,T) the price of a zero-coupon bond paying one euro at T, Eᵀ expectation in its forward measure, πⱼ the current euro price of one euro delivered only in state j, and N₀,Nⱼ the current and terminal values of any positive traded numeraire.

  1. In a finite model price H as the sum of state prices times its state payouts. For the maturity bond H=1, its price is the sum of state prices.
  2. Normalize the state prices by this bond price. The resulting forward probabilities sum to one, and multiplying their expected euro payoff by the bond price restores the original sum.
  3. With state prices 0.45 and 0.50 the bond is 0.95. A payoff of €12 only in state one has forward expectation 12 × 0.45/0.95 ≈ €5.6842 and value €5.40.

When the reasoning holds

  • The numeraire must be positive and tradable under the market model so it defines an equivalent price unit. A claim paying zero in some state cannot generally serve this role.
  • Payments must share the numeraire bond’s maturity for the displayed undiscounted terminal expectation. Payments over multiple dates require correct date-specific handling.

Terms in this lesson
Martingale
A process whose expected future value, given current information, equals its current value under the specified probability measure.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Discount factor
The price today of one unit of currency payable at a particular future date under specified risk assumptions.
How to read the notation
Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.
Conditional probability · P(A | B)
Read the vertical bar as “given.” If four of 100 late-paying customers default, P(default | late payment) in that group is 4%. To find P(late payment | default), start with the group of customers who defaulted.
Summation · ∑
The symbol asks you to add the terms for the listed items. For weights of 0.6 and 0.4 and returns of 5% and 2%, ∑ wᵢrᵢ means 0.6 × 5% + 0.4 × 2% = 3.8%. A standalone capital sigma (Σ) can also name a covariance matrix in portfolio equations.
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.

All notation examples

First example

A stock costs €100, can end at €120 or €80, and cash grows by 4%. For discounted prices to average €100, solve 120q + 80(1 − q) = 104. The pricing weight on the up state is q = 0.60, giving an expected discounted price of €104/1.04 = €100.

Choose a unit of account

A euro at the end of next year is a different dated object from a euro today. A cash account Bₜ that grows by the applicable short rate can serve as a positive traded numeraire. Expressing each asset price as Sₜ/Bₜ measures its value in units of that account.

Under an appropriate pricing measure Q, discounted traded gains are martingales under the usual conditions. For a non-dividend-paying asset this gives Sₜ/Bₜ = Eᵠ[Sₜ₊₁/Bₜ₊₁ | Fₜ]. For dividend-paying assets include reinvested or appropriately discounted dividends in the gains.

Derive state prices and probabilities

A state price is today’s cost of one currency unit delivered only in a specified future state. In a complete one-period market with state prices πⱼ, an attainable claim costs ΣπⱼCⱼ. State prices sum to the current cost of a sure future unit, the discount factor D.

Dividing each state price by D produces pricing probabilities qⱼ that sum to one. Then price is DΣqⱼCⱼ. This rearrangement makes discounting and risk weighting explicit. State prices themselves typically sum to less than one at a positive rate, so treating them as ordinary probabilities mis-scales payoffs.

A different numeraire changes the weights

Choose another positive traded asset N as measuring unit and associated probabilities must change so prices in N units are martingales. In one period, probability of state j under the N measure is πⱼNⱼ/N₀. Positivity and market-consistent N₀ make these weights sum to one.

The currency value of a replicable claim is invariant. Compute N₀ times the weighted mean of Cⱼ/Nⱼ under the new measure. The Nⱼ cancels inside each state and leaves ΣπⱼCⱼ, exactly the original price.

Method

Move between units

  1. Find state prices

    Use traded asset and cash prices to determine or constrain the prices of individual state payoffs.

  2. Normalize as needed

    Divide by the discount factor for cash-numeraire probabilities or multiply by terminal numeraire value divided by its current price.

  3. Convert claims consistently

    A probability measure and its numeraire belong together. Divide the terminal claim by that numeraire and multiply its expectation by today’s numeraire.

Worked case

Price one payoff in two units

One-period state prices are 0.40 and 0.55. A strictly positive traded asset N pays 2 in the upper state and 1 in the lower. A claim pays 3 in the upper state and zero otherwise.

Working

  1. Cash discount factor is 0.95 and cash-measure upper probability 0.40/0.95. Claim price is 0.95 × (0.40/0.95) × 3 = 1.20.
  2. The numeraire price is 0.40 × 2 + 0.55 × 1 = 1.35. Its upper-state measure weight is 0.8/1.35 ≈ 0.5926.
  3. In numeraire units the claim pays 3/2 in the upper state. Its currency price is 1.35 × (0.8/1.35) × 1.5 = 1.20.

Interpretation

The different upper-state probabilities reflect the new unit of account. They do not imply contradictory prices or competing empirical forecasts.

A common mistake

Use the new numeraire’s pricing probabilities while still discounting the currency payoff at the cash rate.

How to correct it

Express the payoff in units of the chosen numeraire before averaging, or return to the cash-numeraire weights. Changing only one side breaks price invariance.

Quick check
At these pricing weights, what is today’s value of a claim paying €10 only in the down state?

The down state carries weight 0.40. Its weighted future payment is €4, which discounts to 4/1.04 ≈ €3.85 in today’s euros.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Two state prices are 0.3 and 0.6. A tradable numeraire pays 1 and 2, respectively. A claim pays 0 and 5.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compute discount factor, cash-measure probabilities and claim price.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Reproduce the price with the alternative numeraire.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

D = 0.9, giving weights 1/3 and 2/3. Price = 0.9 × (2/3) × 5 = 3, equivalently the second state price times 5.

Part B 4 marks

Its initial price is 0.3 + 1.2 = 1.5. Its measure places probability 1.2/1.5 = 0.8 on the second state, where the claim pays 5/2 numeraire units. Price = 1.5 × 0.8 × 2.5 = 3.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why can an interest-rate desk use a maturity bond as numeraire when the eventual payment is already in euros?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would use the maturity bond because its terminal value is exactly one in the payment currency. Pricing a terminal amount then reduces to today’s bond price times a forward-measure expectation. Here the amount has expectation €5.6842 under that measure and current value €5.40. Another numeraire gives the same price only when both the weights and payoff units change together.

Interviewer’s follow-up

May I replace the T-forward expectation with my historical forecast of the floating payment?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Only with a separate justification linking those expectations. Historical probabilities describe outcomes and can include risk premiums. When the payoff is related to rates, the pricing weights and payoff can move together, and even the usual cash pricing measure may differ from the T-forward measure.
