Information through time

A filtration records the information available up to each date. Feasible trading positions must depend on information already observed.

The starting point

Put yourself at the closing bell on Monday. You know the prices and announcements published up to that moment. You do not yet know Tuesday’s opening price. A filtration is a sequence of information sets that grows as observations arrive. It provides a formal clock for what a trader can know.

A strategy is adapted when its value at each date uses information available by that date. For trading gains over the next interval, choose the position before that interval’s price change. A proposed backtest that quietly uses later information can manufacture profits even in a model with entirely unpredictable increments.

Theory and method

Doob martingale and the tower property

Conditional expectation · Law of iterated expectations

A reserve estimate jumps when an inspection report arrives. How would you test whether that jump contradicts unbiased updating?

A claims team re-estimates a future payment whenever it learns whether a site passed inspection. Its best current estimate is the probability-weighted payment within the histories still possible. The report can shift that estimate substantially. Before seeing the report, however, the expected change in the forecast should be zero when the probabilities and information are internally consistent. The claim’s realized cash can still move in either direction.

How the result is built

Mₜ = E[X | Fₜ], and E[Mᵤ | Fₜ] = Mₜ for t ≤ u

X is the integrable terminal payment in euros. Fₜ and Fᵤ are the information sets at dates t and u. M is the forecast in euros, and E is expectation under one specified probability measure.

  1. For a report classified favorable with probability 0.6, remaining payments average €20. In the adverse group, occurring with probability 0.4, they average €80.
  2. The pre-report expectation equals 0.6 × 20 + 0.4 × 80 = €44 because each terminal outcome belongs to one report group.
  3. The revisions are −€24 and +€36. Their probability-weighted sum is zero. More generally, averaging the finer conditional means reconstructs the coarser conditional mean.

When the reasoning holds

  • The information sets must be nested. Comparing estimates made with different non-nested datasets requires reconstructing the information being conditioned on.
  • Use a consistent measure and payment definition. Discounting or changing the scope of insured events can create movement unrelated to information arrival.
Terms in this lesson
Backtest
A historical simulation of decisions made under an explicit rule, using information available at each date.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
How to read the notation
Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.

All notation examples

First example

A share ends Monday at €50. On Tuesday it will move up or down €2. A spreadsheet buys one share before every rise and shorts one before every fall. Its €2 certain gain relies on knowing the sign of Tuesday’s move at Monday’s close, so the trade cannot be implemented.

Represent what is knowable

A two-toss market has four possible histories, UU, UD, DU and DD. Before the first toss a trader cannot distinguish any of them. After observing U first, she knows the outcome belongs to {UU, UD}. The next move remains unknown.

A filtration is a sequence of information collections F₀, F₁, … with Fₜ contained in Fₜ₊₁. A random quantity is measurable at a time if its value can be determined from that time’s information. It must take the same value in states the trader cannot yet distinguish.

Conditional expectations on the tree

At a particular node, discard states inconsistent with the observed history and renormalize the remaining probabilities. The conditional expectation of a payment is its weighted mean within that node. Probabilities must come from the measure relevant to the question, such as actual beliefs for forecasting.

The tower property says that averaging later conditional expectations using today’s information gives today’s conditional expectation. This permits working backward through a tree and clarifies why the value assigned to a contingent claim can change after observing a first move.

Choose positions before the next move

A trading strategy using one quantity over the period from t to t + 1 must choose it using Fₜ. The new position chosen after observing the move applies to the following interval. This predictability requirement prevents a model from converting hindsight into trading gains.

A data field dated Monday but published Wednesday belongs to Wednesday’s information set. An order produced after a closing auction needs an execution price available afterward. The same chronology governs simulated trades and the formal information structure.

Method

Define information at a node

  1. Enumerate histories

    Group paths that have identical observations up to the decision time.

  2. Compute a conditional mean

    Within each group, multiply terminal outcomes by their conditional probabilities and sum.

  3. Audit an action

    Check that histories still in the same group produce the same chosen holding before the next move.

Worked case

A contingent lender payment

There are four equally likely two-step outcomes. A loan pays 120, 90, 80 and 70 in the states UU, UD, DU and DD respectively. No discounting is needed to calculate conditional expectations.

Working

  1. Initially all four outcomes are possible. The unconditional expected payment is (120 + 90 + 80 + 70)/4 = 90.
  2. After an up move, the two possible payments average 105. After a down move, the two possible payments average 75.
  3. Initially the chance of each first move is one half. Averaging the two revised forecasts, (105 + 75)/2, recovers the original 90.

Interpretation

Payments need not be tradable for conditional expectations to be calculated. A no-arbitrage price requires additional market and measure assumptions.

A common mistake

Choosing a holding after seeing the day’s close entitles the strategy to the entire gain since yesterday’s close.

How to correct it

Attach each position to the next price increment and record when a change in holdings could execute. Recompute a backtest after shifting positions to their true execution times.

Quick check
A company publishes its earnings after Tuesday’s regular-market close. Which trade can use that release if the stock next trades on Wednesday?

Information becomes available when it is published. An implementable simulation also needs an execution rule consistent with the next trading opportunity.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

On independent fair tosses a security pays 12 only on UU, 4 on DU and zero otherwise. A trader wants to buy before the second toss only if it will be U.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compute its initial and first-toss conditional expectations.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Is the proposed holding admissible using information from the first toss?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Initially (12 + 4)/4 = 4. After first U the expectation is (12 + 0)/2 = 6. After first D it is (4 + 0)/2 = 2. Their initial probability-weighted mean is 4.

Part B 3 marks

No. At either node the second move is still unknown. The holding would distinguish two outcomes with the same first-toss history. A strategy depending only on the observed first move could be admissible.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A reserve estimate jumps when an inspection report arrives. How would you test whether that jump contradicts unbiased updating?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would compare the pre-report reserve with the probability-weighted post-report reserves using the probabilities available beforehand. Here €44 is consistent with forecasts of €20 and €80, so an adverse update to €80 is economically meaningful. To test for systematic bias I would examine repeated forecast revisions conditional on available information and hold the payment definition fixed.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if management never archives the unfavorable reports?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Then the retained updates are a selected sample. Reconstruct the complete report log or model a defensible observation process. A mean computed only from favorable revisions will mechanically suggest over-reserving even when the original forecasts were consistent.
