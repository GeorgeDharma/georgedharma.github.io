Stochastic Calculus and Continuous-Time Asset Pricing

Stochastic integration

Define the integral using holdings chosen before each price increment. This expresses the gains from an adapted trading strategy.

The starting point

If you hold three shares during a €2 rise, that position makes €6. With changing holdings, divide time into intervals and add the holding chosen at the start of each interval times the price change over that interval. Rebalancing after a move can only affect subsequent gains.

The Itô integral formalizes the limit of these sums for suitable trading strategies and price processes. The left-endpoint convention records that each position uses information available before the next increment. Adding interest, dividends, transaction fees or external deposits requires separate cash-flow terms when the contract or strategy includes them.

Theory and method

Itô isometry for stochastic hedge gains

Stochastic integral · Mean-square norm

How can an option desk measure exposure lost by replacing a continuously changing hedge with a piecewise-constant hedge?

Suppose the ideal hedge varies over a day but the desk freezes its position until the next rebalance. The difference between the two quantities remains exposed to each subsequent shock. A predictable exposure chosen before an unpredictable Brownian increment has zero expected shock gain. Its size still contributes to the variance of accumulated hedge error. Squaring and summing exposures over time gives a direct risk measure in a pure-diffusion model.

How the result is built

E[(∫₀ᵀ eₜ dWₜ)²] = E[∫₀ᵀ eₜ² dt]

T is horizon in years, eₜ is the predictable difference in shock exposure in euros per square root of year, Wₜ is standard Brownian motion, E is expectation and the two integrals are stochastic gains and integrated squared exposure.

  1. For simple holdings expand the square of the sum eⱼΔWⱼ. A later shock has conditional mean zero given earlier holdings and shocks, so cross terms have zero expectation.
  2. The diagonal terms give E[eⱼ²Δtⱼ] because conditional shock variance equals interval length. Summing and extending by mean-square limits gives the displayed equality.
  3. A mismatch of €6 per square root of year for 0.25 years and €2 for the next 0.75 contributes 36 × 0.25 + 4 × 0.75 = 12 squared euros. Root-mean-square shock error is €3.46.

When the reasoning holds

  • Predictability prevents future shocks from being used in their own position sizing. A hindsight hedge invalidates the zero cross-term argument.
  • Finite expected integrated squared exposure ensures the square and expectation exist. Add drift, jump and transaction-cost components separately in a more complete error attribution.

How to read the notation
Integral · ∫
An ordinary time integral adds a rate across small time intervals. A constant payment flow of €3 per month for four months gives €12. When the final term is dW instead of dt, the increments being accumulated are random. The stochastic-calculus lectures show how that changes the rules.
Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.
Time and random increments · dt, dW
Over a short interval Δt, a standard Brownian increment has mean zero and standard deviation √Δt. If a year is one time unit, a quarter-year increment has standard deviation 0.5. A drift term scales with elapsed time itself.
Summation · ∑
The symbol asks you to add the terms for the listed items. For weights of 0.6 and 0.4 and returns of 5% and 2%, ∑ wᵢrᵢ means 0.6 × 5% + 0.4 × 2% = 3.8%. A standalone capital sigma (Σ) can also name a covariance matrix in portfolio equations.

All notation examples

First example

A share follows €20, €22, €21. Hold two shares before the first move and reduce to one after observing €22. Trading gains are 2 × (€22 − €20) + 1 × (€21 − €22) = €3. The proceeds from selling one share move into cash.

Turn a trading record into an integral

Assume a risky asset S and a desk holding Hₖ shares from tₖ to tₖ₊₁. Over that interval the asset earns Hₖ(Sₖ₊₁ − Sₖ). Adding these gains is distinct from adding changes in HₖSₖ because changing the number of shares transfers cash between the stock and cash accounts.

The Itô integral is defined first for such simple predictable holdings, then extended to suitable limiting strategies. Holdings chosen at left endpoints use only observed information. Under appropriate square-integrability, the integral against Brownian motion has mean zero for a strategy beginning with zero accumulated gains.

Derive the mean-square identity

For a simple strategy against Brownian increments, expand the square of ΣHₖΔWₖ. Cross terms have zero expectation because a later increment has conditional mean zero given the previous history. For the diagonal terms, conditional variance of ΔWₖ is its elapsed time Δtₖ.

Thus E[(∫H dW)²] = E[∫H² dt], the Itô isometry. It measures the dispersion of trading gains and supplies a way to define limits of simple strategies. A strategy can have zero expected gain from the shocks while taking substantial variance.

Separate stochastic and ordinary parts

If dS = a dt + b dW, gains ∫H dS contain ∫Ha dt and ∫Hb dW. The first is an ordinary time integral of drift exposures. The second accumulates random shocks. A zero mean for the second term does not imply zero mean for total gains.

Changing to midpoint sampling for Brownian integrals produces a different limit for some integrands. A trading interpretation needs a predictable strategy or a clearly specified financing arrangement. Changing conventions midway alters the quantity being computed.

Method

Reconstruct gains

  1. Itemize each interval

    Record holdings established before the price move and the actual increment over that interval.

  2. Separate financing

    Record purchases and sales at the rebalance price as transfers between cash and stock accounts.

  3. Calculate modeled risk

    For predictable exposure to Brownian shocks, sum or integrate squared exposure over time and take expectation where holdings are random.

Worked case

Two intervals of Brownian exposure

A desk is exposed to 2 units of a standard Brownian shock for the first half-year and −1 for the second half-year. Consider both its ex ante risk and a realized pair of increments +0.3 and −0.2.

Working

  1. The realized sum is 2 × 0.3 + (−1) × (−0.2) = 0.8 units. The signs reflect holdings actually in force during each period.
  2. Both increments have mean zero, so expected cumulative gain is zero. Its variance is 2² × 0.5 + (−1)² × 0.5 = 2.5.
  3. The standard deviation is √2.5 ≈ 1.58. Equal and opposite positions in successive independent periods would still add variance because they are exposed to different shocks.

Interpretation

If the second holding depended on the first observation, it could remain predictable. Its squared size would then be random and the isometry would average that future exposure.

A common mistake

A negative holding subtracts its variance contribution and can cancel the first interval’s variance.

How to correct it

A negative position changes the sign of realized gains. Its squared magnitude is positive. Cancellation in variance requires covariance with an offsetting exposure to the same or correlated shock.

Quick check
On the same path, a trader holds one share before the first move and buys two more at €22. What are the share-trading gains?

The position earns €2 on the rise and loses 3 × €1 on the fall. Buying two shares at €22 exchanges cash for shares and generates no gain at the instant of purchase.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A trader holds 3 units against Brownian motion for 0.2 year and 1 unit for the next 0.8 year. Realized increments are −0.1 and +0.4.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compute realized gains and model variance.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Why would setting the first quantity to the sign of its own future increment invalidate this calculation?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Realized gain is 3 × (−0.1) + 1 × 0.4 = 0.1. Variance is 9 × 0.2 + 1 × 0.8 = 2.6 under independent standard Brownian increments.

Part B 3 marks

The position is determined by the same future shock it multiplies. It is not predictable. The conditional mean-zero and cross-term arguments used for trading gains and the isometry no longer apply in this form.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can an option desk measure exposure lost by replacing a continuously changing hedge with a piecewise-constant hedge?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would express the mismatch in cash sensitivity to the Brownian driver. For a stock hedge that typically includes the number of missed shares times the underlying’s local euro volatility. I would integrate squared mismatch over time. In the stated example this gives €3.46 root-mean-square error. That measures the random diffusion component and leaves execution costs for a separate estimate.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the mismatch reverses sign halfway through, can the two periods cancel?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The realized gains may offset on a given path. Independent future increments still contribute positive expected squared error in each interval. Simultaneous hedges on correlated drivers can offset risk through covariance, which is a different calculation.
