Case 1 · 10 marks

Information and a self-financing record

Part A 4 marks

Find gains on the four paths and calculate their physical expectation.

First gains are +8 or −8 (1). Terminal gains for UU, UD, DU, DD are +14, +2, −6, −10 (2). Their equal-weight expectation is zero (1).

Part B 3 marks

After the first up move, compute the conditional expected terminal gain and explain how the third share is financed.

The two terminal outcomes average (14 + 2)/2 = 8 (1). The extra share costs 24 and must be paid from cash or permitted borrowing (1). Buying it at its current price makes no immediate trading gain (1).

Part C 3 marks

A rival changes the second holding to +3 before each second rise and −3 before each second fall. Diagnose the rule.

The two histories following the same first move are indistinguishable at decision time (1). The chosen position depends on the future increment (1), so the apparent sure gain of 6 on the second move cannot be earned from the stated filtration (1).

Case 2 · 10 marks

Volatility and a nonlinear payoff

Part A 4 marks

Derive f’s local drift and shock coefficient and X’s quadratic variation over a quarter year.

fₜ = 2, fₓ = 6, fₓₓ = 2 (1). Drift is 2 + 6 × 0.5 + ½ × 4 × 2 = 9 (1). Shock coefficient is 6 × 2 = 12 (1). Quadratic variation is 2² × 0.25 = 1 (1).

Part B 3 marks

Calculate the separate security’s one-year mean and median and explain their ordering.

Mean = 100exp(0.03) ≈ 103.05 (1). Median = 100exp(0.03 − 0.045) ≈ 98.51 (1). The lognormal distribution’s positive right tail raises its arithmetic mean (1).

Part C 3 marks

For the original X, give the exact quarter-year mean and standard deviation of its change, and explain why applying f’s initial drift over the whole period is only a local approximation.

The mean is 0.5 × 0.25 = 0.125 and standard deviation 2√0.25 = 1 (2). The drift of f includes 2X × 0.5 and therefore varies when X changes (1).

Case 3 · 10 marks

Replicating a payment and changing measure

Part A 4 marks

Construct and check its replicating portfolio and current value.

Delta is 15/(65 − 45) = 0.75 (1). Terminal cash is −0.75 × 45 = −33.75 and current borrowing 33.75/1.04 (1). Price is 37.50 − 33.75/1.04 ≈ 5.0481 (1). Terminal values are 15 and zero (1).

Part B 3 marks

Calculate the cash-measure up probability and use it to recover the price.

Cash-consistent expected stock is 52 (1). Solving 65q + 45(1 − q) = 52 gives q = 0.35 (1). 0.35 × 15/1.04 ≈ 5.0481 (1).

Part C 3 marks

Calculate the physical expected payoff and show how the likelihood ratio restores the market price.

Physical expectation is 0.55 × 15 = 8.25 (1). Up-state ratio is 0.35/0.55 (1). Weighting the positive payoff gives 0.55 × (0.35/0.55) × 15/1.04 ≈ 5.0481 (1).

Case 4 · 10 marks

The missing state exposure

Part A 3 marks

Derive all positive pricing weights consistent with cash and stock alone.

Normalization requires p₁ + p₂ + p₃ = 1 (1) and stock pricing requires 20p₁ + 30p₂ + 40p₃ = 30 (1). These imply p₁ = p₃ = a and p₂ = 1 − 2a with 0 < a < 0.5 (1).

Part B 3 marks

Give the no-arbitrage interval for the digital before the second contract is quoted and explain the strict endpoints.

The price is 6(1 − 2a) (1), ranging strictly between zero and six (1). At zero or six a state set with a strictly positive payoff advantage would receive zero price, inconsistent with equivalent positive weights (1).

Part C 4 marks

Determine the new state weights and digital price, and explain the role of the second contract.

3p₁ = 1.20 implies p₁ = 0.4, p₃ = 0.4 and p₂ = 0.2 (2). The middle digital now costs 6 × 0.2 = 1.2 (1). The new independently shaped payoff spans the missing state risk and pins down the weights (1).
