Professional workshop · Q03

Approve a quote and finance its hedge across both rebalancing dates

A client asks the desk to sell 100 cash-settled European calls, each on 100 shares. An internal pricing proposal uses the equity analyst’s 65% chance of an up move. Produce an independent model price, initial stock and cash instructions, and a contingent rehedging sheet. The client will pay €13.00 per share unit. The desk requires at least €0.10 per unit after an explicit €0.07 charge for estimated execution and funding frictions above the modeled cash rate. The supervisor must decide whether the order meets that criterion.

From inputs to decision

01

Map the liability before choosing probabilities

The cash liability and its option units determine the delta sign, terminal cash and hedge scale.

  1. The desk sells 10,000 share units. Terminal prices are €144, €96 and €64. The €100-strike call pays €44, zero and zero per unit.
  2. The mixed histories up-down and down-up recombine at €96. Record both histories for the rehedge ledger because the positions held before reaching €96 can differ.

Only two consecutive up moves create the terminal liability, which is then €440,000 for the order.

02

Recover the pricing weights independently of the forecast

The model must reproduce the price of its own stock and cash account before its derivative quote is credible.

  1. Solve 1.20q + 0.80(1 − q) = 1.02. The resulting pricing up weight is q = 0.55, between zero and one. The down weight is 0.45.
  2. The physical up weight 0.65 gives stock expected growth of 1.06 per step. It may represent an equity premium in the analyst’s forecast. Discounting a physical expected call at the cash rate would give 44 × 0.65²/1.02² ≈ €17.87, inconsistent with replication.

Use 0.55 for the no-arbitrage quote and retain 0.65 solely for physical scenario reporting.

03

Roll the terminal payments back two steps

A two-step rollback makes the component price and the contingent hedge on each node independently checkable.

  1. At the first up node the option is worth (0.55 × 44 + 0.45 × 0)/1.02 = €23.72549. At the down node both remaining terminal payoffs are zero, so its value there is zero.
  2. At inception the price is (0.55 × 23.72549)/1.02 = €12.79316 per share unit. Directly discounting 44 × 0.55² by 1.02² reproduces the same value.

The order’s frictionless replication value is €127,931.56 before rounding.

04

Issue time-zero hedge and funding instructions

A dealer short a call owes upside and buys positive delta to offset the exposure. Funding part of the stock purchase from the cash account ensures correct wealth at both next-step nodes.

  1. The stock quantity is (23.72549 − 0)/(120 − 80) = 0.5931373 per unit, or 5,931.37 shares. Its present cost is €59.31373 per unit.
  2. Cash is 12.79316 − 59.31373 = −€46.52057 per unit. Borrow approximately €465,205.69 for the full theoretical hedge. The cash balance grows in magnitude to €47.45098 per unit after one period.

Record a model stock purchase of 5,931.37 shares against model borrowing. An actual 5,931-share order leaves the combined book with roughly −0.37 share of residual delta to manage.

05

Write both node rebalances as self-financing transfers

A supervisor must see the source of cash for each trade. A price tree alone does not tell operations what to buy or repay.

  1. If stock reaches €120, raise delta to (44 − 0)/(144 − 96) = 0.916667. Buy 0.323529 share per unit at €120, financed by another €38.82353. The cash debit after trading is €86.27451 per unit.
  2. If stock reaches €80, the call is worthless in the model and requires delta zero. Sell 0.5931373 share at €80, receiving €47.45098 per unit, exactly enough to repay the accrued initial debt.

No external cash is needed after inception for the theoretical hedge. The actual whole-share program should reconcile residual cash separately.

06

Verify settlement and stress an off-tree price

A pure replication claim is credible only within the modeled terminal states. A gap or a different branch path can create a residual despite correct implementation.

  1. After first up, the cash liability grows to €88 per unit at expiry. At €144 the shares are worth €132, leaving €44. At €96 the shares are worth €88, leaving zero. After first down there is no modeled position or terminal payout.
  2. Stress an off-tree expiry price of €130 after the first up, with the same intervening holding. Hedge wealth is (11/12) × 130 − 88 = €31.1667. The call pays €30, leaving +€1.1667 per unit. At €160 the same hedge leaves €58.6667 against a €60 payoff, a shortfall of €1.3333.

Archive both modeled settlement checks and the uncovered extreme-up move. The latter is a model-risk scenario that the simple tree cannot price uniquely.

07

Decide against the quote hurdle

Commercial acceptance depends on client price, model value, transaction reserve and required margin expressed in the same units.

  1. Gross spread is 13.00 − 12.793156 = €0.206844 per unit. Deducting €0.07 leaves approximately €0.136844 per unit, or €1,368.44 across the order.
  2. The retained margin exceeds the €0.10 hurdle by €0.036844 per unit, or €368.44 for the order. If estimated friction instead reaches €0.12, retained margin becomes €0.086844 and fails the rule.

Approve the €13 indicative bid under the supplied cost and model assumptions, conditional on executable stock and funding quotes, and escalate material off-tree risk.

Completed document

Indicative trade approval and hedge instruction

Decision

Accept €13.00 per unit for the proposed 10,000 units on the supplied terms, subject to the normal quote checks and supervisor review of discrete-model exposure. The retained modeled margin is €1,368.44 after the €700 implementation allowance and €368.44 above the stated minimum.

Pricing evidence

The two independent pricing routes both give €12.79316 per unit. Replacing the model weight of 55% with the equity analyst’s 65% forecast gives €17.87 and fails the traded-stock replication check. Keep the analyst estimate available for scenario planning only.

Execution and funding

Target 5,931.37 shares and approximately €465,205.69 borrowing in the ideal hedge. If filled to 5,931 shares, tag and track the fractional deviation. At a €120 first node increase the model holding to 9,166.67 shares across the order. At €80 liquidate the shares and repay the debit.

Risk boundary and review

The tree’s on-grid liability is reproduced at every node. An off-tree jump from €120 to €160 leaves about €13,333 of uncovered liability on the order with the last delta unchanged. Seek a revised volatility, gap and liquidity review if live prices, funding spreads or execution conditions depart materially from the assumptions.
