Diffusion motion and pricing measures

Pathwise variation and hedge limitations

Learners compute quadratic variation, use a self-financing hedge and separate a replicable payoff from an unspanned one. An existing jump example measures a jump's squared contribution to variation. The two extensions turn such path features into contract prices and risk checks.

New and expanded lessons

Q03.11

Price a jump exposure without double-counting its drift

Q03.04 counts a jump in quadratic variation and Q03.10 illustrates incomplete replication in a finite-state market. This chapter constructs a Poisson arrival model, compensates its drift under a pricing measure and prices a nonlinear claim by conditioning on the jump count.

  • Convert an arrival intensity and mean jump into a compensating risk-neutral drift.
  • Price a contingent payoff from jump-count probabilities and use the discounted stock to check the sign.

Q03.12

Recover barrier crossings hidden between simulation dates

Q03.06 notes that terminal and first-passage events differ. This chapter obtains the conditional crossing probability by reflection, uses it to weight a surviving payoff, and distinguishes continuous monitoring from the actual observation schedule in a contract.

  • Derive the conditional probability that a Brownian bridge crosses a fixed lower barrier.
  • Decide when to apply a survival correction and calculate a discounted path contribution.

Public benchmarks

ETH Zürich and University of Zurich, Switzerland

MSc UZH ETH in Quantitative Finance, Numerical Methods for Finance

The syllabus extends Wiener models to Lévy processes and treats barrier-type contracts, incomplete markets and valuation equations with an integral term for jumps.

Columbia University, United States, Martin Haugh's IEOR E4707 lectures

Local Volatility, Stochastic Volatility and Jump-Diffusion Models

The graduate financial-engineering lecture contrasts volatility diffusions with discontinuous models and develops compound Poisson jumps, jump-diffusion pricing and model incompleteness.

University of Oxford, United Kingdom

Michael B. Giles, Numerical Methods II, Monte Carlo lecture 10

The lecture uses reflection of a Brownian path and the ratio of endpoint densities to obtain a conditional barrier-crossing probability between observation dates.
