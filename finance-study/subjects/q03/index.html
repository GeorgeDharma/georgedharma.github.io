Q03Quantitative finance

Stochastic Calculus and Continuous-Time Asset Pricing

A randomly moving price does not follow ordinary calculus at very short intervals. Continuous-time finance builds a mathematical language for those movements and for trading against them.

Subject worked example

Why log growth differs

Suppose a share follows geometric Brownian motion with annual drift μ = 8% and volatility σ = 20%, with time in years.

Show the working

Itô’s formula gives drift of log price μ − σ²/2 = 0.08 − 0.20²/2 = 6% per year. This is a model expectation for log growth, while the price path remains random.

Course overview

What you will learn

Work with Brownian increments, apply Itô’s formula and derive a replication or martingale pricing result under stated market assumptions.

Theory and assumptions

Brownian motion, stochastic integration, martingales and risk-neutral pricing. Replication links an attainable payoff to a price. Completeness determines whether every relevant payoff has a replicating portfolio and hence a unique replication price.
