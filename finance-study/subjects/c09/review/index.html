New and expanded lessons

C09.01

Going public and choosing a listing route

The private-equity course compared exit routes. It did not teach how an issuer tests whether a particular route supplies enough new cash.

  • Separate the company’s need for cash from its owners’ wish to sell.
  • Compare an underwritten IPO, a direct listing, a SPAC combination and a private financing on committed cash and control.

C09.02

Getting listed and reading a prospectus

The classroom discussed established trading venues and deal due diligence. It lacked a worked admission timetable and a way to read an offering document as a prospective investor.

  • Distinguish regulator, exchange, issuer, auditor and bookrunner.
  • Build an IPO readiness timetable and reconcile a prospectus cover to its use-of-proceeds section.

C09.03

Primary shares, secondary sales and ownership dilution

The venture course covered financing-round dilution. It did not reconcile a mixed public offering, selling-shareholder fees and the issuer’s closing cash.

  • Reconcile each share sold to the issuer or an existing seller and calculate net proceeds.
  • Separate economic ownership, trading float, voting power and accounting book-value dilution.

C09.04

Valuing the issuer and setting an offer range

Existing equity valuation explained enterprise-to-equity bridges. It did not derive the minimum offer price implied by a simultaneous cash target and dilution cap.

  • Translate enterprise-value multiples into comparable per-share values on a consistent pre-financing basis.
  • Find prices at which cash and ownership constraints can both be met.

C09.05

Underwriting, building the order book and allocating shares

Market microstructure covered orders in a trading venue. It did not build an issuance order book or compare a discretionary price with an auction clearing price.

  • Build genuine demand at each candidate price using limit bids.
  • Explain why a discretionary IPO price can differ from a uniform-price clearing calculation.

C09.06

IPO underpricing, the winner’s curse and money left on the table

Existing adverse-selection lessons addressed lending and secondary trading. The IPO allocation-conditioned return and seller opportunity-cost calculation were absent.

  • Measure the offer-to-close return and show who bears the apparent price concession.
  • Derive the expected break-even offer for a subscriber facing different fill rates.

C09.07

IPO overpricing, market timing and investment strategy

Event studies and comparable valuation appeared separately. No lesson showed how a first-day gain can coexist with a rich offer valuation or a loss to a secondary buyer.

  • Test first-day pricing, valuation relative to peers and later investor performance separately.
  • Recommend an issuer decision or a subscription limit using explicit demand, value and risk assumptions.

C09.08

Stabilization, the greenshoe and the aftermarket

The options course taught call payoffs. The IPO-specific stock loan, short covering, optional sales and end of the lock-up period were not traced.

  • Trace the borrowed shares and cash in a covered over-allotment and both ways the banks can close it.
  • Distinguish the greenshoe from a lock-up or an agreement to guarantee a share price.

C09.09

Rights issues, follow-ons and block placements

Payout and venture capitalization were covered, but a renounceable rights issue, ex-rights price and alternatives for an already-listed issuer were not worked through.

  • Reconcile equity wealth for an existing holder who exercises or sells transferable rights.
  • Choose among a rights issue, a marketed follow-on, a primary placement and a shareholder block sale.

C09.10

Originating a bond and pricing the new-issue concession

The fixed-income subject priced existing promises and the treasury subject compared funding. Neither took an issuer from a new bond mandate through its syndicate book to net settlement cash.

  • Infer a new-issue yield from a matched curve and explain the price below or above par.
  • Prepare a pricing wire reconciling benchmark, spread, coupon, issue price, fees and net proceeds.

C09.11

Issuing convertibles and tracing possible dilution

Plain-bond pricing and option valuation appeared in different courses. The issuer had no worked comparison linking a convertible’s cash service, option and contingent share count.

  • Calculate a conversion ratio, conversion value, premium and modeled straight-bond floor.
  • Compare cash redemption with the equity dilution from conversion, and identify the issuer’s refinancing risk.

C09.12

Post-listing strategy, share repurchases and going private

Payout policy and private-equity exits were covered, but the curriculum did not link public ownership, a cash buyback and the separate decision to withdraw a listing.

  • Determine whether a repurchase creates per-share value and how it changes remaining owners’ voting percentages.
  • Distinguish an issuer buyback, a shareholder tender and exchange delisting.

Public benchmarks

Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur

Investment Banking and Capital Market Operations · course proposal

Explicitly groups IPO motives, underwriting, greenshoes, underpricing, rights and follow-ons, buybacks and bond issues into equity and debt capital markets. Used as a subject-coverage benchmark.

University of Oxford · Faculty of Law

Capital Raising and Finance

Examines the financing needs of companies and the legal structure of equity and debt fundraising. Used to compare financing routes and investor protections.

University of Florida · Ritter and Welch

A Review of IPO Activity, Pricing, and Allocations

Distinguishes going-public motives, initial returns, allocations and long-run returns, and discusses competing explanations and the sensitivity of empirical evidence.

NYU Stern · Alexander Ljungqvist, hosted by Dartmouth Tuck

IPO Underpricing

Compares asymmetric-information, institutional, ownership and behavioral explanations for IPO discounts and the evidence used to distinguish them.

Journal of Financial Economics · Kevin Rock

Why new issues are underpriced

Derives adverse selection in allotments when informed investors participate selectively. Grounds the teaching case on conditional fills.

Journal of Financial Economics · Benveniste and Spindt

How investment bankers determine the offer price and allocation of new issues

Models how pricing and allocation can induce investors to reveal information during bookbuilding.

Review of Financial Studies · Purnanandam and Swaminathan

Are IPOs Really Underpriced?

Compares IPO valuations with matched firms, providing the contrast between relative fundamental valuation and a positive initial return.

Euronext

IPO journey

Describes the parallel marketing and documentation tracks, due diligence, investor interactions and the path to pricing.

Euronext

Choosing advisors

Distinguishes the bank, counsel, auditors, listing sponsor and adviser, including venue-specific sponsor requirements.

US Securities and Exchange Commission

Types of Registered Offerings

Compares a traditional IPO, merger with a SPAC and a direct listing from a private issuer’s perspective.

New York Stock Exchange

Direct Listings

Distinguishes existing-holder sales from NYSE direct listings with a primary capital raise in the opening auction.

US SEC · Office of Investor Education and Advocacy

Investor Bulletin · Investing in an IPO

Explains registration documents, public offering terms, shares, pricing, underwriting, stabilizing transactions, and the information available to subscribers.

US SEC · Investor.gov

Initial Public Offerings · Lockup Agreements

Explains contractual limits on insider sales and why investors should check each specific prospectus.

Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing

Main Board new applicants · forms and checklists

Lists Main Board new-listing application, sponsor, marketing, distribution and placee documentation.

Indonesia Stock Exchange

How to IPO

Distinguishes preparation, the IDX listing process, registration with OJK, a public offering and trading on the exchange.

International Capital Market Association · distributed by the Investment Association

ICMA Primary Market Handbook extract · pre-sounding, bookbuilding and allocations

Describes investor meetings, order books and allocation in the European cross-border syndicated institutional bond market. An institutional example, not a universal market rule.

US SEC · Investor.gov

Convertible Securities

Defines contractual conversion into another security and explains terms which can change dilution.

Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing

Main Board Listing Rule 6.11 · withdrawal of listing

Illustrates that exchange withdrawal is subject to separate procedural requirements that depend on the specific venue and circumstances.

Google Inc. · SEC EDGAR

Final IPO prospectus, 18 August 2004

Supplies base primary and secondary share counts, price, underwriting terms, voting provisions, auction terms and maximum additional existing shares.

Google Inc. · SEC EDGAR

November 2004 offering circular · account of final IPO shares sold

Confirms the number of additional shares actually purchased from selling stockholders after the base IPO.

Los Angeles Times · contemporary market report

Google Shares Surge in Debut, 20 August 2004

Records the original first Nasdaq close, on the same pre-split basis as the 2004 offering documents.

IFRS Interpretations Committee

IAS 32 · transaction costs in an equity offering and a listing

The committee distinguishes directly attributable incremental costs of issuing equity, costs of listing existing shares and costs that require a joint allocation.

US Securities and Exchange Commission

Private placements under Rule 506(b)

Distinguishes the requirements for this US private-offering route, including restrictions on solicitation and resale.

European Securities and Markets Authority

EU Prospectus Regulation · Article 1, scope and exemptions

Distinguishes exemptions from the offer prospectus obligation, including offers solely to qualified investors, from admission-to-trading provisions.
