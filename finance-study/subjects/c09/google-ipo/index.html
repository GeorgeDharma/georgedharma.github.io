Historical case · United States, August 2004

Reconstruct Google’s IPO

A headline can tell you the size of an offering and still leave out who received the cash. Use the auction, the prospectus cover and the first close to reconstruct the transaction.

Read the base offer

The offering priced on 18 August. Class A shares began trading on Nasdaq on 19 August. The amounts below use the original 2004 share basis. Mixing these prices with a later split-adjusted price would distort the return.

Shares issued by Google14,142,135New shares. The company receives the proceeds.

Why use an auction?

Google solicited bids stating a price and quantity. A clearing-price calculation asks which price has enough demand for the available shares. The prospectus gave Google and its underwriters discretion over the final price, including pricing below the clearing level. Investors could receive a partial allocation. A bid limit is therefore a maximum willingness to pay, and an indication of demand can differ from the price ultimately agreed.

Auction information helps an issuer discover demand for shares. Measuring fundamental value still requires a view of future cash flows, risk and the outstanding claims. Demand and intrinsic value answer different questions.

Reconcile the proceeds

At $85 per share, the primary leg raised 14,142,135 × $85 = $1,202,081,475 gross. The selling holders’ 5,462,917 shares brought $464,347,945 gross. Add those legs to recover the $1,666,429,420 base offer.

Base public purchase price$1,666,429,420

Proceeds to selling holders after the underwriting discount$451,324,897

The two proceeds lines and the reported commission reconcile to within $1 of the cover total because the components are reported in whole dollars. The company’s operating cash calculation must also consider its other offering expenses. A secondary sale adds trading supply without adding company funds.

Measure the first-day result

Initial return = ($100.34 − $85.00) / $85.00 = 18.05%.

A subscriber allocated 100 shares paid $8,500. Their position was worth $10,034 at the close, a $1,534 paper gain before costs. Someone who bought during trading had a different entry price. The new company cash calculated above stayed fixed after settlement.

Multiplying the $15.34 difference by the 19,605,052 base shares yields $300,741,497.68 of descriptive “money left on the table”. Of this, $216,940,350.90 relates to primary shares and $83,801,146.78 to selling holders. This comparison uses a subsequent market price for a limited float. It does not establish that every share in the original deal could have been sold at $100.34.

Final prospectus, cover and auction procedure · Contemporaneous first-close report

Who supplied the overallotment shares?

The selling holders authorized an option on up to 2,940,757 additional existing shares. A later filing says that 2,929,626 were actually taken, leaving 11,131 of the maximum unused. The total ultimately sold was 19,605,052 + 2,929,626 = 22,534,678 shares. The primary share count stayed 14,142,135. Exercise provided additional seller proceeds and no additional issuer cash.

Prepare the analyst’s answer

A colleague says, “Google raised $1.67bn and the auction undervalued the business by 18%.” Reconcile the cash, define the benchmark behind 18%, and name the extra evidence you would need to make the valuation claim.

Compare with the worked answer

The base offering had $1.666bn of public purchases. Google’s gross primary proceeds were $1.202bn and proceeds after the underwriting discount were $1.168bn before other issuer expenses. The 18.05% is the offer-to-first-close return earned on an allocated share. To judge over- or undervaluation against fundamentals, build a contemporaneous per-share estimate or a properly adjusted peer comparison, state the uncertainty range, and keep market movements and allocations separate.
