Professional workshop · C09

Price the flotation of Marais Instruments

Marais Instruments needs to fund its next production line, while its founder wants a partial exit. Four prices are being discussed. The CFO wants one executable recommendation, a cash and ownership reconciliation, a downside instruction and a reason to postpone if the book moves before signing.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Equity capital markets associate advising the issuer and the board’s pricing committee
What to submit
A pricing-committee memorandum, a share-allocation schedule and a closing funds flow, including a decision for the issuer and the selling shareholder.

From inputs to decision

01

Write down the non-negotiable funding tests

The bank, company and selling family have different objectives. Convert each into an amount before discussing the highest price.

  1. Maximum new shares are 20 × 0.20/0.80 = 5m. At that size, old shareholders collectively retain 20/25 = 80% before transfers to new buyers.
  2. Issuer cash is 5 × P × 0.96 − 2. The €48m minimum requires P ≥ €10.4167. With 2m shares for sale, founder cash requires P ≥ 18/(2 × 0.96) = €9.375.

The issuer sets the tighter cash floor. The bank still needs a supported price and 7m buyers.

02

Rebuild the book at each candidate price

A headline order count combines orders with different limits. Count a buyer only while the price stays within that buyer’s limit.

  1. At €12 only A is eligible, giving 2.6m. At €11 A, B and C provide 2.6 + 2.4 + 1.9 = 6.9m.
  2. At €10.50 D joins for total demand 8.8m. At €10 E joins, giving 11.5m. Reintroducing the duplicate E order would overstate only prices at which E qualifies.

After the 1m stress, demand is respectively 1.6m, 5.9m, 7.8m and 10.5m. Compare against 7m shares.

03

Choose the clearing terms the board can defend

A signed recommendation must pass every board constraint simultaneously.

  1. At €10 the deal has investors but nets only 5 × 10 × 0.96 − 2 = €46m, €2m below target. At €11 and €12 the book cannot cover 7m even before the stress.
  2. At €10.50 net issuer cash is €48.40m. Net founder cash is €20.16m. Base coverage is 8.8/7 = 1.257×, and stressed coverage is 7.8/7 = 1.114×.

Recommend €10.50, 5m primary and 2m secondary. Its €0.40m issuer headroom is thin.

04

Reconcile the cash and closing capitalization

Independently add recipients of all subscriber cash and match the total to price times issued and sold shares.

  1. Gross public consideration is 7 × 10.50 = €73.50m. Issuer gross €52.50m less €2.10m bank fee and €2m other costs = €48.40m. Founder gross €21m less €0.84m = €20.16m.
  2. Recipients check: €48.40m + €20.16m + €2.94m bank fees + €2m other expenses = €73.50m. Only 5m shares are new. The founder retains (12 − 2)/25 = 40%. Remaining old holders have 8/25 = 32%. New buyers own 7/25 = 28%.

Market cap at the offer is €262.50m. Document the 28% offered holding as a case outcome; ownership classifications would be needed to establish formal free float.

05

Write an allocation that can actually settle

Discretionary allocation can manage concentration and avoid allocating ineligible orders. Maintain a fair process under the applicable rules.

  1. An illustrative base allocation is A 2m, B 2m, C 1.6m and D 1.4m, with E zero. The sum is 7m and every allocation is within the submitted order at €10.50.
  2. If A cuts its original order by 1m, A can take only 1.6m. Reassign the 0.4m difference to B, whose limit permits the price and whose order is 2.4m. A 1.6 + B 2.4 + C 1.6 + D 1.4 = 7m. Actual confirmation of investor participation is still required.

Record eligibility, final allocations and the changes. A booked but unconfirmed sale is not settlement cash.

06

Quantify initial returns and the optional sale

The board should understand price performance and whose cash could change if the syndicate covers a short.

  1. At €11.55 first close the allotted return is (11.55 − 10.50)/10.50 = 10%. Descriptive money left on the table is €7.35m, of which 5 × 1.05 = €5.25m is attributable to the new shares and €2.10m to founder sales.
  2. At €9.80 the initial return is −6.67%. If the banks had sold and borrowed 0.7m extra, buying those in the market costs €6.86m against €7.35m short-sale cash, a gross €0.49m difference before costs if no option is exercised. At €11.55, covering via the founder’s option on the stated €10.50 basis instead costs €7.35m.

The option covers existing founder shares. Neither exercise nor a market purchase adds to the issuer’s base €48.40m.

07

Set the contingency and communicate it

A credible pricing decision names the action if price, cost or demand changes before the contracts become binding.

  1. At €11 the 1m stress leaves only 5.9m shares of buyers. With 5m primary, at most 0.9m secondary can be sold. The founder would net at most 0.9 × 11 × 0.96 = €9.504m, below its €18m floor.
  2. If the eligible book at €10.50 falls below 8m shares, stress coverage drops below one. If issuer-only costs rise above €2.4m, available cash drops below €48m. Seek a revised commitment, alter owner objectives by board agreement, or postpone.

Deliver the signed committee terms, final share/cash schedules and instructions to re-open the decision if either threshold fails.

Completed document

Pricing committee decision · Marais Instruments

Authority requested

Approve €10.50 for 5m new shares and 2m secondary shares, subject to refreshed investor confirmations and routine closing conditions. Preserve the €48m cash floor and 20% primary-dilution cap.

Cash, ownership and use

The production line receives €48.40m net issuer funds. The founder receives €20.16m and retains 40% of 25m shares. The €73.50m headline includes both parties and €4.94m of banking and other expenses.

Book quality and downside

Demand of 8.8m falls to 7.8m after the specified 1m withdrawal, covering 7m offered. At €11 coverage fails. A €9.80 first close would generate a 6.67% initial loss for a subscriber at €10.50 and would leave contracted issuer cash unaffected after settlement.

Execution conditions

Return to committee for a withdrawal that takes the eligible book below 8m, issuer-only costs above €2.4m, or a change of seller for the optional 0.7m shares. Treasury will confirm cash receipt before committing factory funds.

The issuer’s fixed expenses rise to €2.9m. At €10.50 and the original 5m new shares, how far below the €48m net target is the company?

Task 2

At €11, with 5m primary and unchanged orders, how many secondary shares can be sold while preserving the 1m stress buffer?

See the full solution

Eligible demand is 6.9m. Subtract 1m stressed withdrawal and 5m primary. Maximum secondary quantity is 0.9m. The value and funding constraints are assessed separately.

Task 3

The IPO closes its first day at €11.13. What is the descriptive money left on the table attributable to the selling founder in the base offer? Exclude optional shares.

See the full solution

The difference is €11.13 − €10.50 = €0.63 per share. Multiply by 2m existing founder shares to get €1.26m. The issuer’s separate component is €3.15m.

Academic and professional references
  • Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur · Investment Banking and Capital Market Operations · course proposal

    Explicitly groups IPO motives, underwriting, greenshoes, underpricing, rights and follow-ons, buybacks and bond issues into equity and debt capital markets. Used as a subject-coverage benchmark.

  • Euronext · IPO journey

    Describes the parallel marketing and documentation tracks, due diligence, investor interactions and the path to pricing.

  • University of Florida · Ritter and Welch · A Review of IPO Activity, Pricing, and Allocations

    Distinguishes going-public motives, initial returns, allocations and long-run returns, and discusses competing explanations and the sensitivity of empirical evidence.

  • US SEC · Office of Investor Education and Advocacy · Investor Bulletin · Investing in an IPO

    Explains registration documents, public offering terms, shares, pricing, underwriting, stabilizing transactions, and the information available to subscribers.

  • Journal of Financial Economics · Benveniste and Spindt · How investment bankers determine the offer price and allocation of new issues

    Models how pricing and allocation can induce investors to reveal information during bookbuilding.
