Theory and terminology

Price discovery by bookbuilding and uniform-price auctions

IPO bookbuilding · book runner · bookrunner · underwriting · auction · best efforts · IPO allocation · partial adjustment

What you should be able to explain

  • Build genuine demand at each candidate price using limit bids.
  • Explain why a discretionary IPO price can differ from a uniform-price clearing calculation.

Who takes distribution risk?

Under a firm-commitment arrangement an underwriting group agrees on identified terms to buy a block and resell it. A best-efforts intermediary seeks buyers under the mandate without the same commitment to take unsold stock. From the issuer’s perspective the important dates are when the risk transfers and which conditions permit cancellation. A mandate signed months earlier may commit a bank to run a process without underwriting a given price.

The lead bookrunner collects orders. Other syndicate members may bring overlapping investors. Discretionary bookbuilding seeks information about demand and price sensitivity, then uses it in a pricing discussion. An auction uses specified rules on accepted orders, price and allocation. A fixed-price subscription may set the price before demand is completely known and ration shares if oversubscribed.

Build a demand curve from limits

A limit bid of €12 is a maximum willingness to pay. It participates at €12 and below. Demand at €11 therefore includes all orders limited at €12 plus those willing to pay €11. Define whether entries are separate quantities or parts of a price ladder for one investor. An account might express 2m at €11 or 3m at €10 rather than submit two independent orders. Adding both would overstate the book.

For a uniform-price classroom auction, sort each independent bid from highest to lowest and accumulate shares until supply is reached. The marginal accepted bid is the clearing price under the stated rules. All accepted shares pay that common price. A real prospectus determines whether the issuer can price lower and how tied orders are treated. Google’s final prospectus describes a modified auction with pricing discretion.

An order book is also a concentration report

Show the largest investors, price limits and order quality at each price. Distinguish nonbinding interest from binding commitments. A high headline subscription multiple can disappear after cleaning one duplicate. Reducing the issue size might increase measured coverage while missing a cash requirement. Agree the allocation objective and document allocation decisions under the applicable rules.

Method

Build the result

D(p) = sum of eligible order quantities with limit ≥ p. Coverage(p) = D(p) / shares offered.

p is the tested offer price. D is cumulative demand in shares after deduplication and any stated filters. A 1.25× coverage ratio means demand for 25% more shares than available at that price.

  1. Validate investor identity, size and limits. Convert all orders to shares or convert currency orders at each candidate price.
  2. At each price retain only orders willing to pay it and sum their eligible quantities. Compare the result with supply.
  3. For a stated uniform auction use the highest clearing price. Under discretionary bookbuilding explain the price and the allocation separately.
Worked case

Price 10m shares with four independent bids

Orders in millions of shares are A 4 at €13, B 3 at €12, C 5 at €11 and D 6 at €10. Supply is 10m shares. For this example higher bids fill first, the marginal bid gets the residual, and every accepted bid pays the marginal price.

Working

  1. At €13 demand is 4m. At €12 it is 4 + 3 = 7m, leaving 3m unsold.
  2. At €11 demand is 4 + 3 + 5 = 12m. The highest fully covered price is €11 with 1.20× coverage.
  3. Fill A 4m, B 3m and C 3m. C receives 3/5 = 60% of its requested quantity. D receives zero. The allocations sum to 10m.
  4. Gross consideration is €110m. At €10 headline demand would be 18m, or 1.80×, but selling all 10m at €10 yields only €100m.

Decision and interpretation

In the defined auction €11 follows the rule. A bookbuilt issuer may have price discretion. A claim that €12 is executable needs at least 3m new eligible shares at €12.

A limit is a maximum acceptable price. The allocation policy and available shares determine the fill.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Investor B withdraws before the book closes. Keep supply at 10m. Rebuild the clearing price, marginal allocation and gross proceeds.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Demand is 4m at €13 and remains 4m at €12. At €11 it reaches 4 + 5 = 9m, still short.
  2. At €10 demand reaches 15m. Fill A 4m and C 5m, then 1m of D’s 6m. D’s fill rate is 1/6 = 16.67%.
  3. The uniform price is €10. Proceeds fall from €110m to €100m. Reducing size to 9m could clear at €11, giving €99m instead.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A bookrunner says a deal is twice covered. Why might you still price below the top of the range?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask at which price it is twice covered, whether orders are duplicated, their price limits, and how much survives a withdrawal by a large investor. A discretionary book also gives weight to order quality, concentration and aftermarket execution. A clearing price estimated from expressions of interest can exceed the highest price at which enough firm orders will settle.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can an issuer insist that all successful investors pay their own limit and immediately raise more cash?

Compare with the follow-up answer

That changes the auction rule. Investors would have an incentive to shade bids, and orders solicited under a uniform-price rule cannot be treated as unchanged willingness to pay under a pay-as-bid rule. Compare resulting strategic bids and the actual disclosed terms.
