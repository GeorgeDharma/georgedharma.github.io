Theory and terminology

Admission to trading, offering disclosure and issuer due diligence

listing requirements · listing sponsor · prospectus · S-1 · IPO process · Euronext · IDX · HKEX

What you should be able to explain

  • Distinguish regulator, exchange, issuer, auditor and bookrunner.
  • Build an IPO readiness timetable and reconcile a prospectus cover to its use-of-proceeds section.

Read the offering document in a useful order

The cover identifies the share class, issue price or indicative range, who sells, and the banks’ economics. The use-of-proceeds section explains how the issuer intends to use its cash. Capitalization and dilution tables show the claims investors buy. Risk factors, the business description, management discussion and financial statements give context to forecast growth, customer dependence and working capital. Voting provisions and selling-holder tables explain who controls decisions and who can exit.

Reconcile copies and dates. A preliminary or draft document can have an indicative range and no final allocation. Look for the final price and share count in the definitive document or pricing announcement. Check whether an additional allotment is new stock or an existing owner’s stock. Exchange permission and securities review address eligibility and disclosure. They provide no assurance of a profitable investment.

Two tracks run in parallel

The documentation track prepares accounts, legal verification, board approvals and a draft offering document, responds to review and completes admission formalities. The marketing track develops the equity story, tests investor interest where permitted, presents to investors and builds a book. Confidentiality and marketing rules matter at every step. At pricing the board and banks can reduce size, change price or postpone within the applicable arrangements. Settlement and commencement of trading have their own dates.

The issuer owns its disclosures. Accountants test financial statements and perform agreed procedures. Counsel reviews the applicable rules and representations. A sponsor or listing adviser assists with eligibility on markets requiring one. The bookrunner collects orders and manages distribution. A transfer agent, registry, depository and the relevant intermediaries must deliver the right shares against payment.

Read the rules for the actual venue

A US domestic registration often uses Form S-1, with a prospectus, alongside an exchange’s separate listing standards. Euronext describes concurrent documentation and marketing work. Its Growth and Access markets require a listing sponsor or adviser under the applicable rules. HKEX publishes Main Board application, sponsor declaration and placing documentation. Indonesia’s IDX describes the listing application and public-offering steps involving OJK. Companies comparing Paris, Hong Kong and Jakarta should check the chosen board, issuer type and live rulebook. A threshold quoted for one market can answer the wrong question elsewhere.

Method

Build the result

Earliest launch = latest completion among all required pre-launch paths. Earliest settlement = launch + book period + closing interval.

A path is an ordered set of dependent tasks. Durations below use the same business-day calendar. Independent paths can run simultaneously. “Launch” in this exercise means opening the live order book after all specified gates.

  1. List the evidence each gate requires, including information needed for disclosure and operating readiness.
  2. Sum the durations within each dependent path. Take the maximum across concurrent paths at a joining gate.
  3. Add genuinely sequential post-launch tasks and compare the result with cash runway. Verify holidays and contingency funding before using actual dates.
Worked case

Can the issuer fund day 24?

At day zero, financial preparation takes 8 business days and then disclosure review takes 10. Independent listing work takes 15, and premarketing preparation takes 12. The order book needs 4 days and settlement another 2. A €9m payment falls due at the end of day 24. There is €3m cash and no other agreed funding.

Working

  1. The financial path completes on day 18. The exchange work completes on day 15 and the marketing preparation on day 12.
  2. The last prerequisite finishes on day max(18,15,12) = 18. Adding four book days gives pricing at the end of day 22.
  3. Adding two settlement days gives day 24. An announcement on day 22 would still leave only €3m before settlement.
  4. A three-day extension to financial review pushes settlement to day 27. The day-24 payment gap is 9 − 3 = €6m.

Decision and interpretation

The base timetable has zero time buffer. Secure at least €6m of suitably timed fallback funding or move the payment. Even day-24 settlement requires the cash to arrive before the payment cut-off.

Quick check
A preliminary prospectus discloses an indicative range. Which document or event establishes the final offer price for your proceeds analysis?

The price used to solicit interest and the final contractual sale price serve different purposes. Match price and number of shares to the same dated terms.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

The exchange work is revised to 21 days, with the other base assumptions unchanged. What settles when? How much would a two-day reduction in disclosure review save?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The three gate dates are now 18, 21 and 12. The exchange path determines day-21 launch.
  2. Add four and two days. Settlement is day 27, leaving a €6m gap on day 24 without a bridge.
  3. Review falling from ten days to eight shortens the financial path to 16. Exchange work still sets launch at 21, so it saves no settlement time.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Management says that the auditors have signed the accounts, so the shares can start trading on Monday. What else has to happen?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Signed accounts are one input. I would check the offering documents, required regulatory steps, exchange admission, distribution arrangements and settlement operations for the chosen venue. I would map which tasks depend on each other, who owns them, and whether updated financial information changes the timetable. The issuer and its advisers still need an executable funding plan.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A final prospectus shows a larger total offer but unchanged net proceeds to the company. Is that a contradiction?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Check the selling-holder and fees tables. Existing owners may have increased their sale, or the larger primary amount may have been absorbed by changed fees or expenses. Reconcile the issuer column separately from the total public purchase price.
