Post-listing strategy, share repurchases and going private

Theory and terminology

Cash and ownership effects of repurchases, take-private offers and delisting

share buyback · share repurchase · tender offer · voluntary delisting · public to private · share count · IPO exit strategies

What you should be able to explain

  • Determine whether a repurchase creates per-share value and how it changes remaining owners’ voting percentages.
  • Distinguish an issuer buyback, a shareholder tender and exchange delisting.

What changes after the opening bell?

Public issuers communicate results, disclose material information under relevant rules, manage investor relations and maintain eligibility. An IPO can be the start of repeated access to equity or debt markets. A locked-up founder needing later liquidity may sell stock in a secondary placement or a later marketed offering. Where the issuer simultaneously raises new money, report the primary and secondary components separately.

The board may choose a regular dividend, an open-market repurchase or a tender specifying volume and price. A dividend pays participating shareholders according to their entitlement. A repurchase pays selling holders and can increase the stake of holders who do not sell. Availability of cash, leverage covenants, liquidity needs and conduct rules constrain the choice.

An EPS increase can conceal an economic transfer

Calculate the pre-transaction value of equity including surplus cash. When repurchasing, subtract the cash consideration and fees from that value and divide by remaining shares. This is a conditional valuation before signaling, tax and business changes. Paying above estimated value transfers some value from non-sellers to sellers. If management has better information and the estimate is wrong, the economic conclusion can reverse.

For earnings per share (EPS) separately deduct lost after-tax interest on cash spent or the cost of new acquisition debt. Use weighted-average shares for actual period financial reporting. A simple full-year pro forma calculation assumes the buyback took place at the start of the period.

Going private and delisting

A controlling holder or external buyer can acquire public shares with its own funds. That money can go to the public sellers without reducing issuer cash. A company can also repurchase or cancel shares under an authorized mechanism. Removal from an exchange is a distinct legal and market event. It can harm the liquidity of remaining minority holders. It does not automatically redeem their shares or extinguish reporting obligations.

A board paper should distinguish the offer, who funds it, approvals and minority protections, the route to exchange withdrawal, and continuing disclosure obligations. HKEX publishes specific withdrawal and takeover-related rules. Other venues set their own conditions. Do not carry one jurisdiction’s vote or squeeze-out threshold into another transaction.

Method

Build the result

Post-buyback economic value per continuing share = (pre-buyback equity value − cash spent − fees)/(old shares − shares retired).

Equity value includes the cash before the repurchase. Shares retired leave the denominator. EPS is after-tax earnings divided by period-weighted ordinary shares and is a separate accounting measure.

  1. Build a pre-buyback per-share value from operating assets and net financial claims, including surplus cash once.
  2. Subtract the actual payment to sellers from total equity, adjust any financing or operating consequences, and divide by shares still outstanding.
  3. Reconcile the transfer to sellers with the change for non-sellers. Calculate EPS from its own earnings and timing assumptions.
Worked case

Higher EPS, lower value for a continuing owner

A company is worth €100m including €25m surplus cash and has 10m shares. It retires 1m shares for €12 each at the start of the year. It expects €8m earnings before the buyback, and cash spent would have earned 2% after tax. Ignore fees, signaling, tax changes and other effects.

Working

  1. Initial value per share is €100m/10m = €10. Spending €12m leaves €88m for 9m remaining shares.
  2. Post-buyback value is €9.7778 per continuing share. Across 9m continuing shares the loss is €2m, equal to the €2m premium paid to retiring holders.
  3. Old EPS is 8/10 = €0.80. Lost cash interest is 12 × 2% = €0.24m. New pro forma EPS is (8 − 0.24)/9 = €0.8622.
  4. An owner keeping 1m shares rises from 10% to 11.11% ownership. The modeled value of that holding falls from €10m to about €9.78m.

Decision and interpretation

Show the board both statements. EPS rises about 7.78% while the continuing share’s modeled economic value falls about 2.22%. A less expensive repurchase or different business valuation gives a different result.

Quick check
Which event mechanically reduces issued ordinary shares outstanding in this example?

Retiring repurchased shares reduces the outstanding count. An ordinary secondary sale changes ownership of existing shares.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Instead repurchase the same 1m shares at €8. Calculate post-buyback value, transfer to continuing holders and pro forma EPS under the same cash-yield assumption.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Spend €8m, leaving €92m for 9m shares, or €10.2222 each. The company pays below the assumed €10 pre-buyback value.
  2. The continuing block gains 9m × €0.2222 ≈ €2m, offsetting what the sellers give up relative to the valuation estimate.
  3. Lost interest is €0.16m. EPS becomes 7.84/9 = €0.8711. The favorable valuation conclusion still depends on the initial €100m being a sound estimate.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A listed company retires 10% of its shares and earnings per share rise. Has management created value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A smaller share count can raise earnings per share even as cash leaves the company. I would compare the repurchase price with a defensible per-share economic value, account for lost interest or new debt service, and check who sells. A buyback, a controlling holder’s tender offer, and removal of the exchange listing have different cash flows, legal approvals and consequences for remaining holders.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A controlling shareholder finances the same purchase personally. Should the issuer deduct the payment from its own cash?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No issuer cash leaves in the stated purchase. The controller pays selling shareholders and the share count stays unchanged unless another step cancels shares. Model the controller’s leverage and the company’s obligations separately, including any guarantees or later distributions.
