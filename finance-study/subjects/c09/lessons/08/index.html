Stabilization, the greenshoe and the aftermarket

Theory and terminology

Covered over-allotment and the greenshoe option

overallotment · over-allotment · green shoe · greenshoe · stabilization · stabilisation · lockup · aftermarket

What you should be able to explain

  • Trace the borrowed shares and cash in a covered over-allotment and both ways the banks can close it.
  • Distinguish the greenshoe from a lock-up or an agreement to guarantee a share price.

The transactions in sequence

Start with a base offer. The syndicate may allocate additional shares to investors and cover delivery by borrowing from a shareholder. It now has a short position: it owes those borrowed shares back. Under a covered over-allotment the option covers that additional quantity. The final prospectus identifies the sellers and maximum size. Some deals use a selling holder’s existing shares. Others can involve a new issue.

When trading is weak, buying shares in the market can close part or all of the short. That buying can support demand while it occurs. When trading is strong, the option enables acquisition on the specified terms to cover shares without purchasing them at the higher market price. Closing through a combination is possible. Count shares bought and option shares exercised separately. Do not add the base offer, temporary borrowing and final option exercise as if all three were permanent new issues.

What the mechanism can and cannot establish

The option has finite capacity, a contractual price, an expiry and permitted uses. Stabilization transactions are subject to local rules and disclosure. A price floor is not assured. A deal can trade well below its offer after buying stops, and prices can fall even while transactions occur. Treat any sample period or percentage as a term to verify.

A lock-up restricts sales by specified holders for a contractual period. It limits potential supply at first. Expiry permits a sale but does not require one. Vesting, securities-law restrictions, trading windows and holder decisions can still matter. The supply effect depends on holders’ incentives and how much the market already anticipated.

Match the share and cash ledgers

Temporary delivery and ultimate ownership are different ledgers. On the trade date label which shares come from the issuer, which from a selling shareholder and which are borrowed. At closing note the cash paid by allocated investors. Later record which shares are purchased in the market and which come from exercising the option. Distinguish an underwriting spread from any differences in short-covering prices.

Method

Build the result

Borrowed shares owed = excess shares sold − market shares bought to cover − shares obtained by option exercise.

Each quantity is measured in the same shares. Cash from short covering before fees equals the excess quantity sold times its sale price less cash spent on market or option shares.

  1. Subtract base sale shares from actual allotments to identify the short.
  2. Compare feasible cover sources and actual contract prices. Respect any separate limits and expiry.
  3. Reconcile the borrowed-share balance to zero and attribute any extra sale proceeds to the option grantor.
Worked case

A 1.5m-share over-allotment

A 10m-share primary IPO prices at €20. The syndicate borrows and allocates 1.5m additional shares. The issuer grants an option on up to 1.5m new shares at €20. Ignore fees, interest and taxes to isolate the share mechanics.

Working

  1. Investors take 11.5m shares and pay €230m. The base new issue supplies 10m and gives the issuer €200m. The remaining €30m is initially associated with the short position.
  2. If market shares cost €18, covering all 1.5m costs €27m. Return them to the lender. The issuer has still issued only 10m shares.
  3. Gross short-covering difference is €30m − €27m = €3m before costs and any contractual sharing. Do not add it to the issuer’s proceeds.
  4. If the market instead trades at €23, exercise the 1.5m-share option at €20 for €30m, deliver those shares to the lender, and close the short. Issuer gross proceeds are then €230m.

Decision and interpretation

Both paths close the same loan of shares. Only the new-share exercise in the strong-market case increases the issuer’s permanent share count beyond the base.

Quick check
If all borrowed over-allotment shares are returned using purchases from ordinary market sellers, who receives the extra market-purchase cash?

Open-market purchases pay the selling market participants. They do not create new issuer shares.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

The bank covers 0.6m shares at €18.50, then exercises the remaining option at €20. Reconcile the short, issuer proceeds and covering cash.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The short is 1.5m. Buying 0.6m leaves 0.9m to be exercised. At the end 1.5 − 0.6 − 0.9 = zero.
  2. Open-market covering costs 0.6 × 18.50 = €11.10m. The option costs 0.9 × 20 = €18m. Total cover cost is €29.10m against €30m originally received.
  3. The issuer sells 10.9m shares for €218m gross. Gross short-covering difference is €0.90m under the exercise assumptions. The proceeds do not include the market purchase.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why can an IPO syndicate buy shares after a weak debut yet exercise its option after a strong one?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The syndicate can initially sell more shares than the base offer and borrow the excess for delivery. It then owes shares to the lender. It can buy them in the market if cheaper, or use the over-allotment option on the disclosed terms when market shares are dearer. I would check the option grantor, maximum size, expiry and applicable stabilization rules before calculating cash or dilution.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Does a lock-up expiring on Friday mean the issuer receives cash on Friday?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Expiry simply permits covered holders to sell if otherwise eligible. An actual sale of existing shares sends cash to those holders. It brings proceeds to the issuer only if a separate primary transaction occurs.
