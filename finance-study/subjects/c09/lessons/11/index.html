Issuing convertibles and tracing possible dilution

Calculate a conversion ratio, conversion value, premium and modeled straight-bond floor.

Theory and terminology

Conversion ratio, bond floor and equity sensitivity of convertible debt

convertible bond · conversion price · conversion premium · bond floor · convertible issuance · hybrid financing

What you should be able to explain

  • Calculate a conversion ratio, conversion value, premium and modeled straight-bond floor.
  • Compare cash redemption with the equity dilution from conversion, and identify the issuer’s refinancing risk.

A bond with an equity decision

A conventional fixed-ratio convertible pays a coupon and offers a specified number of shares upon eligible conversion. Divide face value by conversion price to obtain shares per bond. In the simple example the holder can choose cash principal or shares at maturity. Actual deals may permit earlier conversion, cash or net-share settlement, calls, puts or adjustments after corporate actions. Read the terms before applying a simple payoff.

At maturity the holder compares redemption cash with conversion value. Before maturity there may also be time value in waiting. A modeled bond floor discounts promised debt service using the yield on comparable straight debt. It is an estimate sensitive to credit quality, liquidity and the treatment of default. It is not a guarantee of a minimum cash exit.

Read three different premiums correctly

An initial conversion premium typically compares conversion price with the current ordinary share price. A convertible’s market premium to conversion value compares its own quoted price with the value of shares on conversion. The difference between market price and a modeled bond floor is another number. They use different denominators.

A fixed cash coupon saving can look attractive alongside ordinary debt. It cannot settle the choice alone. Equity volatility, expected dilution, terms around dividends, ranking and the issuer’s ability to refinance the maturity must be modeled together. When credit deteriorates, the stock and the bond floor can both fall.

Model what cash or shares leave the issuer

Work from outstanding bond count. At a given share price, compute shares to deliver if holders convert. Compare with the old outstanding shares using a post-conversion denominator. Cash coupon on face is independent of the market price of the bond. At conversion, specify the treatment of any final coupon in the actual terms.

Method

Build the result

Shares per bond = face / conversion price. Conversion value = shares per bond × share price. Modeled floor = PV of straight debt service at the comparable credit yield.

Face and conversion price must use consistent currency. Conversion value and floor here are currency per bond. The original conversion premium = conversion price / current share price − 1.

  1. Identify settlement form, conversion window and whether the option is held by the investor. Start with a plain fixed-ratio physically settled example.
  2. Discount straight coupons and principal using credit-matched debt. Compare that model estimate with the equity value under conversion.
  3. For the issuer compute the cash debt service under redemption and the new ownership denominator under conversion. Stress both.
Worked case

Financing 50,000 bonds

Each bond has €1,000 face, two years to maturity, €20 annual coupons and a €25 conversion price. Comparable straight debt yields 6%. Shares currently trade at €20. The company has 8m existing shares. Assume solvent repayment, voluntary conversion at maturity only and a final coupon paid under either outcome.

Working

  1. Each bond converts into 1,000/25 = 40 shares. Current conversion value is 40 × €20 = €800. Initial conversion premium is 25/20 − 1 = 25%.
  2. Model the straight-debt floor as 20/1.06 + 1,020/1.06² = €926.66 per bond. A quoted convertible price of €1,010 exceeds this estimate by €83.34.
  3. At maturity with stock at €30, conversion delivers €1,200 per bond. All holders converting creates 50,000 × 40 = 2m shares, or 2/(8 + 2) = 20% of the post-conversion total.
  4. If the stock is €18, shares would be worth only €720. Cash redemption is then preferable in this simplified case. The issuer needs €50m principal plus €1m final coupons.

Decision and interpretation

A board that chooses a convertible for its low coupon must still cover the €51m downside maturity. Treat assumed future conversion with the same care as an uncertain refinancing.

Quick check
A €1,000 bond converts into 25 shares. At a €36 share price, what is current conversion value?

Twenty-five shares at €36 are worth €900. Face value is the separate cash redemption promise.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

New terms set the conversion price at €32 on the same €1,000 face. Compute the initial premium at €20, the share delivery for the entire issue, and what happens at maturity with a share price of €30.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Premium = 32/20 − 1 = 60%. Ratio = 1,000/32 = 31.25 shares per bond.
  2. If converted, 50,000 × 31.25 = 1,562,500 shares in aggregate, ignoring individual fractional-share settlement. Post-conversion share fraction would be 1.5625/9.5625 = 16.34%.
  3. At a €30 share price conversion is worth €937.50, below €1,000 cash. The issuer still faces cash redemption. Lower potential dilution from the new terms comes with less valuable conversion terms for the buyer.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a convertible carry a lower coupon than ordinary debt from the same company?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Each investor also receives a contractual opportunity to exchange the bond into equity. That option can compensate for a lower coupon. The issuer is effectively giving up some upside if its equity does well. If conversion never becomes attractive, it may still owe the full principal in cash. I would present both outcomes to the board alongside credit risk and call or reset terms.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can you value the convertible exactly by just adding a traded stock call to any straight bond from the issuer?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Only under restrictive matching assumptions. The credit state, conversion decision, coupons forgone, call provisions, dilution and settlement terms can interact. A straight-bond estimate plus an option is a useful first decomposition; production pricing must represent the actual payoff and joint credit/equity dynamics.
