Primary shares, secondary sales and ownership dilution

Theory and terminology

IPO proceeds bridge and post-offer capitalization

gross proceeds · net proceeds · primary vs secondary · pre-money · post-money · float · voting control · IPO accounting · share capital and share premium · incremental transaction costs

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile each share sold to the issuer or an existing seller and calculate net proceeds.
  • Separate economic ownership, trading float, voting power and accounting book-value dilution.

Sources of cash and shares

A primary share is created by the issuer. The subscription increases outstanding shares and brings money into the business. A secondary share is already outstanding. Its buyer pays a selling shareholder. The company’s outstanding count changes only through the primary leg, barring other concurrent events such as an option exercise or conversion.

The underwriting spread is the difference between what public subscribers pay and what sellers receive before their additional expenses. State whether the quoted fee is a percentage of gross proceeds and who bears it. Printing, audit and legal costs can be partly fixed. The equity account presentation depends on the financial reporting rules and the nature of the costs. A cash bridge can be built before resolving that accounting classification.

Use the right denominator

Post-offer equity market capitalization equals the price times all outstanding shares of the relevant economic claim, not just shares offered. A continuing owner keeps the same number of shares if they neither sell nor subscribe, but their percentage falls after a new issue. A holder who sells also reduces their numerator. Voting control can differ from economic ownership when the company has classes with different votes per share.

Public float is the amount treated as available to investors under the venue or index definition. Some shares may be held by insiders, strategic investors or holders subject to restrictions. Never assume the fraction offered is identical to regulatory free float. Book-value dilution compares an investor’s price with a specified per-share net asset measure. An ownership percentage change says something different about the claim.

A price and a share count must refer to the same share basis

For an ordinary issue with no fees or information effects, the post-money equity value comprises the pre-existing business plus new cash. Mixing a pre-money valuation with the post-issue share count can mechanically lower the inferred offer price. Check whether options, convertible instruments and employee grants enter a diluted valuation denominator or close as issued shares. State the convention.

Post the new share issue to the accounts

Start with the issuer’s books. Suppose it issues 5m shares for €10 per share, with nominal value €1 each. Before fees, debit cash €50m, credit ordinary share capital €5m and credit share premium €45m. A debit to cash records money coming in. The two equity credits identify who supplied it. A simultaneous sale of the founder’s old shares between investors produces no issuer cash or share capital entry.

Under IAS 32, incremental fees directly attributable to completing the new equity issue reduce equity. Costs for a distinct listing or other activity require a separate assessment. Joint costs must be allocated on a rational, consistent basis. In this example, assume €2.5m of fees qualify as direct issuance costs and €2m relates solely to a separately identifiable listing service expensed in the period. Debit contributed equity €2.5m and listing expense €2m, and credit cash €4.5m. Ignore income tax. Cash rises €45.5m. Contributed equity rises €47.5m and the €2m expense reduces earnings, so the total equity movement also equals €45.5m. That reconciliation explains why two IPOs with similar cash proceeds can report different income statements.

Method

Build the result

Issuer net cash = P × N × (1 − f) − C. Post shares = O + N. Continuing ownership = H / (O + N).

P is euros per share, N is primary shares, f is the issuer’s proportional fee, C is fixed issuer expenses, O is old shares, and H is a particular holder’s shares still owned. Use millions of shares with euros per share to get €m.

  1. Separate N new shares from the S existing shares on the cover. Total buyer cash is P(N + S).
  2. Split the gross cash into issuer PN and selling-holder PS, deducting the appropriate parties’ fees once.
  3. Change the ownership denominator by N. Update each seller’s holdings before dividing by the new total.
Worked case

Reconcile an €80m flotation

The issuer has 20m ordinary shares. At €10 the offer sells 5m new and 3m existing founder shares. The founder starts with 12m shares. The banks take 5% from each selling party’s gross amount. The issuer pays a further €2m of expenses. No other shares or cash movements occur.

Working

  1. Public buyers pay (5 + 3) × 10 = €80m. Of this, €50m is primary and €30m secondary.
  2. The issuer retains 50 × 0.95 − 2 = €45.5m. The founder retains 30 × 0.95 = €28.5m.
  3. Bank fees total €4m and other expenses €2m. The four destinations reconcile to 45.5 + 28.5 + 4 + 2 = €80m.
  4. Post shares are 25m, implying €250m market capitalization at the offer price. The founder now holds (12 − 3)/25 = 36%, down from 60%.

Decision and interpretation

A board needing €48m for a project is €2.5m short. Increasing the founder’s sell-down can enlarge the advertised deal while leaving that shortfall untouched.

Quick check
A company issues 2m shares and a fund sells 1m existing shares at €8. Before fees, how much cash reaches the company?

The subscription for 2m new shares gives the company €16m. The €8m for the existing shares belongs to the selling fund.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Return to the base case. At €10, how many new shares must be sold to net at least €55m? Hold the founder’s 3m sale fixed. What percentage does the founder own afterward?

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Solve 10N × 0.95 − 2 ≥ 55 with N in millions. N ≥ 57/9.5 = 6m shares.
  2. Primary gross cash is €60m. The €3m fee plus €2m costs leaves €55m. Total headline size including the founder is €90m.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A bank announces an €80m offering. How would you find out how much the company actually raises?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I need the new-share count, the existing shares sold, the agreed price and who pays each cost. Only the new shares bring cash into the issuer. I would reconcile its gross proceeds to net cash, build the new denominator, and separately calculate what selling shareholders take home. The total headline deal size includes both groups’ sales.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Would an extra 1m shares sold under an over-allotment option dilute the founder again?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Inspect who grants the option. New shares increase the denominator. A sale by an existing holder leaves it unchanged and reduces that holder’s numerator. An open-market purchase by the stabilizing bank is a third outcome with no extra issue.
