Originating a bond and pricing the new-issue concession

Infer a new-issue yield from a matched curve and explain the price below or above par.

Theory and terminology

Syndicated bond pricing against a secondary-market curve

debt capital markets · DCM · new issue premium · spread talk · coupon · issue price · new issue concession · underwriting syndicate

What you should be able to explain

  • Infer a new-issue yield from a matched curve and explain the price below or above par.
  • Prepare a pricing wire reconciling benchmark, spread, coupon, issue price, fees and net proceeds.

Build the syndicate’s comparison

Debt capital markets (DCM) teams help issuers choose a currency, tenor, ranking, security, documentation and timing. A bookrunner estimates a fair secondary spread from the issuer’s existing bonds or similar borrowers after adjusting for maturity, seniority, options and liquidity. A new-issue concession is the extra yield relative to that estimated fair curve, often measured in basis points. It can be small, positive or negative; the choice of comparables changes the estimate.

In a typical syndicated institutional deal, banks announce a mandate or transaction, communicate initial price thoughts, gather orders, revise guidance and agree size and final terms with the issuer. Indicative demand can shrink when the spread tightens. Allocation, confirmation, signing, settlement and the first secondary trades follow. A treasury auction may instead use predefined bid rules. A syndicated bank loan involves lenders making contractual advances and has different documentation, drawings and covenants.

Separate benchmark movement from spread movement

A benchmark is a chosen government or swap reference in the same currency and near the intended maturity. Yield equals benchmark plus credit/liquidity spread under this simplified quote convention. A lower final spread need not give the issuer cheaper all-in funding if the benchmark rises while the deal is marketed. The same issuer can price at different spreads in two currencies. The swap-adjusted funding cost and cross-currency basis matter to its home-currency comparison.

The coupon might be rounded to a conventional increment. Discount contractual coupons and redemption at the final yield to obtain the issue price. Compare price-based cash and fixed costs using the correct face amount, coupon period, calendar and accrued interest convention.

Translate the pricing wire into issuer cash

The wire records benchmark reference and timestamp, reoffer spread and yield, coupon, price as a percentage of face, size, dates and fees. Net borrowing cost is the interest rate equating the actual net proceeds to debt service. For the same contractual cash flows, smaller net proceeds imply a larger effective rate. Financing statements can also require separate treatment of transaction costs.

Method

Build the result

Issue price per 100 = Σ[coupon cash / (1 + y)ᵗ] + 100/(1 + y)ᵀ. Net cash = face × issue price / 100 − cash fees.

y is annual yield as a decimal in this example, t indexes annual payment dates and T is the years to redemption. One basis point is 0.01 percentage point. Price per 100 is not a percentage applied to an already discounted amount.

  1. Match the currency, tenor and ranking of the comparison bonds. Add a justified concession to the estimated fair spread.
  2. Add final spread to benchmark yield, identify contractual coupons and discount each dated payment at that yield.
  3. Scale the resulting price by face and deduct fees. For all-in cost solve the same cash-flow equation with net proceeds as its initial price.
Worked case

Price a three-year corporate bond

A three-year annual-pay €200m bond uses a 2.50% benchmark. Matched secondary fair spread is 140bp. Investors require a 10bp new-issue concession, giving a 150bp reoffer spread. Coupon is 3.75%, redemption at par. Fees are €0.5m underwriting and €1m other. It settles on a coupon-cycle start with zero accrued interest.

Working

  1. Investor yield = 2.50% + 1.50% = 4.00%. Cash per 100 at the three year ends is 3.75, 3.75 and 103.75.
  2. Discount at 4%: 3.75/1.04 + 3.75/1.04² + 103.75/1.04³ = 99.30623 per 100. The below-par price compensates for coupon below market yield.
  3. Cash before fees is 200 × 99.30623/100 = €198.61245m. Deduct €1.5m to get €197.11245m available to the issuer.
  4. The issuer still owes €7.5m coupon at the end of years one and two, and €207.5m in year three. Its effective yield exceeds 4% because those obligations are funded by lower net cash.

Decision and interpretation

A requirement for €198m net is missed by approximately €0.888m. The desk must review size, fees, timing or other funding, even though the bond was sold in full.

Quick check
A bond priced at 99 per 100 face and issued for €80m face raises how much before fees and accrued interest?

Buyers pay 99% of €80m = €79.2m. The €80m is the issuer’s face-value obligation.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Before pricing, the benchmark rises by 20bp and the final spread tightens by 10bp. Keep the coupon, face, maturity and fees unchanged. What yield and net cash now result?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The benchmark becomes 2.70% and final spread becomes 140bp. The combined yield rises by 10bp to 4.10%.
  2. Price = 3.75/1.041 + 3.75/1.041² + 103.75/1.041³ ≈ 99.03056 per 100.
  3. Gross is €198.06112m and net is €196.56112m. The tighter credit spread still leaves the issuer about €0.55133m worse off than before.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A new bond pays a 3.75% coupon. The treasurer says the funding costs exactly 3.75%. What would you check?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The coupon fixes contractual interest on face value. Investors may buy below par and earn a higher yield, and the issuer may receive less again after fees. I would price the coupon cash flows against the market yield, reconcile net cash at settlement, and calculate the issuer’s effective borrowing rate from those actual proceeds and future payments.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Why can a highly oversubscribed bond still trade weakly the next day?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Duplicate or price-sensitive orders may disappear, the benchmark or general credit market may move, and allocations can go to holders who sell immediately. Quoted order coverage alone does not determine a secondary bid or prove that the new-issue concession was adequate.
