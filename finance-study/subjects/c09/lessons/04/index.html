Valuing the issuer and setting an offer range

Translate enterprise-value multiples into comparable per-share values on a consistent pre-financing basis.

Theory and terminology

Pre-money valuation and minimum feasible IPO price

offer range · IPO discount · comparable companies · offer price · price range

What you should be able to explain

  • Translate enterprise-value multiples into comparable per-share values on a consistent pre-financing basis.
  • Find prices at which cash and ownership constraints can both be met.

Build the equity story from evidence

A credible equity story explains how customers generate revenue, how growth converts into cash, which competitors can erode returns and what the new money buys. Distinguish signed orders from aspirations. Investors can test a forecast by changing volumes, margins, working capital and reinvestment. If the business is loss-making, a revenue multiple may be informative only after matching business models, growth and credible future margins.

Use two valuations when feasible. For comparable companies, select consistent business models, leverage treatment, fiscal periods and accounting. A discounted cash flow estimates operating value from cash generated after reinvestment. For either method, deduct outstanding debt and other senior claims and add genuine surplus cash to reach the value of the old equity. An offer discount can capture execution risk, investor demand or bargaining. There is no universal discount for every IPO.

Price a pre-financing business on pre-financing shares

Suppose operating value of the existing business is €260m and it carries €30m of net debt. With 20m shares outstanding, the pre-money indication is (260 − 30)/20 = €11.50. If new shares are issued at that price, the gross cash increases equity value as the share count rises. If one adds new shares to the denominator while omitting new cash from the numerator, the calculation fabricates a lower price.

Separate issue costs from discretion over price. An issuance fee is a cash outflow. An offer discount is a lower sale price relative to a chosen valuation or observable benchmark. Compare the same equity claim at the same time. A quoted market price for a tiny traded position and the proceeds from placing a large block may differ for economic reasons.

Frame an actionable range

The range has to work for subscribers and the issuer. Calculate the maximum new shares under the ownership limit. Then solve for the lowest acceptable price net of costs. If that price lies above credible demand, increasing optimistic valuation assumptions will not close the funding gap. The board can reduce the investment budget, change the cash mix, renegotiate dilution or defer.

Method

Build the result

Nmax = d × O / (1 − d). Pmin = (net cash required + fixed fees) / [Nmax × (1 − fee rate)].

d is the maximum new investors’ fraction caused by primary issuance alone, O is old shares, Nmax is the maximum number of new shares, Pmin is the minimum feasible offer price. Quantities in millions give a result in euros per share.

  1. Write the new-issue dilution constraint N/(O + N) ≤ d. Multiply both sides by O + N and collect like terms.
  2. At maximum size the issuer has the best chance of meeting its cash target at a low price. Its net cash equals P × Nmax × (1 − fee rate) less fixed fees.
  3. Solve that equality for P. Recalculate the net cash at the rounded executable price. An economically precise floor often needs rounding upward to a valid price increment.
Worked case

A range with one impossible endpoint

A board has 20m old shares, a 20% primary-dilution cap, €48m minimum net cash, a 4% underwriting fee and €2m fixed costs. Banks propose €10–€12. Operating peer multiples span 8–10× on €26m EBITDA. Net debt is €30m.

Working

  1. Peer enterprise values are €208m to €260m. Subtract €30m for old equity of €178m to €230m, or €8.90–€11.50 per existing share.
  2. The dilution cap permits Nmax = 0.20 × 20/0.80 = 5m new shares.
  3. The cash floor is (48 + 2)/(5 × 0.96) = €10.4167, or at least €10.42 on a cent grid. At €10 the issuer receives €46m.
  4. At €10.50 the issuer nets 5 × 10.50 × 0.96 − 2 = €48.40m. The €12 upper point is above this particular peer range and would require a defensible premium.

Decision and interpretation

A €10.50 floor passes the funding test in this case. Validate demand and company-specific quality before using it. If peer multiples fall, the feasible price band can vanish while the minimum-cash requirement stays fixed.

Quick check
Net proceeds are too low at the permitted issuance cap. Which change, on its own, raises issuer net proceeds?

Higher primary price increases cash per new share after the proportional fee. A resale or an allocation change does not change primary proceeds.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

With the same 20m old shares, €48m cash target and costs, the board lowers permitted primary dilution to 15%. What is the new minimum price? Could a €12 offer deliver the target?

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Nmax = 0.15 × 20/0.85 = 3.52941m shares. Reuse the pre-financing share count.
  2. Pmin = 50/(3.52941 × 0.96) = €14.75694, approximately €14.76.
  3. At €12 the issue nets 12 × 3.52941 × 0.96 − 2 = €38.66m, leaving about €9.34m unfunded. Pricing more aggressively alone is unlikely to work if demand stops at €12.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

If peers imply €12 per share, why might a board set a range with a floor of €10.50, and when would that floor be infeasible?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Peer values depend on growth, margins, claims and a consistent forecast period. The equity story may justify a range around the peer indication. Separately, the minimum price must fund the required cash after fees within the board’s maximum new share count. I would reject any proposed floor that fails those arithmetic tests, even if the banks expect strong demand.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A buyer values the post-financing project separately at €20m of net present value. Can you simply add the full project cost to the operating value as well?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Trace which forecasts already include the project and its investment. Add the project’s incremental net present value only once. Treat new financing cash and the use of that cash consistently. Adding the full investment, its future benefits and the new cash can count the same resources several times.
