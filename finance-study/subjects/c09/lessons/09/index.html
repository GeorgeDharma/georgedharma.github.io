Rights issues, follow-ons and block placements

Reconcile equity wealth for an existing holder who exercises or sells transferable rights.

Theory and terminology

Theoretical ex-rights price and subscription-right value

TERP · rights offering · seasoned equity offering · SEO · follow-on offering · placement · accelerated bookbuild · block trade · pre-emption · private placements · PIPE · pre-emption rights

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile equity wealth for an existing holder who exercises or sells transferable rights.
  • Choose among a rights issue, a marketed follow-on, a primary placement and a shareholder block sale.

Why issue again after listing?

A company can raise new common equity for an acquisition, deleveraging or an investment plan. A marketed follow-on can build broad demand but risks price moves during marketing. An accelerated placing can shorten market exposure but often concentrates allocations. A secondary block trade gives an existing holder liquidity; it creates no issuer funding unless paired with a primary sale.

Pre-emption means eligible existing holders can subscribe in proportion to their holdings under the applicable law and terms. In a rights offering specify record date, ex-rights date, subscription ratio, subscription price, deadline, whether rights trade, and treatment of unexercised entitlements. Some offers have a standby underwriter for the residual. Examine fees, conditions and shortfall allocation.

Start from total equity value

Suppose four old shares each worth €12 come with the right to subscribe for one new share at €8. Before other information, the old block is worth €48 and the subscription adds €8 of cash. The five shares are claims on €56, giving €11.20 per ex-rights share. The quoted 33.3% subscription-price discount to €12 says little by itself about a continuing holder’s wealth.

Be precise about the word “right.” If one right is attached to each old share, four rights are needed to subscribe for one new share. One such right is worth €12 − €11.20 = €0.80. The complete subscription entitlement is worth €11.20 − €8 = €3.20. Data vendors and news reports can use either unit.

The model assumptions explain real deviations

The arithmetic assumes full take-up, no fees or market moves, no economic news, the new cash worth its face value and identical shares. Issuing below market can signal need or fund a positive project. Tax, trading restrictions and investor cash constraints can change actual wealth. Distinguish a discounted offer from evidence of distress. A nontransferable right also changes the options available to a holder who cannot subscribe.

Negotiate a private placement or PIPE

A private placement sells to selected investors through an available legal route. A PIPE is a private investment in public equity. The issuer is already public and negotiates securities and terms with selected investors. Consider price, warrants, registration or resale restrictions, shareholder approvals and the voting stake after the issue. A US Rule 506(b) placement restricts general solicitation. In the EU, an offer solely to qualified investors is one exemption from the offer prospectus obligation. Admission to trading raises a separate question. Confirm the actual venue, security and route before applying either example elsewhere.

A listed company with 10m shares trading at €20 needs €18m immediately. A private investor subscribes for 1m new ordinary shares at €19 and charges no separate fee, while advisers receive €0.5m from the issuer. Issuer cash is €18.5m. The investor owns 1/11 = 9.09%. Discount to the last trade is (20 − 19)/20 = 5%. If the investor also receives 0.2m zero-cost warrants exercisable at €22, exercise would bring another €4.4m of future cash. The investor would own 1.2/11.2 = 10.71% then. Future warrant cash cannot fund the immediate €18m obligation. Existing holders bear the dilution and the value of the warrant grant.

Method

Build the result

TERP = (k × cum-rights price + subscription price)/(k + 1), for one new share per k old. One right attached to one old share = cum price − TERP.

TERP means theoretical ex-rights price. The cum-rights price includes the entitlement. The subscription price is cash for one new share. k is old shares per new share, not a percentage discount.

  1. Value k old shares before the right separates. Add the cash received for the new share.
  2. Divide the total by k + 1 economically identical shares. That gives the ex-rights value on the stated assumptions.
  3. Value the right in either unit. Confirm k times the single old-share right equals TERP less subscription price.
Worked case

Exercise, sell or let the rights lapse

A company offers one new share per four old at €8. Its shares trade cum rights at €12. You hold 400 old shares. Rights can be sold at theoretical value. Ignore all other effects.

Working

  1. The theoretical ex-rights price is (4 × 12 + 8)/5 = €11.20. Four rights together are worth €3.20.
  2. Exercise 100 entitlements and pay €800. Five hundred shares × €11.20 = €5,600. Subtract the €800 additional cash and starting wealth is preserved at €4,800.
  3. Alternatively, keep 400 ex-rights shares worth €4,480 and sell 400 rights at €0.80 for €320. Total wealth is again €4,800.
  4. If the rights expire worthless to this holder with no compensation, only €4,480 of stock remains. The modeled €320 loss equals the rights given up.

Decision and interpretation

The issuer’s discount and an individual holder’s realized loss are different quantities. Check whether the actual deal sells the rump for lapsed holders or pays any proceeds back.

Quick check
Under a one-for-four renounceable rights issue, what happens to an investor who sells all rights at fair theoretical value?

The company issues new shares to subscribers. The non-subscribing holder keeps its old shares and realizes the entitlement value in cash.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A 1-for-3 issue at €9 follows a €15 cum-rights price. A holder owns 300 shares. Compute TERP, the value of each old-share right and the wealth effect of selling all rights.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. TERP = (3 × 15 + 9)/4 = €13.50. The value per old share’s right is €1.50.
  2. The 300 ex-rights shares are worth €4,050. Selling 300 rights yields €450. Total €4,500 equals the initial shareholding’s value.
  3. The entitlement to one whole new share is worth 3 × €1.50 = €4.50. Confusing it with one old-share right would triple the sale proceeds incorrectly.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Do existing shareholders automatically lose money when a rights issue is announced at a large discount?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A lower subscription price buys a valuable right. In the mechanical ex-rights model a holder can exercise the entitlement or sell a transferable right and preserve starting wealth, before fees or new information. Someone who lets valuable rights expire can lose value. I would still assess how the proceeds will be invested and whether shareholders can fund or sell their rights.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A controller sells 4m already-listed shares overnight at a 3% discount. Should the CFO show it as a rights issue or primary fundraising?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Neither description fits the stated cash and share flow. The controller transfers existing shares, so the issuer receives zero and outstanding shares are unchanged. Disclosure obligations and changes in ownership or float may still matter.
