Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A cash target and two potential listing routes

A founder seeks €10m secondary proceeds. The company needs €32m net. It has 12m shares before an IPO. A €12 offer would sell 3m new and 1m existing shares. Each seller pays 4% of its own proceeds, and the issuer pays €1m fixed costs. A second route is a resale-only direct listing with the same founder sale but no private financing.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

Price and allocate an issue with information asymmetry

A company offers 5m new shares. Independent bids are 2m at up to €12, 1m at €11 and 4m at €10. Assume all accepted shares pay the marginal price, descending priority, no fees. An investor separately evaluates two equiprobable IPOs worth €12 and €8 with fills of 20 and 80 shares respectively.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Interpret initial returns and the share shortage

A 4m-share IPO at €16 contains 3m primary and 1m selling-holder shares. The day-one close is €17.60; a matched appraisal indicates €15 and the year-end share price is €15.84. No dividends. An additional 0.4m shares were borrowed and sold against an option granted by the selling holder. Later, this firm proposes a 1-for-3 rights issue at €12 when the cum-rights price is €16.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Compare three capital-structure actions

A borrower plans a one-year €50m par redemption bond paying 3%. The benchmark is 2%, comparable spread is 110bp and concession is 20bp. Issuer expenses are €0.20m. A separate proposed convertible has €1,000 face, €25 conversion price and a possible €28 share price at maturity. In an alternative standalone repurchase example a firm is worth €80m including cash, with 8m shares; it retires 1m for €11 each.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A cash target and two potential listing routes

Part A 4 marks

Reconcile all cash recipients in the IPO and decide who clears each cash floor.

Gross €48m splits €36m company and €12m founder. Net company = 36 × 0.96 − 1 = €33.56m. Founder = 12 × 0.96 = €11.52m. Bank €1.92m and other €1m complete the reconciliation. Both pass. Credit primary/secondary split, fee treatment, totals and inference.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate primary dilution and explain the consequence of issuing on the proposed direct-listing route instead.

There are 15m post shares, of which new primary shares are 3/15 = 20%. The stated resale-only alternative adds no shares or company cash and leaves a €32m issuer funding gap, although the founder may sell.

Part C 3 marks

Name two independent pre-pricing gates and distinguish review from investment merit.

Examples include verified financial and risk disclosure, required securities filings, exchange admission work, operating share-delivery readiness and investor demand at a viable price. Identify two with a reason. Completion of a review or admission does not establish the shares’ economic value.

Case 2 · 10 marks

Price and allocate an issue with information asymmetry

Part A 4 marks

Price the 5m sale, allocate the bids and state issuer gross proceeds.

At €11 demand is 3m and at €10 it is 7m. Clear at €10 and allocate 2m, 1m and 2m. Gross cash is €50m. Give credit for cumulative demand, price, allotments and cash.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate the investor’s break-even price across the separate IPOs.

Expected value of shares allocated is (20 × 12 + 80 × 8)/2 = €440. Expected fill is (20 + 80)/2 = 50. Break-even price €8.80 is below €10 because of the state-dependent fill.

Part C 3 marks

If the issuer requires €54m gross and caps size at 5m, can the current book support the funding need?

Minimum price is €10.80. At that level only the €12 and €11 orders survive, totaling 3m. A smaller sale cannot cover the target on these bids. Seek new eligible demand, revisit the funding need, or postpone.

Case 3 · 10 marks

Interpret initial returns and the share shortage

Part A 4 marks

Compute the initial return, relative offer premium and first-close buyer’s return to year end.

Initial return is +10%. Offer premium to modeled €15 is 16/15 − 1 = 6.67%. The aftermarket buyer earns 15.84/17.60 − 1 = −10%. Different dates and comparators explain the different verdicts.

Part B 3 marks

If 0.1m shares are market-purchased and the balance covered using the holder’s option, what extra cash and dilution come to the issuer?

After buying 0.1m, 0.3m remain. Exercise over 0.3m existing holder shares closes the stock loan. Neither covering leg is primary. Incremental issuer cash and dilution from these legs are both zero.

Part C 3 marks

What is the theoretical ex-rights price and what is the entitlement attached to each old share worth?

TERP = (3 × 16 + 12)/4 = €15. The right attached to one old share is €1, and three rights together permit a subscription worth €3. State the no-new-information and no-cost assumptions.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Compare three capital-structure actions

Part A 4 marks

Calculate the new bond’s yield, price per 100 and net proceeds.

Total yield = 2% + 1.1% + 0.2% = 3.3%. One-year price = 103/1.033 = 99.7096. Gross cash = 50 × 0.997096 ≈ €49.8548m; net ≈ €49.6548m. Credit spread, discounting, scale and net cash.

Part B 3 marks

Compare conversion value with par at €28 and explain what changes if the share ends at €22.

The ratio is 1,000/25 = 40. At €28 the shares are worth €1,120, above €1,000. At €22 they are worth €880; solvent cash redemption is preferable on plain voluntary maturity terms. Coupon treatment must match the contract.

Part C 3 marks

What is the economic value per continuing share after the independent repurchase and where does value go?

Pre value was €10. The company spends €11m leaving €69m for 7m = €9.8571 per share. Total loss to non-sellers is €1m, equal to the premium received by the sellers, given the fixed valuation and no other changes.

Academic references for this paper
  • Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur · Investment Banking and Capital Market Operations · course proposal

    Explicitly groups IPO motives, underwriting, greenshoes, underpricing, rights and follow-ons, buybacks and bond issues into equity and debt capital markets. Used as a subject-coverage benchmark.

  • University of Florida · Ritter and Welch · A Review of IPO Activity, Pricing, and Allocations

    Distinguishes going-public motives, initial returns, allocations and long-run returns, and discusses competing explanations and the sensitivity of empirical evidence.

  • Google Inc. · SEC EDGAR · Final IPO prospectus, 18 August 2004

    Supplies base primary and secondary share counts, price, underwriting terms, voting provisions, auction terms and maximum additional existing shares.

  • International Capital Market Association · distributed by the Investment Association · ICMA Primary Market Handbook extract · pre-sounding, bookbuilding and allocations

    Describes investor meetings, order books and allocation in the European cross-border syndicated institutional bond market. An institutional example, not a universal market rule.

  • US SEC · Investor.gov · Convertible Securities

    Defines contractual conversion into another security and explains terms which can change dilution.
