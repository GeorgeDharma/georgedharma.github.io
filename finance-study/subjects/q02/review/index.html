Dependence, fixed effects and honest evaluation

The lectures derive the partial regression coefficient, correct inference for dependence, difference away fixed unit effects, and compare forecasts on data unavailable at estimation. These are prerequisites for the diagnostic and multivariate extensions.

Persistence, long-run links and conditional volatility

Learners propagate a stationary autoregression, relate innovation persistence to forecasts, update a GARCH variance and build an error correction term for two linked levels. The new exercises test the specification underlying these choices.

New and expanded lessons

Q02.11

Decide whether to difference a financial series

Q02.06 studies stationary autoregressions and Q02.07 works with error correction. This chapter estimates and interprets an augmented Dickey–Fuller statistic, including how the hypothesized trend changes the critical value and the decision.

  • Distinguish stationarity around a deterministic trend from a stochastic trend.
  • Use the correct augmented Dickey–Fuller reference distribution and explain an inconclusive result.

Q02.12

Diagnose volatility clustering before and after GARCH

Q02.07 forecasts a GARCH variance and explains persistence. This chapter tests for predictable squared innovations, standardizes them by time-varying volatility and repeats the diagnostic to see what the fitted variance leaves behind.

  • Conduct an ARCH Lagrange multiplier test from an auxiliary-regression R².
  • Compute one conditional volatility and interpret evidence of remaining clustering after scaling.

Q02.13

Forecast two financial series and test predictive spillovers

Q02.06 forecasts one series and Q02.07 models a long-run relation. This chapter estimates the implications of cross-lagged equations, tests a block of predictive coefficients and separates a reduced-form response from an identified economic shock.

  • Produce two-period-ahead forecasts recursively from a vector autoregression.
  • Use a nested equation test for incremental predictability and identify the extra assumptions needed for structural interpretation.

Public benchmarks

University of Milan, Italy

Financial Econometrics, Master's in Economics and Finance, 2021–22

The teaching sequence connects univariate autocorrelation to VAR block tests and impulse responses, then covers unit roots, cointegration and volatility models.

University of Warwick, United Kingdom

IB2B2-15 Financial Econometrics

The published outline includes ordinary regression, panel transformations, trend, unit roots and cointegration. Its outcomes ask students to choose an empirical specification and assess the robustness and power of the methods.

Bocconi University, Italy

Massimo Guidolin, lecture notes on GARCH models

The lectures examine persistence and model diagnostics, including auxiliary regressions of lagged squared residuals and inspection of standardized innovations after fitting volatility dynamics.

Rob J. Hyndman and George Athanasopoulos, Monash University, Australia

Forecasting: Principles and Practice, 3rd ed., Advanced forecasting methods, Vector autoregressions

The vector autoregression section writes coupled equations for several series, distinguishes levels from differencing, and explains how forecasts of each variable feed subsequent forecast horizons.

Christoph Hanck and coauthors, University of Duisburg-Essen, Germany

Introduction to Econometrics with R, Nonstationarity I: Trends

The chapter develops Dickey–Fuller tests for stochastic trends, fits an augmented specification and compares its statistic with critical values that depend on the deterministic terms.
