Professional workshop · Q02

Evaluate a lending-guarantee pilot without overstating the evidence

A development agency introduced a partial loan guarantee in two partner banks at the start of Q3. Two comparison banks retained the old underwriting policy. The agency wants to know whether the pilot increased the probability that an application was approved and whether to fund a broader rollout. Reconstruct the panel from counts, define an estimand, calculate a transparent comparison, examine pretrends, composition and dependence, and draft the decision note. The pilot banks were chosen administratively, with no random assignment.

From inputs to decision

01

Build the outcome and correct unit

The agency’s primary question concerns the average participating bank’s approval-rate change. Counting all applications equally answers a related but numerically different question.

  1. For each bank-quarter define 100 times approvals divided by applications. A’s rates are 47,49,57,58%. B’s are 39,41,46,48%. C’s are 44,46,48,49% and D’s 50,52,54,55%.
  2. Keep denominators in the panel. A bank fixed effect applies to the bank, the intervention begins at the bank, and individual outcomes within a bank-quarter can share the same credit committee and applicant pool.

Create 16 auditable bank-quarter rows. The primary estimand is an equal-bank average effect for the two pilot banks at each post date if the design assumptions hold.

02

Write the counterfactual and model

The missing value is how each pilot bank’s approval rate would have moved without the guarantee. Comparison banks offer an estimate of that change, subject to substantive assumptions.

  1. Use bank and quarter fixed effects with pilot-by-quarter interactions, omit the Q2 interaction as reference, and give each bank-quarter equal weight. Define the outcome in percentage points and the post-date contrasts separately.
  2. Parallel untreated trends, absence of early take-up, no major comparison-bank spillovers and stable outcome measurement are required. Administrative partner selection based on a future sales expansion would threaten this design.

The binary treatment indicator alone does not resolve selection. Pre-policy patterns, applicant mix and contemporaneous underwriting changes form a separate evidence file.

03

Inspect the pre-period contrast

A pre-period comparison detects certain warning signs without proving future untreated trends. The panel offers only one adjacent pre-policy change.

  1. Equal-bank average pilot rates are (47+39)/2=43% in Q1 and (49+41)/2=45% in Q2. Comparison means are 47% and 49%. Both groups gain two points.
  2. The pilot-minus-comparison gap is −4 points at both dates. The Q1-versus-Q2 placebo contrast is zero. Similar past gains can still coexist with different shocks after Q2 because partners were chosen administratively.

Show all group paths and retain the four-point level difference in the exhibit. The level difference alone does not invalidate a parallel-change calculation.

04

Calculate the two post effects

Separate Q3 and Q4 retain timing and show how the result depends on the reference period. Averaging them later should reflect a stated decision target.

  1. Q3 pilot mean is (57+46)/2=51.5%; comparison mean is (48+54)/2=51%. Relative to Q2, the estimate is (51.5−45)−(51−49)=+4.5 points.
  2. Q4 pilot mean is (58+48)/2=53%; comparison mean is (49+55)/2=52%. The estimate is (53−45)−(52−49)=+5 points. Equally weighting the two post quarters gives +4.75 points.

For Q4 the implied no-pilot average is 45 + (52−49)=48%. The observed 53% exceeds that modeled counterfactual by five points.

05

Reweight and audit influential banks

A large pilot bank may account for more applicants and have a different effect. Show both estimands and identify cases that drive their difference.

  1. With applications as weights, Q2 pilot approvals are 139/300=46.333% and controls 150/300=50%. Q3 rates are 160/300=53.333% and 156/300=52%. The weighted Q3 effect is +5 points.
  2. In Q4 the weighted pilot rate is 164/300=54.667% and comparison rate 159/300=53%. Relative to Q2 the weighted Q4 effect is 8.333−3=+5.333 points. The larger pilot A gained nine points from Q2 versus seven at B.

Both weighting choices yield positive estimates. They differ because A’s 200 applicants give it double B’s weight. Raw counts and rates cannot reveal whether the types of applications shifted.

06

Challenge inference and a concurrent policy

Four banks generate only four assignment units. Treating 2,400 applications over these four quarters as independent interventions can substantially overstate precision.

  1. From Q2 to Q4 the bank-level changes are +9,+7,+3,+3 points. The observed treated-pair versus comparison-pair contrast equals 5. As a hypothetical randomization diagnostic only, six equally likely choices of a treated pair would yield differences +5,+1,+1,−1,−1,−5. The exact two-sided tail fraction would be 2/6.
  2. Because treatment was actually chosen administratively, that tail fraction is not a valid randomized-design p-value here. Request partner selection records, longer history, application-level pre-decision risk measures and evidence on concurrent marketing or staff changes.

If a separate new support program raised B’s post approval by two points independently, the equal-bank estimate would include a +1-point contribution from it. The corresponding Q4 pilot-only estimate in that explicit sensitivity would be +4 points.

07

Record the decision and replication requirements

The steering committee needs an action under uncertainty and a record another analyst could rebuild. A plausible positive association is different evidence from a scalable causal effect.

  1. Recommend a larger monitored rollout with a prespecified comparison design if the agency needs stronger causal evidence. Choose an assignment mechanism consistent with fair access and the policy mandate, preferably with more independent banks or an appropriately phased evaluation where feasible.
  2. Archive counts, denominators, data vintage, bank identifiers, launch records, exclusion log, currency-independent approval definition and exact weighting and contrast formulas. Record whether applications shifted between partners or controls after the announcement.

Do not translate approval-rate gains into profits, borrower welfare or expected credit losses without subsequent repayment, terms and borrower outcome data.

Completed document

Guarantee pilot evaluation memorandum

Decision requested

Authorize preparation of a larger monitored evaluation and collection of borrower-risk and repayment outcomes. The two pilot banks show a stronger approval increase after launch, but the administrative assignment and narrow comparison group limit the case for an unconditional national expansion.

Estimated magnitude

The equal-bank estimate is +4.5 points for Q3 and +5.0 points for Q4 relative to the comparison change. The Q4 no-guarantee counterfactual implied by parallel trends is 48% against 53% observed. Giving each application equal weight gives a +5.33-point Q4 estimate.

Identification and uncertainty

Both groups gained two points in the only pre-period comparison. Four independent bank assignments provide little support for routine large-cluster inference, and assignment was administrative. The illustrative randomization distribution cannot serve as a policy p-value. Applicant selection, spillovers and other underwriting reforms remain open explanations.

Evidence and monitoring

Collect further pre-periods, eligibility criteria, dated application risk factors, rejection reasons, pricing, defaults and bank selection records. Prespecify the target estimand and assignment or comparison mechanism, and maintain both bank-level and applicant-level reports. Publish the dated counts and source-to-exhibit reconciliation with any funding decision.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

If B’s Q4 approvals are corrected from 48 to 46 with all else unchanged, what is the revised equal-bank Q4 effect?

See the full solution

The pilot Q4 mean becomes (58+46)/2=52%. Relative to Q2 it rises seven points while controls rise three. Revised estimate is 7−3=4 percentage points.

Task 2

If C’s Q4 approval count rises from 49 to 52 on the same 100 applications, what is the revised application-weighted Q4 estimate?

See the full solution

Pilot change remains (164−139)/300 × 100 = 8.3333 points. The comparison change becomes (52+110−150)/300 × 100 = 4 points. Difference is 4.3333 points.

Task 3

Suppose an independent service explains two points of B’s improvement in Q3 but no effect in A or the controls. What Q3 equal-bank guarantee effect remains under this sensitivity?

See the full solution

The two-point alternative contributes one point to a two-bank mean. Subtract 1 from the original 4.5-point Q3 effect, leaving 3.5 points. This sensitivity is conditional on the assumed contribution and absence of other changes.

Task 4

Can the agency infer that the guarantee improved repayment quality from the approval result?

No repayment, delinquency, exposure or recovery outcomes were supplied. Approval measures access on the observed application pool. Collect matched repayment vintages, terms, borrower risk and follow-up horizons, then define a separate outcome and counterfactual allowing for changed approval selection.
