Theory midterm · 10 marks

A policy study with staggered credit demand

After lessons 1–5, allow 20 minutes. Explain the reasoning behind your answer.

Loan growth in a treated region rises from 2% to 7%. In a comparison region it rises from 3% to 5%. The treated region also opens a large factory at the policy date.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compute a two-period difference-in-differences estimate. State its identifying assumption.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Why do area and time fixed effects leave a problem here?

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    What evidence could strengthen a causal interpretation?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the midterm marking guide

Part A · 4 marks

The treated change is 5 percentage points, the comparison change 2, giving 3. The assumption is equal changes in their untreated potential outcomes over this interval, not equal starting levels. Credit each change, the difference and the assumption.

Part B · 3 marks

Area effects absorb fixed differences while time effects absorb shared shocks. The factory creates a treated-area-specific change coinciding with the policy. Its effect can enter the estimated policy coefficient.

Part C · 3 marks

Inspect several pre-periods, compare similar regions without the factory shock and test exposure of loans closely tied to the factory. Explain remaining threats. Many loans from only two regions also offer little independent cluster-level information.

Problem 1

A treated group changes from 8 to 11 and a comparison group from 6 to 7. Compute the difference-in-differences estimate.

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

(11 − 8) − (7 − 6) = 2 outcome units. A causal reading requires credible parallel counterfactual trends and attention to other coincident changes.

Problem 2

Several banks share one announcement day. Why can independent-firm standard errors be misleading?

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

A common shock can correlate residuals across banks. Address the dependence in the design or inference and report the limits of a small cross-section.

Suggested academic marking scheme

Use these weights when assessing the complete subject. The four problem sets collect the written problems from individual lectures. You can use the 10-mark midterm and the 40-mark subject paper with the weights below.

Problem sets 25%
Four sets per standard subject. Show reasoning, calculations, units and checks. The two practice problems in each subject are worked samples for these sets.
Theory midterm 20%
After the first half of the subject. Explain or derive a model, identify assumptions and state what would change if one failed.
Seminar paper or laboratory 25%
During the second half. Apply the theory to a case, dataset or implementation. Address an alternative explanation and document how to reproduce the result.
Individual final examination 30%
At the end of the subject. Solve an unfamiliar problem, interpret the result and critique a claim. M&A and lending each include a 30-mark examination question with a marking guide.
