Diagnose volatility clustering before and after GARCH

Theory and terminology

ARCH tests and GARCH residual diagnostics

ARCH LM · standardized residuals · volatility clustering

What you should be able to explain

  • Conduct an ARCH Lagrange multiplier test from an auxiliary-regression R².
  • Compute one conditional volatility and interpret evidence of remaining clustering after scaling.

What can be predictable about an unpredictable return?

Tomorrow's sign may be difficult to forecast even when the size of tomorrow's move is predictable. A mean-model residual is the realized return minus the return expected using information available before that observation. Large positive and negative errors both produce large squares. Clusters in their squares therefore point to changes in conditional variance.

Check the mean model first. Leftover mean patterns, data errors, jumps and changes in trading hours can also create apparent volatility dependence. An autocorrelation test on the signed residuals and an ARCH test on their squares ask different questions. Keep the lag order and data frequency fixed in the diagnostic report.

An auxiliary regression isolates the pattern

Engle’s ARCH Lagrange multiplier test starts under a constant conditional variance null. Regress squared residuals on a constant and q lagged squared residuals. Test all q slope coefficients jointly. In the standard large-sample reference, the number of usable rows times the auxiliary R² is compared with a chi-squared critical value with q degrees of freedom.

A significant result indicates dependence at the tested lags. It does not identify a unique volatility process. One might compare a GARCH model, an asymmetric response to negative shocks, a break indicator or a stronger data correction. Very heavy tails and small samples can make asymptotic reference values unreliable.

Test the innovations on a common scale

A GARCH(1,1) variance forecast is yesterday's squared shock times α, yesterday's variance times β, plus a constant ω. Use shocks in decimal-return units and variances in squared decimal returns. The division that makes a standardized innovation uses the square root of its own contemporaneous forecast variance.

After fitting, look at the ordinary and squared standardized residuals. A similar lagged-squares regression provides a residual diagnostic, with reference calibration adapted to the estimation method when needed. Passing one lag test leaves the conditional distribution, asymmetry and tail events to validate separately. A forecast can absorb volatility clustering and still underprice extreme market moves.

Method

Build the result

LM=nR²; hₜ₊₁=ω+αεₜ²+βhₜ; zₜ=εₜ/√hₜ

n is the usable sample in the auxiliary regression, R² its fit, and LM is dimensionless. εₜ is a return innovation, hₜ its forecast variance, and zₜ the standardized residual. ω has variance units, while α and β are dimensionless.

  1. Under constant variance the q squared-error lags should add no explanatory power beyond an intercept. Their joint contribution determines R².
  2. Multiply R² by n and compare with the supplied right-tail cutoff. A large statistic rejects the specified no-ARCH null.
  3. Under a positive fitted conditional variance, divide each εₜ by √hₜ. Test the squares of these scaled errors for persistent dependence using an appropriate residual reference.
Worked case

Check a currency book's daily variance forecast

The two-lag squared-error regression has 120 usable rows and R²=0.090. Use the illustrative large-sample 5% cutoff 5.99 for two restrictions, including as an approximate residual screen. The fitted GARCH uses ω=0.000005, α=0.10 and β=0.85. Today's error is −4% and today's forecast variance was 0.0004. After standardizing the historical sample, R²=0.018 over 120 usable rows.

Working

  1. Raw LM=120×0.090=10.8, above 5.99. The constant-variance specification leaves detectable clustering.
  2. Tomorrow's variance is 0.000005+0.10×(−0.04)²+0.85×0.0004=0.000505 squared return. Tomorrow's volatility is √0.000505=2.247%.
  3. Using today's variance gives today's standardized error of −0.04/√0.0004=−2.00. Using tomorrow's larger forecast here would let today's shock influence its own scale.
  4. The approximate post-fit statistic is 120×0.018=2.16, below 5.99. The chosen diagnostic finds insufficient evidence of remaining two-lag clustering.

Decision and interpretation

Retain the model for subsequent out-of-sample variance and tail validation. The smaller statistic documents the specified lag test and cannot certify the model's quantiles or the absence of other forms of dependence.

Quick check
Which number should standardize today's return residual?

Scaling by the pre-observation standard deviation makes the error dimensionless and preserves the diagnostic's time ordering.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A desk tests three squared lags using 160 usable rows. The raw auxiliary R² is 0.060. After a variance fit the standardized squared residuals give R²=0.052. Use the supplied approximate 5% cutoff 7.81 for both screens. Interpret.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The raw statistic is 160×0.060=9.60. It exceeds 7.81, so the constant-variance null is rejected.
  2. The post-fit statistic is 160×0.052=8.32, also above 7.81. Scaling reduced the statistic but left evidence of three-lag dependence.
  3. Inspect residual plots, mean fit, market regimes and possible asymmetric responses. Compare revised forecasts on a held-out period before approving the risk use.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How would a risk analyst check whether a volatility model has actually absorbed clustering?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I first model the conditional mean and test whether lagged squared errors predict the next squared error. I then divide each error by its own forecast volatility and repeat the diagnostic on the standardized series. I also test the economic risk forecast out of sample.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the raw residuals have virtually zero first-order autocorrelation, can the ARCH test still reject?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Yes. Signed innovations can have almost no autocorrelation while their magnitudes or squares cluster. A conditional variance model targets the latter dependence.
