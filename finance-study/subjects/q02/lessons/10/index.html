Replication and interpretation

Report uncertainty, alternate specifications and inclusion rules. A credible null result or a benchmark-sensitive finding can answer the research question honestly.

The starting point

A replication record lets another researcher reconstruct the result and evaluate its meaning. Preserve input vintages, sample rules, event dates, model choices and a transformation log. Distinguish decisions made before inspecting returns from sensitivity checks motivated by the results. Record how missing observations change the studied population.

Uncertainty and specification sensitivity describe different things. A confidence interval summarizes sampling uncertainty under a chosen model and assumptions. A change in sign across reasonable benchmarks reveals dependence on what was defined as normal. Both can be reported even when neither supports a simple directional claim.

Theory and method

Two one-sided tests for equivalence

TOST · Equivalence margin

Your bank study finds an insignificant difference. What would you need to conclude that any difference is economically negligible?

A capital committee may treat differences within half a percentage point as immaterial while caring about anything larger. An ordinary two-sided test against exactly zero asks if the data rule out no difference. Equivalence asks whether the data rule out differences at or beyond both materiality boundaries. The acceptable margin must be chosen using the decision context before inspecting estimates. A modest estimate can still be too imprecise to justify the committee’s desired conclusion.

How the result is built

Equivalence at level α requires the (1−2α) interval for θ to lie inside (−δ,+δ) under matching TOST assumptions

θ is the target effect in percentage points, δ a prespecified positive materiality margin, α the size of each one-sided test, and the interval uses the chosen estimator’s sampling model.

  1. Test the lower null θ≤−δ against a larger effect and the upper null θ≥+δ against a smaller effect. Both null components must be rejected.
  2. With α=0.05 and a large-sample normal estimate, construct a 90% interval using 1.645 standard errors on each side.
  3. For estimate +0.15, SE 0.20 and δ=0.50, the interval is [−0.179,+0.479]. Both endpoints lie inside [−0.50,+0.50], satisfying this equivalence criterion.

When the reasoning holds

  • The margin must reflect the economic decision and be set independently of the result. Widening it until the test passes undermines the evidence.
  • Use a valid standard error and interval procedure for the study’s dependence and sampling design. The 1.645 multiplier is a large-sample normal approximation.

Terms in this lesson
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Percentage point
The difference between two percentages. A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises one percentage point.
Standard error
An estimate of how much a statistic would vary across repeated samples under the assumed sampling process.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.

All notation examples

First example

Suppose an illustrative event study estimates a group difference of +1.2 percentage points against the local index with a 95% interval from −0.6 to +3.0. Against a regional index it estimates −0.3. The local estimate is compatible with both signs, and the estimated direction also changes with benchmark choice.

Reconstruct the entire analysis

A published table is the end of several transformations. A replicator needs the data vintage, selection dates, identifier map, currency conversion, exclusions, missing-value rules, model specification and code or equivalent formulas. A change in a benchmark file can change a result even with identical estimation steps.

Keep an observation count after each filter and the reasons records were removed. A bank that disappears during distress may be economically informative. Treating its absence as a routine missing value can alter the group the results describe.

Separate robustness from searching for significance

A robustness check changes a defensible modeling choice to assess sensitivity. State each choice’s economic interpretation, such as removing a concurrent event or replacing a domestic market benchmark with a regional one. A favorable specification selected from many after inspecting their signs has weaker evidential force.

Discuss confidence intervals alongside estimated effects. A wide interval containing zero can still include economically important gains and losses. To argue practical equivalence, define a meaningful margin beforehand and show the data rule out effects outside that margin under the assumptions of the test.

Report limits with numbers

If weighting changes the sign, show the distribution of weights and influential firms. If a benchmark alters the result, explain what type of common market movement each benchmark removes. Changes in the sample and changes in the model can occur together and should be disentangled where data allow.

A replication with new observations tests external stability. An exact reconstruction with the original data tests computational reproducibility. Both tasks add value, but success at one does not establish the other.

Method

Build the evidence table

  1. Tie each estimate to a sample

    Record dates, firm counts, group membership and the number of common observations to reveal composition differences.

  2. Report size and uncertainty

    State units, confidence interval construction and dependence assumptions. Include a practical reference magnitude for interpretation.

  3. Classify the comparison

    Label prespecified analysis, sensitivity analysis, exact replication and new-sample extension distinctly in the research narrative.

Worked case

A benchmark-sensitive bank ranking

An illustrative group difference is +2.0 percentage points against a domestic market, with standard error 1.5. Against a regional index on the same firms it is −0.4 points, with standard error 1.0. Use 1.96 for illustrative normal intervals.

Working

  1. The domestic interval is 2.0 ± 2.94, or −0.94 to +4.94. The regional interval is −0.4 ± 1.96, or −2.36 to +1.56.
  2. The signs and economic comparators differ. Neither interval excludes zero at this approximate confidence level. Neither proves that the underlying difference is negligible.
  3. A direct statistical test of the difference between estimates requires their covariance because they use the same outcomes. Comparing significance stars is insufficient.

Interpretation

A reader can defend a narrower claim that the estimated direction depends on benchmark choice in this sample and that both estimates are imprecise.

A common mistake

An insignificant result is evidence that two groups are identical.

How to correct it

Specify the largest difference that would be practically negligible. Examine whether the confidence region is narrow enough to exclude important alternatives under a suitable equivalence design.

Quick check
Which statement best reflects those results?

An interval containing zero can also contain economically meaningful differences. Reporting the interval and the alternative benchmark describes what the research can and cannot distinguish.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A replication obtains −1.0 point with standard error 0.6. The original obtained +0.5 using a larger sample and different data vintage.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate an illustrative normal 95% interval and interpret it.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Design a sequence that can isolate sources of the replication difference.

Part A 4 marks

The interval is −1.0 ± 1.176 = [−2.176, 0.176]. It includes zero and substantive negative effects. Its interpretation relies on the standard-error assumptions and normal approximation.

Part B 4 marks

Start with the original code and vintage, reproduce the original result, then change sample membership on the original vintage, then update data while holding sample and model fixed. Record inclusion changes and whether interactions among changes remain.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Your bank study finds an insignificant difference. What would you need to conclude that any difference is economically negligible?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask the committee to define the largest difference it regards as tolerable and then test whether both larger negative and larger positive effects are excluded. With a ±0.50-point margin our 90% interval fits narrowly inside at SE 0.20. I would report that margin and interval together with the specification and sample to make the claim interpretable.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What happens if the committee had prespecified a tighter ±0.25-point margin?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The same upper endpoint +0.479 exceeds +0.25, so equivalence is not established at that margin. A larger, better-measured sample or a more informative design might narrow uncertainty. The original result cannot simply be relabeled immaterial.
