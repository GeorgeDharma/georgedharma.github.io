Theory and terminology

Vector autoregression and Granger predictability

VAR · Granger causality tests · forecast spillovers

What you should be able to explain

  • Produce two-period-ahead forecasts recursively from a vector autoregression.
  • Use a nested equation test for incremental predictability and identify the extra assumptions needed for structural interpretation.

Forecast each variable with the same information set

A vector autoregression, or VAR, stacks several time-series equations together. Each outcome is regressed on its own past and the past of every included series. One series can forecast a second even if the second does little to forecast the first. Ordinary least squares can estimate each reduced-form equation when the lagged regressors are valid for forecasting.

Specify transformations, release times and lag length before estimation. A later closing market observed on the same calendar day might be unavailable at the early-closing market’s forecast origin. For an ordinary stable VAR in levels, the variables should be stationary under the specification. Cointegrated nonstationary levels can call for a vector error correction model that retains the long-run relationship.

Read rows as equations and columns as inputs

In a first-order VAR the first row of the coefficient matrix produces the next first outcome, and the second row produces the next second outcome. With variables centered and intercept zero, multiply the matrix by today's observed vector for a one-step forecast. For two steps multiply by the forecast vector again. Unknown future mean-zero surprises drop out of these conditional means.

A one-unit reduced-form innovation in the first component, with the second held fixed, propagates through the first column of the coefficient matrix at the next date. If the innovations tend to occur together, that hypothetical experiment may not represent an economically isolated event. A recursive ordering or an external instrument is an additional structural assumption that needs a credible market explanation.

What a Granger test can establish

To test whether funding adds predictive content for credit, compare the full credit equation with one that excludes all funding lags. Use the same estimation rows and the same other regressors, including credit's own lags. The restriction is a joint one if there are several funding lags. A significant coefficient is conditional on the chosen variables, sample and timing.

The familiar nested F reference below assumes the usual equation-level error conditions and an appropriate stationary specification. Robust Wald inference or a more specialized procedure may be needed under heteroskedasticity, breaks or nonstationarity. A common omitted news source can cause predictive lead–lag patterns without a causal transmission from the observed funding series.

Method

Build the result

x̂ₜ₊ₕ=Aʰxₜ; F=[(SSEᵣ−SSEᵤ)/q]/[SSEᵤ/(n−k)]

xₜ is today's column of centered series, A is the one-lag coefficient matrix, h is a forecast horizon in observation periods. SSEᵣ and SSEᵤ are restricted and unrestricted sums of squared equation errors, q the number of restrictions, n the common sample and k the full equation's number of estimated coefficients.

  1. Given xₜ, next period's expected innovation is zero. Taking conditional expectations gives x̂ₜ₊₁=Axₜ.
  2. Repeating the same conditional expectation gives x̂ₜ₊₂=A(Axₜ). This extrapolation assumes the estimated relationship persists.
  3. Excluding a set of lags can only increase in-sample SSE. Compare the increase per restriction to the unrestricted residual variance estimate, then apply the supplied F cutoff.
Worked case

Funding and credit spread departures from normal

Both market series are stationary deviations in basis points. In order (credit, funding), the estimated VAR rows are (0.50,0.20) and (−0.10,0.40), both with zero fitted intercepts. Today x=(10,−5). In the credit equation, removing funding's one lag raises SSE from 484 to 550 squared bp. There are 69 usable rows and three full-equation coefficients including the intercept. Use a 5% F cutoff of 3.99.

Working

  1. One step ahead, credit is 0.50×10+0.20×(−5)=4.0 bp and funding is −0.10×10+0.40×(−5)=−3.0 bp.
  2. Feed (4,−3) back into both equations. Two-step forecasts are (2.0−0.6,−0.4−1.2)=(1.4,−1.6) bp.
  3. The restricted fit loses 550−484=66 squared bp for one restriction. The full residual variance estimate is 484/(69−3)=7.333 squared bp.
  4. F=66/7.333=9.0 exceeds 3.99. Last period's funding spread adds predictive information to this fitted credit equation under the stated test.

Decision and interpretation

Retain the cross-lag for forecast evaluation. Any story that a policy-induced funding move caused a credit change also needs evidence that separates it from joint news and timing effects.

Quick check
What does rejecting the joint null on funding lags in the credit equation establish under the test assumptions?

The restrictions compare conditional predictive equations. Contemporaneous economic identification requires additional assumptions.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A two-series VAR for centered variables has rows (0.6,0.1) and (0.2,0.5) and current readings (0,10) bp. The first equation's full and restricted SSE are 80 and 82, n=83, k=3, q=1 and the 5% cutoff is 3.96. Forecast twice and interpret the test.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. One-step forecasts are (1,5) bp. The large second component contributes to both forecasts despite today's first component being zero.
  2. Two-step forecasts are (0.6×1+0.1×5, 0.2×1+0.5×5)=(1.1,2.7) bp.
  3. F=(82−80)/(80/80)=2.00, below 3.96. The fitted coefficient changes the forecasts, but this test gives insufficient evidence of incremental predictive content.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Does yesterday's funding strain help predict today's credit move after allowing for credit's own history?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I compare otherwise identical credit equations with and without lagged funding terms. Then I inspect joint forecasts and their out-of-sample performance. Predictive improvement describes incremental information. Interpreting an effect of a funding intervention requires further identification.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can permuting the labels change the numerical forecast or the claimed structural shock?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Consistently permuting components, rows and columns only relabels the reduced-form forecast. A recursively identified structural shock can change because reordering changes which variable is allowed to respond immediately.
