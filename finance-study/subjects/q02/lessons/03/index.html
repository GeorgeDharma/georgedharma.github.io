Inference under dependence

Choose uncertainty estimates that reflect heteroskedasticity, serial or cluster dependence. Merely adding more price observations does not create independent firms.

The starting point

A hundred daily returns from one bank share the same balance sheet, management and market environment. Treating every observation as an independent draw can understate uncertainty. Point estimates and standard errors answer different questions. The former measure the relationship, while the latter estimate how variable that estimate would be across comparable samples.

Heteroskedasticity means noise differs in size across observations. Serial dependence means errors at nearby dates move together. Clustered standard errors allow dependence inside specified groups under assumptions about independence across groups and enough independent clusters. Common day shocks may require time controls or a second clustering dimension.

Theory and method

Cluster-robust sandwich covariance

Cluster score · Clustered standard errors

Why do standard errors clustered by bank add up scores inside a bank before squaring them?

Branch approvals are affected by a shared bank credit committee. During a policy tightening one bank can produce many negative residuals together. Counting each residual as an independent surprise omits their cross-products. A cluster covariance estimate first aggregates score contributions within the bank, then measures dispersion of these bank-level contributions across banks. This preserves within-bank dependence under an asymptotic argument with sufficiently many independent clusters.

How the result is built

V̂ = (XᵀX)⁻¹ [Σg sg sgᵀ] (XᵀX)⁻¹, sg=Xgᵀeg

X is the full regression-design matrix, Xg its rows in cluster g, eg its fitted residual vector, sg the cluster score, V̂ the unadjusted one-way cluster covariance of OLS coefficients, and ᵀ transpose.

  1. OLS residual variation affects coefficients through the inverse of XᵀX. The middle expression measures variation of the aggregate scores driving that adjustment.
  2. Expanding sgsgᵀ includes cross-products of rows within the cluster, accommodating their dependence. Cross-cluster independence allows the cluster contributions to be summed.
  3. For an intercept-only regression with n=16 and four cluster residual sums −4,−2,1,5, the unadjusted variance is (16+4+1+25)/16² = 46/256. Its square root is about 0.424 outcome units.

When the reasoning holds

  • Clusters should capture the relevant dependence and be sufficiently independent across groups. Common date shocks across banks may need separate treatment.
  • Four groups illustrate the formula but give poor ordinary large-cluster inference. Consider design-based methods or carefully justified small-cluster adjustments and report the number of independent assignments.

Terms in this lesson
Standard error
An estimate of how much a statistic would vary across repeated samples under the assumed sampling process.
Correlation
A measure of how closely two series move together in a linear pattern.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

A panel contains 12 banks observed for 100 days. A new capital policy is imposed once on each bank, and bank shocks persist. The 1,200 rows provide more detail than 12 snapshots, but they do not create 1,200 independent policy assignments. With only 12 clusters, ordinary large-cluster approximations also need scrutiny.

Independent observations and independent shocks

A panel can contain thousands of rows but only a handful of distinct policy changes. Standard errors quantify sampling uncertainty under assumptions about how observations vary together. A common shock to several rows creates covariance terms that affect the variance of an average or coefficient.

For equally variable independent observations, variance of their average is σ²/n. With groups of m observations sharing a common within-group correlation ρ and independent groups, it becomes (σ²/n)[1 + (m − 1)ρ]. The bracket is a design effect under this simplified model.

Match the uncertainty estimator

Heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors allow error variance to differ across rows while typically relying on independent observations. A suitable autocorrelation-robust estimate handles serial dependence over dates. Clustering by firm permits arbitrary within-firm dependence subject to assumptions about many independent firms.

A market-wide shock on a given day creates dependence between firms. Calendar controls can absorb common additive shocks, while two-way clustering can address remaining date and firm dependence when both dimensions support inference. The choice follows how treatment and shocks are generated.

Few clusters and inference limits

Conventional cluster formulas rely on an increasing number of independent clusters, so ten groups observed for many dates can be challenging. Small-sample corrections, suitable resampling or design-based inference may help under their specific assumptions. Report the number and structure of clusters.

A robust standard error changes uncertainty around an estimate. It cannot correct a confounded coefficient or an outcome measured using future prices. Identification and sampling variation require separate arguments.

Method

Map a dependence structure

  1. Identify assignment

    List where treatment can vary independently and whether one decision is copied to several rows.

  2. Trace correlated shocks

    Consider borrower groups, firms, policy dates and common economic news. Specify a defensible clustering or time-series design.

  3. Show sensitivity

    Report independent group counts and how confidence intervals change under plausible dependence assumptions, alongside an unchanged point estimate when appropriate.

Worked case

Twenty branches per bank

An illustrative panel has 25 independent banks and 20 branch outcomes per bank. Each observation has standard deviation 10 and correlation 0.20 with other branches of its own bank. Treat all banks symmetrically.

Working

  1. Ignoring dependence gives 500 observations and a standard error of 10/√500 ≈ 0.447 for the overall mean.
  2. The design effect is 1 + 19 × 0.20 = 4.8. The standard error grows by √4.8 to roughly 0.980.
  3. The equivalent independent sample size is 500/4.8 ≈ 104 for this mean and this correlation model. It does not imply that there were 104 independent assignments.

Interpretation

The calculation illustrates why a bank-level lending policy cannot acquire 500 independent replications simply by recording its branches separately.

A common mistake

Use firm-clustered errors to remove all omitted firm characteristics from the slope.

How to correct it

Clustering changes the estimated uncertainty. Firm fixed effects or another model design address certain persistent confounders, and changing confounders may require further identification.

Quick check
An economy-wide shock moves every bank on the same date. What problem could remain after clustering only by bank?

Bank clustering addresses dependence inside banks. A common date shock cuts across the groups, so its treatment should follow the design and the number of available time periods.

Independent exam problem

A survey has 30 independent districts and 5 firms per district. The standard deviation per firm is 12, with within-district correlation 0.25.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate the standard error of the overall equal-weight mean with and without the simplified dependence adjustment.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Could a large national news shock break that calculation? Under what condition?

Part A 4 marks

With n = 150 the independence value is 12/√150 ≈ 0.980. The design effect is 1 + 4 × 0.25 = 2. The adjusted value is about 1.386.

Part B 4 marks

Yes. A shock affecting several districts creates cross-district dependence, which the formula assumes away. Describe whether the shock is conditioned on, removed by design or explicitly modeled.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why do standard errors clustered by bank add up scores inside a bank before squaring them?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Bank-level shocks can make many branch residuals point in the same direction. Aggregating before squaring retains the cross-products generated by that common shock. I would cluster at a level justified by assignment and dependence and report the number of banks. With four banks the formula is informative about mechanics, but a routine large-sample significance claim would be fragile.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Does the bank-clustered estimator also correct bias from lending rules targeted at distressed banks?

Compare with the follow-up answer

It changes uncertainty around the chosen coefficient. If targeting links the policy to an unobserved determinant of lending, identification can still fail. Address selection through policy design, a credible comparison, or appropriate pre-treatment controls separately.
