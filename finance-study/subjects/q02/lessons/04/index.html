Panels and fixed effects

Compare an entity with itself over time to absorb time-invariant differences. Common trends and time-varying confounders can remain.

The starting point

A panel follows the same firms over multiple periods. A firm fixed effect gives each firm its own baseline, absorbing persistent features such as long-standing business mix. The slope is then identified by changes within firms that are observed at more than one value of the explanatory variable.

Time fixed effects absorb shocks common to all firms on a given date. Neither kind automatically removes an unobserved change that affects one group more and coincides with the treatment. Also, a characteristic that stays constant within every firm cannot have a separate slope in a firm fixed-effects model.

Theory and method

Mundlak decomposition of within and between effects

Correlated random effects · Within estimator

Firms with high average leverage invest less, but firms raising their own leverage invest more. Is that a contradiction?

A cross-section compares different firms, while a panel also compares each firm with its earlier self. Mature companies can have chronically high leverage and weak investment growth, even as a temporary debt issue funds a factory expansion within one company. Splitting leverage into its firm mean and the departure from that mean exposes the two comparisons. The sign of one slope need not constrain the sign of the other.

How the result is built

yᵢₜ = a + βW(xᵢₜ−x̄ᵢ) + βB x̄ᵢ + uᵢₜ

i identifies a firm, t a date, y investment units, x leverage units, x̄ᵢ its sample firm mean, a an intercept, βW within-firm slope, βB between-firm slope and u unexplained outcome.

  1. Write x as its time average plus a centered deviation. The sum of centered deviations is zero within each firm.
  2. With slope controls handled consistently, the centered term uses only within-firm variation while the firm-mean term captures between-firm variation. The Mundlak formulation models the relation of the entity effect to regressor means.
  3. For two firms with x pairs (2,4) and (7,9) and y pairs (8,10) and (6,8), both within slopes are +1. The firm means (3,9) and (8,7) imply a between slope (7−9)/(8−3)=−0.4.

When the reasoning holds

  • A causal within interpretation requires credible exogeneity of within-entity changes. A new investment opportunity that triggers borrowing can drive both series.
  • For a random-effects interpretation, specify the conditional model for entity effects and include appropriate regressor means. Demeaning or Mundlak terms alone do not resolve endogenous time-varying shocks.
Terms in this lesson
Percentage point
The difference between two percentages. A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises one percentage point.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Regression
A way of estimating how an outcome varies with one or more observed inputs.
How to read the notation
Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.
A slope or exposure · β
In a regression a coefficient describes how the fitted outcome changes with one unit of its input, holding the other included inputs fixed. A market beta of 1.2 gives a fitted change of 1.2 percentage points for a one-point change in market return.

All notation examples

First example

A bank’s lending grows from 4% to 8% after installing new software. Another bank’s lending rises from 3% to 6% over the same dates. The first bank’s within change is 4 percentage points. Subtracting the other bank’s 3-point movement gives a 1-point comparative change, whose interpretation still depends on assumptions.

What fixed effects remove

Imagine comparing old industrial banks with young online banks. Their persistent business models may influence both funding mix and profits. An entity fixed effect gives each bank a separate baseline. Coefficients then use changes around the bank’s own baseline rather than permanent between-bank differences.

For yᵢₜ = αᵢ + βxᵢₜ + eᵢₜ, take the time mean for each bank and subtract it. This gives yᵢₜ − ȳᵢ = β(xᵢₜ − x̄ᵢ) + (eᵢₜ − ēᵢ). The unknown αᵢ disappears because it is constant over time.

Time effects and identification

Date fixed effects similarly absorb an unrestricted intercept for each date. They can capture a market-wide movement affecting everyone equally in the model. A policy that hits every bank at exactly the same time then shares the same pattern as the date effects and has no separately identified level coefficient.

A staggered policy or different group exposures may leave usable variation, but the remaining identifying assumptions must be credible. A changing bank-specific shock correlated with rollout can still bias the coefficient. Fixed effects do not automatically control for such shocks.

Distinguish within and between regressions

A bank whose capital ratio never changes contributes no within variation for that regressor. Its many observations can still help estimate other quantities. Similarly, a country indicator fixed within a bank is absorbed by bank effects. Changing country rules can generate a different estimable variable.

With exactly two periods, subtracting one date from the other eliminates a constant bank effect directly. An intercept in the differenced regression represents a common time change and helps link two-period fixed effects to a comparative change analysis.

Method

Construct a within estimate

  1. Compute entity changes

    Identify the first and second observations for each bank, then subtract earlier values from later values using the same units.

  2. Compare changes

    Fit the proposed relation between bank-specific changes and consider any common drift in outcomes across the observations.

  3. Audit time variation

    Check how many firms changed the regressor and whether other events coincided specifically with the changes used to estimate the coefficient.

Worked case

Two banks adopt at different times

A treated bank’s lending changes from 10 to 16 after adoption of a screening system. A bank without adoption rises from 8 to 11. Assume a two-period model with bank and date intercepts and an adoption indicator.

Working

  1. The treated change is 6 and the comparison change is 3. The persistent starting gap of 2 disappears after differencing.
  2. The common date change estimated from the comparison is 3. The fitted adoption coefficient for the differential change is 6 − 3 = 3 loan units.
  3. This number measures the additional change in the treated bank. Attributing it to screening assumes the untreated trend would have matched and no concurrent treated-only shock occurred.

Interpretation

If a new branch also opened only in the treated bank, the estimate combines any effect of screening with any independent branch-opening effect.

A common mistake

Adding enough fixed effects always separates a national policy from the national business cycle.

How to correct it

If both vary identically across all sampled entities on each date, date effects absorb their common time pattern. Seek credible additional variation or narrow the claim.

Quick check
Every bank remains in the same country during the study. What happens to a country indicator after including a full set of bank fixed effects?

Country membership is constant within each bank and can be reconstructed from its bank indicators. Time-varying country exposures can pose different questions if the design identifies them.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Bank A’s fee and volume change by (+1, +4). B’s change by (0, +2). A two-period model allows a common date change.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Recover the fee coefficient and time change.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    If both banks raised fees by exactly one point, could the coefficient still be separated from the date change?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

The comparison bank identifies a common volume gain of 2. The remaining treated gain is 4 − 2 = 2 per one-point fee change. Within differencing removes permanent bank baselines.

Part B 4 marks

No. Each change would combine the same one-unit fee movement with the shared time effect. Their separate contributions lack independent variation in these two banks and periods.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Firms with high average leverage invest less, but firms raising their own leverage invest more. Is that a contradiction?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

There is no contradiction. The within estimate uses a company’s deviation from its own average, whereas the between estimate uses differences in company means. Here each within slope is positive one and the between slope is negative 0.4. I would report which comparison matches the decision and investigate whether financing follows unobserved investment opportunities.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can this decomposition identify the causal effect of a country indicator that never changes within a firm?

Compare with the follow-up answer

A country level indicator has no within-firm variation. A between-firm estimate can compare countries under additional assumptions about persistent differences. It cannot inherit a within-firm causal interpretation simply because the dataset has multiple dates.
