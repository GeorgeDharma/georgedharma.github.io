Regression and omitted variables

Derive a slope as an association conditional on included variables. A missing factor related to both exposure and outcome can bias the estimate.

The starting point

If secured loans appear to default more often than unsecured loans, the lender may be demanding collateral from customers already judged riskier. A regression slope summarizes how an outcome varies with an included predictor, conditional on other variables in the model. Borrower selection can alter its interpretation.

An omitted variable biases a slope for a proposed causal effect when it both helps determine the outcome and varies systematically with the included predictor, after relevant controls. Adding a relevant pre-existing risk measure can address part of the problem. A poorly measured control or another hidden characteristic can leave bias.

Theory and method

Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem

Partial regression · Residual-on-residual regression

How can a lender recover the coefficient on loan spread after controlling for industry without explicitly interpreting each industry intercept?

Suppose industrial and retail firms face systematically different lending conditions. To compare spreads within an industry, first remove the industry mean from both the spread and the loss outcome. The remaining values describe how each firm differs from its own sector. Frisch–Waugh–Lovell says the slope from these residuals matches the coefficient in the multiple regression with industry indicators. This is an algebraic statement about a linear fit rather than a claim that all risk differences disappeared.

How the result is built

β̂x = (x̃ᵀỹ)/(x̃ᵀx̃), with x̃=MZx and ỹ=MZy

x is the spread, y the loss outcome, Z the control columns including the intercept, MZ the projection removing their linear fit, tildes residuals, ᵀ transpose, and β̂x units of loss per spread unit.

  1. Project both columns onto the complement of the controls. With industry indicators this means subtracting the relevant group mean.
  2. Within two groups suppose x residuals are (−1,1,−2,2) and y residuals (−1,1,−3,3). Their cross-product sum is 14 and x squared sum is 10.
  3. The conditional slope is 1.4. Re-estimating the original regression with separate industry intercepts produces exactly the same slope when model, weights and rows match.

When the reasoning holds

  • There must be variation in x left after the controls. A spread determined exactly by industry yields a zero denominator.
  • The full and partial regressions must use the same rows, weighting and control space. For inference use degrees of freedom and covariance treatment appropriate to the full model.
Terms in this lesson
Regression
A way of estimating how an outcome varies with one or more observed inputs.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
Percentage point
The difference between two percentages. A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises one percentage point.
How to read the notation
A slope or exposure · β
In a regression a coefficient describes how the fitted outcome changes with one unit of its input, holding the other included inputs fixed. A market beta of 1.2 gives a fitted change of 1.2 percentage points for a one-point change in market return.
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
A change · Δ
ΔS commonly means the new asset price less the old price. A move from €50 to €53 gives ΔS = €3. In options, delta can also name a sensitivity. A delta of 0.6 means a €1 rise in the underlying predicts about a €0.60 rise in the option, for a small move.

First example

Suppose more leverage raises default risk and highly leveraged firms are charged larger spreads. A regression of default on spread alone mixes borrower risk with any effect of expensive borrowing. Even a precisely estimated positive slope cannot untangle these channels by itself.

Define conditional association

In a regression of loan losses on interest rates and borrower characteristics, the rate coefficient measures a fitted difference conditional on the included characteristics. Comparing borrowers at the same leverage can differ from comparing all high-rate loans with all low-rate loans.

A causal interpretation requires an assumption that unobserved determinants remaining in the error do not vary systematically with rate after controls. Contract negotiation, borrower quality and lender screening can each violate it.

Derive omitted-variable bias

Suppose the true model is y = α + βx + γz + u with u uncorrelated with x. Taking covariance with x gives Cov(x,y) = βVar(x) + γCov(x,z). Dividing by Var(x) gives the simple-regression population slope β + γδ, where δ is the slope from regressing z on x.

The two components needed for bias are an association between the omitted characteristic z and outcome y, and an association between z and included regressor x. Units matter because δ translates one unit of x into units of z.

Choose controls using timing

A characteristic determined before a financing decision, such as last year’s leverage, may help separate borrower selection. A characteristic resulting from that decision, such as subsequent investment, may lie on the causal pathway. Controlling for it can change the effect being estimated.

A control measured with error may also leave residual confounding. Report what variation remains and why it is informative. Check whether there is overlap, such as high- and low-rate loans for borrowers with comparable starting risk.

Method

Diagnose a regression comparison

  1. Draw the proposed links

    Specify which characteristic influences the loan’s price and the later outcome and when each is observed.

  2. Multiply the signs

    Write the sign and units of γ and δ. Their product gives the direction of the omitted component under the simple linear assumptions.

  3. Test what is measurable

    Compare specifications on the same borrowers. A coefficient change can reflect sample composition as well as omitted variables if missing data alter membership.

Worked case

A pricing-policy coefficient

A bank estimates loss in percentage points on the contractual spread in percentage points. Suppose the structural spread slope is +0.20, each risk-score unit adds 0.50 loss points, and a one-point higher spread is associated with a 1.4-unit higher initial risk score.

Working

  1. The omitted score contributes 0.50 × 1.4 = 0.70 additional loss points per spread point.
  2. The population slope without the score is +0.20 + 0.70 = +0.90. Most of the simple slope comes from differences in borrowers.
  3. If otherwise the same lender preferentially grants risky customers concessions, δ could be negative. With δ = −1 the simple slope would be −0.30, even with the same underlying +0.20 effect.

Interpretation

This illustration assumes linear relationships and no remaining covariance of x and u. In a real credit file those are claims to defend using lending procedures and data.

A common mistake

A coefficient retains the same meaning whenever another control is added.

How to correct it

Clarify whether the new variable removes confounding, lies downstream of treatment or changes the observations included. A numerically smaller result can have several explanations.

Quick check
Suppose true default risk rises with leverage, and a bank grants its highest leveraged borrowers lower rates because of negotiated concessions. What direction does omitted leverage push the estimated rate slope, all else equal?

Leverage raises default risk but is negatively related to the observed rate in this setup. Leaving it out attributes some high risk to low rates, pulling the estimated rate coefficient downward.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Trading volume is regressed on a fee measured in percentage points. Assume the true fee effect is −4 volume units, prior demand adds 3 units per score point and demand rises 2 score points per fee point.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Derive the fee-only slope.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Does adding realized post-fee volume growth as a control solve demand selection? Explain.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

The omitted contribution is 3 × 2 = +6 volume units per fee point. Adding the true −4 produces a simple slope of +2. A positive observed slope can coexist with a negative structural fee effect.

Part B 4 marks

It may use a consequence of fees or overlap with the outcome. Use appropriately timed demand measures or a credible design. Explain the required conditional independence or other identifying assumption.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a lender recover the coefficient on loan spread after controlling for industry without explicitly interpreting each industry intercept?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would residualize spread and loss against exactly the same complete industry control set, then regress the second residual on the first. The answer here is 14 divided by ten. This shows which loan-level variation identifies the spread slope. A borrower characteristic varying within an industry can still confound a proposed causal interpretation.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if all high-spread loans in a sector also have lower collateral coverage?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Industry controls leave this within-sector relationship intact. Depending on the causal question I would add a defensible pre-loan collateral measure or use a credible design for spread assignment. FWL explains the algebra of conditioning and cannot supply that identifying assumption.
