Decide whether to difference a financial series

Distinguish stationarity around a deterministic trend from a stochastic trend.

Theory and terminology

The augmented Dickey–Fuller test

ADF · unit root · differencing · stationarity

What you should be able to explain

  • Distinguish stationarity around a deterministic trend from a stochastic trend.
  • Use the correct augmented Dickey–Fuller reference distribution and explain an inconclusive result.

What would a shock do?

In a stationary level series the distribution is stable through time under the model, and a transitory shock eventually loses its effect. A trend-stationary series fluctuates around a predictable time trend. Removing that deterministic trend leaves a stationary series. For a random walk, an innovation changes the level permanently. Removing a fitted straight line leaves the stochastic trend in place.

Confusing these processes matters. Two unrelated random walks can yield a large regression coefficient and conventional significance. Conversely, differencing a stationary level discards information about the level to which it returns and can introduce unnecessary short-run dynamics. The economic object, its measurement regime and how shocks propagate guide the specification.

Rearrange persistence as a testable coefficient

Suppose a level equals an intercept plus ρ times its previous level plus noise. Subtract yesterday's level from both sides. The coefficient on yesterday's level becomes γ=ρ−1. A unit root corresponds to γ=0. A negative γ is the direction of return towards a stable level or trend, assuming the other autoregressive roots behave appropriately.

Augment the equation with lagged changes to capture short-run serial dependence. Select a defensible lag order and inspect remaining residual serial correlation. A sluggishly mean-reverting series may look very much like a random walk over a short sample. Failure to reject a unit root therefore does not establish it as fact.

Why an ordinary t table fails

Under the null, the lagged level accumulates many past shocks. Its variation grows through the sample. Although the estimated coefficient divided by its standard error looks like a familiar t statistic, its null distribution is different. An intercept, a trend or neither changes that distribution. Match the reference values to the actual equation, sample convention and chosen test.

Choose deterministic terms before looking at which version rejects. A known policy break or redefinition of a spread can mimic persistence. Inspect the series around documented dates and compare forecasting performance under plausible alternatives. A unit-root decision on a single series is separate from a cointegration test on fitted residuals, which needs its own critical values.

Method

Build the result

Δyₜ=a+bt+γyₜ₋₁+ΣⱼφⱼΔyₜ₋ⱼ+uₜ; ADF=γ̂/SE(γ̂)

Δyₜ is the change in level y over one period ending at t. a is an intercept, b is trend per period, φⱼ weights lagged changes, γ is level adjustment, uₜ is the innovation. A hat marks an estimate and SE its standard error. The sum runs over the selected lags.

  1. The elementary AR(1) rearrangement gives γ=ρ−1 and therefore γ=0 at a unit root. In an augmented equation γ summarizes the level effect after accounting for lagged changes.
  2. Estimate the equation with the planned deterministic terms. Divide the estimated level coefficient by its standard error. Use the critical value for this exact specification.
  3. Reject the unit-root null against the stationary alternative when the statistic is more negative than the critical value. Report the assumptions and residual checks alongside the decision.
Worked case

A monthly funding spread under two economic stories

A treasury team has 144 monthly spreads measured in basis points, with no known definition change. It pre-selects an intercept and two lagged changes. The estimated γ is −0.170, standard error 0.055. The supplied 5% critical value is −2.90. A separate model with an intercept and trend estimates −0.130, SE 0.050, with cutoff −3.45.

Working

  1. For the primary specification, ADF=−0.170/0.055=−3.09. Compare it with −2.90 on the left tail.
  2. The primary equation rejects a unit root at 5%. This is evidence consistent with mean reversion around a level after the fitted short-run dynamics.
  3. For the trend specification, the statistic is −0.130/0.050=−2.60. It is above −3.45, so the trend version fails to reject.
  4. Report both results and the rationale for the pre-selected level model. Inspect residual serial correlation, breaks and out-of-sample spread forecasts. The two estimates come from separate fits.

Decision and interpretation

Use the stationary-level candidate for a documented trial if the constant-level economic case and residual checks hold. The sensitivity result weakens confidence in a permanent classification.

Quick check
The ADF statistic is −3.10 against the supplied cutoff −3.45 for the chosen model. What follows?

The rejection region is to the left of −3.45. A negative coefficient alone is insufficient to distinguish a unit root from a highly persistent stationary alternative.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A researcher pre-specifies an intercept and a time trend for a 15-year monthly price index. The fitted level coefficient is −0.105 with SE 0.035. The supplied 5% trend cutoff is −3.43. The intercept-only cutoff is −2.89. Decide how to report the result.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Compute −0.105/0.035=−3.00. The fitted model includes a trend, so use −3.43.
  2. Since −3.00 exceeds −3.43, the test fails to reject the unit root. The intercept-only cutoff cannot be applied to this regression.
  3. Keep a differenced specification in consideration, check lag adequacy and known breaks, and compare forecasts. This finite-sample result does not prove the index is a random walk.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Should a Treasury analyst model a funding spread in levels or model its change?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I ask what a shock should do economically and whether a deterministic trend belongs in the level. I test a unit root against that alternative using lagged changes and the appropriate critical value. Persistent or conflicting evidence calls for comparing both specifications on future periods.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the series is stationary around a deterministic trend, will differencing always improve a forecast?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. A fitted level trend can retain long-horizon information. Differencing a stationary deviation can add moving-average dependence and create extra estimation error. Compare models against predeclared forecast criteria.
