Event studies

Define announcement, estimation and event windows before examining results. Allow for market exposure, thin trading, overlapping events and correlated security returns.

The starting point

An event study asks how a security moved around news relative to a normal-return model. Set an announcement time, identify the first tradable session and estimate the normal relationship using an earlier window separated from the event. An abnormal return equals the observed return minus the model’s expected return.

Sum the abnormal returns across a prespecified short window to obtain a cumulative abnormal return, or CAR. Other announcements, delayed trading and common shocks can contaminate the interpretation. For a group of stocks reacting to one event, uncertainty calculations should also account for their tendency to move together.

Theory and method

Market-model event-study prediction error

Abnormal return · MacKinlay event-study methodology

Why is uncertainty in a one-day abnormal return sometimes greater than ordinary residual volatility?

A listed bank reacts to a policy surprise on a day when the whole market also moves. The research team first estimates a normal market relationship on earlier dates and then predicts the bank’s normal return on the event day. Even if the model is correct, a future residual is random and the historical market sensitivity was itself estimated. A market move far from the estimation-window average amplifies this second source of uncertainty.

How the result is built

Var(AR₀ | X)=σ²[1+1/n+(Rm,₀−R̄m)²/Sxx]

AR₀ is event-day abnormal return, σ² the market-model innovation variance, n number of independent estimation dates, Rm,₀ the event market return, R̄m its estimation mean, Sxx the sum of squared estimation market-return deviations, and X the fixed regressor data.

  1. A future prediction error equals the future innovation minus the normal-return estimation error. Under independent errors their variances add.
  2. The OLS uncertainty in the fitted mean at a new market move is σ²[1/n+(Rm,₀−R̄m)²/Sxx]. Add one σ² for the unexpected event-day outcome.
  3. With σ=1%, n=25, R̄m=0, Sxx=0.01 in decimal-return squared, a +2% market move yields factor 1+0.04+0.04=1.08. The event-day standard deviation is 1.0392%.

When the reasoning holds

  • The simple formula assumes a stable linear model, homoskedastic errors and an event observation independent of estimation residuals. Event-induced volatility can invalidate it.
  • This is a single-security prediction error. Multiple securities reacting to one event require treatment of cross-sectional dependence for group inference.

Terms in this lesson
Percentage point
The difference between two percentages. A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises one percentage point.
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
How to read the notation
Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.
A slope or exposure · β
In a regression a coefficient describes how the fitted outcome changes with one unit of its input, holding the other included inputs fixed. A market beta of 1.2 gives a fitted change of 1.2 percentage points for a one-point change in market return.

All notation examples

First example

For a bank with daily model intercept zero and market sensitivity 1.2, a +1% market day implies an expected +1.2% return. The actual bank return is −0.3%, so its abnormal return is −1.5 percentage points. If the next day contributes +0.4 points, the two-day CAR is −1.1 points.

Define news and normal returns

A share’s ordinary market exposure can create a gain on an announcement date even if the news changes nothing for that company. A market model estimates Rᵢₜ = αᵢ + βᵢRₘₜ + εᵢₜ over an earlier estimation window. Its fitted normal return supplies a benchmark for the event.

Abnormal return ARᵢₜ is observed return less fitted normal return. A short-window cumulative abnormal return sums these deviations. This arithmetic convention measures short-window deviations in return points. A compounded buy-and-hold comparison is a different statistic, especially over long horizons.

Design the window before inspecting returns

News released after a close usually first enters the next tradable session. Match exchange holidays, market time zones and trading suspensions to the announcement. A wide window can capture slow incorporation or leakage, but it can also admit unrelated earnings or policy news.

Estimation should avoid including event reactions in the normal-return fit. Fit a market model with adequate historical observations and consider whether its relation is stable. An alternative local or regional market index changes the definition of normal performance and may change the sign of the estimated effect.

Inference for a common event

Average abnormal returns across firms only after defining weights and missing returns. Common policy news can induce correlated residuals across banks, so cross-sectional sample size can overstate independent information. Uncertainty from the estimation of normal returns can also matter for inference.

A shareholder response concerns the market’s revision to expected value at announcement time. It cannot establish realized long-term profitability, welfare effects or managerial intentions alone. Check for competing announcements before drawing an event-specific interpretation.

Method

Build an event record

  1. Timestamp the release

    Identify local exchanges, first tradable session, estimation interval and declared event window.

  2. Calculate the counterfactual return

    Apply the fitted intercept and sensitivity to each day’s observed market move. Keep signs and percentage-point units visible.

  3. Assess attribution

    Sum within-security abnormal returns, aggregate across firms under stated weights and document concurrent news and dependence.

Worked case

A two-day index announcement

An Indonesian bank has estimated daily intercept 0.10% and market beta 1.2. The market gains 1% on day zero and falls 0.5% on day one. The bank returns +1.8% and −1.0% respectively.

Working

  1. On day zero expected return is 0.10% + 1.2 × 1% = 1.30%, giving abnormal return +0.50 percentage points.
  2. On day one expected return is 0.10% − 0.60% = −0.50%. Actual −1.0% gives another abnormal return of −0.50 points.
  3. The two-day CAR is zero. An investor still experienced nonzero daily price movements, and a one-day study would have reported a positive reaction.

Interpretation

A result reversing between horizons should lead the analyst to check event timing, news on day one and how the window was selected.

observed = [0.005, -0.010, 0.003]
market = [0.002, -0.015, 0.004]
alpha, beta = 0.001, 1.2
abnormal = [r - (alpha + beta * m) for r, m in zip(observed, market)]
print(f"{100 * sum(abnormal):.2f}%")

The three-day cumulative abnormal return is +0.58%. The stock fell on day 0 and still outperformed its predicted return. To make a statistical claim, estimate the uncertainty in that gap and investigate other simultaneous news. One window is evidence of an association.

A common mistake

A positive CAR proves that the announcement caused a permanent increase in shareholder wealth.

How to correct it

Describe a benchmark-relative short-window price reaction and its uncertainty. Establishing a permanent effect or isolating a concurrent announcement requires additional data and a separate design.

Quick check
An announcement is released two hours after the local exchange closes on Tuesday. With no after-hours trading, where can the first regular-session reaction appear?

Daily returns must line up with when investors could trade. Using the release’s calendar date without the exchange calendar can assign the reaction to the wrong session.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A firm has α = 0 and β = 0.8. Its two returns are +0.6% and +0.5%, and its market returns are +1% and zero. A sector announcement overlaps the second day.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate the two abnormal returns and the CAR.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Explain what a separate one-day and a sector-benchmark result would test.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Expected returns are +0.8% and zero. Abnormal returns are −0.2 and +0.5 points, giving a two-day CAR of +0.3 points.

Part B 4 marks

A one-day estimate studies the first reaction and avoids the second day’s overlap if timing permits. A sector benchmark asks whether the firm moved beyond sector-wide returns and can absorb part of the event if it affects the whole sector.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why is uncertainty in a one-day abnormal return sometimes greater than ordinary residual volatility?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would include both the bank’s event-day innovation and uncertainty in its fitted normal return. Under the classical market model the latter increases when the event market move is far from the training average. In the supplied sample a two-point market move yields 1.039% uncertainty against 1% residual volatility. Correlated banks and concurrent news require additional design work.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Does lengthening the estimation window always improve the interpretation?

Compare with the follow-up answer

More stable observations generally reduce parameter uncertainty, but an older period can span a different business mix, trading regime or beta. Check parameter stability, thin trading and announcement contamination rather than choosing a window by size alone.
