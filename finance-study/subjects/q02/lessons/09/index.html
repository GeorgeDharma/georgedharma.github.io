Forecast comparison

Train on earlier information and evaluate on later dates. Compare with a naive rule and report performance across regimes and horizons.

The starting point

A forecast is a decision made with information available at a particular time. Preserve this timing during evaluation. At a sequence of historical origins, fit using earlier data and score forecasts on ensuing realizations. Use the same targets, information cutoffs and forecast horizon for the candidate model and benchmark.

Mean absolute error averages error magnitudes. Root mean squared error gives extra weight to large misses. The metric should reflect the consequence of the forecasting error. A model can improve one metric while making unusually large mistakes, and strong average performance can depend on a single period.

Theory and method

Diebold–Mariano forecast comparison

Loss differential · Heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent variance

A new model reduces average forecast loss by 0.30. Does that difference provide statistical evidence of better prediction?

Two forecasts on the same dates face the same difficult and easy outcomes. Comparing their losses date by date exploits that pairing. The Diebold–Mariano approach asks whether expected loss differs for the specified forecast procedures, horizon and loss function. Forecasts with overlapping targets often generate serially dependent loss differences. The sampling risk of their average must therefore reflect both contemporaneous variance and dependence across dates.

How the result is built

DM = d̄ / √(Ω̂/n), Ω = γ₀ + 2Σₖ₌₁∞ γₖ

dₜ = loss of A minus loss of B at date t, d̄ the sample mean, n number of forecast origins, γₖ autocovariance of d at lag k, Ω its long-run variance, Ω̂ a suitable estimate and DM the comparison statistic.

  1. Align forecast targets and calculate each date’s paired loss differential. Positive values favor B because B incurred less loss.
  2. Estimate the long-run variance with a dependence-aware method and a justified lag rule. Divide it by n to obtain the estimated variance of the mean loss difference.
  3. With n=100, d̄=0.30 and Ω̂=2.25, the standard error is 0.15 and DM=2.00. An approximate two-sided normal comparison is borderline near 5% and depends on the design.

When the reasoning holds

  • The loss-differential process needs appropriate stationarity and weak dependence for the usual asymptotic test. A break can make a single pooled mean misleading.
  • Nested models, repeated tuning and small samples can change the reference distribution. A standard DM number is not a universal model-selection correction.
Terms in this lesson
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
How to read the notation
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

Across three future dates the candidate’s absolute errors are 1, 1 and 7. The benchmark’s errors are 3, 3 and 4. Their mean absolute errors are 3 versus 3.33, favoring the candidate. Their root mean squared errors are √17 ≈ 4.12 versus √(34/3) ≈ 3.37, favoring the benchmark.

Define the forecasting contract

A treasury team’s one-week forecast must use information available when cash is committed. Label each forecast by its origin, target, horizon and actual release time. Otherwise a model that sees revised data or later asset prices can appear more accurate than a live forecast could be.

For a fair benchmark use the same origins, horizon, target definition and realized observations. A naive rule can be a last observation for a level series, a seasonal previous value for seasonal demand, or zero change when short-horizon returns resist prediction.

Choose a loss with an economic meaning

Absolute error penalizes a miss in direct proportion to its size, while squared error makes a double-size miss four times as expensive. Minimizing expected absolute error selects a conditional median, while minimizing expected squared error selects a conditional mean under finite moments.

For asymmetric costs, a quantile forecast may be preferable. Cash shortfalls can cost more than equal surpluses. Stating the loss function before evaluation avoids switching criteria after seeing which model looks better. Percent errors become unstable when realized values approach zero.

Compare paired, chronologically valid errors

Rolling-origin evaluation trains through each origin and predicts later values. Compute the loss difference for each date between candidate and benchmark. Pairing removes differences in how difficult individual dates were for both methods and makes the comparison interpretable.

Overlapping multi-day targets can create serially correlated loss differentials. An uncertainty calculation should reflect that dependence. Report performance across plausible regimes, alongside the pooled comparison, without selecting the best period as the sole headline result.

Method

Evaluate the decision rule

  1. Freeze the information set

    For each forecast restore only data released by its origin. Fit all transformations on permitted past observations.

  2. Score the same realizations

    Calculate errors using identical units, horizon and decision loss for both methods. Keep a dated paired record.

  3. Inspect influential dates

    Identify whether the apparent improvement reflects broad gains or a small group of outliers. Report both persistent and episodic findings.

Worked case

A costly cash shortfall

Three weekly cash needs are 100, 90 and 120. Model A predicts 105, 95 and 100. Model B predicts 95, 100 and 115. Underpredicting costs two units per unit of shortfall. Overpredicting costs one per unit of excess.

Working

  1. Model A overpredicts by 5 and 5, then underpredicts by 20. Its total loss is 5 + 5 + 40 = 50.
  2. Model B underpredicts by 5, overpredicts by 10 and underpredicts by 5. Its total is 10 + 10 + 10 = 30.
  3. Mean absolute errors are 10 for A and 20/3 ≈ 6.67 for B. Here both rankings agree, but the stated treasury objective gives the 50 versus 30 comparison operational meaning.

Interpretation

Three origins provide limited evidence about performance during a crisis. Preserve the forecasts and assess later dates before asserting a stable advantage.

A common mistake

Select the benchmark or the loss function on the test period that produces the clearest win.

How to correct it

Choose a feasible benchmark and loss relevant to the decision before testing. Document alternative criteria as sensitivity results and retain an independent period if redesign follows.

Quick check
A desk’s stated objective is to minimize squared forecast errors. Which method wins this three-date comparison?

The candidate’s large miss contributes 49 squared units. Under the chosen loss function the benchmark’s squared-error total of 34 beats the candidate’s 51.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Absolute errors on four dates are (0, 0, 0, 8) for A and (2, 2, 2, 3) for B.

  1. Part A · 5 marks

    Under MAE and RMSE, which wins? Show both comparisons.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What should be checked before calculating a standard error on weekly losses from overlapping four-week targets?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 5 marks

MAE is 2 versus 2.25, favoring A. RMSE is √(64/4) = 4 for A and √(21/4) ≈ 2.29 for B, favoring B.

Part B 3 marks

Adjacent forecasts can share realized outcomes. Examine serial dependence in paired loss differences and use an appropriate dependence-aware standard error, block design or nonoverlapping origins under explicit assumptions.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A new model reduces average forecast loss by 0.30. Does that difference provide statistical evidence of better prediction?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would first establish a common decision loss and matched out-of-sample origins. With a long-run variance estimate of 2.25 the 0.30 gain is two standard errors in this example. I would report the magnitude, its uncertainty and behavior across regimes. A large test statistic alone would not establish meaningful implementation savings or undo prior model selection.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If errors are evaluated on many alternative losses and you report only the lowest p-value, can the same reference distribution be used for that winner?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The single prespecified comparison no longer describes the selection process. Report the full family of tested objectives, justify the operational primary loss and use an appropriate multiple-comparison design or new untouched data for the selected procedure.
