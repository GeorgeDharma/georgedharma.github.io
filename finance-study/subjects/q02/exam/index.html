Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A bank-lending policy and the unit of analysis

A city pilots an underwriting rule. Loan approval rises from 52% to 62% there and from 45% to 51% in a comparison city. For a separate randomized invitation, take-up rises from 30% to 50%, and annual firm profit rises by an average €180 for invited firms.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

An announcement and shared market shocks

An announcement arrives after Monday’s market close. Tuesday and Wednesday are trading days. A stock with α = 0.1% and β = 1.5 returns +1.4% on Tuesday and −0.2% on Wednesday. Market returns are +0.6% and −0.4%. Twenty banks are observed around the same news.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A mean forecast and a variance forecast

A centered spread in basis points obeys xₜ = 0.5xₜ₋₁ + εₜ. Its latest value is +12. For a separate return series, daily variance obeys hₜ₊₁ = 0.00002 + 0.1εₜ² + 0.8hₜ. The latest return innovation is −0.04 and hₜ = 0.0002.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

A sample selection and replication audit

Three branches begin with loan balances of 50, 30 and 20 and finish at 55, 27 and 30. A vendor drops the last branch because it changed its identifier. A researcher reports thousands of branch-day rows from six banks as independent policy observations.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A bank-lending policy and the unit of analysis

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the comparative approval change and distinguish it from the pilot city’s observed change.

The observed pilot change is +10 points (1), the comparison change +6 (1), giving a difference-in-differences estimate of +4 percentage points (1).

Part B 3 marks

State identifying conditions and name one concrete threat in this design.

Parallel untreated trends are required (1), along with relevant conditions such as no anticipation or spillovers (1). A subsidy launched only in the pilot city at the same date could independently improve approval (1).

Part C 4 marks

Calculate the invitation ratio, identify the group it can describe and explain why randomizing an invitation alone is insufficient.

The ratio is 180/(0.50 − 0.30) = €900 (1). It can describe compliers, firms induced to borrow by the invitation (1), under relevance, independence and monotonicity (1) and exclusion, which would fail if invitations also offered useful business services (1).

Case 2 · 10 marks

An announcement and shared market shocks

Part A 4 marks

Define day zero and calculate both abnormal returns and the CAR.

Tuesday is day zero (1). Normal returns are +1.0% and −0.5% (1). Abnormal returns are +0.4 and +0.3 percentage points (1). CAR is +0.7 points (1).

Part B 3 marks

Explain why twenty banks may provide less independent information than twenty unrelated events, and name a practical check.

A single common event can correlate bank residuals (1). Measure dependence using a defensible portfolio, historical resampling or other suitable inference design (1). Document competing bank or macro news (1).

Part C 3 marks

An alternative model gives CAR −0.1 with an approximate interval [−1.0, +0.8]. What should a report say?

The direction changes under the alternative definition of normal return (1), the interval includes zero and economically meaningful effects (1), and comparing the two estimates formally requires their dependence or a paired analysis (1).

Case 3 · 10 marks

A mean forecast and a variance forecast

Part A 3 marks

Give the spread’s one- and two-step forecasts and explain their interpretation.

They are +6 and +3 basis points (2). These are conditional means given zero-mean future innovations, so realized spreads need not follow them (1).

Part B 4 marks

Calculate the return series’ next variance and standard deviation and its long-run variance.

Next variance is 0.00002 + 0.1 × 0.0016 + 0.8 × 0.0002 = 0.00034 (2). Next standard deviation is about 1.84% (1). Long-run variance is 0.00002/(1 − 0.9) = 0.0002 (1).

Part C 3 marks

Describe a fair out-of-sample assessment of the spread forecast and the uncertainty issue for overlapping horizons.

Use rolling or expanding origins with only information available when forecasts are issued (1), apply the same loss, horizon and dates to a simple feasible benchmark (1), and consider dependence in paired multi-step forecast errors (1).

Case 4 · 10 marks

A sample selection and replication audit

Part A 3 marks

Compute growth of the complete loan book and mean growth of its branches.

Complete book grows from 100 to 112, or 12% (1). Branch growth is +10%, −10% and +50% (1). The equal-branch mean is 50/3% ≈ 16.67% (1).

Part B 3 marks

Recalculate aggregate growth after the omission and specify two records needed for replication.

Retained book grows from 80 to 82 = 2.5% (2). Record the identifier crosswalk and dated source vintage or explicit filter/removal log (1).

Part C 4 marks

Assess the independence and causal claims and propose one evidential repair for each.

Bank-level policy assignments occur in six banks and within-bank errors can correlate (1), so report cluster structure and consider suitable small-cluster/design-based inference (1). An observed pre/post change alone provides no untreated counterfactual (1). A credible contemporaneous comparison or independent policy variation with defended assumptions addresses this (1).
