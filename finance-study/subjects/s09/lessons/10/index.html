Conduct and evidence

A fair-execution claim needs timestamps, order instructions and market conditions. Separate an unfavourable result from evidence of a flawed process.

The starting point

A portfolio manager buys shares shortly before a market decline. That result alone cannot tell a reviewer whether the trader handled the order fairly. Review what the trader knew when choosing a route, including size, urgency, stated price limits and available quotes.

A useful record links time-stamped instructions, quotes with executable sizes, routing decisions, fills and fees. A displayed alternative is relevant only if the trader could access enough of it under the client's conditions. Timestamps also help distinguish late execution from a late investment decision.

Theory and method

Best-execution review using executable opportunity sets

Transaction-cost analysis · Counterfactual routing

What evidence would persuade you that a better displayed quote was a genuinely superior route for the whole client order?

A buyer needing 2,000 shares immediately sees two venues with different sizes, prices and fees. The lower first quote can cover only a small fraction of the order. A defensible review constructs the schedules the broker could actually execute with the instructions, available market access and contemporaneous depth. This produces a bounded comparison and leaves uncertainty visible. It can then investigate whether payments, routing preferences or stale information influenced the choice without claiming to have observed an unexecuted trade.

How the result is built

Compare C(r) = Σqᵢpᵢ + F(r) + U(r) across feasible routes r

C is buyer euro implementation cost, qᵢ and pᵢ quantities and prices across fills, F total fees net of rebates accruing to the client, U a specified endpoint cost for unresolved quantity if relevant, and r a route satisfying the client's instructions.

  1. For 2,000 shares, A offers all at €12.04 plus €4 fees. B offers 100 at €12.00 and 1,900 at €12.05 plus €2. Assume simultaneous firm orders and no split.
  2. A costs €24,084. B costs €1,200 + €22,895 + €2 = €24,097. A saves €13 under the stated constraint.
  3. If splitting is allowed and both fees apply, buy 100 at B and 1,900 at A. The combined cost is €1,200 + €22,876 + €6 = €24,082, saving €2 versus A alone.

When the reasoning holds

  • Counterfactual depth must be executable at the relevant time and include size, fee incidence and any access constraints. A screen snapshot alone gives incomplete evidence.
  • The client's purpose matters. A deliberately passive, discreet or time-constrained mandate may make an apparent immediate route infeasible or less appropriate.
First example

A broker obtains €8.02 for each of 4,000 shares purchased. A screenshot from one minute earlier shows €8.00 for 50 shares. Investigate the market when the order arrived, and how much could have traded at each price before judging the route.

What a fair comparison can establish

A client may care about total cost, likelihood of completing, speed, price protection or discreet handling. Document the client's priority before evaluating a route. A small market order and a confidential block order can have different feasible alternatives at the same timestamp.

An unfavourable fill can be consistent with good execution in a moving market. Conversely a favourable outcome can conceal a routing conflict. Review decision quality against the information and alternatives the trader could genuinely access at the time.

Reconstructing the alternatives

Time synchronization matters because the best quote can change within the reporting delay. A firm quote at sufficient size on a venue open to the client is stronger evidence than an indicative price, a stale screen or an order behind a long queue.

Compare all-in amounts. Rebates may benefit the broker or client depending on the contract. A venue with an apparent price advantage can be worse after fees, lower fill probability or additional impact on the rest of the order.

Sampling and conflicts of interest

Comparing only completed trades can favour routes with low fill rates because their missed opportunities are absent. Group similar orders by side, size, urgency, security and market state. Include intended quantity and outcomes for cancellations and partial fills.

Investigate whether compensation, affiliated venues or information leakage influenced routing. The correct legal obligation is jurisdiction- and client-specific. An analytical review can distinguish evidence of a possible conflict from a definitive legal finding.

Method

Writing a defensible execution review

  1. Recover instructions

    Collect the original order, decision and arrival times, price limits, urgency, commission arrangement and permitted venues.

  2. Simulate feasible routes

    Use accessible depth, actual timestamps, fees and plausible partial fills. Identify assumptions where a counterfactual execution cannot be observed.

  3. Record conclusion and uncertainty

    Separate measured differences from suspected causes, identify evidence that could overturn the finding and refer material conflicts for independent review.

Worked case

A cheap quote with an expensive remainder

A client wants 1,000 shares immediately. Route X offers all 1,000 at €15.04 with €2 total fees. Route Y offers 100 at €15.00 and the other 900 at €15.08 with €1 total fees. Assume all quoted quantities are firm and simultaneous.

Working

  1. X costs 1,000 × €15.04 + €2 = €15,042. Y costs 100 × €15 + 900 × €15.08 + €1 = €15,073.
  2. The better displayed first price on Y does not establish better whole-order execution. X is €31 cheaper under the stated alternatives.
  3. If routing can split simultaneously and incur both stated fees, using 100 at Y and 900 at X costs €1,500 + €13,536 + €3 = €15,039. This feasible route improves another €3 if both fills are achievable.

Interpretation

Latency, minimum fees or quotation withdrawal can erase the small split benefit. A review should identify what could actually have been executed before claiming a missed saving.

A common mistake

Comparing the trade with a later day's lowest price substitutes hindsight for a feasible alternative when the decision was made.

How to correct it

Time-align the client's instructions, accessible size, route choices and costs. Identify which assumptions about the unchosen route are uncertain.

Quick check
A client challenges a large fill. Which evidence is the strongest starting point for reviewing the execution decision?

These records reconstruct feasible choices at the decision time and compare them with the client's instructions. A later close may be useful context but reflects information and trading after execution.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A buyer needs 500 shares immediately. Route A offers all at €10.06 with no fee. Route B offers 50 at €10 and 450 at €10.10 with a €2 fee. Assume both books stay firm and splitting is prohibited.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compare total cash cost and the preferable route on these terms.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Name two records needed to evaluate a real router paid by the venues and one reason a single observed fill cannot establish misconduct.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

A costs €5,030. B costs €500 + €4,545 + €2 = €5,047. A is cheaper by €17 despite B's lower best displayed offer.

Part B 3 marks

Obtain synchronized executable depth and routing or payment records with the client's mandate. Unobserved cancellation, access restrictions or urgency can change feasible choices. Compensation creates a question to investigate rather than proof by itself.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

What evidence would persuade you that a better displayed quote was a genuinely superior route for the whole client order?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask for the time-stamped instruction, venue-level prices and sizes, fee treatment, and routing access. In this record A beats B by €13 on the whole order, even though B has the better first quote. A split might improve another €2. I would verify that tiny improvement against actual latency and client permissions before concluding a routing control failed.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The broker earns a €10 private rebate at B that the client never receives. Does that make B cheaper for the client?

Compare with the follow-up answer

It does not enter the client's cash cost. B remains €24,097 under the assumptions, and the broker's rebate introduces a conflict worth examining. Obtain the compensation agreement, order records and decision rationale before drawing a conclusion about actual conduct.
