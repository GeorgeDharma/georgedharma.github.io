Choosing between a lit book, a midpoint cross and a dealer quote

Compare cost, execution certainty and information exposure across three venue mechanisms.

  3. Examine sample quality

Theory and terminology

Execution across fragmented markets

midpoint crossing · lit order book · dealer quote

What you should be able to explain

  • Compare cost, execution certainty and information exposure across three venue mechanisms.
  • Derive the minimum crossing probability that justifies waiting under a stated deadline.

What the trader can actually buy

A lit order book displays at least part of its executable buying and selling interest. An aggressive buyer takes displayed offers until the order fills or the allowed price is reached. A dealer responding to a request for quote may commit capital for the full specified size until an expiry time. Record whether a quote is firm, its side and its size.

A midpoint crossing system can match opposite orders at the midpoint of a quoted reference market. The orders inside the system are not publicly displayed before trading. A reference price does not guarantee a counterparty. Venue rules can also restrict matching on a stale, locked or unusually wide reference market.

Price a failed attempt

Compare a complete route policy, including what the trader does if nothing matches before the deadline. If a market is moving, non-fills may occur precisely when buyers are competing for scarce sellers. The executable fallback price conditional on failure can therefore differ from the current offer or the unconditional forecast.

Include fees in each branch before averaging. A hard client limit, need for immediate certainty or inability to execute the fallback changes which routes are permissible. A route with an attractive average can have an unacceptable worst feasible outcome.

Examine sample quality

Estimate crossing probability from comparable size, asset, time, spread, urgency and minimum-quantity instructions. An average calculated from completed midpoint trades omits all failed attempts. Report attempts as well as executions.

Fragmentation also affects price discovery. The displayed market may supply the reference price used by a crossing venue. Changes in where informed or uninformed traders go can change trading costs for other participants. The private saving on a single order does not identify that market-wide effect.

Method

Build the result

Expected buy price after a cross attempt = pM + (1 − p)F. When F > I > M, crossing beats a certain immediate price I if p > (F − I)/(F − M).

p is the probability of a full midpoint fill within the time allowed. M is its all-in euros per share. F is the expected all-in fallback price conditional on failure. I is a firm immediate all-in alternative for the full size. The objective minimizes expected cash paid.

  1. Write the two mutually exclusive outcomes, including what is purchased by the deadline in each case.
  2. Weight their complete all-in costs by their probabilities. Rearrange pM + (1 − p)F < I to isolate the required success probability.
  3. Compare against every feasible firm route and a client’s price and timing constraints before releasing the instruction.
Worked case

A dealer’s quote expires at the start of the crossing window

Buy 2,000 shares by 10:05. The lit bid/offer is €49.96/€50.04 with enough offer depth and a €0.005 fee per share. A dealer offers the full size now at €50.042 all-in. A midpoint attempt fills all shares at €50.00 plus €0.002 with 65% estimated probability. Otherwise the desk must buy at an expected €50.105 all-in. No hard limit binds.

Working

  1. Immediate lit cost is €50.045 per share, or €100,090. Immediate dealer cost is €50.042, or €100,084, making the dealer the better certain alternative.
  2. The cross costs €50.002 per share on success. Conditional failure costs €50.105. The expected price is 0.65 × 50.002 + 0.35 × 50.105 = €50.03805.
  3. Expected expenditure is €100,076.10. Expected saving is €7.90 against the dealer, although a failed attempt is expected to cost €126 more than the firm dealer quote for this order.
  4. The threshold against the dealer is (50.105 − 50.042)/(50.105 − 50.002) = 61.17%. At a revised 55% match rate, the expected price rises to €50.04835 and the dealer becomes preferable.

Decision and interpretation

Choose the crossing policy if expected cost is the mandate and 65% is credible. The dealer’s offer expires now, so it cannot be reused as a cheap fallback five minutes later.

Quick check
A certain all-in offer is €10.02. A cross would cost €10.00, and its conditional fallback €10.05. At what success probability are the expected prices equal?

Solve 10.05 − 0.05p = 10.02. This gives p = 0.03/0.05 = 60%. The certain offer is preferable below that threshold for this objective.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A 1,000-share buy can execute at €20.03 now. A crossing attempt costs €20.00 if filled and €20.08 on failure, all-in. Compare the routes at a 50% fill rate and find the threshold.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The expected attempt costs 0.50 × 20.00 + 0.50 × 20.08 = €20.04 per share, or €20,040.
  2. Immediate execution costs €20,030, saving €10 at that probability.
  3. Break-even success probability is (20.08 − 20.03)/(20.08 − 20.00) = 62.5%. Above that probability the crossing policy has lower expected cost.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Should a trader wait for an undisplayed match when a dealer will fill the entire order immediately?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would compare the entire route, including the price conditional on missing the cross. Here the dealer costs €50.042 for certain and the attempt averages €50.03805, saving only €7.90 on 2,000 shares. It needs more than 61.17% success to beat the dealer. A mandate requiring complete and immediate execution changes that decision even if the estimated average price looks attractive.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The client imposes a hard maximum price of €20.05. Can you keep the original expected-cost answer at a 70% match rate?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The assumed fallback of €20.08 violates the instruction. The stated guaranteed-completion crossing policy is infeasible. The immediate €20.03 route fits. A modified cross-and-cancel policy would require permission to leave shares unfilled and a separate opportunity-cost calculation.
