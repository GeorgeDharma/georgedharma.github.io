Order choice

A market order prioritizes immediate execution. A limit order constrains price but can remain unfilled or execute when the market is moving against it.

The starting point

An investor who must obtain a share immediately can submit a market buy order. It takes the available offers, so the eventual average price depends on the book when the instruction reaches it. A limit buy order instead specifies the most the buyer is willing to pay per share.

A limit order can execute immediately at its limit or better if suitable shares are available. Otherwise it may wait or expire. A resting order also faces selection risk because others may be especially willing to sell when the share's underlying value has fallen.

Theory and method

Price–time priority and queue depletion

FIFO matching · Passive limit-order fill

A trade prints at your buy limit while your order stays unfilled. Could the matching engine be behaving correctly?

A buyer posts behind 600 shares already waiting at €10.00. In a price–time queue, a sale reaching that price first trades against older eligible buys. Cancellations by the traders ahead can shorten the wait, while cancellations behind the buyer do nothing. A printed trade at the limit confirms only that some shares traded there. Predicting the buyer's fill requires reconstructing the relevant queue changes, price priority and the order's validity at the venue.

How the result is built

f = min[Q, max(0, S − A + C)]

f is the customer's filled shares, Q its order size, S sell shares reaching this exact queue, A initial eligible shares ahead, and C cancellations ahead before the sells, all in shares.

  1. Take Q = 200 and A = 600 at the best bid. Earlier cancellations remove C = 150 shares, leaving 450 ahead before any new sell flow.
  2. If 520 eligible sell shares reach this queue, the first 450 fill earlier orders. The next 70 reach the customer.
  3. The min with Q prevents an apparent fill greater than the submitted amount. A further 130 shares arriving with no intervening priority changes would complete the order.

When the reasoning holds

  • The example assumes displayed FIFO and a single price queue. Hidden priority, auctions, pro-rata matching or multiple venues change the mechanics.
  • C counts cancellations ahead before the relevant executions. Aggregated cancellations without order position and event timing do not prove a specific fill.
Terms in this lesson
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
First example

The best offer is €25.10. A limit buy at €25.00 cannot take that offer. It can execute later at €25.00 or less if sell interest arrives, subject to the venue's priority and the order's validity.

Limits, queues and execution risk

A limit protects a worst acceptable execution price but gives no promise of quantity. On a price–time venue, a buy limit added at an existing bid queues behind earlier interest at that price. Transactions observed at that price may consume earlier orders without reaching the new buyer.

An immediately executable buy limit is marketable up to its limit. Its remaining quantity may rest or cancel depending on time-in-force. An immediate-or-cancel instruction takes available eligible quantity now and cancels what remains. A fill-or-kill condition requires the specified whole quantity immediately.

Why a passive order might be expensive

A resting buy order can save the spread if filled while the underlying value is stable. It is also likely to execute when news makes sellers urgently willing to trade. Its conditional price performance after a fill can therefore look worse than average market performance.

Consider both the path where the order executes and the path where prices rise away and it does not. If the investment decision remains in force, a later replacement purchase can be costly. Expected cost includes both outcomes on a comparable share count.

Choosing an order around a deadline

A fund needing cash by a specific time faces an opportunity cost from missing the sale. A patient fund with a strict valuation ceiling faces a different trade-off. Price protection, deadline and required quantity cannot all be guaranteed by a single order instruction.

Venue priority, valid price increments, fees and the possibility of a market halt also matter. A stop order activated by a price condition may become a market or limit order depending on its terms. A trigger price alone does not guarantee the eventual fill price.

Method

Evaluating an order instruction

  1. Express the constraint

    Write down the maximum purchase price or minimum sale price, deadline and admissibility of partial execution.

  2. Examine feasible fills

    Compare available depth and queue position with the desired amount, including what happens to unfilled shares.

  3. Compare complete paths

    Estimate execution and replacement costs in both favourable and adverse outcomes using the same endpoint and investment objective.

Worked case

A patient buyer compares two instructions

A buyer needs 100 shares by the close. It can buy immediately for €20.10. A passive €20 limit is expected to fill in full with probability 60%. Otherwise it must buy at an expected €20.40 at the deadline. Assume valid probabilities and no fees.

Working

  1. Immediate execution costs €2,010. The passive strategy costs either €2,000 on a fill or €2,040 if replacement is needed.
  2. Probability-weighted cost is 0.60 × €2,000 + 0.40 × €2,040 = €2,016. The apparent €0.10 saving on a passive fill is offset by the cost of non-execution.
  3. A higher fill probability or lower replacement price can change the ranking. The implied break-even fill probability solves 20p + 20.40(1 − p) = 20.10, giving 75%.

Interpretation

A buyer whose mandate prohibits a purchase above €20 cannot plan to buy at €20.40. Its feasible alternative is leaving the order incomplete, which changes the decision.

A common mistake

Evaluating only orders that filled omits the expensive cases where prices moved away.

How to correct it

Keep a record of intended quantities and benchmark unfilled shares at an economically justified deadline.

Quick check
A buyer wants 500 shares and cannot pay above €14. An immediate fill would be welcome, and partial execution is acceptable. Which instruction expresses that preference?

The buy limit protects the maximum per-share price. It may fill in part or in full at €14 or a better price, or remain unfilled if sellers demand more.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A €12.04 offer is available now. A buy limit of €12 fills with 50% probability. Otherwise the investor buys the same quantity at €12.12 at the close. Ignore fees.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find expected execution price under the passive plan and the cheaper choice on expectation.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the fill probability that makes passive and immediate expected costs equal.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Expected price is 0.5 × €12 + 0.5 × €12.12 = €12.06. Buying at €12.04 costs €0.02 less per share in expectation.

Part B 3 marks

Solve 12p + 12.12(1 − p) = 12.04. It gives p = 0.08/0.12 = two thirds. Above that probability the passive choice is cheaper on these assumptions.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A trade prints at your buy limit while your order stays unfilled. Could the matching engine be behaving correctly?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. Under price–time priority, the older 600 shares come first, reduced here by 150 cancellations that are confirmed ahead of us. A 520-share sell then reaches only 70 of our 200 shares. I would review the venue-level messages, timestamps and priority rules. A consolidated print from another market or a cancellation behind our order cannot establish a missed fill.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose 120 of the 150 cancellations actually belonged behind you. What is the new fill?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Only 30 cancellations reduce the earlier queue, leaving 570 shares ahead. The 520-share sell finishes before reaching our order, giving zero fill. There are still 50 eligible shares ahead assuming no other events.
