Clearing and settlement

Match trade terms, collateral and final delivery. Delivery versus payment links securities delivery to payment, addressing a specific settlement risk.

The starting point

Agreeing a trade creates an obligation. Clearing determines who owes cash and securities and can involve a central counterparty that stands between the original parties. Settlement occurs when the agreed transfers are completed under the system's rules.

Delivery versus payment makes final delivery of securities conditional on final payment. This reduces the risk of handing over the entire asset and receiving nothing in exchange. Participants still need cash and securities on time, and can face replacement costs after a failed trade.

Theory and method

Delivery versus payment and multilateral netting

CPMI–IOSCO Principle 12 · Settlement finality

Does multilateral netting make delivery versus payment unnecessary for the residual settlement?

Three clearing members may each buy and sell securities on the same settlement date. Netting can shrink the amounts each party must transfer, reducing the liquidity needed to meet those obligations. A second question is whether a party can pay its residual cash while failing to receive the residual securities. Delivery versus payment links the finality of the two legs. Even a small net obligation can create principal risk if its two legs are settled separately.

How the result is built

Net cashᵢ = cash receivableᵢ − cash payableᵢ; final securities delivery ⇔ final linked payment

i identifies a clearing member, positive net cash is a euro receipt, negative net cash is a payment, and ⇔ means each leg becomes final if and only if the linked leg does under this DvP mechanism.

  1. Member A owes B €8m, B owes C €7m and C owes A €6m. Gross outgoing payments sum to €21m before netting.
  2. On a common eligible cash obligation set, A receives €6m and pays €8m, hence owes net €2m. B receives net €1m and C receives net €1m.
  3. Net outgoing cash totals €2m and balances the two receipts. Apply the linked settlement mechanism to remaining exchange-of-value obligations to address principal risk.

When the reasoning holds

  • The obligations must be eligible for recognized netting in the same system, currency and date. Cross-entity or different-date flows may require gross funding.
  • DvP addresses principal exchange. Variation in replacement prices, collateral funding and operational deadlines still require management.
Terms in this lesson
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Counterparty
The other party to a financial contract or trade.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
First example

A buyer owes €1m for bonds. With linked delivery and payment, unavailable cash prevents final bond delivery under the stated arrangement. The seller retains the bonds but may have to find another buyer after prices have fallen.

From execution to finality

Execution agrees price, quantity and counterparties. Confirmation checks that both records describe the same transaction. Clearing establishes obligations and may net offsetting trades. Settlement transfers cash and ownership with the legal finality defined by the system. Each stage can fail for different reasons.

A central counterparty interposes itself between clearing members under its rules and manages default exposures through resources such as collateral and default funds. It changes the pattern of exposures. It cannot remove the need for its members to provide resources or meet settlement deadlines.

Principal risk and replacement cost

If cash and securities settle independently, a party may hand over the full principal while receiving nothing if the other leg fails. Delivery versus payment, or DvP, conditions final securities transfer on final payment and addresses that particular risk.

If a trade fails before either leg settles, a rise or fall in the replacement price can still hurt one side. Margins can mitigate this replacement exposure. Cash and securities must still be sourced on time, so liquidity risk survives even under a sound DvP design.

Gross versus net obligations

Gross settlement processes transactions individually, which can require considerable intraday funding. Netting can reduce the amount of cash or securities that participants have to deliver. Its benefits depend on enforceability, timing and whether the same counterparties or central system support the offsets.

Shorter settlement windows can reduce the time that replacement exposure is open but leave less time for reconciliation, foreign exchange and securities lending. A nominally short cycle still creates pressure if payment systems operate in different time zones.

Method

Diagnosing a failed trade

  1. Locate the failure

    Determine whether instructions mismatched, cash was unavailable, securities were missing or final transfer already occurred on one side.

  2. Separate the amounts at risk

    Distinguish full principal, replacement value and the amount temporarily needed for funding.

  3. Verify netting and finality

    Check the legal set, system rules and actual settlement time before reducing reported obligations or releasing collateral.

Worked case

A payment linked to delivery

A fund contracts to buy 10,000 shares at €30, settlement by DvP. On the due date the seller cannot deliver. Neither leg settles and the fund replaces the trade at €30.40 the same day.

Working

  1. Contract principal is €300,000. Because no cash transfer becomes final without delivery, the fund has not lost that €300,000 to the defaulting seller.
  2. Replacement requires 10,000 × €30.40 = €304,000. Relative to the original agreement the economic replacement cost is €4,000 before recovery or compensation.
  3. If it had paid the €300,000 irrevocably before any linked delivery, the exposure could include that entire principal in addition to funding the replacement, subject to any recovery.

Interpretation

Whether the fund actually recovers the €4,000 depends on the contractual and clearing remedies. DvP is a settlement mechanism rather than a guarantee against all economic losses.

A common mistake

Treating the contract's full notional as the loss whenever a DvP trade fails can greatly overstate the exposure.

How to correct it

Establish whether either leg transferred irrevocably, then calculate the price difference on the replacement transaction.

Quick check
A settlement platform delivers shares only if the corresponding cash payment becomes final. Which exposure does this mechanism most directly address?

The link addresses principal risk on the exchange of assets for cash. Market movements and the need to fund or deliver on time still matter to the parties.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A seller agreed to deliver 2,000 bonds at €99 through DvP. The buyer fails, neither leg settles, and the seller resells at €97.50.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find contractual principal and replacement loss before recoveries.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What would change if title to the bonds had already transferred irrevocably without linked payment?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Contract principal is €198,000. Resale raises €195,000. The seller's replacement loss is €3,000. It retains the bonds until resale.

Part B 3 marks

The seller could be exposed to the unpaid full principal, with recovery depending on the claim. The transfer would lack the specified DvP protection on that exchange.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Does multilateral netting make delivery versus payment unnecessary for the residual settlement?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

No. Netting reduces the €21m gross cash movements in this example to €2m of outgoing net cash. It does not itself ensure a member receives securities when cash becomes final. I would verify both the enforceability of the net obligations and the link between final delivery and payment. If trading prices move before completion, replacement-cost exposure can remain despite both controls.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the €6m C-to-A payment is due a day later and cannot join the earlier netting set. What are day-one net cash positions?

Compare with the follow-up answer

A owes €8m, B receives €8m and pays €7m for a €1m receipt, and C receives €7m. Net outgoing day-one cash is €8m. The separate €6m next-day payment cannot reduce day-one funding.
