Market-abuse surveillance and evidential escalation

Distinguish an unusual message pattern, reasonable suspicion and a legal finding.

Theory and terminology

Market-conduct surveillance

order cancellation · spoofing indicators · evidential review

What you should be able to explain

  • Distinguish an unusual message pattern, reasonable suspicion and a legal finding.
  • Reconstruct the order evidence and assess false positives and missed events without treating an alert score as proof.

Begin with the mechanism and the records

Quotes are offers to trade that can be cancelled when information changes, inventory moves or risk limits bind. Cancellation alone has many ordinary explanations. Market manipulation can involve misleading indications of supply or demand. A surveillance review asks how the order sequence, surrounding market information and actual trading relate.

Preserve original timestamps, order identifiers, accepted amendments, fills and cancellations, as well as the broker’s or desk’s approved instructions. Align clocks across venues and distinguish separate client orders from repeated amendments to the same order. Summary counts cannot reconstruct these facts later.

Set an evidential threshold

A monitoring alert identifies activity that deserves attention. An analyst should compare alternative explanations with contemporaneous evidence, identify the owner of further work and protect confidentiality. An unexplained anomaly may require escalation before every question is resolved.

For a firm within the UK reporting regime illustrated by the FCA guidance, the designated process considers reasonable grounds to suspect market abuse or an attempt and reports without delay when that threshold is met. The responsible authority makes any legal finding. Other jurisdictions have their own scope, process and terminology.

Test the monitor as well as the trader

Precision asks how many flagged cases meet an independently chosen review standard. Sensitivity asks what fraction of all cases meeting that standard the monitor found. Sampling only alerts can estimate precision but cannot uncover missed cases.

Compare like trading activity and include known cases, independently sampled non-alerts, and documented reasons for closure. The reference label can describe whether a case warrants investigation. A legal finding is a different outcome. Define the label so a result is not misrepresented to the board.

Method

Build the result

Cancellation fraction = cancelled distinct orders / submitted distinct orders. Precision = TP/(TP + FP). Sensitivity = TP/(TP + FN).

For a fixed window, count orders once under a defined end-state rule. TP is a flagged case independently judged to meet the specified review standard, FP a flag that does not, and FN an unflagged case that meets it. All three are case counts.

  1. Freeze the event window and unique-order convention, then reconcile cancellations, fills and orders still open to submitted orders.
  2. Reconstruct context and identify both corroborating and exculpatory records. Keep the alert, regulatory reporting decision and legal determination distinct.
  3. Assess calibration using independent labels from flagged and unflagged activity. Record who applies the reporting threshold and on which evidence.
Worked case

Two equal cancellation ratios on a UK trading desk

The control counts each distinct order once by its final status. On desk A, 120 orders end as 108 cancellations, 10 fills and 2 live orders. Desk B has 20 orders, 18 cancellations and 2 fills. A public issuer announcement is timestamped just before desk A’s repricing. On B, a restricted review finds repeated opposing executions linked to the cancellations, while the feed currently lacks one venue’s timestamps.

Working

  1. A’s cancellation fraction is 108/120 = 90%. B’s is 18/20 = 90%. Reconcile both totals before drawing any comparison.
  2. For A, verify announcement time, market-wide quote movement, algorithm risk settings and actual fills. The public event supplies a testable explanation. Preserve the record and document whether its chronology supports closure.
  3. For B, refer the interlinked order and execution record to the authorized surveillance officer and request the missing venue timestamps. Record the uncertainty. If reasonable suspicion is already established, the firm’s reporting process addresses it without waiting for definitive proof.
  4. In a separate calibration sample, 6 of 48 flags meet the independent investigation standard. Precision is 12.5%. No non-alerts were checked, so sensitivity cannot be calculated from these 48 records.

Decision and interpretation

Do not assign guilt from either cancellation ratio. Preserve and escalate B’s evidence for a reasoned decision, and test the monitor’s blind spots with a sample outside the alert population.

Quick check
A review checks every flagged case but no unflagged cases. Which statistic can it estimate against its independent investigation standard?

The reviewers can count qualifying cases among the flags. Unflagged qualifying cases remain unknown, so the sensitivity denominator is incomplete.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

In a reviewed set of 200 cases, a detector flags 30. Independent reviewers classify 6 of those as meeting the investigation threshold and find 2 further qualifying cases among the 170 non-alerts. Calculate precision and sensitivity and decide what to report internally.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. There are 6 true positives, 24 false positives and 2 false negatives against this review standard.
  2. Precision is 6/30 = 20%. Sensitivity is 6/(6 + 2) = 75%. The detector missed one quarter of independently qualifying cases.
  3. Report both measures and inspect the two missed cases for a shared blind spot. Document that investigation-worthiness is the label. The calculation gives no finding of legal wrongdoing by individual traders.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

If two desks cancel 90% of their orders, should both automatically be reported for manipulation?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

No. Both 90% ratios usefully identify activity to inspect. Contemporaneous public news can explain one repricing sequence, while linked opposite-side executions and incomplete records justify a deeper review of the other. I would preserve the full event chain, record alternative explanations, escalate through the authorized officer and follow the reporting deadline once the applicable suspicion threshold is met.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A manager suggests closing every alert because only 20% qualified in the test. Is that justified?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. The 20% measures average precision in this sample. Six flags did qualify and individual evidence determines the next step. Assess whether the sample is representative, improve review prioritization and recheck sensitivity before changing a control on which the firm relies.
