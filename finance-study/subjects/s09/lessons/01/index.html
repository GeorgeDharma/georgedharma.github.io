Trading venues

Compare a dealer market, a continuous limit-order book and a call auction. The matching rule changes how orders interact.

The starting point

A pension fund wants to exchange bonds for cash. On a dealer market it can ask a dealer for a price and trade against the dealer's inventory. In a continuous order book, incoming buy and sell instructions interact under the venue's published priority rules.

A call auction accumulates orders and crosses them at a scheduled time, usually at a single price selected by a published rule. Concentrating orders can help when trading interest is thin. The wait until the auction creates a different timing risk for someone who needs cash immediately.

Theory and method

Uniform-price call auction

Walrasian clearing · Order imbalance

Two auction prices match the same number of shares. Can you tell a client which price will clear?

A share reopens after an announcement. Orders accumulated during the halt can cross at one price. A buy order contributes to demand at every price up to its limit, while a sell order contributes to supply at or above its limit. Several prices can produce the same maximum trade, so the exchange needs an explicit rule for residual imbalance and remaining ties. An analyst reconstructing the auction must apply that particular venue's rules before assigning fills.

How the result is built

V(p) = min[B(p), A(p)]; I(p) = B(p) − A(p)

p is candidate price in euros per share, B cumulative eligible buy shares, A cumulative eligible sell shares, V matched volume, and I signed imbalance in shares.

  1. Buy orders are 100 shares at €52 and 40 at €51. Sell orders are 100 at €50 and 20 at €52. Evaluate €50, €51 and €52 only.
  2. At €50 and €51 eligible buys are 140 and sells 100. At €52 eligible buys are 100 and sells 120. Each candidate matches 100 shares.
  3. If the published tie-break minimizes absolute imbalance, €52 wins with 20 unmatched shares rather than 40 at either lower price.

When the reasoning holds

  • Orders must participate in the same auction and remain valid through the cutoff. Cancellations change the cumulative curves.
  • The stated tie-break is part of this exercise. Some venues use reference prices or further rules and need their own calculation.

First example

At an auction, suppose €20 would match 80 shares and €21 would match 110. If these are the only candidate prices and the rule maximizes matched volume, the auction selects €21. A continuous book could instead trade at several prices as orders arrive.

How parties find one another

A dealer can provide immediate execution by buying or selling from its own book, taking inventory risk until it offsets the position. A broker generally seeks a counterparty on the client's behalf. The same institution can act in different capacities in different trades. The distinction concerns who actually takes the other side.

In an order-driven market, prices and quantities come from participants' outstanding instructions. In a quote-driven market, dealers communicate terms at which they will transact. A request for quote can be useful for a large bond trade when little firm size is displayed publicly.

Continuous and periodic matching

A continuous limit-order book processes each incoming order against executable resting interest under its priority rules. Price–time priority first rewards the best price, then the oldest order at that price. A venue using pro-rata allocation instead shares eligible executions according to size.

A call auction waits until the designated time and picks a common clearing price under published rules. For each candidate price, matchable volume is the smaller of all eligible buy quantity and all eligible sell quantity. Ties need the exchange's stated tie-break rules.

Market design and price discovery

Continuous trading gives a prompt way to act when news arrives. A scheduled auction pools buyers and sellers who might otherwise just miss one another. Pooling can be particularly helpful near an opening after substantial overnight news.

Venue selection depends on the investor's quantity, urgency, likelihood of a fill and information exposure. A price indication for 100 shares does not establish the cost at which a large institutional order could actually trade.

Method

Clearing a simple call

  1. Collect eligible orders

    At each candidate price include buy limits at or above that price and sell limits at or below it.

  2. Calculate crossing volume

    Take the smaller cumulative quantity on each side. A larger imbalance cannot create counterparties that are absent.

  3. Apply published priorities

    Use the volume-maximizing price and any stated tie-break, then allocate scarce fills using the venue's published rule.

Worked case

A small reopening auction

Three buy orders are 80 shares at €11, 60 at €10 and 40 at €9. Sell orders are 50 at €9, 70 at €10 and 90 at €11. Consider those three prices only.

Working

  1. At €9 all 180 shares of buy interest are eligible but only 50 shares are offered. Matched volume is 50. At €10 there are 140 buyers' shares and 120 sellers' shares, matching 120.
  2. At €11 just 80 shares of buying interest meet 210 offered shares. The €10 candidate maximizes volume at 120, leaving 20 eligible buy shares unmatched.
  3. If better-priced orders execute first, the €11 buyer gets 80 and €10 buyers collectively receive 40. A separate allocation rule is needed if several buyers share the €10 level.

Interpretation

Submitting first at an inferior price does not defeat the auction's price priority under these assumptions. Altering the rule could change individual fills while leaving the total matched quantity unchanged.

A common mistake

Counting only orders quoted exactly at a candidate price understates executable interest.

How to correct it

A buyer willing to pay more and a seller willing to accept less both qualify at the candidate price.

Quick check
A firm sends its order at 09:15. The venue collects orders and crosses them together at 09:30 under a common clearing rule. Which mechanism is this?

The batching and scheduled cross identify the auction. The firm gives up the chance of an immediate match in return for participating in the pooled trading interest.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

Buy interest is 50 shares at €22 and 90 at €21. Sell interest is 70 at €20, 50 at €21 and 40 at €22. Restrict the clearing price to €20, €21 or €22 and maximize volume.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find matched volume at each candidate and the clearing price.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    If better-priced buyers receive priority, how much of the €21 buy order executes?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

At €20 there are 140 willing buy shares and 70 sell shares, matching 70. At €21 volumes are 140 and 120, matching 120. At €22 volumes are 50 and 160, matching 50. The price is €21.

Part B 2 marks

The €22 buyer receives all 50 of the 120 available fills. The remaining 70 go to the €21 buyer, leaving 20 at its limit unexecuted.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two auction prices match the same number of shares. Can you tell a client which price will clear?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Maximum volume alone does not select the price here. Each candidate matches 100 shares. Under the stated next rule, €52 has only 20 shares left unmatched, versus 40 at €50 or €51. I would name the venue and its tie-break before predicting an actual auction. I would also check whether a client's order receives priority at the selected price.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If a buyer cancels 40 shares at €51 and the rule still minimizes imbalance, is €52 still uniquely selected?

Compare with the follow-up answer

At €50 and €51, eligible buys and sells now both equal 100, giving zero imbalance. At €52 the imbalance remains minus 20. A further tie-break is needed between €50 and €51, so the original €52 result no longer follows.
