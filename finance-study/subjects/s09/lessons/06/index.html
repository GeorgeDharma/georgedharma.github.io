Price impact

Distinguish temporary liquidity pressure from a lasting change in the market’s information. A trade can move the price through either channel.

The starting point

A large purchase can push a quoted price up because nearby sellers are used up, or because others infer that the buyer knows favourable news. New sellers can replace exhausted liquidity. An information revision can last after the order ends.

To study impact, record a benchmark just before the order and midquotes immediately afterward and later. Compare with market-wide changes and public news. Reversal is consistent with temporary pressure, but an individual observation rarely identifies the cause by itself.

Theory and method

Kyle's lambda

Signed order-flow price impact · Price informativeness

Can a large positive price-impact coefficient prove the latest buyer held private information?

A dealer observes net buying but cannot separate informed trades from unrelated liquidity demand. In Kyle's stylized equilibrium the price responds to aggregate signed order flow because it carries information about the asset's value. Lambda describes the price revision per unit of unexpected net buying. An empirical regression can estimate a related slope, but public news, execution timing and inventory pressure can also move both flow and prices. The unit, clock and benchmark therefore belong beside every estimate.

How the result is built

Δm = λx + ε; λ̂ = Cov(x, Δm)/Var(x)

Δm is midpoint change in euros per share, x unexpected signed net purchases in thousands of shares, λ price change per thousand shares, ε unexplained price change, and Cov and Var are sample covariance and variance.

  1. In a linear pricing approximation subtract the expected flow so zero unexpected flow has zero predicted midpoint revision, or estimate an intercept separately.
  2. The least-squares slope equates sample flow-price covariance to slope times flow variance. A covariance of 0.12 and flow variance of 4 give λ̂ = €0.03 per share per thousand net shares.
  3. Net buying of 5,000 shares then predicts a €0.15 midpoint rise. A measured €0.22 rise leaves a €0.07 residual under this model.

When the reasoning holds

  • The linear slope is local to trade size, horizon and market conditions. An exceptionally large block can encounter a different depth regime.
  • To interpret the slope as informational pricing, consider other shared causes and use synchronized midpoint changes. A transaction-price spread bounce can distort the estimate.
Terms in this lesson
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
First example

The midquote is €30 before a purchase, €30.18 immediately afterward and €30.05 later, with a flat sector index. Of the immediate €0.18 rise, €0.13 reversed and €0.05 remained at that observation time. Other news could still explain some of the residual.

A trade through time

Record the midpoint before an order, its actual execution price and the midpoint after a specified delay. A buy that pays above the old midpoint may simply cross the spread. A later increase in the midpoint is separate from paying that spread.

A lasting midpoint move can reflect information inferred from the trade. A temporary move can reflect inventory pressure or depleted depth that disappears as new orders arrive. An unrelated news event during the measurement window complicates either interpretation.

Separating effective and realized spread

Use a sign of +1 for a buyer-initiated trade and −1 for a seller-initiated trade. Doubled effective spread is twice the signed difference between execution price and pretrade midpoint. Realized spread uses the later midpoint instead, approximating what a liquidity supplier retained over that interval.

Twice the signed midpoint change accounts for the difference. Effective spread equals realized spread plus that measured impact by arithmetic. The identity holds even if the economic explanation of the price movement remains uncertain.

The benchmark horizon changes the answer

A five-second comparison describes a different outcome from a thirty-minute or closing-price comparison. A slower reaction may reflect a series of correlated trades. Longer windows admit more market or issuer news that has little to do with the original order.

Study many comparable orders and adjust for common market movements, trade direction, size and volatility. A randomized execution comparison, when feasible and consistent with the client's instructions, can help separate execution-policy effects from the decision to trade when a price move is expected.

Method

Decomposing a trade

  1. Fix direction and clocks

    Use synchronized trade and quote records and decide whose order demanded immediate liquidity.

  2. Compute three spreads

    Use the same multiplier convention and midpoint horizon throughout the calculation, including trades with price improvement.

  3. Interpret cautiously

    Compare the sign, persistence and broader market movement before calling the observed price change permanent information impact.

Worked case

A share is repriced after a buy

At 10:00 the midpoint is €40.00. A customer buys at €40.12 and the five-minute midpoint is €40.08. Ignore fees. The customer demanded liquidity, so use a positive trade sign.

Working

  1. Doubled effective spread is 2 × (€40.12 − €40.00) = €0.24. Doubled realized spread is 2 × (€40.12 − €40.08) = €0.08.
  2. The measured midpoint impact is 2 × (€40.08 − €40.00) = €0.16. The €0.08 realized spread plus €0.16 impact reconciles to €0.24.
  3. At a later midpoint of €40.02 the same trade shows only €0.04 measured impact and €0.20 realized spread. Changing the observation horizon changes the decomposition without changing the actual price paid.

Interpretation

A sector announcement between the timestamps could explain some of the move. The identity measures the move. Attribution requires additional evidence.

A common mistake

Calling the entire execution-price gap market impact incorrectly includes crossing the initial spread.

How to correct it

Measure execution relative to the pretrade midpoint and subsequent movement in that midpoint as separate quantities.

Quick check
A stock midpoint rises from €100 to €100.40 during buying and later returns to €100.10. What part of the immediate move has reversed?

The midpoint falls €0.30 from €100.40 to €100.10. The remaining €0.10 is an observed difference from the initial midpoint, which on its own does not identify private information.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A seller initiates a trade at €29.92. The pretrade midpoint is €30.00 and the later midpoint is €29.95. Use doubled spreads.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate effective spread, realized spread and midpoint impact.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    If the market index fell at the same time, can the €0.10 be attributed entirely to the sale? Explain.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

With sign −1, effective spread is €0.16, realized spread is 2 × (29.95 − 29.92) = €0.06, and midpoint impact is 2 × (30.00 − 29.95) = €0.10.

Part B 2 marks

No. The arithmetic measures a signed midpoint change. Compare with the index, similar securities and contemporaneous news to judge how much plausibly relates to the order.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a large positive price-impact coefficient prove the latest buyer held private information?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

No. I would describe lambda as the fitted sensitivity of the midpoint to unexpected signed flow over the chosen window. Five thousand net buys predict a €0.15 move here. The original model explains why flow may convey information, but one observation does not identify the buyer's motive. I would check public news, related markets, prior order splitting and subsequent reversal.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A release moves the sector €0.12 while this stock rises €0.22. Does the model's raw residual still measure stock-specific news?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The raw residual remains €0.07 relative to flow alone. To isolate stock-specific movement I would estimate and remove the stock's appropriate sector exposure, then refit or jointly estimate the flow coefficient. Subtracting €0.12 without addressing direction and overlap could misattribute the same news to flow.
