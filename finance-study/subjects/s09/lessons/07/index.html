Transaction-cost analysis

Compare fills with a stated arrival benchmark. Include fees and the opportunity cost of any unfilled portion when measuring implementation shortfall.

The starting point

A fund decides to buy shares at an observed benchmark price. Implementation shortfall compares its actual result with what an immediate complete trade at that benchmark would have achieved. A price rise adds cost to a buy and benefits a sale, so record the direction.

For a buy, add the excess price paid on executed shares, explicit fees, and the opportunity cost on unfilled shares measured at a specified endpoint. These components can be negative if the market moves favourably. Use the same share count and benchmark across them.

Theory and method

Perold implementation shortfall

Paper portfolio comparison · Opportunity cost of non-execution

How can a broker outperform the average market price on completed trades while the investment decision still loses value?

A manager decides to buy an entire position at one moment. A paper portfolio instantly owns it at the decision price. The real portfolio may wait, pay fees or finish the day with part of the position missing. Marking both portfolios at a common endpoint isolates the lost wealth attributable to implementation under that benchmark. The broker's average on completed shares omits unfilled shares and any price move before it received the order.

How the result is built

IS = Σqᵢ(Pᵢ − Pᴅ) + (Q − Σqᵢ)(Pₑ − Pᴅ) + F

For a buy, Q is intended shares, qᵢ filled shares at execution price Pᵢ, Pᴅ decision and Pₑ endpoint price in euros per share, F explicit euro fees, Σ sums fills, and IS is positive euro cost. Reverse price signs for a sale.

  1. For a buy of 800 shares at a €30 decision, 300 fill at €30.10 and 300 at €30.20. Filled excess cost is €30 + €60 = €90.
  2. The remaining 200 are marked at €30.50, creating €100 opportunity cost. Add €10 fees to reach €200 total.
  3. Divide by intended €24,000 notional and multiply by 10,000 for 83.33 bp. The fill-only average €30.15 does not include the €100 missed-position cost.

When the reasoning holds

  • Record intent and benchmark before seeing fills. Changing the decision time after the fact can manufacture favourable comparisons.
  • The endpoint must correspond to a valid decision horizon. If the manager genuinely cancels the investment thesis, document when and why.
Terms in this lesson
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Basis point
One hundredth of a percentage point. A rise from 4.00% to 4.01% is one basis point.
How to read the notation
Basis points and percentage points
A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises by one percentage point, or 100 basis points. Its increase relative to the original rate is (5 − 4) ÷ 4 = 25%.

All notation examples

First example

A fund intended to buy 1,000 shares at €10. It buys 800 at €10.10, pays €8 in fees, and marks the final 200 at €10.30. Its shortfall is €80 + €8 + €60 = €148 versus the fully invested paper portfolio.

The paper portfolio benchmark

Implementation shortfall asks how much wealth the investor gave up relative to executing the full investment decision at a designated starting price. For a buy, the ideal paper portfolio owns the full desired quantity from that moment, while the actual one may hold fewer shares at different prices.

Specify the decision price, arrival price and horizon used to mark unfilled shares. A buyer harmed by a later rise may have positive shortfall even when completed fills match the arrival quote. A declining market can give a negative cost, a saving relative to the benchmark.

Decision delay and execution costs

Split the intended quantity into executed and unexecuted shares. An exact bridge may separately charge all intended shares for the price movement from decision to arrival, then value executed and unexecuted components relative to arrival. Keep this convention consistent to avoid counting delay twice.

Explicit commissions, exchange fees and rebates affect wealth independently of prices. Ordinary market movement can drive a large fraction of total shortfall. The calculation describes a full implementation outcome, while evaluation of the trader requires evidence about controllable choices.

From euros to comparable rates

Convert money cost into basis points of the original intended notional by dividing total shortfall by intended shares times decision price, then multiplying by 10,000. Using the smaller filled amount in the denominator answers a different question and makes low-fill orders look inconsistent.

A volume-weighted market price can benchmark the trades completed during a window. It does not automatically price the opportunity cost of trades that never took place. A trader could beat that average while an unfilled order loses heavily.

Method

Building a reconciled cost report

  1. Freeze intent

    Capture the side, intended shares and time-stamped decision price before the trading outcome is known.

  2. Measure a complete bridge

    Include arrival delay, execution on filled shares, unfilled opportunity cost and explicit charges under a single convention.

  3. Check the denominator

    Reconcile to the simple decision-benchmark calculation, then express costs relative to original intended notional.

Worked case

A broker receives a buy order after prices rise

A fund decides to buy 5,000 shares at €20.00. The arrival midpoint is €20.10. It buys 3,000 shares at €20.14, pays €30 fees and marks the remaining 2,000 shares at €20.25.

Working

  1. From decision to arrival, all 5,000 intended shares incur 5,000 × €0.10 = €500. Executed shares incur 3,000 × €0.04 = €120 further cost relative to arrival.
  2. The unfilled shares add 2,000 × (€20.25 − €20.10) = €300, and fees add €30. Total shortfall is €950.
  3. As a check use the original benchmark directly by calculating 3,000 × €0.14 + 2,000 × €0.25 + €30 = €950. Relative to €100,000 intended notional that is 95 basis points.

Interpretation

The €500 earlier delay should be traced to when the desk actually received a complete order. Assigning it automatically to the executing trader would misidentify responsibility.

A common mistake

Charging all shares for decision-to-arrival drift and then marking unfilled shares against the decision price counts their delay twice.

How to correct it

Use arrival price for every post-arrival component, or use decision price throughout without a separate delay component.

Quick check
A buy decision covers 100 shares at €20. Sixty fill at €20.10, fees are €2 and the remaining 40 are marked at €20.25. What is total shortfall?

Executed shares cost €6 above benchmark. Unfilled shares add €10 of opportunity cost and fees add €2. The components total €18.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A manager decides to sell 1,000 shares at €50. Arrival is €49.90. 700 sell at €49.80. 300 remain unsold at an endpoint price of €49.50. Fees are €10.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate seller shortfall from the original decision benchmark.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Reconcile delay and post-arrival costs and express total cost in basis points.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Filled shares cost 700 × (€50 − €49.80) = €140. Unfilled shares add 300 × (€50 − €49.50) = €150. Including fees, shortfall is €300.

Part B 4 marks

Delay costs €100. Post-arrival execution costs €70, opportunity costs €120 and fees €10, totaling €300. Relative to €50,000 intended notional the cost is 60 bp.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a broker outperform the average market price on completed trades while the investment decision still loses value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would compare the complete order with the decision portfolio. The two fills cost €90 above the decision price, the 200 missing shares cost €100 by the endpoint, and fees add €10. The whole decision therefore lost €200 or 83.33 basis points. A broker's average can still look attractive against trading during its assigned window. Those measures evaluate different parts of the process.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The manager personally delayed routing until the midpoint reached €30.12. What portion is decision-to-arrival cost on the intended quantity?

Compare with the follow-up answer

All 800 intended shares incur 800 × €0.12 = €96 of pre-desk delay. The remaining €104 is post-arrival execution, missed-position cost and fees measured relative to arrival. Keep the benchmarks consistent so the unfilled shares' delay is counted once.
