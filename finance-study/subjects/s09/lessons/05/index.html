Adverse-selection models

Informed traders can trade against stale quotes. A dealer anticipates losses to informed flow and may require a spread from remaining customers.

The starting point

Suppose a dealer commits to sell before knowing whether the next buyer has private information. If a buyer is more likely to trade after learning good news, a purchase itself is informative. The value conditional on seeing a buy can exceed the value before anyone trades.

Adverse-selection models let the dealer adjust the offer to the value expected after a buy and the bid to the value expected after a sale. Information can therefore create a spread even with competitive dealers and zero inventory costs.

Theory and method

Glosten–Milgrom sequential trade model

How does an observed customer buy change a competitive dealer's quote when only some customers are informed?

A dealer trades before knowing whether the next customer has information about the asset's payout. An informed customer prefers buying in the high-value state and selling in the low-value state. An uninformed customer can arrive on either side for unrelated reasons. An observed buy therefore changes the dealer's probability of the high state. A competitive dealer prices the side on which it will actually trade from that updated probability. Repeated buys may shift beliefs again, even without a public announcement.

How the result is built

Ask = E[V given buy]; Bid = E[V given sell]; P(H given buy) = P(buy given H)P(H)/P(buy)

V is terminal euros per share, H the high-value state, P an event probability, and E the probability-weighted mean. The ask applies when the customer buys.

  1. Take high and low values €120 and €80 with equal priors. A quarter of traders are informed, others buy or sell with equal probability. Buy likelihoods are 0.625 in H and 0.375 in the low state.
  2. The overall buy probability is 0.5. A buy gives high-state probability 0.625 and ask = €105. A sale gives high-state probability 0.375 and bid = €95.
  3. If draws are conditionally independent given the state, two buys give odds (0.625/0.375)² = 25/9, so high-state probability is 25/34 and conditional value about €109.41.

When the reasoning holds

  • A competitive risk-neutral dealer with no inventory or operating costs isolates the informational component. Actual spreads include further components.
  • Repeated updating here treats customer types as new draws conditional on the same hidden payoff. One fund splitting a mechanical order invalidates that independence.

First example

A share will ultimately be worth €98 or €102. Suppose a buy implies a 75% probability of €102, while a sale implies only 25%. The conditional values are €101 after a buy and €99 after a sale. A competitive risk-neutral dealer using these beliefs could quote €99 and €101.

How a trade changes beliefs

In a simple model a share has one of two future values. Some customers know which one. Others trade to meet unrelated cash or portfolio needs. Even when traders' identities are hidden, the direction of an order contains information because informed customers tend to buy before high payouts.

Bayes' rule combines the prior chance of a value with the probability of observing a buy at that value. Divide their joint probability by the total chance of a buy. This gives a posterior probability different from the prior even before public news appears.

Competitive quotes under asymmetric information

A risk-neutral dealer facing competition expects zero average profit on the marginal trade if it quotes the expected value conditional on that trade direction. Its offer reflects what a buy reveals, and its bid reflects what a sale reveals.

Liquidity traders may pay part of the spread that compensates for trades with better-informed counterparties. More informed order flow can widen the information component of the spread. Actual quotes also include fees, price ticks, inventory and market power.

Limits of a trade-sign explanation

A buy can signal favourable fundamental information or one large fund dividing a mechanical index purchase into pieces. Both create patterns in trade signs. Their later price effects can differ depending on how other participants interpret them.

A quote update after a trade is evidence that beliefs, inventory or both changed. Correlations among order signs can arise from splitting or common news. Distinguish an informational hypothesis from a claim that the customer possessed legally restricted information.

Method

Updating a two-state valuation

  1. State the prior

    Write each fundamental value and the probability assigned to it before observing the trade.

  2. Weight by the order likelihood

    For each state multiply its prior probability by the chance of a buy in that state. Normalize using the total chance of a buy.

  3. Price the two sides

    Weight values by posterior probabilities for buys and for sells separately. State assumptions about inventory and dealer costs.

Worked case

A competitive dealer interprets a buy

A share is worth €110 or €90 with equal probability. Twenty percent of customers know its state and buy only in the high state, selling only in the low state. Other customers buy or sell with equal probability independent of value.

Working

  1. Probability of a buy given high value is 0.20 + 0.80 × 0.5 = 0.60. Given low value it is 0.40. Overall buy probability is 0.5.
  2. After a buy, high value has probability (0.5 × 0.60)/0.5 = 0.60. Conditional expected value is 0.6 × €110 + 0.4 × €90 = €102.
  3. Symmetry gives high value probability 0.40 after a sale and a conditional value of €98. The model's competitive bid and offer are €98/€102, a €4 information spread.

Interpretation

If informed participation fell to zero, direction would reveal nothing and this component would vanish. Real-world operating and inventory costs could still sustain a spread.

A common mistake

Using the unconditional €100 expectation on both sides overlooks that trades occur selectively across fundamental states.

How to correct it

Update the probability distribution conditional on which customer action reaches the dealer before calculating its expected payoff.

Quick check
The dealer expects a share to be worth €54 after seeing a buy and €50 after seeing a sale. Ignoring other costs, which quote covers these conditional expectations?

The dealer buys when a customer sells and sells when a customer buys. The conditional value on each side accounts for what the customer's order reveals.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A share is worth €60 or €40, each with probability one half. Given high value, a buy has probability 0.7. Given low value it has probability 0.3. All other events are sales. Assume competitive risk-neutral dealers with no other costs.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compute the high-state probability after a buy and the resulting offer.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the bid and spread, then identify one omitted cause of a real spread.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Buy probability is 0.5 × 0.7 + 0.5 × 0.3 = 0.5. The high-state posterior is 0.35/0.5 = 0.70. The offer is 0.7 × €60 + 0.3 × €40 = €54.

Part B 3 marks

After a sale high-state probability is 0.30. Bid is €46 and spread €8. Inventory risk, tick size or operating costs could also affect observed quotes.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How does an observed customer buy change a competitive dealer's quote when only some customers are informed?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A buy is more likely when the share is worth €120. Bayes' rule moves my probability of that state from one half to five eighths, giving €105 as the conditional expectation. A competitive quote of €105 on the offer and €95 on the bid balances conditional expected value on each side. A second independent buy raises the estimate further to about €109.41.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What happens after the first buy if you learn the customer trades only to rebalance an index and its trade is independent of value?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Conditional on that reliable identity, a buy supplies no information about the hidden payout, so the posterior stays at the prior and its expected value is €100. In practice a dealer may still require compensation for carrying resulting inventory or executing the trade.
