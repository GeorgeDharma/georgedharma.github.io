Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

Auction or immediate execution?

An auction has buy orders for 120 at €16 and 80 at €15. Sell orders are 50 at €14, 100 at €15 and 100 at €16. It maximizes matched volume and gives more aggressive prices priority. A different investor separately faces an unchanged continuous book with 50 offered at €15.02 and 150 at €15.10.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

What did the dealer learn?

A share will be worth €80 or €120 with equal probability. The chance of a customer buy is 0.65 in the high state and 0.35 in the low state. Assume competitive risk-neutral dealers, no costs and a sale otherwise. Separately a dealer who has accumulated stock shifts a €98/€102 quote to €97/€101 while the public market is stable.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A buy order that was only partly executed

A manager decides to buy 2,000 shares at €25. The desk receives the order with the midpoint at €25.10. It buys 1,200 at €25.16 and pays €12 fees. At the review endpoint the remaining 800 shares are marked at €25.35.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

A settlement failure and a routing dispute

A DvP buyer agrees to acquire 3,000 shares at €40. The seller fails, neither leg settles and replacement costs €40.20 per share. Separately another immediate buy of 400 shares could use venue A for all at €10.06 plus €3, or venue B for 100 at €10.00 and 300 at €10.10 plus €2. Splitting is prohibited.

Case 1 · 10 marks

Auction or immediate execution?

Part A 4 marks

Determine matched volume at €14, €15 and €16, the auction price and the fill on the €15 buy order.

At €14 match 50, at €15 match 150, and at €16 match 120. At €15 the more aggressive €16 buyer receives 120 first and the €15 buyer receives 30.

Part B 3 marks

Find the average cost of immediately buying 150 shares in the separate continuous book.

Fifty cost €751 and 100 cost €1,510. Total €2,261 divided by 150 equals about €15.0733 per share.

Part C 3 marks

A strict buy limit of €15.05 with immediate-or-cancel replaces the continuous order. What executes and why could this represent a different economic outcome?

Only 50 execute at €15.02 and 100 cancel. Price is protected but a future replacement purchase or missed exposure can create costs that the average price on 50 shares omits.

Case 2 · 10 marks

What did the dealer learn?

Part A 4 marks

Find buy probability, the high-state posterior after a buy and the model's bid and offer.

Buy probability is 0.5. Posterior high probability after a buy is 0.65, giving an offer of €106. After a sale it is 0.35, giving a bid of €94.

Part B 3 marks

Decompose the separate dealer's quote change and relate it to inventory.

The midpoint falls €100 to €99, while width stays €4. Lowering both sides can encourage purchases from the dealer and discourage additional client sales into its long inventory.

Part C 3 marks

What evidence would help distinguish the inventory response from a common-information change?

Examine the dealer's signed position and hedge, other venues' synchronized midpoints, subsequent prices as the inventory unwinds, and any public announcement. Either hypothesis can produce a quote revision after a trade.

Case 3 · 10 marks

A buy order that was only partly executed

Part A 4 marks

Calculate total implementation shortfall and its basis points against intended decision notional.

Filled shares cost 1,200 × €0.16 = €192. Unfilled shares cost 800 × €0.35 = €280. Including €12 fees gives €484. On €50,000 intended notional this is 96.8 bp.

Part B 3 marks

Reconcile the same total into decision delay and post-arrival shortfall.

Decision delay costs 2,000 × €0.10 = €200. The filled post-arrival portion costs €72 and the unfilled portion €200, plus fees €12, totaling €284 after arrival.

Part C 3 marks

If the full 2,000 could have been bought at arrival for €300 extra impact above arrival, compare that path with the actual post-arrival outcome, and qualify the interpretation. Ignore fees on the alternative.

The alternative post-arrival cost is €300 versus actual €284, a €16 higher realized cost. It would have removed non-execution risk earlier. An ex-post comparison does not prove the slower route was optimal in advance.

Case 4 · 10 marks

A settlement failure and a routing dispute

Part A 3 marks

Calculate contractual principal, replacement cost difference and the full principal actually transferred in the failed DvP exchange.

Principal is €120,000. Replacement cost rises €600 to €120,600. No principal was transferred in the failed linked exchange.

Part B 3 marks

Compare costs on the second order and identify the preferable route under the stated conditions.

Venue A costs €4,027. B costs €1,000 + €3,030 + €2 = €4,032. A is cheaper by €5 despite B's best first price.

Part C 4 marks

Give two reasons why the actual settlement or routing review could differ, and the evidence each would require.

Recovery of replacement loss depends on contractual and clearing remedies. Inspect settlement logs and the relevant contract. Feasibility of quoted fills depends on time, executable size and actual venue access. Reconstruct synchronized instructions, quotes and fees.
