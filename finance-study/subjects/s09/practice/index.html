Theory midterm · 10 marks

A patient buyer and a moving quote

After lessons 1–5, allow 20 minutes. Explain the reasoning behind your answer.

The best ask is 100.10 for 50 shares and the next is 100.30 for 100. A buyer needs 100 shares now. Alternatively it can post a limit bid at 100.00. Ignore fees.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate the average aggressive execution price and cost versus the best ask.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Which risks does a posted buy limit shift onto the trader?

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    How do inventory risk and adverse selection lead dealers to change quotes?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the midterm marking guide

Part A · 4 marks

Buy 50 at 100.10 and 50 at 100.30. Total is 10,020, average 100.20, and the order costs 10 more than pricing all 100 at 100.10. Give credit for the fills, total, average and benchmark comparison.

Part B · 3 marks

The limit caps the price of an execution. The order can remain unfilled or partly filled behind earlier orders. It can also be filled just before adverse news drives the value lower.

Part C · 3 marks

An already long dealer may shade quotes to discourage further purchases and induce sales from its inventory. A dealer facing better-informed counterparties needs compensation for trading on the wrong side of news. State which evidence would distinguish inventory from informed order flow.

Problem 1

The best bid is 99 and the best offer 101. What is the quoted spread relative to the midpoint?

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

The midpoint is 100 and the spread is 2. Relative quoted spread is 2%, or 200 basis points. One side of the spread is about half that distance from the midpoint.

Problem 2

Why might a passive buy order fill just before the price falls?

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

Other traders can sell against a stale buy quote when they obtain negative information. Assess the subsequent price move and the probability of fills as well as explicit spread costs.

Assessment criteria
  • Apply the right theory and state the assumptions.
  • Show each step with consistent units, dates and currency.
  • Interpret the result, test an alternative and explain what could change the decision.
Suggested academic marking scheme

Use these weights when assessing the complete subject. The four problem sets collect the written problems from individual lectures. You can use the 10-mark midterm and the 40-mark subject paper with the weights below.

Problem sets 25%
Four sets per standard subject. Show reasoning, calculations, units and checks. The two practice problems in each subject are worked samples for these sets.
Theory midterm 20%
After the first half of the subject. Explain or derive a model, identify assumptions and state what would change if one failed.
Seminar paper or laboratory 25%
During the second half. Apply the theory to a case, dataset or implementation. Address an alternative explanation and document how to reproduce the result.
Individual final examination 30%
At the end of the subject. Solve an unfamiliar problem, interpret the result and critique a claim. M&A and lending each include a 30-mark examination question with a marking guide.
