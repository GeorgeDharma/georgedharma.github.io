Missing observations

Record why data are absent. Filling a market holiday and filling a suspended stock can imply very different economic assumptions.

The starting point

A blank price can mean a market holiday, a trading suspension, a missing data delivery, or a company that no longer trades. Those situations imply different things about what an investor could observe or do.

Flag the reason when known. Carrying forward a price treats the last observation as a valuation assumption. It does not demonstrate a new trade occurred. A run of filled prices can artificially lower estimated volatility. Keep the original missing values available when testing the consequences.

Theory and method

Missing-completely-at-random, missing-at-random and nonignorable missingness

MCAR · MAR · MNAR

Only half of high-risk accounts report returns, while all low-risk accounts report. When could weighting the observed values recover the portfolio mean?

A high-risk account might report less often because the reporting process is onerous for its product class. If reporting within that class is unrelated to how the account actually performed, observed high-risk accounts can represent the others using known class weights. If customers with especially poor outcomes hide them, a class adjustment alone leaves selection inside the class. The distinction concerns the mechanism causing absence, which generally cannot be proven using only the returns that arrived.

How the result is built

Estimated target mean under within-group representativeness = Σg target share_g × observed group mean_g.

g is a documented group, target share its share of all eligible accounts including missing ones, and the observed group mean is the average of known returns in that group. MCAR means missingness independent of data, MAR allows dependence on observed attributes and MNAR can still depend on the unobserved outcome.

  1. The register contains 80 low-risk and 20 high-risk accounts. All 80 low-risk reports average +2%. Only 10 high-risk reports arrive and average −6%.
  2. The 90 observed returns average (80 × 2% + 10 × −6%)/90 ≈ +1.11%. Restoring original group shares gives 0.80 × 2% + 0.20 × −6% = +0.4%.
  3. If the 10 unreported high-risk accounts actually average −16%, the full mean is (160−60−160)/100 = −0.6%. Weighting by class misses that within-class selection.

When the reasoning holds

  • The population class weights and eligibility register must be known. A register dropping failed accounts can hide part of the target even before weighting.
  • For the weighted estimate, reporting within each class must be independent of unobserved outcomes after the observed predictors are included. This is a substantive assumption requiring external evidence or sensitivity checks.

Varying an assumption to see how the answer or decision changes.
First example

A share last traded at €50 on Monday, is suspended Tuesday and reopens Wednesday at €40. Its observed change from Monday to Wednesday is −20%. Marking Tuesday at €50 yields a Tuesday accounting return of zero under that policy, but there was no Tuesday trade establishing that price.

Missing is a statement about observation

A missing value can mean the field does not apply, the event did not occur, a machine failed, trading was suspended, or the vendor has not delivered the record yet. These reasons lead to different economic conclusions. A valid zero amount means an observed value of zero. Record the reason separately from the numeric value.

When missingness is related to performance, analysing only complete rows can bias an estimate. For example riskier issuers may stop reporting before failure. In contrast, if a random transmission failure is unrelated to prices after conditioning on known factors, the effect of complete-case analysis may differ. Explain the mechanism before choosing an imputation rule.

What filling really assumes

Forward filling carries the latest observed value ahead. It can be appropriate for a slowly changing descriptive label when the last definition genuinely remains in force. Applied to a suspended share, it produces a stale valuation mark and an apparent zero daily move. No buyer or seller has demonstrated that the mark is executable.

Linear interpolation fills an intermediate point using both earlier and later observations. It may be reasonable for a retrospective chart under an explicit assumption, but it introduces future information into a real-time trading simulation. A zero fill makes the stronger claim that the unobserved outcome is exactly zero. Each operation answers a different question.

Preserve the denominator and show sensitivity

An equal-weight portfolio holding a missing asset has an unknown total return unless the missing return can be recovered or bounded. Renormalizing the remaining assets describes an observed-assets-only portfolio. Keeping the original weights and using zero assumes no change in the missing holding. Neither method simply reproduces the full portfolio without an assumption.

A sensitivity analysis makes the uncertainty visible. Assign a credible low and high outcome to the missing position and pass both through the original portfolio weights. Report how much of the portfolio weight is affected and whether the key decision changes across those bounds. Preserve raw records so a later correction can replace the assumption.

Method

Choose a missing-data policy

  1. Classify the absence

    Identify holidays, suspensions, delistings, reporting lags or data errors using metadata and independent event records.

  2. Match the use case

    A reporting valuation, backtest and descriptive chart may require different treatments. State whether an assumed value can be traded at or merely marked.

  3. Quantify exposure

    Report observed coverage by economic weight and compare explicit scenarios for material gaps before presenting an aggregate.

Worked case

The suspended third holding

An equal-weight portfolio has three holdings with daily returns +6%, −3% and unknown. The third was suspended, so there is no verified execution price that day. The risk team asks for the whole portfolio’s range if the missing return is between −15% and +3%.

Working

  1. Observed-only renormalization gives (6% − 3%)/2 = +1.5%, which describes the two-traded-holding portfolio.
  2. Forward-marking the third at zero gives (6% − 3% + 0%)/3 = +1.0% on the full original weights.
  3. At the proposed bounds the portfolio ranges from (6 − 3 − 15)/3 = −4% to (6 − 3 + 3)/3 = +2%. Coverage of actual returns is two thirds of starting capital.

Interpretation

The sign of the full portfolio return is therefore uncertain. Reporting only +1.5% would conceal a decision-relevant exposure.

A common mistake

A suspension is filled with a sequence of zeros and the resulting low standard deviation is presented as evidence of low economic risk.

How to correct it

Flag stale marks, distinguish measured from latent moves, and compare reopening prices or risk scenarios. The missing transaction evidence cannot establish stability.

Quick check
Which quantity can be established directly from these observations?

The Monday and Wednesday prices are observed. The Tuesday execution price and intraday movements cannot be recovered from the missing record.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A portfolio allocates 70% to a bond returning +2% and 30% to a missing holding. The missing holding’s return is plausibly between −10% and +4%.

  1. Part A · 5 marks

    Calculate the complete-case return after renormalization, the zero-filled full return and the bounded full-portfolio range.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Why would interpolating with tomorrow’s recorded price be problematic in a same-day trading simulation?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 5 marks

Observed-only return is +2%. Zero-filled portfolio is 0.70 × 2% = +1.4%. Bounds are 1.4% + 0.30 × (−10%) = −1.6% and 1.4% + 0.30 × 4% = +2.6%.

Part B 3 marks

Tomorrow’s value was unavailable at the simulated decision time. Interpolation can be labelled as a retrospective estimate for a chart, but using it as current tradable evidence introduces look-ahead bias.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Only half of high-risk accounts report returns, while all low-risk accounts report. When could weighting the observed values recover the portfolio mean?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would first recover the complete account register and compare reporting rates by class. If missingness is explainable by observed class and the reporting accounts represent the rest inside each class, the 80/20 weighted answer is 0.4%. I would show a within-class adverse scenario because poorly performing high-risk accounts could be selectively silent, in which case the adjustment is insufficient.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can a comparison of the 90 observed returns alone establish that the 10 missing ones average −6%?

Compare with the follow-up answer

It cannot establish that claim directly because their outcomes are unobserved. Recover independent statements, later liquidation values or external servicing data, and compare observable predictors of nonresponse. Without additional evidence I would present the range obtained under plausible missing outcomes alongside the assumption-based estimate.
