Pre- and post-money values

Separate the value assigned before the new investment from the cash added at closing. Calculate ownership on the agreed share-count basis.

The starting point

Pre-money value is the value assigned to the company’s existing equity immediately before a round. In a simple primary investment for newly issued shares, post-money value equals pre-money value plus new company cash. An investor’s stake is its investment divided by post-money value.

Distinguish new cash going into the company from a purchase of shares from an existing owner. The latter is a secondary sale and does not increase company cash. Option pools, convertibles or different share rights can change the agreed ownership denominator.

Theory and method

Pre-money option-pool shuffle

Fully diluted capitalization · Post-financing option reserve

Two term sheets offer €8m pre-money and €2m primary financing. Why might founders finish with different ownership?

A founder hears that a €2m investor will own 20% after an €8m pre-money round. One term sheet also requires an unissued employee pool equal to 10% of the post-financing capitalization and prices the pool into the pre-money share count. The investor’s 20% can stay fixed while the founders absorb the future employee reservation. Another agreement can create the pool after financing and dilute founders and investors together. Timing changes economic ownership.

How the result is built

Investor fraction f = I/(V + I). Pre-funded founder fraction = 1 − f − g

I is the euro primary investment, V headline pre-money equity, f new investor fraction, and g the agreed employee option reserve as a fraction of the post-close fully diluted count. Existing founders are the only other holders here.

  1. At €8m pre-money and €2m primary, f = 20%. A post-close 10% reserve charged entirely before the round leaves founders 70%.
  2. With 700,000 founder shares, 70% corresponds to 1m fully diluted post-close shares. The investor receives 200,000, the option reserve is 100,000 and the share price is €2m/200,000 = €10.
  3. If the pool is instead created after an 80/20 deal and dilutes both by 10% of the final total, founders own 72%, investor 18% and employees have 10%.

When the reasoning holds

  • The agreement must specify whether the pool is measured on the final fully diluted denominator and who bears the expansion. A reserve of 10% of the old share count has another result.
  • The simplified cap table has no prior options, convertibles or preferred conversion adjustments. Include them before solving a real round.
Terms in this lesson
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Secondary shares
Existing shares sold by their holder. The sale transfers ownership and the seller receives the proceeds.
Primary shares
New shares issued by the company. Their sale brings money into the company and increases the outstanding share count.
First example

A founder owns 600,000 shares. A round values existing equity at €6m, implying €10 per share. An investor pays €2m into the company for 200,000 new shares. It owns 200,000/800,000 = 25%, and the post-money equity value is €8m.

What exactly does a valuation describe?

A pre-money valuation is the agreed equity value immediately before the new company financing. In a straightforward primary round with identical rights, post-money value equals pre-money value plus new cash paid to the company. These are negotiation terms and implied prices, not a guarantee that the company could be sold for that amount tomorrow.

Use the agreed share-count basis. Basic outstanding shares may omit options, warrants or convertible instruments that participate economically. A fully diluted capitalization table includes the securities the agreement says count as if converted or exercised. Changing this base can transfer ownership even when the stated pre-money value is unchanged.

A primary purchase differs from a secondary transfer

A primary issue creates new company shares and adds cash to the business. A secondary transaction transfers existing shares from an owner to a buyer. It changes who owns the shares but contributes no operating cash. A combined transaction can contain both, and each leg needs a separate calculation.

For a simple priced issue, pre-money value divided by the agreed pre-round share count gives the issue price. New primary investment divided by price per share yields new shares. Then add any secondary shares purchased to the buyer’s existing holding and divide by total post-primary shares.

Check rights and optional securities

Two securities with apparently identical percentages can have different economic value because of preferences or conversion rights. The simple post-money formula establishes headline ownership, while a payout waterfall is needed to value different classes. A preferred price need not imply every existing ordinary share could sell at exactly that price.

An option pool reserved before financing can dilute existing holders more than the new investor if the pricing convention includes the reserve in the pre-round denominator. State whether unused options are included and whether a top-up occurs before or after the investor acquires shares.

Method

Reconcile a priced round

  1. Fix the share basis

    Write each existing investor and every security included by the agreement. Verify the pre-money price per share on this exact basis, documenting any options or conversions.

  2. Separate cash destinations

    Show primary capital going into the company and secondary cash going to sellers. Calculate issued shares and transferred shares independently at their stipulated prices.

  3. Check the closing register

    Recompute each owner’s shares and percentage. Total shares increase only for new issues, and the overall post-money company value includes only new company cash under the simple assumptions.

Worked case

A mixed financing for a medical supplier

Before the round the founders own all 900,000 fully diluted ordinary shares. Pre-money equity is €9m. One investor buys €3m of newly issued shares and €1m of existing founder shares, both at the same issue price. No other securities or preferences apply.

Working

  1. Issue price is €9m/900,000 = €10. The primary leg produces 300,000 new shares and €3m company cash. The secondary leg transfers 100,000 existing shares for €1m paid to founders.
  2. The investor owns 400,000 of 1.2m shares, or 33.33%. Founders retain 800,000 shares, or 66.67%. Reconcile the registered shares to 1.2m rather than adding the secondary shares twice.
  3. Headline post-money company value is €12m. The total €4m paid by the investor is not all invested in operations. Treating the post-money as €13m would overstate cash the company receives.

Interpretation

The transaction sets a reference price, not an assurance of liquidity or identical value after a new investment strategy. Reassess the economics if the new securities have different rights.

A common mistake

Using total cash paid by a new investor as the primary round size even when part is paid directly to founders.

How to correct it

Follow bank recipients. The company’s funding and new shares come from its primary issue. A secondary acquisition affects only who owns existing shares.

Quick check
A new investor contributes €3m for newly issued ordinary shares at a €9m pre-money value. With no other changes, what percentage does the investor acquire?

Post-money value is €9m + €3m = €12m. Divide the contribution by €12m. Dividing by €9m compares new cash only with the value of the pre-existing equity.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A startup has 600,000 shares at €6m pre-money. A buyer invests €2m in new shares and buys €0.5m of old shares from one founder at the same price.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute purchase price per share, company funding and post-money value.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Calculate the investor’s final ownership and explain the effect of the secondary sale.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The price is €10 per share. The company receives €2m, issues 200,000 shares and has €8m headline post-money value.

Part B 3 marks

It owns 200,000 new plus 50,000 transferred shares, or 250,000/800,000 = 31.25%. The secondary sale raises the investor’s stake but adds no company cash and no new shares.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two term sheets offer €8m pre-money and €2m primary financing. Why might founders finish with different ownership?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask which fully diluted share count sets the issue price. In the pre-funded version the investor keeps 20%, employees receive a 10% reserve and founders keep 70%. In the post-funded version the new pool reduces both initial owners proportionally, leaving founders 72% and the investor 18%. I would show closing share counts and convertibles as well as the headline pre-money term.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The investor insists on 20% and the post-close pool rises to 15%, still charged before the financing. What happens to the effective price?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Founders retain only 65%. Their 700,000 existing shares imply about 1,076,923 total fully diluted shares. The investor gets about 215,385 shares for €2m, roughly €9.29 per share. The larger pre-funded reserve lowers the price per existing-equivalent share.
