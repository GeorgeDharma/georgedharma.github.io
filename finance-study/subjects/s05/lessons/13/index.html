Converting post-money SAFEs into a priced venture round

Calculate how much ownership several post-money cap SAFEs imply before new round money.

Theory and terminology

Post-money SAFE conversion

SAFE · valuation cap · priced equity round

What you should be able to explain

  • Calculate how much ownership several post-money cap SAFEs imply before new round money.
  • Convert that ownership into actual shares and distinguish a discount-only conversion.

An early promise becomes shares later

A simple agreement for future equity, or SAFE, brings cash into a start-up today and defines what the investor receives if specified future events occur. A common version converts when a priced equity financing sets a price per share. The standard YC form generally has no interest or maturity date. A convertible note is a debt instrument that can have interest, maturity and repayment terms. Check the actual document. Singapore’s VIMA suite separately provides a convertible note and a pre-Series A Convertible Agreement Regarding Equity (CARE). A CARE holder must use that signed instrument’s definitions before applying any ownership formula.

A valuation cap limits the price used to calculate conversion. A discount-only form prices the investor’s shares below the new-money share price by an agreed factor. Some agreements have other combinations and choices. An 80% conversion price means paying 80 cents for each dollar of new-money share price, a 20% discount.

Place ownership on the correct side of the round

For the post-money cap-only agreements illustrated here, investment divided by cap gives the stake implied by the cap after the seed instruments but before the priced round’s new cash. Add the seed fractions to find how much legacy holders retain. This shortcut assumes the cap conversion governs, all instruments use the stated compatible denominator and no other adjustment applies.

If the financing is at a sufficiently low price, the financing-price route may give the SAFE more shares than the cap-based estimate. A pre-money cap uses a different capitalization relationship. Additional option-pool increases or pro rata purchases can also change the table. A useful model explicitly labels every column “after SAFEs” or “after new financing.”

Translate percentages into instruments and test the price

If the legacy founders have 8.5 million common shares and must retain 85% after conversions, the pre-new-money capitalization must be 8.5m/0.85. Use the resulting total to calculate each SAFE’s share count. Dividing its cash by that count gives the implied conversion price. Compare it with the new-money price under the contract.

Finally issue the round shares. If the priced investor buys 20% of the post-round company, each existing holder keeps 80% of its previous percentage unless it invests more. A before-and-after ownership table should add to 100% in each column. Cash the founder has already received under the SAFEs is not new cash raised in the priced round.

Method

Build the result

sᵢ = Iᵢ/Kᵢ; pre-round shares = L/(1 − Σsᵢ); post-round percentage of SAFE i = sᵢ × (1 − n).

For each compatible cap-only SAFE i, sᵢ is its implied pre-new-money fraction, Iᵢ cash invested and Kᵢ its post-money cap in the same currency. L is existing legacy shares and n the new investors’ final ownership fraction. Assume no pool increase or additional purchases.

  1. Calculate each seed fraction under a binding cap and sum them. The remaining percentage belongs to legacy shares.
  2. Divide the actual legacy share count by its percentage to obtain the post-conversion total. Assign each SAFE its share fraction.
  3. Calculate the new-money price and share issue. Test the conversion route and check both capitalization totals.
Worked case

Two early investors, then a priced round

Founders hold 8.5m shares. Seed A invested $0.6m at a $6m post-money cap and seed B invested $0.4m at an $8m post-money cap. Both are cap-only, both caps govern, and there is no option pool. The next round invests $5m at a $20m pre-money value measured after SAFE conversion.

Working

  1. A is entitled to 0.6/6 = 10% before the round and B to 0.4/8 = 5%. The founders retain 85%.
  2. Pre-round shares are 8.5/0.85 = 10m. A receives 1m shares at an implied $0.60 and B gets 0.5m at $0.80.
  3. New investors pay $20m/10m = $2 per share, above both cap conversion prices. Their $5m buys 2.5m shares. The final denominator is 12.5m.
  4. The founders hold 8.5/12.5 = 68%, A owns 8%, B owns 4% and the new investors own 20%. Together they total 100%.

Decision and interpretation

Show founders both the 15% already committed to seed investors before the priced round and the final 68% founder outcome. A lower-priced financing would require recomputing the applicable conversion route.

Quick check
A binding post-money cap implies 12% before an equity round. New-money investors then buy 25% of the company. No one else invests. What stake does the SAFE holder retain?

The pre-round owners retain 75% of the final company. The SAFE stake becomes 12% × 75% = 9%.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A company has 9m founder shares. It raised €0.9m on a binding €9m post-money cap SAFE. It then receives €4m at a €12m pre-money value after conversion. Find each post-round ownership percentage.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. SAFE ownership before round money is 10%, leaving 90% for the founders. The pre-round share total is 9m/0.90 = 10m. The SAFE receives 1m shares.
  2. The new price is €12m/10m = €1.20. The €4m investment buys about 3.333m shares, producing a 25% new-money stake.
  3. Founders hold 90% × 75% = 67.5%, the SAFE holds 10% × 75% = 7.5%, and new investors hold 25%.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

When an investor invests $600,000 at a $6 million post-money SAFE cap, do they own 10% after the next priced round?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

When the cap governs, the investment implies 10% immediately before new priced-round money under the stated capitalization definition. The next round’s new shares dilute that stake. The precise result also depends on the instrument’s conversion terms, option-pool treatment and whether the investor subscribes for extra shares.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Consider an unrelated €120,000 discount-only SAFE with an 80% price factor and a €1.50 round price. How many shares does it receive?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Its conversion price is €1.50 × 0.80 = €1.20, giving 100,000 shares. Its ownership percentage additionally requires the fully diluted share count after all relevant issuances.
