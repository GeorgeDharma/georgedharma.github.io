Debt schedules

Model interest, mandatory amortization, cash sweeps and covenants. Test whether cash remains adequate before counting a possible distribution.

The starting point

A debt schedule follows each loan from its opening balance through interest, scheduled principal payments and optional repayments. The amount shown as earnings is rarely the cash available to service debt. Taxes, investment, working capital and a minimum operating balance can consume cash first.

A cash sweep requires some specified share of surplus cash to repay principal. The contract defines surplus, order of payments and repayment limits. Recalculate future interest after principal declines. A distribution to owners must also satisfy cash needs and the applicable loan restrictions.

Theory and method

Debt sculpting to a debt-service coverage ratio

CFADS · Scheduled amortization

How would you calculate the largest three-year loan a business can repay while keeping a 1.25 times annual coverage ratio?

A business with seasonal expansion may generate little cash in the first year but more later. An equal principal installment can fail early despite adequate total cash. Debt sculpting sets each service payment to an amount supported by that year’s cash available for debt service, then divides it into interest and principal. Discounting the permitted payments at the loan rate gives the initial loan if it must be fully amortized by maturity.

How the result is built

Serviceₜ = CFADSₜ/k. D₀ = Σₜ Serviceₜ/(1 + i)ᵗ

CFADSₜ is cash available before loan interest and principal in year t, k the minimum coverage ratio, Serviceₜ scheduled interest plus principal, i the annual loan rate and D₀ maximum opening principal under these terms.

  1. With cash of €3.75m, €5.00m and €6.25m and k = 1.25, permitted annual service is €3m, €4m and €5m.
  2. At 10% the present value of those payments is €3m/1.10 + €4m/1.10² + €5m/1.10³ = €9.7896m. Interest in year one is about €0.979m and principal repayment is about €2.021m.
  3. Carry the remaining €7.769m forward. With unrounded balances, year two closes at €4.545m and the final €5m pays precisely the last principal and interest.

When the reasoning holds

  • CFADS includes taxes, working capital and necessary investment under the loan definition. A debt schedule based on EBITDA alone can overstate capacity.
  • The loan has one fixed rate, annual year-end service and no balloon or fees. Other covenants, reserve accounts or borrowing-base limits may bind first.

Terms in this lesson
Covenant
A promise or condition in a financing agreement, such as maintaining an agreed level of cash.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Working capital
Money tied up in day-to-day operations. For a deal, agree which receivables, stock and payables count.
Cash buffer
Money kept available to meet upcoming bills or unexpected payments.
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Check how the seller calculates and adjusts this measure.
First example

A loan opens at €20m. Interest of 5% on the opening balance is €1m. Cash available before debt service is €5m. After interest and €2m mandatory principal, €2m remains above the required cash buffer. A 50% sweep repays €1m extra, leaving €17m principal.

How a borrowing balance changes

Loans can require mandatory principal payments, permit voluntary prepayment, or require a sweep of cash remaining after defined uses. Begin with opening principal and follow each drawing and repayment. Cash interest pays for use of funds. It generally does not reduce principal. Payment-in-kind interest, when expressly agreed, adds to principal instead.

For an annual teaching model, the interest basis might be opening debt. Real loans may use daily or average balances or floating reference rates plus a spread. Use exactly the convention supplied. Financing scenarios can change both the cash charge and the resulting closing debt.

Cash available for a sweep

Start with cash the company can actually use after operating needs, taxes, investment and any required buffer. Deduct interest and required amortization in the contractual order. Define the sweep percentage and apply it only to positive eligible excess, subject to remaining principal and any permitted exemptions.

A distribution to the sponsor follows loan covenants, contractual restrictions and cash needs. The fact that a model shows a year-end surplus does not mean it is freely distributable. A working-capital peak shortly after year-end can make that apparent surplus necessary for operations.

Covenants test specific definitions

A leverage covenant may compare defined net debt with agreed earnings. An interest-coverage rule may compare earnings or available cash with interest. The loan agreement can define different add-backs, testing dates and cure rights than an analyst’s default calculation. Display both the economic measure and contractual test if they differ.

An incurrence covenant may restrict a new action, whereas a maintenance covenant can require ongoing compliance at specified tests. A forecasted failure can block borrowing, require a waiver or trigger other consequences under the case terms. Do not assume every breach produces immediate liquidation.

Method

Build a repeatable debt roll

  1. Calculate the charge

    Apply the specified rate to the stated balance and period. Keep cash interest and any contractually capitalized interest separate.

  2. Test cash and principal

    Subtract required payments in order, apply permitted surplus repayment and use the exact buffer definition. Update the next opening balance and interest basis.

  3. Check contractual headroom

    Calculate the stated covenant and compare with its threshold under base and downside cases. A ratio that still passes today may fail after profits fall next year.

Worked case

A term loan with a cash sweep

Opening principal is €30m at 6% on opening debt. The company has €8m cash available before debt service, already after its required buffer. Mandatory amortization is €3m. Half of remaining positive cash must repay principal. Covenant net debt must not exceed 3 times €8m EBITDA, and end-year eligible cash is €1m. Any surplus left after the sweep must be transferred into a supplier reserve excluded from this covenant.

Working

  1. Interest is €1.8m. After interest and €3m mandatory repayment, eligible surplus is €3.2m. Sweep 50%, or €1.6m.
  2. Closing principal is €30m − €3m − €1.6m = €25.4m. Net debt for the test is €24.4m after deducting €1m eligible cash.
  3. Leverage is €24.4m/€8m = 3.05 times. The limit is €24m of net debt, so the test misses by €0.4m despite sufficient cash for the required schedule. The unswept €1.6m goes into the restricted supplier reserve, so it cannot be netted.

Interpretation

The covenant may require a waiver or another contractual remedy. Show a separate scenario where cash is retained and eligible instead, rather than changing the stated covenant inputs after seeing a breach.

A common mistake

Using opening gross debt for an end-period net-leverage covenant or deducting cash unavailable under the agreement.

How to correct it

Follow the contract’s date, netting rules and earnings definition. Do not substitute an economically appealing balance without reconciliation.

Quick check
A loan opens at €12m. The borrower pays €1m scheduled principal and €0.5m extra principal after paying all interest. What is closing principal?

Both principal payments reduce the loan balance. Interest paid is a financing cost for the period and does not reduce the original amount borrowed.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

Opening debt is €16m at 5% on the opening balance. Available cash before service and after buffer is €4m. Required principal is €1.2m, with 50% sweep on remaining cash. Closing eligible cash is €0.5m and covenant EBITDA €5m.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute interest, sweep and closing principal.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Test a maximum 2.5 times net-leverage covenant.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Interest is €0.8m. After interest and €1.2m principal, €2m remains, so sweep €1m. Closing principal is €16m − €1.2m − €1m = €13.8m.

Part B 3 marks

Net debt equals €13.8m − €0.5m = €13.3m. Leverage is 2.66 times, above 2.5. Permitted net debt is €12.5m, so excess is €0.8m.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How would you calculate the largest three-year loan a business can repay while keeping a 1.25 times annual coverage ratio?

Compare with a spoken answer

I would translate the coverage covenant into the cash the lender can receive each year. The company can pay €3m, €4m and €5m while maintaining 1.25 times coverage. Discounting those contractual payments at 10% gives the opening amount, roughly €9.79m. Then I would roll principal forward and verify the last balance is zero. That second calculation catches interest timing and rounding errors.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Year-one CFADS falls by €0.55m before closing while later amounts stay fixed. How much must the opening loan shrink?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Permitted first-year service falls by €0.55m/1.25 = €0.44m. Its present value at 10% is €0.40m. The maximum loan falls by €0.40m to roughly €9.39m. If the original loan were already outstanding, a waiver, added equity or revised schedule would be needed instead.
