Exit choices and liquidity

A sale, public listing or continuation vehicle changes timing, fees and execution risk. Test the return with a delayed exit.

The starting point

An exit turns an ownership stake into cash for investors. A trade sale transfers the company to a buyer. A public listing can create a market price while restrictions or limited trading volume delay sales. A continuation vehicle lets a sponsor roll assets into a new fund, raising questions about pricing and choice for existing investors.

Compare the amount shareholders can actually receive, transaction costs, timing and uncertainty. An attractive quoted price does not create liquidity if the shares cannot be sold then. Test delays and any remaining risks before comparing offers.

Theory and method

Continuation-fund roll-versus-sell analysis

Secondary buyout · GP-led secondary transaction

How would you compare a cash exit with rolling a stake into a continuation fund managed by the same sponsor?

A fund owning an aging asset can sell to a new vehicle managed by the same team. Existing investors can sometimes sell for cash or reinvest their sale proceeds into that new vehicle. Treat rolling as a fresh investment at today’s transfer price. New fees, carry, remaining leverage and time to exit affect what the investor ultimately collects. The manager’s involvement on both sides makes independent pricing and genuine choice economically important.

How the result is built

Roll NPV = Σₜ E[Dₜ]/(1 + h)ᵗ − P

P is the euro cash available today if the investor sells, Dₜ later distributions to the rolling investor after all new fees and carry, h the risk-appropriate annual required return and t years after the decision.

  1. A stakeholder may sell for €12m cash now. The alternative is a two-year roll with expected €16m gross and €1m new fees and carry assumed paid at exit. Its expected net receipt is €15m.
  2. At a 12% hurdle the roll is worth €15m/1.12² = €11.96m, about €0.04m below the certain selling price. Comparing €16m directly with €12m exaggerates the roll’s appeal.
  3. For equality, net exit proceeds must reach €12m × 1.12² = €15.0528m, or €16.0528m gross with unchanged €1m terminal charges.

When the reasoning holds

  • Cash alternative is genuinely executable and the roll receives the same economic stake assumed in the cash-flow forecast. An imposed haircut, extra commitment or different rights changes P or Dₜ.
  • A risky expected exit requires matching required returns and scenario analysis. An interested manager’s mark is not independent evidence of a future third-party bid.

Terms in this lesson
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
How to read the notation
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

A sponsor can sell today for €24m net or expects €30m net in three years. At a 10% annual required return the delayed cash is worth about €22.54m today. The immediate bid is higher under those assumptions, even before considering possible exit-price uncertainty.

An exit price is not always cash

A trade buyer can offer cash, shares or a mix. A stock market listing may establish a quoted price while the fund remains unable to sell all its holdings before a lock-up ends or without affecting the price. Separate amount quoted, fraction sold, time of sale and costs paid.

A continuation vehicle buys assets from an older fund and lets some investors take cash while others roll into a new fund managed by the same sponsor. That creates a valuation and incentive question because the manager participates on both sides. Independent price evidence and a genuine choice help evaluate terms.

Compare cash on the same date

A high future sale price can be inferior to a lower certain bid today. Discount the actual amounts available to the investor after fees, debt repayment and any retained exposure. If all of those are already netted, do not subtract them again. Use one discount rate only when the assumptions justify it.

An earn-out ties part of consideration to later performance. Expected nominal payment equals the weighted payoffs under plausible outcomes. Discount it to today and assess whether the buyer controls the accounting measure or business decisions used to determine the payment. Collectibility may matter even if the target meets its goal.

Liquidity, choice and incentives

Selling part of a position can reduce concentration while retaining upside exposure. The unsold part remains at risk, so valuing it at the sale price for a small initial block can be optimistic. Model alternative prices and delays for its eventual sale.

Fund maturity can also push a manager toward accepting an immediate exit. Check whether a proposed extension creates real extra value or mainly postpones recognition of losses. Compare what investors would receive if they sold now, rolled over, or supported a different buyer under the same obligations.

Method

Build a proceeds timeline

  1. Specify the cash to the fund

    Translate the headline offer into net equity consideration and immediate saleable cash. Record any escrow, retained shares or contingent consideration separately.

  2. Value delayed cash

    Set the dates, probabilities and possible amounts of later payments. Discount each component and compare the value range with a certain immediate alternative.

  3. Trace control and conflicts

    Identify who can influence the contingent metric, how a price was determined in a related-party transaction and whether investors have credible alternatives.

Worked case

A cash bid versus a staged listing

A trade buyer offers the fund €28m cash today net of all costs. A listing provides €12m net now and leaves shares expected to sell for €20m net in two years. Use 12% for those delayed proceeds. No interim dividends. The fund can choose either alternative.

Working

  1. The listing’s delayed component is €20m/1.12² = €15.94m. Its total present value is about €27.94m, just below the trade buyer.
  2. The break-even future sale price solves €12m + future/1.12² = €28m, giving approximately €20.07m. A small fee, delay or price change can reverse the conclusion.
  3. If the €20m sale moves to year three, present value falls to €12m + €20m/1.12³ ≈ €26.24m. The timing assumption matters even if the quoted block value does not move.

Interpretation

A near tie in assessed present value need not imply identical risks. The listing leaves a concentrated illiquid stake, and evidence on the lock-up and market depth is material.

A common mistake

Treating the listing market capitalization multiplied by the ownership percentage as cash received on listing day.

How to correct it

Distinguish shares sold now from shares retained. Forecast the timing, fees and possible realizable value of the remainder and value each actual receipt.

Quick check
A fund can receive €11m today or a certain €12.1m in one year. At a 10% discount rate with no other effects, which has more present value?

€12.1m/1.10 is €11m. If the later payment were uncertain or delayed, a new comparison would be needed.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

An immediate net sale yields €16m. A rival pays €12m today and a €6m earn-out in one year if a goal is met, otherwise zero. Estimated success probability is 70%, and the discount rate for the expected earn-out is 10%.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compare the two offers in present value.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the break-even success probability and identify a contractual concern.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Expected earn-out is €4.2m and its present value is €3.818m. The rival package is about €15.818m, so immediate €16m leads by about €0.182m under these estimates.

Part B 3 marks

Solve €12m + €6m × p/1.10 = €16m, giving p = 73.33%. Check who controls the sales or earnings metric and whether the buyer must support the activities needed to achieve it.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How would you compare a cash exit with rolling a stake into a continuation fund managed by the same sponsor?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would model the rollover as writing a €12m cheque today. Its expected proceeds after the new layer of fees are €15m two years later, which does not quite earn the required 12%. I would also verify the transfer price against independent bids and check whether rolling investors face new capital commitments. The manager’s continued involvement may preserve expertise while creating a conflict in setting the transfer price.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A third party offers €12.8m for immediate sale on identical terms. How does that change the required terminal gross value for the roll?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The opportunity cost becomes €12.8m. At 12% for two years, the rolling stake needs €16.05632m net, or €17.05632m gross after the stated €1m charges. The new bid therefore strengthens the case for cash unless the continuation forecast or terms also improve.
