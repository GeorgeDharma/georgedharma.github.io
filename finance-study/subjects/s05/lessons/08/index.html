Returns and attribution

Compare the money multiple with annualized return. Break value creation into operating change, debt reduction, timing and the entry-to-exit multiple change.

The starting point

A money multiple divides total cash received by cash invested. It tells you the size of the return but ignores how long it took. For one investment and one exit, the annualized return is the multiple raised to one divided by the number of years, minus one.

Trace value change through operations, market pricing and financing. Enterprise value can rise because operating earnings grew or because buyers pay more per euro of earnings. Equity value can also rise when cash generated during ownership repays debt. These are different claims about performance.

Theory and method

Leveraged-buyout value creation bridge and dividend recapitalization

Sponsor MOIC · IRR timing effect

A sponsor says an early debt-funded dividend created value because its investment return rose. What would you test?

A dividend recapitalization borrows against a portfolio company and pays the funds to owners before exit. The sponsor receives cash sooner, which can increase the internal rate of return even when the same euro amount is removed from exit equity. To separate timing from value, compare all dated cash flows, the remaining net debt, financing costs and risk. Trace whether the dividend came from genuine operating cash or a new senior claim.

How the result is built

MOIC = (Σ distributions + exit equity)/Σ contributions. IRR solves Σₜ CFₜ/(1 + IRR)ᵗ = 0

MOIC is multiple on invested capital, CFₜ the sponsor’s dated euro cash flow with contributions negative, t years since investment, IRR the annual internal rate. Exit equity already deducts outstanding new debt.

  1. A sponsor pays €40m and receives €60m after two years. MOIC is 1.5 and IRR is √1.5 − 1 = 22.47%.
  2. Move €15m from exit to a debt-financed distribution at the end of year one, holding the total distribution constant and ignoring added interest solely to isolate timing. Cash flows become −€40m, +€15m, +€45m.
  3. The multiple stays 1.5. Solve 40(1 + r)² − 15(1 + r) − 45 = 0 to obtain about 26.46% annually. The timing difference raises this measure without increasing total euro proceeds.

When the reasoning holds

  • Earlier cash has real opportunity value, but borrowings add financing expense and default risk in practice. The no-cost example isolates one mechanism only.
  • IRR can be ambiguous for repeatedly changing cash-flow signs. Inspect NPV at a stated hurdle and the money multiple alongside IRR.

Terms in this lesson
Enterprise value
Value of the business operations attributable to its capital providers. A bridge adjusts it to the amount due to shareholders.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Net present value (NPV)
Today’s value of incoming cash minus today’s value of outgoing cash.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
First example

Buy a business at 8 times €5m earnings, financed with €20m debt and €20m equity. Sell at 8 times €6m earnings with €10m debt left. Exit equity is €38m. The €18m gain consists of €8m higher operating value and €10m lower debt. With a three-year holding period and no interim cash, the 1.9 times multiple implies about 23.9% annually.

Three measures answer different questions

The money multiple measures how much an investor gets for each unit of capital paid in. It ignores time. An annualized return accounts for how quickly the money was received. With one outflow and one later inflow, the annualized rate is the multiple raised to one divided by holding years, minus one.

With several flows, the internal rate of return is the discount rate that sets net present value to zero. An early dividend can raise the internal rate without changing total cash materially. Compare multiples, timing and actual cash value together, particularly when borrowing pays out a dividend before the exit.

Trace the increase in company value

In a simple buyout model, enterprise value equals operating earnings times an entry or exit multiple. A rise in operating value can come from stronger earnings or from a higher price the market pays per euro of earnings. Separating these mechanisms matters because managers may influence operations more directly than the exit market.

The attribution convention must specify where to place the interaction between changing earnings and multiples. One exact bridge first values the earnings change at the entry multiple, then values the multiple change on exit earnings. Another convention can be valid if its contributions sum correctly and are stated.

From enterprise value to sponsor cash

Equity equals enterprise value less net debt and other senior claims under a simple capital structure. Debt repayment increases the sponsor’s share of a given enterprise value. It is funded by operating cash, asset sales or additional injections. Investigate the source before calling every euro operational success.

Add interim distributions and subtract later capital contributions when measuring sponsor cash returns. Avoid adding debt paydown as an extra distribution if exit equity already reflects the lower remaining debt. Fees, outside investors and different share rights can make sponsor cash differ from total equity value.

Method

Reconcile return drivers

  1. Recreate entry and exit

    Calculate enterprise and equity values using consistent definitions, ownership and net debt. Include transaction fees and interim flows as stated.

  2. Bridge the change

    Value earnings growth at the entry multiple, multiple change on exit earnings, then add net debt reduction and other identifiable changes. Verify the reconciliation.

  3. Assess timing and repeatability

    Compute the money multiple and annualized return under the actual cash dates. Identify gains that rely on multiple expansion or new leverage rather than sustainable cash generation.

Worked case

A sponsor exits a components business

At entry earnings are €6m, the trading multiple is 7 times and net debt €20m. At exit three years later earnings are €8m, multiple 8 times and net debt €14m. The sponsor owns all equity. Ignore fees, distributions and extra contributions.

Working

  1. Entry enterprise value is €42m and equity €22m. Exit enterprise value is €64m and equity €50m. Sponsor value increases €28m.
  2. Earnings growth contributes (€8m − €6m) × 7 = €14m. Multiple expansion adds (8 − 7) × €8m = €8m. Net debt falls €6m. The three sum to €28m.
  3. Sponsor multiple is €50m/€22m = 2.273. Over three years annualized return is approximately 31.5%. If the exit multiple stayed at seven, exit equity would be €42m and the return multiple 1.909.

Interpretation

The statement that all gains came from “improved operations” would overstate the evidence. Explain separately how debt fell, whether growth consumed capital and what supported the rerating.

A common mistake

Adding sale price improvement, earnings growth and multiple expansion as three independent gains.

How to correct it

The last two jointly explain the enterprise sale price change under a chosen bridge. Add only the change in senior claims and actual interim cash after that.

Quick check
An investment doubles with one exit and no interim cash. Which holding period produces the higher annualized return?

The two-year rate is about 41.4% and the five-year rate about 14.9%. Amounts received match as a multiple, but capital is tied up for different lengths of time.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

At entry a company earns €4m, trades at 6 times and has €10m net debt. At exit it earns €5m, trades at 7 times, with €8m net debt. Holding time is two years, with no other cash flows.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate entry and exit equity and attribute the gain.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Compute sponsor multiple and annualized return.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Equity moves from €24m − €10m = €14m to €35m − €8m = €27m. Gain is €6m from earnings at the entry multiple, €5m from the higher multiple on exit earnings and €2m from debt reduction.

Part B 3 marks

The multiple is €27m/€14m = 1.929. Annualized return is the square root of 1.929 minus one, approximately 38.9%. A shorter holding time would raise that annualized figure without increasing euro proceeds.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A sponsor says an early debt-funded dividend created value because its investment return rose. What would you test?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would compare an operating bridge, a debt bridge and the investor’s dated cash. Taking €15m early and reducing exit equity by €15m leaves the same total cash, but the IRR rises because the receipt came earlier. That alone does not establish an operational improvement. I would calculate NPV at the fund’s hurdle and test new interest, covenant headroom and downside loss to the company.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The dividend borrowing costs €1.5m of incremental cash interest at exit. At a 10% hurdle does the recap still add NPV relative to the original?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The year-one €15m is worth €13.636m today. Exit proceeds fall by €16.5m relative to original, also worth €13.636m at a 10% two-year discount. Incremental NPV is zero under these simplified cash flows, even though the timing pattern and risk borne by the company changed.
