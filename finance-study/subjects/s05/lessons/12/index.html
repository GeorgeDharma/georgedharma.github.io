Comparing fund waterfalls and recovering excess carry

Compute interim and final LP and GP distributions for two waterfall designs.

Theory and terminology

Private-equity distribution waterfalls

carried interest · whole-of-fund · deal-by-deal · clawback

What you should be able to explain

  • Compute interim and final LP and GP distributions for two waterfall designs.
  • Separate the contractual clawback from the cash available to satisfy it.

Whose capital has been returned?

A limited partner, or LP, supplies capital. The general partner, or GP, can earn carried interest, a contractual share of investment profits. In a whole-of-fund waterfall the specified pool of capital must be returned before the GP participates in profits. Under a deal-by-deal structure, proceeds may first return the cost attached to the specific realized investment and then release carry.

Read what “capital” includes. Fees, realized write-downs, reserves and a preferred return can delay carry. Some agreements require prior loss restoration before paying on an individual deal. For a clean comparison the cases assume the LP funds all investment costs upfront, no management fees, GP capital, hurdle, taxes or interim reserves, and a final contractual fund-level 20% profit share.

Keep a distribution ledger by exit date

For every sale, note gross proceeds, reimbursed capital, profit available for carry, cash distributed to LPs and cash allocated to the GP. A cash multiple called DPI divides cumulative distributions to investors by paid-in capital. Early carry changes DPI and the timing of investor returns even when final net proceeds later converge.

A deal-by-deal payment rewards an early winner while capital is still at risk in unsold companies. A whole-of-fund method can have a different cash schedule while distributing exactly the same ultimate contractual profit. Keeping exited and remaining cost together on the same worksheet makes that timing difference visible.

Convert the final true-up into a cash plan

At final liquidation calculate lifetime profit on the agreed base, apply the carry rate and compare it with cumulative GP allocations. Excess carry can be repayable. An escrow is a segregated balance holding part of prior carry. It provides a source for repayment, while the remainder may still have to be collected from recipients or guarantors.

An obligation to repay is distinct from the ability to repay. Check the enforceable cap, treatment of tax, timing of interim tests and creditworthiness. For performance reporting, classify a clawback receipt separately so a later user can trace the adjustment to earlier carry.

Method

Build the result

Final GP entitlement = c × max(P − K, 0); clawback = max(early carry − final entitlement, 0).

c is the contracted fund-level carry percentage in this simplified no-hurdle example, P is all gross investment proceeds and K all covered invested capital. Cash values use the same currency. Early carry includes amounts held in escrow on the GP’s behalf.

  1. Allocate each sale under the actual interim rule. Track cumulative GP allocation even when partly escrowed.
  2. Recompute the agreed final entitlement once all costs and proceeds are known.
  3. Apply escrow to an excess, then identify the residual collection. Verify final LP plus GP amounts equal gross proceeds.
Worked case

One early winner and one late loss

The LP supplies €80m at inception for two €40m deals. Deal A sells for €90m, and later deal B sells for €10m. Carry is 20%. A deal-by-deal structure escrows 30% of carry paid. Ignore the time value of money in the final reconciliation.

Working

  1. Under deal-by-deal rules A makes €50m. Allocate €10m carry and €80m to the LP. Escrow €3m of that carry, leaving €7m initially available to the GP. B returns its €10m to the LP.
  2. Gross fund proceeds are €100m and net profit is €20m. Final carry is €4m. The clawback is 10 − 4 = €6m, funded with €3m escrow plus €3m from the GP.
  3. Final distributions are €96m for the LP and €4m for the GP. They reconcile to €100m. The LP’s gross cash received just after sale A was €80m, a DPI of 1.00.
  4. Using the whole-of-fund rule, sale A first returns €80m capital. Its remaining €10m gives €2m carry and €8m more to the LP. Sale B gives €2m additional carry and €8m to the LP. Final totals are again €96m and €4m.

Decision and interpretation

The whole-of-fund LP receives €88m at the early exit, giving DPI of 1.10. It also avoids the €3m unsecured collection arising after the escrow in this deal-by-deal example.

Quick check
A fund ends with $5m of carry entitlement after allocating $9m earlier. A $2m escrow is available. How much remains to collect from the GP after escrow?

The overpayment is $4m. Applying $2m escrow leaves $2m to collect.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A fund invests $30m in each of two deals. The first returns $70m and the second zero. The contract pays 20% deal carry initially and requires a final fund-level 20% profit share. One-quarter of early carry sits in escrow. Calculate the true-up.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The first exit has $40m deal profit and pays $8m provisional carry. The fund as a whole has $70m proceeds less $60m cost = $10m profit.
  2. Final carry should be $2m. Excess paid is $6m.
  3. Escrow contains $2m. A further $4m must be collected. Assuming full repayment the LP ends with $68m and the GP with $2m.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a private-equity manager owe investors cash after a fund has already made money?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A deal-by-deal contract may pay the manager carry after an early winner. If later investments lose money, aggregate profits support less carry than was already paid. A clawback reconciles the overpayment. Escrow and guarantees determine how readily investors collect it.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the GP can pay only $1m beyond the escrow, how much does the LP actually receive?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The LP receives $62m on the first exit, plus $2m escrow and $1m from the GP, for $65m in total. It has a remaining $3m claim against the recipient under the assumed contractual obligation.
