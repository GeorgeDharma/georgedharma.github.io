Professional workshop · C04

Set a disciplined bid for a minority stake in a regional bank

A pension investment subsidiary is considering a secondary purchase of 25% of Rivière Commercial Bank on 31 December 2027. The seller asks €280m. The investment director wants a standalone valuation that ties retained capital to distributable cash, a second independent valuation of the same equity claim, and a ceiling that meets both internal investment rules. The seller receives the purchase proceeds. The target receives no new capital. Do not assume control synergies, a liquidation or an additional minority discount beyond the case rules.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Financial institutions group valuation analyst supporting an investment director
What to submit
A financial institutions valuation committee paper giving a price ceiling payable to the seller, a bank equity roll-forward, independent dividend and residual-income cross-checks, a capital floor test, and an exit position if negotiations fail.

Case inputs

Illustrative case prepared for this workshop.

From inputs to decision

01

Set up the claim and market hurdle

A bank's deposits help it produce interest earnings and are already reflected in profit and capital. Subtracting the entire deposit base in an industrial enterprise bridge would value a different claim. The investor is buying a pro-rata share of existing common equity.

  1. Use the equity discount rate under CAPM. The sum of 3% and 1.2 times the 5% market premium is 9% per year. Apply this euro nominal return to common dividends and residual income on the same dates.
  2. Set the starting book at 1,000 and the purchased fraction at 25%. Keep the €3m buyer-only fee outside bank earnings. It influences the buyer's maximum seller price, while the bank's own value remains a standalone equity estimate.

Carry a 9% equity hurdle into both valuation methods and separately track seller consideration and buyer all-in outlay.

02

Roll the three-year bank forecast

Each year's earnings depend on opening common capital. Freezing the original equity base would understate the earnings created by reinvesting retained profit, whereas treating all earnings as dividends would ignore capital needed for lending.

  1. In 2028 income is 1,000 × 12% = 120. Dividend is 48 and closing book 1,072. In 2029 income is 1,072 × 11% = 117.92, dividend 47.168 and closing book 1,142.752.
  2. In 2030 income is 1,142.752 × 10% = 114.2752. Dividend is 45.71008 and closing book is 1,211.31712. Verify each close equals opening book plus earnings less the dividend.

These three dividend forecasts are the explicit common cash payments. The same opening equity sequence will support a separate equity-charge calculation.

03

Check that dividends can actually be made

A theoretical dividend discounted at 9% is useful only when the retained balance satisfies the stated capital policy. Test the ratios under both the planned payout and the case's stress conditions.

  1. Divide base closing books of 1,072, 1,142.752 and 1,211.31712 by year-end RWA of 9,000, 9,500 and 10,000. Ratios are 11.911%, 12.029% and 12.113%, all above 11.5%.
  2. In stress, first earnings fall from 120 to 85. Dividend falls to 34 and closing book to 1,051, a ratio of 11.678%. Keeping the same payout rule gives closing capital of 1,120.366 and 1,187.58796 later, ratios 11.793% and 11.876%.

The assumed cash payments pass the case's annual closing-capital test. The smallest cushion appears in stress 2028 at €16m of capital above the €1,035m floor.

04

Value the continuing bank from dividend capacity

The 40% explicit payout is not a permanent assumption. In steady growth a bank can distribute earnings remaining after retaining enough capital for the 3% increase in book. A stable annual ROE of 10% on the 2030 ending book generates earnings well above that growth requirement.

  1. Expected 2031 dividend is (0.10 − 0.03) × 1,211.31712 = 84.7921984. Capitalize at 0.09 − 0.03 to get end-2030 equity value of €1,413.2033m.
  2. Present value is 48/1.09 + 47.168/1.09² + (45.71008 + 1,413.2033)/1.09³ = €1,210.2859m. The 25% stake is worth €302.5715m before deal costs.

The terminal payout rises to 70% of earnings because book growth of 3% uses 30% of earnings at a 10% ROE. The terminal capital-to-RWA ratio remains constant by assumption.

05

Reproduce the value with residual income

The director asks for a cross-check that does not accidentally pay shareholders twice for retained capital. Under clean surplus, the value of operating earnings above the required charge on opening equity can be added to current book.

  1. Explicit residual income is 120 − 0.09 × 1,000 = 30 in 2028, 117.92 − 0.09 × 1,072 = 21.44 in 2029 and 114.2752 − 0.09 × 1,142.752 = 11.42752 in 2030. Its total present value is €54.3927m.
  2. From 2031 onward, residual income begins at (0.10 − 0.09) × 1,211.31712 = 12.1131712 and grows at 3%. Its continuing value at the end of 2030 is 201.88619. Add opening book 1,000, explicit residual-income PV 54.3927 and 201.88619/1.09³ to recover €1,210.2859m.

The two methods reconcile. The residual terminal figure of 201.886 is the premium over ending book, not the €1,413.203 total continuing bank value.

06

Reprice the credit and long-run competition stress

A near-term loss and a long-run reduction in franchise returns have different effects. The former lowers the near-term income and future book base. The latter removes excess returns from the continuing franchise even if the bank remains profitable.

  1. The stress dividend sequence is 34, 46.244 and 44.81464. With final ROE equal to the 9% equity cost, terminal value equals 2030 closing book of €1,187.58796m. Discount the three dividends plus terminal book at 9%.
  2. Stressed total common equity is €1,021.75624m, or €255.43906m for a quarter stake. As a cross-check, stress residual incomes are −5, +21.02 and +11.20366 with zero terminal excess value. Their PV plus 1,000 gives the same result.

The drop from base stake value of 302.571 to 255.439 is economically important. Market median P/B would miss the stress erosion of excess returns.

07

Compare the quoted deal and set a walk-away price

The price discussed with the seller must exclude the buyer's fee, while both committee rules apply to all-in outlay. A modest discount to the optimistic model alone cannot satisfy the mandate.

  1. The €280m ask plus €3m fee costs €283m. The 90%-of-base rule permits 0.90 × 302.57147 = 272.31432 all-in, equivalent to €269.31432m payable to the seller. The stress rule permits €255.43906m all-in or €252.43906m payable.
  2. The lower ceiling is €252.43906m. Offer up to €252m in whole millions, with fees raising the outlay to €255m. Implied whole-bank seller equity at €252m for 25% is €1,008m or 1.008× opening common book. The seller's €280m implies 1.12×. The apparent peer-range fit at the ask does not override either policy.

Decline the current request and authorize a whole-million bid of €252m subject to the specified capital, provisioning and stake-rights review. Reopen the case if new evidence changes sustainable ROE or capital eligibility.

Completed document

Valuation committee recommendation · Rivière Commercial Bank

Position

Decline the seller's €280m request. Authorize an indicative €252m seller bid for 25% subject to diligence, with an absolute model ceiling of €252.439m and buyer fees capped at €3m.

Valuation evidence

Two common-equity methods reconcile to €1,210.286m base equity, or €302.571m attributable to the stake. Our 1.210× base multiple is close to the supplied 1.20× peer median, but the range from 1.05× to 1.30× is only a market cross-check because normalized ROE and capital needs vary.

Downside and price controls

The €35m loss and 9% terminal ROE reduce stake value to €255.439m. The lower of the base-margin limit and stressed-value limit determines the walk-away figure. At €252m plus fees, all-in spending is €255m, €0.439m below the binding stress limit.

Diligence before signature

Verify loan-loss staging, normalized net interest income, which amounts qualify as capital, risk-weight assumptions and minority dividend or transfer rights. The narrow stress headroom makes an adverse adjustment to capital eligibility or expected distributions material to the bid.

Execution

Communicate a seller-price ceiling separately from buyer-paid costs and request no change to the stake's economics. Return to committee for any revised fee, loss estimate, required capital buffer or contractual distribution restriction that would change the stated policy calculations.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

What is the maximum extra after-tax credit loss in 2028 before the 11.5% year-end capital floor is just reached, if dividend payout remains 40% of still-positive earnings?

See the full solution

Required capital is 9,000 × 11.5% = 1,035. With extra loss L, closing equity is 1,000 + 0.60 × (120 − L) = 1,072 − 0.60L. Solve 1,072 − 0.60L = 1,035, giving €61.6667m. This is the total incremental loss relative to base, not the extra amount beyond the €35m stress.

Task 2

Buyer fees rise from €3m to €7m and all valuations stay unchanged. What is the exact new maximum seller price under both limits?

See the full solution

The binding all-in limit remains the lower of €272.31432m and €255.43906m. Deduct €7m of buyer expense. Maximum seller consideration is €248.43906m, or €248m if quoting only whole millions.

Task 3

With base explicit earnings intact, what is whole-bank value if terminal ROE falls to the 9% cost of equity while growth remains 3%?

See the full solution

Future residual income after 2030 is zero because ROE equals the required return. Add opening common book 1,000 and explicit residual-income PV of 54.3927. Whole-bank value is €1,054.3927m. The lost €155.8932m versus base is the discounted continuing premium.

Task 4

Would you subtract deposits and all outstanding bank borrowings from this equity estimate before calculating the 25% stake?
