Value, price and claims

Map who owns each claim. The business operations and the common shareholders are different valuation objects.

The starting point

Suppose someone asks what a company is worth. First ask what the buyer receives. The operating business can support lenders, preferred investors and common shareholders. One ordinary share is a claim on only part of the value left after senior claims are satisfied.

A price is an observed transaction or quotation. A valuation is an estimate based on future benefits, their risk and timing. Comparing price with estimated value is meaningful only when they refer to the same claim, currency and date.

Theory and method

Law of one price and valuation by replication

Contingent claims · State-contingent payoff · Intrinsic value

Two analyst models have the same expected payoff. Can they still describe securities with different values?

A bond and a share might both average €100 across a boom and a recession. If one pays most of its cash in a recession, when money is scarce, investors can value its protection. Replication requires matching payoffs in each relevant state, on the same dates and to the same legal owner. Equal averages alone do not meet that condition. Tracing the state-by-state payment can reveal what a proposed comparable or discount rate is actually pricing.

How the result is built

V = Σₛ qₛ Xₛ when state prices are available

Xₛ is the future cash delivered in state s. qₛ is today's price of a claim to one euro delivered in that state only. The sum is over mutually exclusive, exhaustive states. V is value today.

  1. Use traded claims to establish state prices, when possible. If a euro delivered only in a boom costs 0.35 and one in recession costs 0.55, a certain future euro costs 0.90.
  2. For a claim paying 120 in boom and 80 in recession, value is 120 × 0.35 + 80 × 0.55 = 86.
  3. Interchanging the two state payments gives 80 × 0.35 + 120 × 0.55 = 94. With equal physical state probabilities both expected payoffs are 100, but recession protection commands more.

When the reasoning holds

  • The simple pricing formula requires attainable state prices consistent with no arbitrage. Incomplete markets may leave some claims without a unique replication price.
  • State prices combine discounting and risk valuation. They generally differ from ordinary probabilities and need not sum to one.

Terms in this lesson
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Enterprise value
Value of the business operations attributable to its capital providers. A bridge adjusts it to the amount due to shareholders.
First example

An operating business is worth €150m, with €12m of excess cash, €50m of debt and no other claims. The common shareholders’ claim is 150 + 12 − 50 = €112m. With 8m shares, that is €14 per share. The €150m figure describes the operations.

Name the owner of every cash flow

An acquisition team may ask for the value of a factory business while an investor asks for the value of an ordinary share. The factory generates operating cash available to several claimants. A common shareholder receives what is left after senior contractual obligations and any other equity preferences.

Draw a claims map before forecasting. Show which businesses are included, who owns each, what debt or preferred shares exist and whether cash on the balance sheet is essential to operations or surplus. A company with substantial cash or several classes of shares makes this first step particularly important.

Distinguish operating, equity and per-share values

An operating valuation discounts cash from the business after operating reinvestment but before payments to lenders. A common equity valuation discounts cash that can be paid to common shareholders after financing commitments. An equity bridge translates between them when all included assets and claims are treated consistently.

The word enterprise value is used with different practical conventions around leases, pensions, cash and minority interests. State the calculation used in the exercise. Per-share value additionally requires a share count appropriate to the claim, with any potential dilution handled consistently rather than casually assuming every security is ordinary equity.

Compare price with an estimate on the same basis

Market capitalization is the observed price per share multiplied by the appropriate outstanding shares. An analyst's intrinsic value is a conditional estimate based on expected cash, growth, risk and dates. A gap between the two invites examination of assumptions and says little until the measurement bases agree.

A control buyer may expect benefits unavailable to a passive shareholder. A minority quoted price can therefore differ from an acquisition valuation that includes buyer-specific improvements. Identify whether a scenario values the standalone operations, the combination or one group's share of any gains.

Method

Frame a valuation brief

  1. Define the object

    State ownership percentage, included operations and date. Label any value as a business, all equity, common equity or one share.

  2. Bridge claims

    Match financing, surplus assets and other owners to the operating cash included. Use market or fair claims values where the case supplies them.

  3. Compare

    Convert both the estimate and market price to the same claimant and date. Explain one business assumption the price difference might reflect.

Worked case

A manufacturer with preferred equity

Operating assets are estimated at 210. Surplus cash of 18 was excluded from those operations. Market debt is 70 and preferred stock has a claim worth 14. There are 12 ordinary shares and no other claims. A share trades at 11.

Working

  1. Adding surplus cash gives 228 of value available to financing claimants. Deducting 70 debt leaves 158 for all equity investors.
  2. Deduct the preferred claim of 14 to obtain common equity of 144. Estimated common value per share is 144/12 = 12.
  3. Observed common market capitalization is 11 × 12 = 132. The comparable gap is 12 in total or 1 per common share. Comparing 132 with 210 would mix a market equity value with operating assets.

Interpretation

The price may reflect different cash forecasts, risk assumptions or overlooked claims. Separating these possibilities is more informative than calling a stock cheap by inspection.

A common mistake

Comparing the quoted share price with operating value divided by shares before deducting debt.

How to correct it

Identify who has first claim on the operating assets. Only the residual value attributable to ordinary shareholders is comparable with their share price.

If the market quotes €13 per share, what is the corresponding market value of the common shares?

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A group has operations worth 500, surplus investments of 25, debt worth 180, preferred shares worth 20 and 25 common shares. Common shares trade at 12. The operations exclude the surplus investments.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find common equity and per-share estimated value.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the operating value implied by the observed common price under the given bridge.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Why might a strategic acquirer use a different operating value?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Common equity is 500 + 25 − 180 − 20 = 325. Per common share this is 325/25 = 13.

Part B 3 marks

Market common equity is 25 × 12 = 300. Implied operating value is 300 + 180 + 20 − 25 = 475.

Part C 2 marks

Incremental benefits available only after combination, integration costs or a different operating plan can change the cash flows. Identify and value the incremental effects before attributing them to standalone owners.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two analyst models have the same expected payoff. Can they still describe securities with different values?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. Expected cash averages over the circumstances in which it arrives. A euro paid in a severe recession can be more valuable than one paid in a boom. Here the two risky claims both average €100, but the defensive payoff is worth €94 and the cyclical one €86. I would require matching claimant, timing and state exposure before using one security as a replicating comparable.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What trade would you examine if an otherwise identical duplicate of the defensive claim sold for €91?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Buy the €91 claim and sell the fully replicating €94 package, if short selling and settlement are feasible. Future state payoffs cancel and the initial difference is €3 before transaction, borrowing and collateral costs.
