Appraise a rent-producing property through lease-up and stabilization

Construct net operating income from rents, occupancy and operating costs on a stated convention.

  3. Keep the exit and financing separate

Theory and terminology

Direct capitalization and discounted property cash flows

lease-up · stabilized income · capitalization rate

What you should be able to explain

  • Construct net operating income from rents, occupancy and operating costs on a stated convention.
  • Discount transitional property cash and a future sale, and stress the exit capitalization rate.

Work from the lease schedule

Start with the annual contractual or market rent at full occupancy. Allow for vacancy and collections, add eligible property income, and deduct recurring operating expenses such as maintenance, insurance and property taxes under the case convention. The result is net operating income, or NOI, before debt service and income tax.

Tenant improvements, leasing commissions and major capital spending can absorb cash without being recurring NOI expenses. State the convention in the model. Sale comparables and cap rates must use the same convention, particularly where replacement reserves are treated differently.

Stabilized income and direct capitalization

A capitalization rate, or cap rate, relates an observed property value to a representative annual NOI. Dividing forward stabilized NOI by an appropriate market cap rate can support a stabilized property value. It assumes the income and cap rate represent comparable risk, growth, upkeep and occupancy.

If current vacancy is unusually high, capitalizing today's NOI treats a temporary shortfall as persistent. Applying stabilized NOI immediately can also overstate as-is value by ignoring lost rent, waiting and lease-up expenditure. Explicitly model the transition and then capitalize the income following the exit date.

Keep the exit and financing separate

For an end-of-year-two exit, use expected year-three NOI as the sale numerator if the cap rate is a forward rate. Deduct selling costs and discount the sale and final holding-year cash from year two. Count each rent year once.

The cap rate and discount rate play different roles. A cap rate converts an annual stabilized income into an asset price. A discount rate prices dated, potentially varying cash flows. Valuing the property before debt allows an independent check of loan balances, debt service and equity terms afterward.

Method

Build the result

V₀ = Σₜ (NOIₜ − Kₜ)/(1+r)ᵗ + [NOIₙ₊₁/c × (1−s)]/(1+r)ⁿ

V₀ is as-is unlevered property value today, t each holding year through n, NOI annual property net operating income, K tenant improvements and capital costs excluded from NOI, r discount rate, c forward exit cap rate and s selling cost as a share of gross price.

  1. Forecast annual occupied rent and operating expenses. Compute cash after costs excluded from the chosen NOI convention.
  2. Capitalize the first stabilized income following sale at the market exit cap rate and deduct selling costs.
  3. Discount each year's operating cash and the net sale proceeds from their actual dates. Stress a higher exit cap and delayed occupancy.
Worked case

A two-year lease-up

Potential annual rent is €2.4m. Occupancy is 70% in year one and 90% in year two, ancillary income is €0.12m each year and operating expenses are €0.80m and €0.78m. Tenant improvements or capex are €0.60m and €0.20m. Expected stabilized year-three NOI is €1.80m, exit cap 6%, selling costs 2% and discount rate 8%. All cash is at year end.

Working

  1. Year-one NOI is 2.4 × 0.70 + 0.12 − 0.80 = €1.00m. After €0.60m improvements, year-one cash is €0.40m.
  2. Year-two NOI is 2.4 × 0.90 + 0.12 − 0.78 = €1.50m. After €0.20m capex, operating cash is €1.30m.
  3. End-year-two gross exit value is 1.80/0.06 = €30m and net sale proceeds are €29.4m.
  4. As-is value is 0.40/1.08 + (1.30 + 29.4)/1.08² = €26.69m. At a 7% exit cap net proceeds fall to €25.20m and as-is value to €23.09m.

Decision and interpretation

Use the explicit lease-up DCF on these assumptions. Obtain evidence for new tenants' credit, the timing of completion and comparable exit pricing before relying on the result.

Quick check
A property is sold at the end of year two on a forward capitalization basis. Which income belongs in the exit numerator?

A forward exit cap at the end of year two capitalizes the buyer's first subsequent property NOI. Holding-year cash and debt financing are handled separately.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

An investor will hold a property one year. It generates €0.95m NOI and requires €0.15m leasing spend. Next year's stabilized NOI is €1.20m. The forward exit cap is 6%, selling costs 3% and discount rate 10%. Find today's unlevered property value.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The holding-year cash is €0.95m − €0.15m = €0.80m.
  2. Gross sale at year end is €1.20m/0.06 = €20m and net sale is €19.4m.
  3. Today's value is (€0.80m + €19.40m)/1.10 = €18.36m. The use of a 6% exit cap presumes comparable stabilized property risk and income.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

An office building is partly empty today but has signed leases starting next year. Should its current rent be divided by a market capitalization rate?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would model the signed leasing schedule, vacancy and cash required to get the space ready. Once the property reaches a representative occupancy, I can capitalize the next year's NOI using a cap rate consistent with comparable buildings. That stabilized value is a future amount. I would deduct selling costs, discount it alongside cash during lease-up, and test a slower fill rate or higher exit cap.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A tenant default delays stabilization by another year. Can the model retain the original year-one exit calculation?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Only if the buyer demonstrably pays a value consistent with the delayed lease-up. Otherwise forecast the extra vacancy, incentives and timing, extend the explicit period or lower the expected sale price to reflect the unfinished transition.
