Build and interpret comparable acquisition multiples

Convert the amounts paid to sellers into a comparable whole-business value for each acquisition.

In this lecture

  1. Recover the price of the claim purchased
  2. Align earnings and liabilities
  3. Whose gains are embedded in the price?
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. A common lease basis changes both numerator and denominator
  6. Two acquisitions of industrial distributors
  7. Independent exam problem
  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Acquisition multiples and comparable transactions

transaction comparables · enterprise value · normalized EBITDA

What you should be able to explain

  • Convert the amounts paid to sellers into a comparable whole-business value for each acquisition.
  • Normalize acquisition multiples, identify embedded buyer benefits and explain a range for a new target.

Recover the price of the claim purchased

A press release may quote cash paid for shares, total consideration including a deferred amount, or a precomputed enterprise value. Read the transaction definition before adding debt. Use the stipulated or defensible fair value of contingent consideration, not automatically its maximum. Value stock consideration consistently at the date used for the deal comparison.

A partial purchase is not automatically a whole-company price. The remainder may have different voting rights, liquidity or future purchase terms. Unless the facts support a pro-rata gross-up, seek a disclosed full enterprise basis or exclude that transaction from the main set.

Align earnings and liabilities

Choose a denominator, such as last-twelve-months EBITDA immediately prior to announcement, for each deal. Normalize the treatment of leases and nonrecurring costs on both sides. Putting lease liabilities into one enterprise value while comparing it with another whose EBITDA already deducts rent can produce a false premium.

An acquisition occurs in a particular financing and strategic environment. Consider date, stake acquired, geography, control, distress, growth, transaction size and competition. Preserve the raw amounts and the transformation so a second analyst can reproduce the comparison.

Whose gains are embedded in the price?

An observed acquisition reflects negotiation. The seller may capture some anticipated synergies or control improvements and leave the rest to the buyer. Public peers can provide a separate landmark for standalone claims. Applying a precedent control premium to a control-deal multiple can count the same improvement twice.

If evidence identifies part of the selling price attributable to bidder-only benefits, present a separate sensitivity with that allocation. A management estimate of total synergy alone does not establish the portion paid to the seller.

Method

Build the result

Deal EV = Peq + FVearn + D − C ; m = Deal EV / comparable EBITDA

Peq is the price paid for all ordinary shares excluding contingent consideration already displayed separately, FVearn its contingent consideration at fair value, D the comparable debt-like liabilities assumed and C acquired surplus cash. m is a multiple in times. The simplified example excludes preferred and minority claims.

  1. Verify that the starting price is equity consideration and state whether contingent claims are already included.
  2. Bridge to enterprise value on one lease and cash convention. Normalize historical earnings on that same basis.
  3. Compute deal multiples, compare their quality and apply an appropriate range to target earnings. Bridge target enterprise to common equity using the target's own claims.
Worked case

Two acquisitions of industrial distributors

Use a convention where operating leases are excluded from debt and rent reduces EBITDA. Deal A pays €108m cash for all shares plus €6m fair-value earnout, assumes €20m debt and acquires €8m surplus cash. EBITDA after operating rent is €14m. Deal B's stock consideration is €150m, debt €36m including €12m operating leases and cash €6m. Its €20m EBITDA is before €2m operating rent. The target's comparable EBITDA is €12m, debt €25m and cash €5m.

Working

  1. Deal A enterprise value is 108 + 6 + 20 − 8 = €126m. Dividing by €14m gives 9.00×.
  2. For B use debt of 36 − 12 = €24m. Enterprise value is 150 + 24 − 6 = €168m. Comparable EBITDA is 20 − 2 = €18m.
  3. B is 168/18 = 9.333×. A simple midpoint under the supplied comparable-deal assumption is 9.1667×.
  4. The target EV range is €108m to €112m, midpoint €110m. Subtract its €20m net debt for a common equity range of €88m to €92m and midpoint €90m.

Decision and interpretation

Quote the range with the deal screens and exclusions. If one precedent bundled unusually large buyer-specific benefits, show its separate impact rather than adding another assumed premium.

Quick check
A precedent's EBITDA is before operating rent, and its debt includes the associated lease liability. What should you do when comparing it on a rent-expensed basis?

Both sides must use one convention. Excluding operating leases from debt goes with earnings after operating rent.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

An acquirer buys 100% for €90m ordinary equity plus a separate contingent payment worth €4m today. Assumed debt is €18m and surplus cash €4m. Comparable pre-deal EBITDA is €12m. A candidate target has €10m comparable EBITDA and €15m net debt.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Enterprise value for the precedent is 90 + 4 + 18 − 4 = €108m. The comparable multiple is 108/12 = 9×.
  2. Applying 9× implies €90m target EV and €75m target common equity. This is an estimate based on a single precedent.
  3. Do not add an automatic control premium. The observed purchase already conveys control. Screen the buyer's benefits, market conditions and comparable accounting before deciding how much weight to give the figure.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two acquisitions each appear to cost nine times earnings. What must you check before using either to price a third business?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would reconstruct what was paid for the shares, identify contingent consideration and add assumed debt only if the quoted figure excludes it. Then I would put debt, rent, EBITDA and cash on consistent terms across the precedents. Finally I would ask who captured any synergies and why the buyer paid that price. A historical control transaction can already include a premium, so I would not automatically add another.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if the contingent payment is already included in the published €90m equity figure?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Its fair value must enter once. Revised enterprise value is 90 + 18 − 4 = €104m and its multiple becomes 8.667×. Target common equity becomes around €71.67m before other judgment adjustments.
