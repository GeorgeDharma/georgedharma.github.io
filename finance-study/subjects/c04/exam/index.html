Case 1 · 10 marks

Value a growing operating business

Part A 3 marks

Calculate year-1 operating free cash and year-2 sustainable free cash.

After-tax year-1 operating income is 45. FCFF is 45 + 6 − 11 − 4 = 36. The steady reinvestment fraction is 3%/12% = 25%, so year-2 FCFF is 48 × 0.75 = 36.

Part B 4 marks

Value the operations, identifying the date of terminal value.

At the end of year 1 the terminal value is 36/(0.09 − 0.03) = 600. Time-zero operations are (36 + 600)/1.09 = 583.49. Year-1 cash and the terminal claim share the year-1 discount factor.

Part C 3 marks

Bridge to common equity and state how a lower incremental return affects the result with growth unchanged.

Equity is 583.49 + 10 − 90 = 503.49, or 50.35 per share. A lower return on new capital requires more reinvestment for the same growth, reducing steady free cash and terminal value if other inputs hold.

Case 2 · 10 marks

Peer estimates and observed claims

Part A 4 marks

Determine the normalized operating and common values under the midpoint peer assumption.

Normalized EBIT is 24, giving operating value 24 × 9 = 216. Parent common equity is 216 + 8 − 70 − 12 = 142. Estimated price per share is 142/12 = 11.83.

Part B 3 marks

Infer the operating value and normalized operating multiple embedded in the observed share price.

Observed equity is 120. Reversing the bridge gives 120 − 8 + 70 + 12 = 194. The observed operating multiple is 194/24 = 8.08×.

Part C 3 marks

Suppose closures recur and applying an add-back is inappropriate. Revalue and interpret.

Use forecast EBIT of 22. Midpoint operations become 198. Parent equity becomes 198 + 8 − 70 − 12 = 124, or 10.33 per share. The smaller valuation gap shows why repeated charges and peer accounting must be examined.

Case 3 · 10 marks

Bank earnings, capital and implied return

Part A 4 marks

Calculate year-1 earnings, retention and dividend capacity, then estimate value.

Earnings equal 250 × 12% = 30. Retention required is 250 × 4% = 10. Dividend capacity is 20. Model equity is 20/(0.09 − 0.04) = 400, or 1.6× book.

Part B 3 marks

Show the same value from growing residual income.

Opening equity charge is 250 × 9% = 22.5. Year-1 residual income is 30 − 22.5 = 7.5. With stable ROE and 4% book growth residual income grows 4%. Value is 250 + 7.5/(0.09 − 0.04) = 400.

Part C 3 marks

Infer the ROE that would justify the observed price with the same growth and required return.

Observed P/B is 350/250 = 1.4. Solve 1.4 = (ROE − 0.04)/(0.09 − 0.04), giving ROE = 0.04 + 1.4 × 0.05 = 11%. Compare this with normalized credit losses and returns on retained capital.

Case 4 · 10 marks

A reverse valuation in nominal cash

Part A 4 marks

Find the nominal discount rate and implied nominal growth supporting the observed value in the first model.

The consistent nominal rate is 1.05 × 1.02 − 1 = 7.1%. Inverting the perpetuity gives g = 0.071 − 9/180 = 2.1%. Substituting 9/(0.071 − 0.021) returns 180.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate the year-1 nominal cash required if growth is 1%, and express it in today's purchasing power.

Required nominal cash is 180 × (0.071 − 0.01) = 10.98. Real year-1 cash is 10.98/1.02 = 10.76 in today's purchasing power.

Part C 3 marks

An analyst asserts the price proves long-run growth is 2.1%. Evaluate that conclusion.

That growth is implied only by year-1 cash of 9 and the assumed rate and perpetuity. The alternative 10.98 cash/1% growth also produces 180. Evidence on reinvestment, mature margins and sustainable cash would help judge the alternatives.
