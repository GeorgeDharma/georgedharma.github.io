Bond and liability matching

Match near-term payments with cash or suitable bonds and study duration at longer horizons. Stress cash flows that change with rates or inflation.

The starting point

A pension scheme expecting a fixed payment can arrange an eligible bond that repays on the same date in the same currency. Cash-flow matching connects specific receipts to specific obligations, subject to credit quality and the reliability of promised cash flows.

For a longer series of uncertain payments, duration estimates how much present value changes with a small parallel move in yields. Match the money sensitivity of assets and liabilities. Equal durations alone do not give equal money sensitivities when their present values differ.

Theory and method

Redington immunization

Present-value, duration and convexity matching

An insurer owes 100 in two years. How can two zero-coupon assets around the liability maturity protect today’s surplus against small parallel yield changes?

An insurer can put half its money into a one-year payment and half into a three-year payment around a fixed two-year liability. With a flat yield of zero, today’s values match and the average cash-flow dates line up. Small yield changes then affect both sides by the same amount to first order. The more dispersed asset payments have greater curvature, or convexity, so the asset value bends upward more strongly than the liability value as yields move away from the starting point.

How the result is built

S(y₀) = 0, S′(y₀) = 0, S″(y₀) > 0

S is the present value of assets less liabilities in currency units at the annual effective flat yield y. y₀ is the starting yield. The first derivative S′ measures slope per unit of yield and the second S″ measures curvature per squared unit of yield.

  1. At a starting yield of zero, assets paying 50 in year one and 50 in year three have value 100. The liability paying 100 in year two also has value 100.
  2. At zero yield, the slopes are −(50 × 1 + 50 × 3) and −100 × 2. Both equal −200, so the initial change in surplus per small yield change is zero.
  3. Asset curvature is 50 × 1 × 2 + 50 × 3 × 4 = 700. Liability curvature is 100 × 2 × 3 = 600. In this example the exact surplus simplifies to 50y²/(1 + y)³.

When the reasoning holds

  • The result compares a single flat annual effective yield applied to every date. A twist that changes one-, two- and three-year rates by different amounts requires separate maturity exposures.
  • Cash flows are fixed and creditworthy. A callable bond can change its payment date after a rate move and a year-two claim may require an asset sale before the year-three bond matures.
Terms in this lesson
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Duration
A measure used to estimate how strongly a bond price responds to a small interest-rate change.
How to read the notation
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

Assets of €80m with modified duration five fall about €4m when yields rise one percentage point. Liabilities worth €100m with duration four also fall about €4m. The surplus is approximately protected against that small parallel shock, though curve shape and inflation can still matter.

Cash-flow matching versus immunization

A cash-flow matched bond portfolio has scheduled receipts that cover promised payments at the dates and in the currencies required. A zero-coupon bond that pays a fixed amount on the exact liability date avoids reinvestment of intervening coupons, provided the issuer pays.

An immunized portfolio aims to reduce sensitivity of its surplus to rate changes. Modified duration approximates the percentage drop in present value for a small yield rise of one unit in decimal terms. Matching money duration of assets and liabilities offsets their first-order parallel rate effects.

Why value and duration both matter

Money duration equals present value times modified duration. If assets are worth less than liabilities, equal durations leave a larger euro sensitivity on the liability side. A surplus hedge solves asset value times asset duration equal to liability value times liability duration for the relevant curve.

A combination of short and long bonds can match a chosen duration using market-value weights. This does not guarantee identical cash flows across dates. A curve twist can affect the instruments differently even when their sum of parallel sensitivities matches.

Convexity, inflation and horizon

Bond values respond nonlinearly to large yield movements. Convexity describes the curvature omitted by the linear duration approximation. Different asset and liability convexity can make a first-order hedge imperfect even under a parallel move.

Liability cash amounts may also change with wages, inflation, exercise options or life expectancy. A nominal bond cannot guarantee an inflation-linked cash payment. Revalue and rebalance as time passes, because remaining durations change and contributions or payments alter balance-sheet sizes.

Method

Constructing a surplus hedge

  1. Specify the claim

    Establish liability cash amounts, indexation, currency, present value and appropriate yield curve.

  2. Match first-order exposure

    Calculate the liability's money duration and choose a feasible asset duration at its actual present value.

  3. Stress imperfect matches

    Test nonparallel, inflation and larger rate moves, and ensure cash remains available on actual payment dates.

Worked case

A pension book uses two bond funds

A pension scheme holds €90m assets against €100m liabilities with modified duration 6. Its available bond funds have durations 2 and 8. Assume the liability and both funds initially respond to the same parallel shift.

Working

  1. Liability money duration is 100 × 6 = 600 in €m-years. To match it with €90m assets, required asset duration is 600/90 = 6.6667.
  2. If w is the share in the duration-eight fund, then 2(1 − w) + 8w = 6.6667, so w ≈ 77.78%. Allocate €70m long and €20m short.
  3. The two asset exposures total 70 × 8 + 20 × 2 = 600. A 0.5-point rate rise reduces both assets and liabilities by about €3m, leaving the initial €10m deficit approximately unchanged.

Interpretation

The hedge stabilizes the deficit's sensitivity under this shock. It does not generate the €10m needed to close the deficit or match each pension payment.

A common mistake

Matching duration percentages while ignoring the asset shortfall can leave large money exposure.

How to correct it

Compare money changes under a one-basis-point shift, then verify the liability calendar and separate key-rate exposures.

Quick check
Assets are €100m with modified duration three. Liabilities are €75m with duration four. What is the approximate change in surplus for a parallel 1-percentage-point yield rise?

Assets fall by about €3m and liabilities fall by about €3m. Surplus equals assets minus liabilities, so these first-order changes offset even though the two durations differ.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A scheme owns €80m assets and owes liabilities valued at €100m with duration 4. Available bond funds have durations 2 and 7. Use the same parallel curve and ignore convexity.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find required asset duration and the allocation that matches liability sensitivity.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    For a 0.5-point yield fall, estimate the change in assets, liabilities and surplus.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Required asset duration is 100 × 4/80 = 5. Solve 2(1 − w) + 7w = 5 to find w = 60% long, or €48m long and €32m short.

Part B 3 marks

Both money durations are 400 in €m-years. A 0.005 yield fall raises each present value about €2m. Surplus is approximately unchanged at first order.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

An insurer owes 100 in two years. How can two zero-coupon assets around the liability maturity protect today’s surplus against small parallel yield changes?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would start by matching present values and first derivatives. Both sides are worth 100 and initially lose about two currency units for a one-percentage-point rise. The assets have greater convexity, producing a local minimum in surplus at the matched yield. This is a valuation result. I would still show how the year-two payment will be made, including the terms on which the three-year asset could be sold.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Only the two-year spot rate falls to −1%. The one- and three-year rates stay at zero. Does the cushion survive?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. The assets still value at 100, while the liability rises to 100/0.99² = 102.03. The resulting deficit is about 2.03. The example changes one point on the curve and violates the common-shift assumption. A two-year matching cash flow or key-rate hedges would address that particular exposure.
