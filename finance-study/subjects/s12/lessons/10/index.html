Stewardship and client reporting

Explain voting, mandates, fees and conflicts. A client report should connect the sources of return with the ability to meet the investor’s future obligations.

The starting point

Investment managers often vote shares on behalf of their clients. Stewardship means overseeing the assets entrusted to them, including engagement and voting where the mandate permits. A conflict can arise when the issuer seeking a favourable vote also buys services from the manager.

A client report should reconcile beginning assets, cash flows, investment gains and fees to the closing balance. Explain material risks, voting decisions and how much of the client's future obligation is funded. Percentage performance alone can conceal an approaching cash shortfall.

Theory and method

Funded-status reconciliation

Pension surplus bridge · Funded ratio

Can a pension manager report a positive net investment return while trustees discover the funding deficit has widened, and which cash movements explain the difference?

A pension trustee signs a report covering assets today and the present value of the benefits already promised. Investment gains help the asset side. Contributions also help, but come from the sponsor. An extra year of service and revised assumptions can raise promised benefits independently of investment performance. Benefit payments already allowed for reduce both assets and obligations. A bridge that states how each side changed lets members distinguish decisions made by the investment manager, sponsor and actuary.

How the result is built

S = A − L; F = A/L; ΔS = ΔA − ΔL

A is the market value of plan assets and L is the present value of promised benefits at the same date, in a common currency. S is funding surplus in currency units, negative for a deficit. F is the unit-free funded ratio. Δ denotes closing less opening value.

  1. Opening assets of €100m and obligations of €110m give a €10m deficit and 90.91% funded ratio. Gross investment gain of €7m, €1m fees, €3m contributions and €5m benefits take assets to €104m.
  2. Obligations become €117m after €4m interest, €2m newly earned benefits, €6m assumption changes and €5m benefits paid. Both sides use the same €5m benefit cash flow.
  3. Surplus moves from −€10m to −€13m. The change reconciles as +€6m net investment gain + €3m contributions − €4m interest − €2m new benefits − €6m remeasurement = −€3m. The closing funded ratio is 88.89%.

When the reasoning holds

  • Assets and obligations must be for the same group, date, currency and valuation basis. A new discount curve or a transfer of members warrants explicit attribution before comparisons.
  • An equal benefit payment cancels in the currency surplus bridge if it discharges an obligation of the same carrying value. A settlement above or below that value generates a separate gain or loss.
Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
First example

A pension account begins with €10m, earns €0.6m before fees, pays €0.1m fees and distributes €0.8m in benefits. It closes at €9.7m. If the present value of remaining promised benefits is €11m at the same date, funding is about 88.2%.

Stewardship as delegated work

A portfolio owner may delegate voting and engagement to its manager. The owner should specify objectives, voting authority, escalation choices and reporting. Engagement can involve requesting disclosures, meeting directors or voting, but activity counts alone do not establish that the owner influenced a real outcome.

A manager responsible for several funds can face conflicting mandates. Securities lending can transfer voting rights during the loan, depending on arrangements. Decide whether recalling shares for a material vote justifies the lost lending income under the client's policy and actual deadline.

Conflicts and accountability

A manager might advise a company on pensions while voting on that company's directors. A conflict policy should identify the economic interest, assign independent review where appropriate, record the decision and show how it follows client objectives. Disclosure alone does not explain how the conflict was handled.

A credible engagement record distinguishes the manager's action, the issuer's response and the outcome observed. Company improvements can also follow regulation, other investors' efforts or operating changes. Claims of attribution need evidence beyond the timing of a conversation.

Reporting the funding objective

Reconcile opening assets, contributions, withdrawals, investment result and costs to closing assets. Then compare assets with the value and timing of liabilities using a stated basis. Assets can gain while the funded ratio deteriorates if updated benefit promises, inflation or lower discount rates raise liabilities faster.

Distinguish an investment manager's performance from the feasibility of the owner's spending plan. State the benchmark and fees behind reported returns, explain material risk changes and identify decisions the trustees can take before upcoming payment dates.

Method

Writing the trustee report

  1. Reconcile money first

    Show beginning balance, net external flows, gross investment gains, transparent fees and ending market value.

  2. Revalue the objective

    State current liability assumptions, funding ratio, near-term liquidity and the source of changes since the prior report.

  3. Report decisions and evidence

    Record material votes, conflicts and engagement outcomes, with a checkable decision trail and any required action or review date.

Worked case

A good return with a weaker funding ratio

A pension plan starts with €100m assets and €100m liabilities. It receives €3m contributions, pays €5m benefits, earns €8m gross investment income and pays €1m fees. At year-end an actuarial review puts remaining liabilities at €112m.

Working

  1. Assets close at €100m + €3m − €5m + €8m − €1m = €105m. Net investment gain is €7m. An exact time-weighted return would need subperiod valuations around material cash flows.
  2. Funding moves from 100% to €105m/€112m = 93.75%, a deficit of €7m despite the investment gain. Explain which assumption changes increased the liability valuation rather than blaming or praising the asset manager for the entire move.
  3. If a sponsor-affiliated issuer is due for a board vote, describe the related commercial relationship, independent review and rationale under the plan's voting policy, separately from the numerical investment report.

Interpretation

These figures answer different trustee questions about how the money changed, whether promised payments remain covered, and who exercised ownership rights on the plan's behalf.

A common mistake

Reporting only the manager's gross return can hide external cash movements, fees and a rising liability value.

How to correct it

Reconcile assets, then explain funding and liquidity on the same date. Disclose material stewardship decisions with their evidence.

Quick check
A pension client reports €45m in assets and €50m in the present value of liabilities at the same date. Which statement belongs in its report?

€45m divided by €50m gives 90%. Also explain changes in both asset values and liability assumptions so readers can judge how the funding position evolved.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A foundation begins with €60m, receives €2m gifts, spends €4m, earns €5m gross investment gain and pays €0.6m fees. Remaining committed grants are valued at €65m.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find closing assets, net gain, funding ratio and monetary gap.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    A manager claims that six engagement meetings caused a company's emissions decline. What evidence and qualification would strengthen the report?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Assets finish at €62.4m. Net investment gain is €4.4m. Coverage is €62.4m/€65m = 96%, so commitments exceed assets by €2.6m.

Part B 3 marks

Record what change was requested, dates, issuer response and independently measured emissions against a boundary and baseline. Consider other plausible causes and report contribution or association unless causal evidence supports more.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a pension manager report a positive net investment return while trustees discover the funding deficit has widened, and which cash movements explain the difference?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. This plan gained €6m after fees on its investments, but its liabilities grew faster than the assets after the other cash movements. The deficit widened by €3m and the funded ratio fell to 88.89%. I would present a signed bridge showing sponsor cash and actuarial changes separately. In the same report I would document any stewardship decisions, their rationale, voting outcome and the person accountable for follow-up.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The plan holds the sponsor’s shares and the sponsor asks the manager to support a related-party transaction. What belongs in the trustee record?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Record the ownership and the conflict, disclose it to the designated independent reviewer, and follow the plan’s decision and voting authority. Record the evidence on member interests, who was recused, the vote actually submitted, confirmation of execution and the review date. The sponsor’s contribution to funding is a separate entry and cannot substitute for that analysis.
