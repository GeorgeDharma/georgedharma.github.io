Fair dealing and allocation of partially filled block orders

Apply a documented pre-trade allocation policy to eligible accounts and reconcile an average execution price and costs.

Theory and terminology

Fair allocation of partially filled orders

pro rata allocation · block orders · fair dealing

What you should be able to explain

  • Apply a documented pre-trade allocation policy to eligible accounts and reconcile an average execution price and costs.
  • Identify how mandate differences, rounding and post-trade changes should be governed.

Eligibility comes before capacity

Clients can have different mandates, cash availability, tax restrictions, concentration limits and trading instructions. Fair treatment does not require giving an unsuitable security to every account in the firm. Determine which accounts can participate and record each intended order before execution reveals whether the opportunity was attractive.

A shared order or block lets the same security be traded for several accounts. Preallocation records the requested amount for each account and the rule for a shortage. It limits the opportunity for a manager to assign a favorable fill to a preferred client after seeing the market.

Apply the disclosed allocation policy

A pro-rata policy allocates the fill in proportion to each eligible order. Other appropriately designed, documented methods can address minimum denominations or portfolio constraints. Representative clients should receive the treatment their policy promises.

An average execution price is appropriate under the specific block policy used here. Total consideration for all fills divided by the total quantity gives the common execution price, with shared commissions allocated as stated. Record a fixed rounding method for whole shares or minimum lots and monitor recurring advantages.

Keep execution quality and inter-client fairness separate

Good prices for the block do not settle whether its distribution across clients was fair. Review time-stamped allocations, changes before release, exceptions, client cash and mandate capacity, fees and final custody records. A large client’s commercial importance creates a conflict if it determines access to a scarce opportunity.

Some policy exceptions can be justified, for example a newly notified restriction that makes an account ineligible. Preserve the evidence, obtain the required independent review and apply the pre-existing rule to the remaining eligible accounts. A price move alone supplies no investment justification for rewriting the allocation.

Method

Build the result

Allocation to i = Q × qᵢ/Σⱼqⱼ. Average block price = Σₖ (nₖpₖ)/Q. Shared commission per unit = F/Q.

Q is filled shares, qᵢ the pre-recorded eligible request for account i, and Σⱼqⱼ the total eligible request. Each venue fill k has nₖ shares and price pₖ in euros per share. F is total shared commission in euros. This case has no rounding residual.

  1. Verify the account approvals and total the eligible requests at the time the order is released.
  2. Multiply each account’s proportion of the requests by the actual fill, then enforce the documented rounding and size rules.
  3. Calculate the common execution price and commission, reconcile all units and cash to the broker, and investigate post-execution changes.
Worked case

A 6,000-share secondary-market block across three mandates

Eligible accounts A, B and C request 6,000, 3,000 and 1,000 shares before execution. A fourth account is barred by its mandate. The block buys 2,000 at €24.90 and 4,000 at €25.05 and incurs €180 of commissions. Under its published policy the manager allocates pro rata at the average price and shares commissions by units. Client B pays the highest management fee.

Working

  1. The eligible order totals 10,000 shares and fills 6,000, or 60%. Allocate 3,600 to A, 1,800 to B and 600 to C.
  2. Broker consideration is 2,000 × €24.90 + 4,000 × €25.05 = €150,000. All three clients receive the same €25.00 average before commission.
  3. Commission is €180/6,000 = €0.03 per share. A pays €90,108, B pays €45,054 and C pays €15,018. The three totals sum to €150,180 and the share count sums to 6,000.
  4. The ineligible account receives zero. Preserve the mandate evidence and preallocation timestamps. B’s fee level plays no role in the shares, prices or expenses.

Decision and interpretation

Approve the reconciled allocations. A request to give B the cheaper first print after execution would break this block’s stated policy and require escalation before any release.

Quick check
Requests are 400 and 600 shares. A pro-rata block fills 750, with EUR 75 total commission split by shares. What should the smaller account receive and pay in commission?

The smaller account placed 40% of the eligible request and receives 40% of the 750-share fill, or 300. Its 40% share of €75 commission is €30.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Three suitable clients pre-order 1,200, 800 and 500 units of a bond fund in a secondary block. The broker executes 1,250 at an aggregate consideration of €50,500 plus €125 commission. The same pro-rata average-price rule applies and individual units are permitted. Prepare all three allocations.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Eligible requests total 2,500. The 50% fill gives A 600, B 400 and C 250 units.
  2. The common price is €50,500/1,250 = €40.40. Commission is €0.10 per unit. Each client pays €40.50 per allocated unit.
  3. Cash debits are €24,300, €16,200 and €10,125, totaling €50,625 including the €125 fee. Record the order trail and positions.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How should a manager allocate a scarce block fill when one eligible client pays twice the fee of another?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would preserve the eligible orders recorded before trading and apply the disclosed shortage rule equally to them. In this block the completion rate is 60%, so the high-fee client’s 3,000-share request receives 1,800 at the same average price and fee per share as the others. I would reconcile the units and cash and investigate changes made after anyone knew the execution outcome.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A 100-unit trading lot applies to final account allocations and only 1,200 units can settle. Can the desk give the rounding remainder to the highest-fee client?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Apply the disclosed whole-lot and residual-allocation procedure to the feasible 1,200 units and reconcile the broker discrepancy or residual separately. The contract needs a rule, such as a documented rotation among similarly eligible accounts. Fee preference and a rule invented after viewing the result are poor grounds for allocating a favorable residual.
