Private-fund commitments, capital calls and liquidity pacing

Reconcile liquid assets, private NAV and unfunded commitments without confusing a promise with an investment.

Theory and terminology

Private-market commitment pacing

capital calls · denominator effect · liquidity stress

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile liquid assets, private NAV and unfunded commitments without confusing a promise with an investment.
  • Size a new commitment under base and stressed cash and allocation limits.

Three balances answer three different questions

A commitment is an obligation to supply capital when called under the partnership agreement. Signing it need not move cash on day one. Paid-in capital is the amount already contributed. Net asset value, or NAV, estimates the current value of the interest already held. Unfunded commitment is the amount still callable under the stated terms.

A call transfers cash from the investor’s liquid portfolio into the private fund position, initially assuming the amount is invested at cost. A distribution reverses that transfer. Subject to gains, fees, losses and external spending, neither transfer should create total wealth.

Estimate when each movement can occur

Allocators forecast calls from remaining commitments and distributions from fund age, assets, exits and plausible market conditions. A mature fund might normally distribute cash, yet droughts in the sale market can delay those proceeds precisely when other managers still need cash.

Private marks often arrive later than public prices. A decline in liquid securities can raise the measured private allocation even if private positions are unchanged. This denominator effect can coincide with a real funding shortfall. Report NAV exposure, outstanding callable commitments and dated liquidity separately.

Solve for the promise the investor can actually keep

Model each existing fund or vintage before adding the proposed commitment. Estimate what percentage of the new promise is called within the horizon. Test a realistic stress with higher calls, lower distributions, weaker saleable assets and a reserve for beneficiary payments.

A commitment ceiling is scenario-specific. Some agreements permit recallable distributions, recycling, borrowing or penalties following late settlement. Include any such provisions in the unfunded and dated cash schedules. A year-end surplus alone cannot fund a call due in March.

Method

Build the result

L₁ = L₀ + market change − existing calls + distributions − spending − cK. N₁ = N₀ + existing calls − distributions + cK + private revaluation. Private weight = N₁/(L₁ + N₁).

L is the liquid portfolio, N private NAV, K a proposed new commitment and c the fraction of K called during the horizon. Subscripts 0 and 1 mean opening and closing. All money amounts are in millions of euros. The example has no recallable distributions, fund fees or private revaluation.

  1. Roll liquid resources and private NAV separately and check their sum against total investment gains and investor spending.
  2. Find the largest K satisfying both a minimum liquid reserve and a maximum private weight. Solve each bound separately and take the tighter one.
  3. Reconcile remaining callable promises as opening unfunded plus new commitments minus valid calls, then test the timing of cash availability.
Worked case

An endowment signs its next private-market vintage

Opening assets are €80m liquid and €20m private, with €20m existing uncalled commitments. Required closing liquidity is €46m and private NAV must stay at or below 45% of closing assets. Annual endowment spending is €6m. In base conditions existing funds call €8m and distribute €4m, liquid marks do not change, and 30% of new K is called. In stress the liquid portfolio first falls 15%, existing calls rise to €12m, distributions fall to €2m and 50% of K is called. No private revaluation or other flows occur.

Working

  1. Base closing amounts are L = 70 − 0.30K and N = 24 + 0.30K. Total assets are €94m. Liquidity permits K up to €80m and the 45% private ceiling permits (0.45 × 94 − 24)/0.30 = €61m.
  2. At the proposed €40m, base liquidity is €58m and private NAV €36m, or 38.30% of total. Remaining unfunded is €20m + €40m − €8m − €12m = €40m.
  3. After the 15% public loss, liquid assets start the stress at €68m. Closing stress values are L = 52 − 0.50K and N = 30 + 0.50K, totaling €82m. Liquidity caps K at €12m and the private cap gives (36.9 − 30)/0.50 = €13.8m.
  4. At €12m, stressed liquid resources are €46m and private NAV is €36m, or 43.90%. Unfunded is €20m + €12m − €12m existing calls − €6m new calls = €14m.

Decision and interpretation

Under both stated closing constraints, €12m is the maximum. Check actual call dates against the benefit reserve before signing. At €40m the stressed liquid balance would fall to €32m.

Quick check
A plan’s stress forecast leaves €30m liquid before calls on a new commitment. It must retain €18m and assumes 60% of the new commitment is called. No other constraint binds. What is the largest new commitment?

Available cash is €12m. A €20m commitment would call 0.60 × €20m = €12m, leaving exactly the required €18m reserve.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A second investor begins with €60m liquid and €40m private. It spends €5m, pays €8m existing calls and receives €4m distributions. No assets change in value, and 40% of a new commitment is called. Minimum liquidity is €30m and maximum private allocation 60%. What is the binding limit?

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Closing liquid resources are €51m − 0.40K and private NAV is €44m + 0.40K. Total remains €95m after spending.
  2. Liquidity gives K ≤ (€51m − €30m)/0.40 = €52.5m. The private-weight cap permits NAV of 0.60 × 95 = €57m, giving K ≤ (€57m − €44m)/0.40 = €32.5m.
  3. Private allocation binds at €32.5m. Liquid resources then equal €38m, above the reserve. The uncalled amount of the new promise is still €19.5m.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A trustee wants a €40m private fund commitment. How much can the portfolio promise while remaining able to pay calls and benefits during a downturn?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would size the promise against the cash that could actually be called, then check private NAV as a share of the stressed total portfolio. Here €40m looks affordable in the base case but leaves €32m liquid under stress, below the €46m reserve. A €12m promise meets that reserve and the private cap at year-end. If distributions arrive late, a dated cash schedule can require a lower amount.

Interviewer’s follow-up

In the worked endowment stress, the €2m distribution arrives after all calls and benefits. The €46m reserve must hold every day. What changes?

Compare with the follow-up answer

At the earlier low point, liquidity is €68m − €12m − €6m − 0.50K = €50m − 0.50K. Requiring at least €46m permits K of only €8m. The later distribution restores closing cash but cannot settle earlier obligations.
