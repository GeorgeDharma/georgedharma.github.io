Suitability, client capacity and conflicted product advice

Translate verified spending, concentrations and stress tolerance into a maximum suitable trade under a written mandate.

Theory and terminology

Investment suitability and capacity for loss

client risk capacity · investment constraints · conflicts

What you should be able to explain

  • Translate verified spending, concentrations and stress tolerance into a maximum suitable trade under a written mandate.
  • Document the advisory role, fees, product constraints and an independently supported recommendation.

Identify the relationship and decision owner

Advising a client requires understanding their financial circumstances, commitments, investment experience, objectives and restrictions. A willingness to see an investment fall is a preference. The ability to pay tuition or living expenses during that same fall is a resource constraint. Establish both before recommending anything.

The professional suitability principle used here evaluates a recommendation in the portfolio context. Rules and duties can differ by jurisdiction and relationship. Confirm who owns the assets, who may authorize a change, and whether an advisory or execution-only service is being provided.

Look through the proposed instrument

A note marketed as income can contain issuer credit risk, capital loss, early redemption conditions and an inability to sell at a stated value. List the cash flows under ordinary and adverse states, the investor’s exit rights, earliest usable redemption date, investment minimum and all relevant fees.

Calculate the same portfolio limits after adding the proposed position. The calculations below assume funding from a low-risk liquid reserve, so the entire product stress is incremental. Funding the trade by selling another risky asset would require removing the asset sold from exposures and the stress calculation.

Keep the decision record

An advisory record should state current client facts, date, product economics, alternatives, portfolio effects, compensation interests and the decision. Disclose firm or adviser incentives that could affect the recommendation. Disclosure alone cannot make a product consistent with a binding mandate.

If facts change, reassess them independently and update the mandate through the authorized process. If a client requests an inconsistent trade, follow the relevant advisory or execution procedure and document the client’s instructions. An unsolicited request does not retrospectively justify presenting an unsuitable transaction as advice.

Method

Build the result

Allowable purchase = minimum of spare liquid funds, illiquidity headroom, issuer headroom and remaining stress-loss budget / incremental loss fraction. A purchase must also meet the product minimum.

All headrooms and the purchase are currency amounts. Spare liquid funds are liquid resources minus the protected reserve. The incremental loss fraction is the proportion of each euro purchased lost under the specified stress. Existing holdings and proposed trade use the same measurement date.

  1. Ring-fence committed payments and the documented emergency reserve first.
  2. Apply written limits to total portfolio value and subtract existing exposures. Estimate the incremental stress loss based on the actual funding asset.
  3. Choose the tightest headroom, enforce feasible lot sizes and product minimums, then check other terms that cannot be reduced to a numerical limit.
Worked case

An adviser reviews a three-year note for a private client

The client has €500,000, including €160,000 of safe liquid assets. Protect €90,000 tuition due in six months and €40,000 emergency cash. Existing illiquid holdings are €60,000 with a 25% limit, and exposure to the note issuer is €20,000 with a 10% limit. Existing stressed loss is €31,000 against a €55,000 budget. The note could lose 60%, is illiquid and has a €50,000 minimum. The distributor earns 1.5% of the sale. Fund it only from safe liquid assets.

Working

  1. Liquidity headroom is €160,000 − €90,000 − €40,000 = €30,000.
  2. Maximum additional illiquid exposure is 25% × €500,000 − €60,000 = €65,000. Issuer headroom is 10% × €500,000 − €20,000 = €30,000.
  3. Remaining stress capacity is €24,000. At a 60% loss fraction it supports at most €40,000. The tightest limit is therefore €30,000.
  4. The smallest €50,000 lot exceeds that limit. It also gives the distributor €750 compensation. Record the incentive and decline to recommend the note. Compare suitably liquid, permitted alternatives on their actual risk, income and costs.

Decision and interpretation

The appropriate recommendation is no purchase on these facts. A high gross coupon or the client’s signed risk acknowledgment does not release cash reserved for tuition.

Quick check
A client has €80,000 spending headroom, €15,000 issuer headroom and €30,000 stress headroom. The investment starts at €20,000 and can only be purchased in whole lots. What is the largest permitted purchase under these figures?

The tightest portfolio constraint permits only €15,000, below the smallest available lot. No positive purchase satisfies all restrictions.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

An €800,000 client has €230,000 safe liquidity and must retain €180,000. Illiquid assets are €90,000 against a 20% limit. Issuer exposure is €30,000 against a 10% limit. The client can absorb €12,000 additional stress loss and a proposed product loses 40% in that stress. The minimum is €25,000 and increments are €5,000. Assume the whole allocation comes from safe liquidity. Find the largest permitted purchase.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Liquid headroom is €50,000. Illiquidity headroom is 0.20 × €800,000 − €90,000 = €70,000.
  2. Issuer headroom is €80,000 − €30,000 = €50,000. The stress ceiling is €12,000/0.40 = €30,000.
  3. The minimum of the four limits is €30,000. It meets the minimum and is in valid €5,000 increments. That is a ceiling, and suitability still depends on the product and client evidence.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A client likes the yield on an illiquid note. Why can the appropriate recommendation be a zero purchase even when the client accepts its advertised risks?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A client can understand a risk and still have insufficient liquid capital or issuer headroom to take it. In the worked file, only €30,000 can be added without breaking a binding constraint. The minimum note is €50,000. I would document the household’s specific payments, compare the total-portfolio stress and disclose the distributor commission. The resulting advisory recommendation is zero purchase.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The product’s binding withdrawal terms change after the analysis and the client now needs cash in three months. Is the old approval enough?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Reopen the dated assessment. Verify the payment, the instrument’s exit, a credible exit price and the safe resources left for the deadline. Retain the earlier file but document the changed facts, revised limit and new recommendation before taking an advisory action.
