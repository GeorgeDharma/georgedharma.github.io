International allocations

Measure local investment returns and currency effects separately. The degree of hedging should reflect the investor’s liabilities and cost of implementing the hedge.

The starting point

An investor paying bills in euros earns two returns on a foreign asset. The asset can rise or fall in its own currency, and that currency can strengthen or weaken against the euro. Multiply the two growth factors for the unhedged return in euros.

A currency hedge can reduce mismatch with the investor's liabilities. Decide how much to hedge, what future amount the contract covers and when it settles. The contracted forward rate, trading costs and a changing asset value can affect the realised result.

Theory and method

Minimum-variance currency hedge ratio

Optimal hedge ratio · Regression hedge

A pension plan can sell currency forwards against an international equity sleeve. Why can the volatility-minimizing amount differ from the sleeve’s entire current foreign value?

A foreign equity sleeve makes or loses home-currency money through equity prices and exchange rates. A forward contract pays off on a currency movement. Selling the currency can offset fluctuations in the combined position, even though equity prices may move at the same time. The desk asks how much of a change in the entire sleeve tends to accompany one unit of change in the forward. Hedging this estimated sensitivity leaves the part of the investment return least related to the forward.

How the result is built

Rh = Ru − hF; h* = Cov(Ru,F)/Var(F)

Ru and Rh are unhedged and hedged home-currency portfolio returns for the period. F is the profit on one unit of long foreign forward exposure divided by its initial reference notional. h is short-forward notional divided by portfolio value, and h* minimizes variance. Cov and Var measure covariance and variance of decimal returns.

  1. Let the unhedged portfolio’s annual volatility be 12%, forward return variance 0.0064 and covariance between the two returns 0.0048.
  2. Expand hedged variance as 0.12² + h² × 0.0064 − 2h × 0.0048. Its slope is 2h × 0.0064 − 2 × 0.0048.
  3. Set the slope to zero to obtain h = 0.75. The minimum variance is 0.0108, giving 10.39% volatility. Selling 100% instead raises forecast volatility to 10.58%.

When the reasoning holds

  • Portfolio and forward gains must use a common base currency, observation period and notional convention. Quoting the opposite currency direction can reverse the economically intended trade.
  • The variance objective excludes expected carry, collateral liquidity, transaction costs and the investor’s spending currency obligations. Any of these can justify a different approved hedge.
Terms in this lesson
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Forward
A contract to exchange an agreed amount at a set price on a future date.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
How to read the notation
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

A euro investor owns a dollar fund that gains 10% in dollars. If each dollar buys 5% fewer euros at the end, the euro holding grows by 1.10 × 0.95 = 1.045. The unhedged euro return is 4.5%.

Two compounding returns

Let the exchange rate be home currency per unit of foreign currency. An unhedged foreign investment has a home-currency growth factor equal to the asset's local growth factor times the exchange-rate growth factor. The interaction term matters when either change is large.

The direction of the quotation is crucial. A rise in euros per dollar increases the euro value of a given dollar asset. A rise in dollars per euro does the opposite. Translate the beginning and ending cash amounts directly if the reported quote is unfamiliar.

What a forward hedge covers

To hedge an initially known foreign exposure, a home investor can contract to sell foreign currency later at a stated forward rate. The hedge payoff in home currency is foreign amount sold times forward rate minus final spot rate, under cash settlement or equivalent delivery.

A hedge for the initial principal leaves the uncertain foreign investment gain or loss exposed. A hedge for the eventual full value would need that value known or the hedge adjusted dynamically. Forward points, fees, collateral and hedge timing affect the result.

Choosing a hedge ratio

A home-currency pension liability gives a reason to reduce currency exposure on foreign assets. An investor expecting foreign-currency expenses can have a natural offset. Currency correlations with the assets and the investor's labor income can also change total risk.

Compare asset and currency decisions separately. A foreign fund manager may outperform in local currency while currency losses dominate the client's result. Benchmark hedged and unhedged mandates according to their stated policy and document any active currency decision.

Method

Valuing a hedged foreign position

  1. Translate the opening amount

    Record the invested foreign principal and spot in home-currency-per-foreign-unit terms.

  2. Value the two legs

    Translate final foreign asset value at final spot, then add the correctly signed forward payoff on contracted foreign units.

  3. Compare like with like

    Divide final total home wealth by opening home wealth, alongside an unhedged alternative, using the same fee and cash assumptions.

Worked case

A euro investor hedges principal only

A euro fund buys $100,000 of US shares at €0.90 per dollar. Shares rise 12% in dollars, the dollar ends at €0.81, and the fund had agreed to sell $100,000 forward at €0.89. Ignore costs and collateral income.

Working

  1. Opening wealth is €90,000. Ending shares are worth $112,000 × €0.81 = €90,720. The unhedged euro gain is €720, or 0.8%, equal to 1.12 × 0.90 − 1.
  2. The forward pays $100,000 × (€0.89 − €0.81) = €8,000. Combined ending wealth is €98,720 and return is €8,720/€90,000 ≈ 9.69%.
  3. The $12,000 investment gain remained exposed to final spot, and the forward rate was below the initial spot. A 100% hedge of starting principal therefore does not guarantee exactly the local share return.

Interpretation

If the investor owed dollar tuition rather than euro pensions, some currency exposure could directly match a liability. A hedge should be judged against the investor's actual purpose.

A common mistake

Treating the forward exchange rate as a forecast of final spot mistakes a contracted conversion price for a reliable prediction.

How to correct it

Use the promised forward price to calculate contractual payoff and stress a range of future spot rates for residual exposure.

Quick check
A Japanese asset gains 8% in yen while the yen buys 10% fewer euros. Ignoring costs, what does an unhedged euro investor earn?

The euro growth factor is 1.08 × 0.90 = 0.972. That is 2.8% below the starting value. Adding the percentage changes omits their interaction.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A fund starts with $200,000 at €0.80 per dollar. The asset earns 5% in dollars. Final spot is €0.76 and the fund sold $150,000 forward at €0.79. Ignore other costs.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find ending unhedged wealth and return in euros.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Find hedge payoff and total return. What foreign amount was left exposed at the end?

Part A 3 marks

Initial value is €160,000. Ending assets are $210,000 × €0.76 = €159,600. Unhedged return is −€400/€160,000 = −0.25%.

Part B 4 marks

Hedge pays €4,500, giving €164,100 final wealth and 2.5625% return. The ending $210,000 asset exceeds the $150,000 hedge by $60,000.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A pension plan can sell currency forwards against an international equity sleeve. Why can the volatility-minimizing amount differ from the sleeve’s entire current foreign value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would sell forwards on 75% of the sleeve for the stated risk objective. The ratio is the covariance of the total sleeve with the forward divided by the forward’s own variance. This recognizes that foreign equities and the exchange rate can offset or reinforce one another. If the purpose were to lock the home value of a known fixed foreign receivable, I would specify that cash flow separately and match its amount and date.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The manager also has to post home-currency cash if the short forward loses value. What would you ask before adding the hedge?

Compare with the follow-up answer

I would size the potential cash call as the forward notional times a plausible adverse price move and compare it with cash that can settle on the margin date. A 10% adverse move on €75m equivalent implies roughly €7.5m before netting or collateral thresholds. A volatility-minimizing trade can still make meeting benefits harder during a squeeze.
