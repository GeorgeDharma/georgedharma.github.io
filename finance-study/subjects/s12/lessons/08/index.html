Attribution

Decompose active performance into allocation and security selection, handling interactions and currency consistently. Reconcile the parts to the observed relative return.

The starting point

Suppose a fund beats its benchmark. It may have given more weight to a successful sector, selected better securities within sectors, or done both. Performance attribution separates these choices under a stated calculation rule and checks that their effects add up.

For a single period, measure allocation from differences in sector weights at benchmark sector returns. Measure selection from differences in sector returns at benchmark weights. The interaction is the weight difference times the return difference. Compare on a consistent currency and fee basis.

Theory and method

Brinson–Fachler attribution

Arithmetic allocation, selection and interaction · Holdings-based attribution

A manager overweighted equities, underweighted bonds, and beat each local index by one point. How can you show whether allocation also helped without giving the same gain to two decisions?

A client chooses a policy mix and hires a manager who can change both the amount invested in each sector and the investments within it. A sector overweight helps if the sector’s benchmark beats the overall benchmark. A manager can also add value by selecting better holdings at the policy weight. Because both decisions operate on the same money, there is a cross-product when extra capital is placed in a sector where the manager also picked different securities. Attribution can measure all three components in one period.

How the result is built

Aᵢ = (wPᵢ − wBᵢ)(bᵢ − B), Sᵢ = wBᵢ(pᵢ − bᵢ), Iᵢ = (wPᵢ − wBᵢ)(pᵢ − bᵢ)

For sector i, wPᵢ and wBᵢ are beginning portfolio and benchmark weights. pᵢ and bᵢ are sector portfolio and benchmark returns. B is the total weighted benchmark return. Aᵢ, Sᵢ and Iᵢ are allocation, selection and interaction contributions in return units.

  1. Policy weights are 50%, 30%, 20% in equities, bonds and cash. Their returns are 6%, −4%, 1%, giving B = 2%. The manager holds 60%, 20%, 20% and earns 7%, −3%, 1%, giving 3.8%.
  2. Equity allocation contributes 0.10 × (6% − 2%) = +0.40 point. The bond underweight contributes −0.10 × (−4% − 2%) = +0.60 point. Allocation totals +1.00 point.
  3. At policy weights, selection is 0.50 × 1% + 0.30 × 1% = +0.80 point. Equity and bond interactions are +0.10 and −0.10 point, offsetting. All three effects sum to the observed +1.80 points.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use beginning weights, mutually exclusive sectors, all holdings and matching currency and fee conventions. Unclassified cash or ending weights can break reconciliation or assign performance to the wrong decision.
  • This is one-period arithmetic attribution. A year spanning different holdings and cash moves requires an explicit linking method or a valid annual calculation using the appropriate holdings data.
Terms in this lesson
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Percentage point
The difference between two percentages. A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises one percentage point.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
First example

A benchmark holds 50% shares earning 10% and 50% bonds earning zero, so it earns 5%. A fund holds 60% shares earning 12% and 40% bonds earning zero, returning 7.2%. Its 2.2-point lead splits into 1 point allocation, 1 point selection and 0.2 points interaction.

Reconstructing active return from holdings

A one-period arithmetic performance attribution compares a portfolio with a benchmark, starting with beginning-of-period weights and total returns for comparable groups. Active return means portfolio return minus benchmark return in percentage points. Ensure group weights add to one and both portfolios have consistent currency and fees.

Sector classification must reflect the decisions being assessed. If a global manager chooses countries first and securities second, a purely industry-based report may explain the arithmetic but fail to represent the actual management process.

One exact decomposition

In a common convention, allocation sums each weight difference times the benchmark sector return. Selection sums benchmark weights times the portfolio-minus-benchmark sector return. Interaction sums each weight difference times that same sector-return difference. These three terms reconstruct the total active return.

For a fully invested comparison, subtracting the overall benchmark return from each sector benchmark return in the allocation term leaves total allocation unchanged because weight differences sum to zero. It can improve interpretation of an overweight in a sector that lagged the aggregate benchmark.

What attribution cannot prove

A positive selection term identifies a favourable return from the particular names within the groups. It does not prove selection skill. Size, value, duration or currency exposure inside a group may explain the result. Cross-check exposures with a factor analysis and the mandate's decision process.

One-period arithmetic effects cannot generally be added across periods to reproduce a compounded total. Multi-period attribution needs an explicit linking method. If currency effects are separated, include interactions or use a consistent conversion before attributing local asset choices.

Method

Checking an attribution table

  1. Build group contributions

    Multiply portfolio weights by portfolio group returns and benchmark weights by benchmark group returns, then sum each side.

  2. Compute the three effects

    Use the same group definitions and one named weighting convention. Retain positive and negative active weights.

  3. Reconcile and interpret

    Add all effects to observed portfolio-minus-benchmark return before interpreting allocation, security choice and residual interaction.

Worked case

Stock picking is strongest in the underweight sector

Benchmark weights are 60% industrials and 40% utilities. Respective benchmark returns are 5% and 10%. The fund holds 40% industrials earning 8% and 60% utilities earning 9%.

Working

  1. Benchmark returns 0.6 × 5% + 0.4 × 10% = 7%. Fund returns 0.4 × 8% + 0.6 × 9% = 8.6%, so the active result is +1.6 percentage points.
  2. Allocation is (−0.2 × 5 + 0.2 × 10) = +1.0 point. Selection is 0.6 × 3 + 0.4 × (−1) = +1.4 points.
  3. Interaction is −0.2 × 3 + 0.2 × (−1) = −0.8 points. Total +1.0 + 1.4 − 0.8 = +1.6. Selection was strongest in the area the fund underweighted.

Interpretation

The attractive utilities benchmark helped the allocation decision even though the fund's own utilities lagged. This difference between sector direction and within-sector selection explains why separate terms matter.

At the board

Reconcile a change before explaining it

A unit sells 100 items at €10 in the budget and 120 at €9 in the actual period. Calculate the price and volume explanations relative to an explicit baseline. A manager can be rewarded differently depending on where the interaction is assigned.

TermCalculationRevenue effect
Volume at old price(120 − 100) × €10+€200
Price on old volume(€9 − €10) × 100−€100
Interaction(120 − 100) × (€9 − €10)−€20

The effects add to +€80, the difference between actual revenue of €1,080 and budgeted revenue of €1,000. A two-term bridge instead prices the 120 actual units at the €1 shortfall, giving a −€120 price effect and +€200 volume. Both reconcile. As in portfolio performance attribution, identify which convention allocates the interaction before interpreting who contributed what.

A common mistake

Combining selection measured at portfolio weights with a separate conventional interaction term double-counts their joint effect.

How to correct it

State which weights define selection. If interaction is already included in a two-term formula, do not add it again.

Quick check
A fund outperforms by 1.8 percentage points in a one-period attribution. Allocation contributes 0.7 points and selection 0.8. What interaction reconciles the figures?

Subtract the two identified contributions from 1.8. The remaining positive 0.3 points completes the decomposition under the stated method.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

Benchmark weights are 50% technology, 50% health. Sector returns are 4% and 8%. A fund holds 70% technology earning 6% and 30% health earning 9%. Use the stated three-effect convention.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find portfolio return, benchmark return and active return.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Compute allocation, selection and interaction and interpret the sign of allocation.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Portfolio earns 0.7 × 6% + 0.3 × 9% = 6.9%. Benchmark earns 6%. Active return is +0.9 percentage points.

Part B 4 marks

Allocation is 0.2 × 4 − 0.2 × 8 = −0.8 points. Selection is 0.5 × 2 + 0.5 × 1 = +1.5. Interaction is 0.2 × 2 − 0.2 × 1 = +0.2. The overweight in the weaker benchmark sector hurt.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A manager overweighted equities, underweighted bonds, and beat each local index by one point. How can you show whether allocation also helped without giving the same gain to two decisions?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would show the client that the mix decisions earned one percentage point and security selection at policy weights earned another 0.80. The equity overweight amplified good stock selection by 0.10 point and the bond underweight reduced the benefit of good bond selection by the same amount. The check is that 1.80 equals 3.80% minus 2.00%. It explains the sources of this period’s return. A test of skill would need repeated observations.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The investment director wants one number for selection including interaction. Does that change the sector conclusions?

Compare with the follow-up answer

It changes the labels. With selection plus interaction, equity security selection contributes 0.60 point and bond selection contributes 0.20 point, each using its actual portfolio weight. Allocation stays 0.40 and 0.60, and the overall 1.80 points stays intact. Disclose the convention when comparing managers whose reports use different allocations of the cross-product.
