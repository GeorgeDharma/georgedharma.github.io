Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A university fund with grants and a contribution

A fund begins with €20m, promises €1m scholarships at year-end and targets preservation of opening purchasing power after payment. Expected inflation is 2%. Ignore fees. Its equity/bond allocation starts at €12m/€8m. In a separate allocation exercise before the scholarships, equities rise 25%, bonds stay flat and €2m new cash arrives. The strategic equity target is 60%.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

Interest-rate and currency hedges for a pension plan

A euro plan has €100m assets and liabilities worth €120m with modified duration five. Its bond funds have durations two and seven and respond to the same parallel rate shift. Separately it buys $100,000 foreign shares at €0.80 per dollar, earns 10% locally and sells $80,000 forward at €0.79. Final spot is €0.72.

Fund costs are 0.8 percentage points and the investable tracker costs 0.2 points. Use beginning weights and the three-effect attribution convention taught in the course.

A client's contributions, performance and funding

Remaining liabilities at that date have present value of €240m. The manager also votes shares in a company that buys other services from it.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A university fund with grants and a contribution

Part A 3 marks

Find required nominal portfolio return without new contributions for the year-end spending objective.

Real preservation requires €20.4m after scholarships, or €21.4m before payment. Required nominal return on €20m is 7%.

Part B 4 marks

In the allocation exercise calculate the post-market weights before new cash and the trade needed after new cash to restore the target.

Equity rises to €15m against €8m bonds, giving 15/23 = 65.22%. After €2m inflow the total is €25m and target equity €15m. Invest all cash in bonds, ending €15m/€10m without selling shares.

Part C 3 marks

Explain why the 7% target does not establish capacity to risk the next scholarship payment and give a measurable mandate term.

The return is a planning requirement, while the grant is binding on a due date. A stated euro liquidity reserve or dated cash-matching rule can protect that payment, consistent with other funding and risk constraints.

Case 2 · 10 marks

Interest-rate and currency hedges for a pension plan

Part A 4 marks

Find required asset duration and the two fund allocations.

Liability money duration is 120 × 5 = 600, so assets need duration six. With long weight w, 2(1 − w) + 7w = 6 gives 80% long and 20% short, or €80m/€20m.

Part B 4 marks

Calculate unhedged and hedged euro returns on the separate equity position.

Opening is €80,000. Ending asset value is $110,000 × 0.72 = €79,200, a −1% return. Forward gains $80,000 × (0.79 − 0.72) = €5,600. Total €84,800 gives +6%.

Part C 2 marks

Name one exposure left after each hedge.

A yield-curve twist, convexity or inflation-linked liabilities can defeat the duration match. Ending dollars exceed the contracted currency hedge by $30,000, which remains exposed.

Case 3 · 10 marks

How the manager earned the result

Part A 4 marks

Calculate gross returns, gross active return and net active advantage against the tracker.

Benchmark earns 5.2%, fund 7.5% and gross active return 2.3 points. Fund net is 6.7%, tracker net 5.0%, so net advantage is 1.7 points.

Part B 4 marks

Decompose gross active return into allocation, selection and interaction.

Allocation is 0.1 × 10 − 0.1 × 2 = 0.8 points. Selection is 0.4 × 2 + 0.6 × 1 = 1.4 points. Interaction is 0.1 × 2 − 0.1 × 1 = 0.1 point, totaling 2.3.

Part C 2 marks

Does positive selection establish skill? Name two further checks.

Examine repeated risk-adjusted outcomes and sampling error, consider unmeasured factor exposures inside sectors, and check benchmark fit and fees. One period's reconciliation cannot establish skill.

Case 4 · 10 marks

A client's contributions, performance and funding

Part A 3 marks

Calculate two-year total and annualized time-weighted return.

Subperiods earn 20% and €231m/€220m − 1 = 5%. Compound return is 26%. Annualized return is √1.26 − 1 ≈ 12.25%.

Part B 4 marks

Calculate annual money-weighted return and closing funded ratio. Explain the return difference.

Solve 100(1 + r)² + 100(1 + r) = 231 to obtain 10%. Funding is 231/240 = 96.25%, a €9m gap. More client capital earned the lower second-year return.

Part C 3 marks

What should trustees be told about the vote and the potential commercial conflict?

Identify the commercial relationship, voting authority, policy, independent review or recusal, actual decision and rationale for beneficiaries. Distinguish the manager's actions from any later outcome it cannot causally attribute to itself.

  1. S12.11 · Suitability, client capacity and conflicted product advice

    An €800,000 client has €230,000 safe liquidity and must retain €180,000. Illiquid assets are €90,000 against a 20% limit. Issuer exposure is €30,000 against a 10% limit. The client can absorb €12,000 additional stress loss and a proposed product loses 40% in that stress. The minimum is €25,000 and increments are €5,000. Assume the whole allocation comes from safe liquidity. Find the largest permitted purchase.

  2. S12.12 · Fair dealing and allocation of partially filled block orders

    Three suitable clients pre-order 1,200, 800 and 500 units of a bond fund in a secondary block. The broker executes 1,250 at an aggregate consideration of €50,500 plus €125 commission. The same pro-rata average-price rule applies and individual units are permitted. Prepare all three allocations.

  3. S12.13 · Private-fund commitments, capital calls and liquidity pacing

    A second investor begins with €60m liquid and €40m private. It spends €5m, pays €8m existing calls and receives €4m distributions. No assets change in value, and 40% of a new commitment is called. Minimum liquidity is €30m and maximum private allocation 60%. What is the binding limit?
