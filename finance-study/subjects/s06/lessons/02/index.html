Cash forecasting

Separate committed payments from uncertain receipts. Track the size and timing of forecast errors to choose an appropriate cash buffer.

The starting point

A short-term cash forecast puts expected collections and payments on specific dates. Payroll and bond interest may be known. Customer cash often arrives later than the invoice due date. Tag assumptions by certainty and identify which missing receipt could make the firm miss a payment.

Compare forecast and actual cash by week. A one-week delay produces a shortfall now and a possible recovery later. A permanently unpaid invoice lowers total cash as well. Forecast both effects and choose a buffer or committed facility sized to the shortfall the business needs to survive.

Theory and method

Liquidity-at-risk quantile and scenario funding

Cash-flow-at-risk · Peak cash shortfall

Your CFO wants enough standby funding to meet the floor in 95% of modeled days. Why might this still leave a large emergency exposure?

A treasury forecast can supply several starting and ending balances with probabilities. A 95% coverage target chooses the smallest committed resource sufficient in scenarios covering at least that probability. It says little about how severe the remaining tail can be. When one late buyer and one restricted transfer can coincide, the worst few cases may require much more funding. Show the unwatched tail alongside the quantile and verify access to the line in the very states that produce the shortfall.

How the result is built

Dₛ = max(0, B − Cₛ). L₉₅ = smallest L such that Pr(Dₛ ≤ L) ≥ 95%

Cₛ is unfinanced end-of-day cash in state s, B the required operating floor, Dₛ the euro funding needed in that state and L₉₅ the 95% funding quantile under the modeled distribution.

  1. At a €0.4m floor, state cash of €1.0m, €0.1m and −€0.6m implies shortfalls of zero, €0.3m and €1.0m. Assign probabilities of 80%, 15% and 5%.
  2. Zero covers 80% of cases. A €0.3m line covers 80% + 15% = 95%, so €0.3m is the discrete 95% quantile.
  3. In the severe 5% state another €0.7m is needed beyond that line. Expected uncovered shortfall is 5% × €0.7m = €0.035m, while the actual rare deficit is much larger.

When the reasoning holds

  • The probabilities must reflect the realistic joint timing of collections and payments. An undocumented Monte Carlo model cannot repair unrealistic independence assumptions.
  • A credit line counts only if drawable when operational funds are short. Linked covenants can make the apparent cover disappear precisely in the tail.
Terms in this lesson
Cash buffer
Money kept available to meet upcoming bills or unexpected payments.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
First example

Start Monday with €200,000. Payments of €140,000 are certain and expected customer receipts are €100,000. The base case ends at €160,000. If receipts slip a week, cash ends at €60,000. A required €80,000 minimum leaves a €20,000 stress shortfall.

Forecast what settles, not what is billed

An invoice due Monday and an expected bank receipt Monday are different pieces of information. Cash forecasting translates customer payment behavior, committed payroll, taxes and supplier terms into dated settlement estimates. Short horizons need detailed evidence for large items. Longer horizons can use operating drivers where individual payments remain unknown.

Categorize flows by certainty and who can change them. Payroll may be fixed. A dividend can sometimes be deferred subject to agreement. A customer receipt may move even after a verbal promise. Build a central forecast and a small set of internally consistent alternatives.

Compare amounts and timing of errors

Actual minus forecast cash should be traced to components. A €100,000 payment moved from Tuesday to Thursday raises Tuesday cash without improving the week’s total. A permanent customer default reduces the week and future cash. If the forecast treats a timing difference as permanent, the apparent error contaminates later decisions.

A repeated tendency to overforecast collections indicates bias. Use average signed error to see direction and average absolute error to see typical size, both calculated at a consistent horizon and sign convention. Positive and negative errors can cancel in a signed mean while leaving substantial uncertainty.

Choose liquidity for the bad date

A buffer protects the ability to make essential payments if forecast receipts disappoint. Size the buffer or standby credit against plausible shortfalls, taking account of unused line fees and the cost of idle cash. A buffer based only on average ending cash can miss a single large payment concentration.

Do not assume that many receipts are independent. A transport disruption, banking holiday or common buyer can delay several at once. Test an explicit joint downside. The needed funding is the gap at the worst date in that scenario, not the sum of every imagined deficit if some reverse.

Method

Evaluate a forecast

  1. Reconcile forecast drivers

    For each expected material cash movement, record date, amount, owner and evidence. Separate committed outflows from customer receipts whose timing can change.

  2. Roll alternative calendars

    Compute daily or weekly balances under base, late-receipt and genuine loss cases. If a payment slips, move it to its new date rather than deleting it twice.

  3. Validate and size cover

    Compare actual and prior forecasts, explain timing and amount errors, and find the largest shortfall against the operational floor before backup funding can arrive.

Worked case

A payment concentration over two days

The business starts Monday with €220,000 and requires a €50,000 floor. On Monday it expects €140,000 and must pay €250,000. Tuesday it expects €100,000 and must pay €90,000. In a stress case the Monday receipt is delayed until Tuesday.

Working

  1. Base closing cash is €110,000 Monday and €120,000 Tuesday. Both exceed the floor and the two-day net movement is −€100,000.
  2. With the delay, Monday closes at −€30,000 before funding, €80,000 below the floor. Tuesday then receives both amounts and closes at €120,000 before financing.
  3. An €80,000 standby draw available Monday preserves the floor and can be repaid Tuesday if both receipts arrive. A facility first accessible Tuesday cannot prevent the Monday shortfall.

Interpretation

The two-day ending balance is identical in both scenarios. The daily calendar reveals a liquidity need a weekly aggregate would conceal.

A common mistake

Estimating buffer needs from the variance of quarterly net cash when the essential obligations settle on particular days.

How to correct it

Use a horizon matching payment deadlines and model correlated delays. Compare the worst dated resource shortfall with funding actually drawable by then.

Quick check
A week begins with €120,000. Fixed cash payments are €100,000 and a €50,000 receipt might move to the following week. What is this week’s lowest ending cash in those two cases?

If the receipt slips, only the €100,000 payment moves cash this week. Next week may recover the delayed receipt, but this week still needs to function at €20,000.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

Three weekly receipt forecasts were €100k, €120k and €80k. Actual receipts were €70k, €130k and €60k. Use actual minus forecast. Next week starts with €90k, fixed payments €120k and minimum €30k.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate mean signed and mean absolute receipt error.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What receipts must clear next week to preserve the floor, and can the three errors alone guarantee a buffer?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Errors are −€30k, +€10k and −€20k. Mean signed error is −€13.33k, suggesting overforecast receipts in this small sample. Mean absolute error is €20k.

Part B 3 marks

Need €120k + €30k − €90k = €60k. Three historical observations cannot establish a reliable worst case. Examine concentrated receipts, date-specific stress and the availability of standby funding.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Your CFO wants enough standby funding to meet the floor in 95% of modeled days. Why might this still leave a large emergency exposure?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would quote both the coverage rule and the residual tail. A €0.3m line funds the floor on 95% of the stated distribution. On the remaining 5% of days the business still needs €0.7m beyond the line. I would identify the actual backup source for that scenario and show the cost of moving from 95% coverage to full modeled coverage.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The severe scenario probability rises from 5% to 8%, reducing only the ordinary state probability. Does the 95% line still suffice?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Ordinary is now 77% and late-customer remains 15%, so the €0.3m line covers only 92%. Under these three discrete amounts the required 95% line jumps to €1m. The sudden change reflects a discrete threshold rather than a gradual change in every cash balance.
