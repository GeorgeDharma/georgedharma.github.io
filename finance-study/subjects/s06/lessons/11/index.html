Locating transit risk and comparing delivered cost under Incoterms

Distinguish where transit risk transfers from the location to which freight is paid.

  3. Build one landed-cost bridge for both offers
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. A €2,000 invoice difference becomes a €500 landed difference
  6. A buyer compares two rail-and-road quotations
  7. Independent exam problem
  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Incoterms and landed cost

trade terms · transit risk · freight

What you should be able to explain

  • Distinguish where transit risk transfers from the location to which freight is paid.
  • Compare two delivered-cost quotations and compute the ordinary CIP insurance minimum.

Read the sale contract on two maps

An Incoterms rule allocates specified delivery obligations, costs and transit risks as part of a sale. The contract should identify the rule, the named place and the version, such as Incoterms 2020. The sales price, payment date, transfer of legal title and documentary-credit requirements require their own terms. A letter of credit can pay on complying documents while damaged goods give rise to a separate insurance or sale-contract claim.

For carriage paid to, abbreviated CPT, and carriage and insurance paid to, abbreviated CIP, distinguish the agreed delivery point from the named destination. The former is where the seller hands over the goods and transfers the risk; the latter is how far the seller pays for carriage. Record the exact carrier, place and time so the buyer knows when transport insurance must operate.

Find the party responsible for insurance

Under CPT the seller arranges carriage but has no obligation under the rule to buy cargo insurance for the buyer. The buyer can arrange its own cover. Under CIP the seller ordinarily obtains cover equivalent to Institute Cargo Clauses A for at least 110% of the contract price in the contract currency, unless the parties agree otherwise. Inspect the certificate, beneficiaries, exclusions and claims procedures.

The insured sum is a coverage limit for the agreed risks. It does not guarantee a 110% payout on any loss. Deductibles, exclusions, evidence of damage and the insurable interest of the claimant matter. Cargo insurance also protects a different event from export credit insurance, which can cover eligible nonpayment.

Build one landed-cost bridge for both offers

Start with the invoiced amount. Add only transportation legs, insurance, import costs and other charges that remain for the buyer under the quoted arrangements. Check whether unloading is already included in the carriage contract. Use supplied duty amounts or a documented customs valuation. Duties can depend on local rules beyond the quoted price.

The lowest invoice may cease to be advantageous once the buyer purchases equivalent insurance. Different cover, franchise deductibles or schedules can also make two quotes economically different even if their arithmetic costs are equal. Ask the risk team to check policy equivalence before choosing purely on price.

Method

Build the result

Comparable landed cash = invoice + uncovered carriage + buyer-funded cover + specified import and local costs; ordinary CIP minimum insured sum = 1.10 × contract price.

Amounts use the invoice currency. Uncovered carriage is the part of the journey outside the quoted freight. Buyer-funded cover means the extra premium actually paid by the buyer. The second formula assumes unmodified CIP 2020 terms.

  1. Mark the carrier handover and the paid-to destination separately. Locate any loss relative to the handover.
  2. Obtain a quote for equivalent insurance and add only charges the invoice excludes.
  3. Compare buyer cash outlay, then check insured sum and policy scope against the contract.
Worked case

A buyer compares two rail-and-road quotations

Packaging equipment is handed to the first carrier at a seller’s Hamburg depot. Both quotes pay carriage to an Ankara terminal. The CIP contract price is €124,000. The CPT quote is €122,000 and equivalent independent cover would cost €1,500. Both exclude €8,000 of supplied import charges and €3,000 of final trucking. There are no other charges.

Working

  1. CIP requires an ordinary minimum insured sum of 1.10 × €124,000 = €136,400. The seller arranges and pays for the contractual cover.
  2. CIP landed cash is €124,000 + €8,000 + €3,000 = €135,000.
  3. CPT landed cash with equivalent cover is €122,000 + €1,500 + €8,000 + €3,000 = €134,500. The difference is only €500.
  4. Damage after carrier handover but before the Ankara terminal occurs while the buyer bears transit risk in both versions. Under CIP the buyer can invoke the seller-arranged policy for a covered loss.

Decision and interpretation

On the assumed equal insurance and delivery service, CPT saves €500. The buyer should confirm coverage is active from the first-carrier handover and that the insurer accepts the route.

Quick check
Under an unmodified CIP 2020 sale, a compliant seller hands over goods to the first carrier as agreed. Who bears covered transit risk from then to the named destination?

CIP separates the early transfer of risk from the seller’s duties to pay carriage and obtain insurance to the named destination.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A CIP shipment is quoted at $210,000. A CPT shipment to the same destination is $207,000 and equivalent insurance is $4,000. Both exclude $11,000 import costs and $4,000 in final carriage. Calculate comparable cash and the ordinary CIP minimum insured sum.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. CIP landed cash is 210,000 + 11,000 + 4,000 = $225,000.
  2. CPT landed cash is 207,000 + 4,000 + 11,000 + 4,000 = $226,000. CIP is cheaper by $1,000 on the assumptions.
  3. Ordinary CIP minimum insurance is 1.10 × 210,000 = $231,000. That is a coverage amount, not an additional cash cost.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

If an exporter pays freight all the way to the buyer’s city, who bears a loss halfway there?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Under the CPT and CIP terms illustrated here, risk ordinarily transfers at delivery to the carrier at the agreed handover point, typically the first carrier. The seller pays carriage to the named destination. Under CIP it also arranges cargo insurance for the buyer’s transit risk, subject to the policy terms.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What changes if the equipment is damaged in the seller’s warehouse before it ever reaches the agreed carrier handover?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Transit risk has not yet passed under these contracts. The seller bears the loss at that point. The buyer’s policy for the later transit leg does not move the contractual delivery point.
