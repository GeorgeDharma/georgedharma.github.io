Short-term funding

Compare overdrafts, revolvers, commercial paper and internal lending. A committed source can carry an explicit fee even when no funds are drawn.

The starting point

An overdraft allows a balance below zero under its terms. A revolving facility allows repeated drawing and repayment up to a limit. Commercial paper is short-term debt issued to investors and must be repaid or refinanced when it matures. A cheaper quoted interest rate can come with greater refinancing risk.

Compare interest on amounts drawn, commitment or facility fees, access conditions and maturity on a common basis. Committed capacity can be valuable during market stress if the borrower meets the conditions. Internal group lending also depends on where cash sits and whether it can move in time.

Theory and method

All-in annualized cost of discounted commercial paper

Discount yield · Money-market yield · Usable proceeds

Two lenders quote 5%, but one deducts interest from the face amount before handing over the money. Are the rates comparable?

A company issues a short-term note and promises a fixed face amount at maturity. Buyers pay less than face today because the quoted discount is withheld at issue. Treasury therefore receives less usable money than the amount used to quote the discount rate. Fixed issue fees shrink usable money again. Compare the cash actually received and repaid over the same number of days with loan interest and any commitment fee, and separately compare access to replacement funding.

How the result is built

P = F(1 − d n/b) − A. Annualized simple funding cost = [(F − P)/P] × b/n

F is face repaid in euros, d quoted annual discount rate, n days to maturity, b agreed day-count base, A cash issue fee and P usable proceeds after discount and fee.

  1. For a €10m face note at a 5% bank discount for 90 days on a 360-day year, the discount is €0.125m. With a €0.025m fee, the issuer receives €9.85m.
  2. The issuer pays back €10m after borrowing €9.85m. Holding-period funding cost is €0.15m/€9.85m = 1.523%.
  3. Multiply by 360/90 for 6.091% annualized simple cost. A fully drawn loan at 5.8% simple with no fees over the same 90 days costs less per usable euro.

When the reasoning holds

  • Both instruments must provide the same usable principal on the same dates. A face amount of €10m does not deliver €10m usable cash under discount pricing.
  • Annualized simple cost differs from an effective compounded annual rate. The latter requires assumptions about reinvestment or repeatable refinancing.
Terms in this lesson
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
First example

A €10m revolver is drawn by €4m for a full year. Interest is 5% on the drawing and the unused €6m carries a 0.5% commitment fee. Ignoring other charges, annual cost is €200,000 + €30,000 = €230,000.

Financing the amount and the period actually needed

A seasonal importer may need money for sixty days between paying a manufacturer and receiving customer cash. Choosing a funding source requires the amount drawn, time used, rollover risk, fees and currency. A one-year facility rate and a short-term issue discount are not comparable until expressed as cash costs over the same period.

An overdraft allows an account to run below zero subject to the bank terms. A revolving line permits repeated drawing and repayment within a limit. Commercial paper is short-term debt sold to investors. Its apparent low rate may rely on issuing replacement paper when old notes mature.

Availability has a price

A committed line can charge an annual fee on unused capacity in addition to interest on drawn amounts. That fee buys standby access subject to contractual conditions. Uncommitted funding can be cheaper until markets tighten just as the company needs it. Distinguish commitment from unrestricted access under every circumstance.

The effective annual cost of a financing can exceed its quoted rate because of up-front fees, discounts or compensating balances. For a simple short-dated borrowing, calculate cash paid for the amount of usable cash actually received. Specify the 360- or 365-day basis and whether an annualized simple comparison or compounding is required.

Funding and currency must match

Borrowing internally from another entity can save external interest when cash can legally move and both sides are funded in the correct currency. It can also create an internal currency or maturity mismatch. Check the lending entity’s own payment calendar before treating its current balance as permanently available.

A foreign-currency loan with lower quoted interest may have a higher home-currency cost if its repayment currency appreciates. Compare fully hedged or clearly specified unhedged costs and the date on which any margin must be provided. Financing strength is the ability to meet payments in the required conditions.

Method

Compare funding alternatives

  1. Align the terms

    Write the required principal, actual use period, currency, day count and repayment date. Estimate total draws and unused commitments over each relevant segment.

  2. Add all cash charges

    Calculate interest on drawings, fees on the contractual base and source-specific issue or setup costs. Compare equivalent usable funding and note any separate standby value.

  3. Stress refinancing

    Model what happens if the market or internal lender cannot renew at maturity. Estimate the cost or funding shortfall that offsets the headline price advantage.

Worked case

A seasonal draw on a revolving line

An importer draws €3m for 90 days on a €5m revolver. Drawn interest is 6% yearly and unused fee is 0.6% yearly. Use 360 days, constant balances and compare costs for this quarter only. A 90-day €3m alternative costs 5% simple interest plus €9,000 fees.

Working

  1. Revolver interest is €3m × 6% × 90/360 = €45,000. The unused €2m costs €3,000 for the same period. Total quarter cost is €48,000.
  2. The alternative has €37,500 interest and €9,000 fees, total €46,500. It saves €1,500 on these matched cash requirements and assumptions.
  3. If the alternative has to be rolled and a foreseeable €3,000 standby arrangement is required only for that option, its comparable cost rises to €49,500. The committed revolver then costs €1,500 less.

Interpretation

A fee payable on the revolving line regardless of which source is drawn is different from a fee avoided by selecting the alternative. Confirm whether the comparison concerns new facilities or an existing sunk commitment.

A common mistake

Applying the unused fee to the full credit limit while also charging drawn interest on the drawn amount.

How to correct it

Read whether the fee applies to unused capacity or the whole facility. Apply the stated base and use period. Some real agreements deliberately charge on the total commitment.

Quick check
A €5m committed line is entirely undrawn for a full year. Its unused commitment fee is 0.4%, with no other fees. What does the company pay?

The charge compensates for keeping capacity available. Multiply the unused €5m by 0.004. Interest on drawn funds is zero in this scenario.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A company draws €2m against a €4m line for 60 days. Drawn interest is 5.4%, unused fee 0.9%, on a 360-day basis. A competing short loan costs €19,000 all in for the same period.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute the line cost and compare the headline alternatives.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Identify a fact that can change the ranking.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Interest is €18,000 and unused fee €3,000, making €21,000. The short loan appears €2,000 cheaper if the line fee would be avoided and usability is identical.

Part B 3 marks

If the line remains in place under either decision, its unused fee basis changes and must be modeled in both cases. Alternatively a €3,000 backup expense required only for the short loan lifts its cost to €22,000 and makes the line cheaper in the original comparison.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two lenders quote 5%, but one deducts interest from the face amount before handing over the money. Are the rates comparable?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would put both alternatives on a dated cash-flow basis. The discounted note leaves us €9.85m, not its €10m face. Repaying €10m in ninety days costs €150,000 on the actual borrowing, or about 6.09% on a 360-day simple basis. The 5.8% loan is cheaper if equally available. I would also check backup line costs and the risk that the paper market closes at maturity.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What face must the company issue to receive exactly €10m when the 5% discount and fixed €25,000 issue fee are unchanged?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Solve €10m = F × (1 − 0.05 × 90/360) − €0.025m. Face must be €10.025m/0.9875 = about €10.152m. The larger face matters for debt limits and the amount of cash that must be refinanced at maturity.
