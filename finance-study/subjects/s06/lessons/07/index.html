Documentary credit

A bank’s obligation depends on the relevant undertaking and complying documents. Distinguish a document discrepancy from a physical defect in the goods.

The starting point

A documentary credit sets out which documents a bank requires, who may present them and the deadlines. The bank examines the presentation against the credit’s terms and applicable rules. A seller can deliver excellent goods and still face a discrepancy if a required document is late or inconsistent.

Conversely, apparently complying documents do not establish that the physical goods are flawless. Buyers can manage quality through contracts or a required inspection certificate. A discrepancy may need correction or a waiver through the stated process, so the seller should not budget on automatic payment after shipment.

Theory and method

Independence principle in documentary credits

Complying presentation · Advising and confirming banks

Your cargo arrived undamaged but the bank refuses to honour the presentation. Which obligation must the exporter examine first?

An exporter, importer and banks can have different contracts concerning the same shipment. The issuing bank’s undertaking is triggered by a complying presentation under the credit’s terms and applicable rules. A confirming bank expressly adds an undertaking, while an advising bank can communicate the credit without assuming the same payment duty. The physical sale may perform perfectly while a stipulated document is missing or late. Work out the documentary deadline and required evidence before projecting cash.

How the result is built

Latest effective presentation date = earlier of credit expiry and shipment date plus stated presentation allowance

All dates follow the credit’s specified calendar, permitted presentation place and applicable rules. The shipment date is the actual qualifying transport date. An undertaking first identifies which bank has promised what payment and when.

  1. A hypothetical credit allows ten calendar days after actual shipment and expires on 30 June. Shipment on 12 June sets a presentation deadline of 22 June, assuming no holidays or special rule.
  2. List every stipulated document and the issuer capable of supplying it. A full set delivered on 24 June misses the binding deadline even if the goods arrived in good condition on 18 June.
  3. A correction, amendment or waiver needs the appropriate agreement and time. Until verified, move the bank receipt into a delayed or failed scenario and quantify funding rather than treating acceptance as automatic.

When the reasoning holds

  • Read the actual credit and any rules incorporated by reference. The ten-day period in the example is a contractual teaching assumption, not a universal deadline.
  • Independence concerns the documentary undertaking. Fraud exceptions, sanctions or local law can affect real enforcement and require the actual banking or legal analysis.
First example

A credit calls for a transport document showing shipment by 30 June. The presented document shows 2 July. Even if the buyer likes the goods, the date is a discrepancy. The exporter should identify the available correction or waiver process and update its cash forecast.

Four contracts can surround one shipment

A documentary credit sits alongside the sale contract, transport arrangements and arrangements between banks and their customers. The seller must understand the specific bank undertaking. An issuing bank promises to honour complying documents under the credit’s terms. A confirming bank adds its own undertaking when it explicitly confirms.

An advising bank can authenticate and pass on the credit without itself becoming a guarantor. Identify the applicant, beneficiary, issuing bank, confirming bank if any, the amount, required documents, presentation place, expiry and payment timing. A credit payable at a future date does not create cash at sight.

Compliance concerns the documents

The bank examines the stipulated presentation against the credit and applicable rules. An invoice, transport document, insurance evidence or inspection certificate may be required. A certificate requirement creates documentary evidence, not a bank inspection of the actual goods. The underlying buyer can retain ordinary contract remedies for defects.

Strict calendar and wording conditions can make timely performance difficult. Compare shipment deadline with the latest date documents can be issued, the presentation period after shipment and the expiry. If dates contradict realistic operations, request an amendment before shipment rather than relying on a friendly buyer to waive problems later.

What happens when documents differ?

A discrepancy may result in refusal or a request for a waiver through the relevant process. Correction can work if the factual record supports it and the corrected document can still be presented in time. Documents must accurately describe events. Changing a true late shipment date on paper would misrepresent performance.

A buyer’s willingness to accept the goods does not automatically amend the bank’s undertaking. Confirm whether a valid waiver or amendment has been obtained and from whom. In cash planning, model delayed payment, finance cost and fallback recovery rather than treating an uncertain waiver as cleared cash.

Method

Prepare a presentation matrix

  1. Work backward from expiry

    List exact dates for latest shipment, allowed presentation period, expiry and payment. The binding last presentation date is the earliest relevant limit under the case terms.

  2. Assign each document

    For every required record identify issuer, data, signature and delivery lead time. Compare names, currency, shipment facts and permitted tolerances across the presentation.

  3. Manage a discrepancy

    Separate a correctable documentation error from a real performance problem. Identify possible amendment or waiver, estimate cash delay and preserve alternative rights under the sale contract.

Worked case

A compliant shipment and a late presentation

A hypothetical credit requires shipment by 10 June, presentation within seven calendar days after shipment, and expiry on 20 June. Shipment occurs on 8 June. Accurate transport evidence arrives 13 June, but the exporter presents its complete documents on 16 June. No holiday rule applies.

Working

  1. The seven-day deadline following 8 June is 15 June. This binds before the 20 June expiry. The shipment met 10 June, but presentation on 16 June is late under the stated terms.
  2. The documents were available 13 June. Earlier submission by 15 June could have protected compliance if all other requirements were met. The expiry date alone did not grant until 20 June.
  3. Assume €300,000 payment is delayed thirty days while a waiver is sought and the exporter funds the gap at 6% on a 360-day basis. Incremental financing cost is €1,500, before any risk of eventual nonpayment.

Interpretation

The next shipment needs a monitored presentation timetable. A fresh contractual amendment could address recurring deadlines but cannot be assumed to have cured this existing discrepancy.

A common mistake

Using the credit expiry as the sole deadline and treating a successful physical delivery as proof the bank must pay.

How to correct it

Check every documentary condition and the earliest applicable presentation date. Address physical defects under the separate sale arrangements.

Quick check
A bank receives the documents required by the credit, and the presentation complies. The buyer later reports a concealed defect in the goods. What distinction matters?

The bank’s undertaking concerns the complying presentation. An ordinary quality dispute under the sale contract requires its own evidence and remedy under the assumed terms.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A credit requires shipment by 12 September, presentation within five calendar days after actual shipment, and expiry on 18 September. Shipment occurs 11 September. Complete documents are ready 17 September. No holiday exceptions apply.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Identify the binding presentation deadline and the problem.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What legitimate action and cash consequence should treasury consider?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Five days after 11 September is 16 September, earlier than 18 September. The goods shipped on time but the documents were not ready until a day after the operative deadline.

Part B 3 marks

Seek any valid amendment or waiver through the bank process, and do not assume it is automatic. Forecast delayed or failed receipt and bridge the actual gap. The truthful shipment facts must remain unchanged.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Your cargo arrived undamaged but the bank refuses to honour the presentation. Which obligation must the exporter examine first?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would read the issuing or confirming bank’s documentary undertaking first. With shipment on 12 June and a ten-day presentation period, the operative date is 22 June. Complete documents on 24 June miss it despite timely goods. I would identify the actual discrepancy, seek a legitimate amendment or waiver if available, and update treasury’s cash forecast while retaining the separate rights under the sale contract.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The bank tells you it only advised the credit. The issuer in the buyer’s country is unable to remit. What additional fact would materially improve your expected recovery?

Compare with the follow-up answer

I would establish whether a solvent bank expressly added confirmation covering this credit and presentation, and whether the stated conditions were satisfied. Simply advising the credit does not add an equivalent payment undertaking. I would also check maturity, transfer restrictions and alternative remedies against the buyer.
