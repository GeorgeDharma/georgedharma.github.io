Governance and performance

Assign dealing, confirmation and payment authority. Evaluate a hedge against its stated objective and the underlying exposure as a whole.

The starting point

Treasury controls divide the authority to enter a trade, confirm it independently and release a payment. A dealer who books foreign currency and can also approve settlement could conceal an error. Independent confirmation compares the trade with the counterparty’s terms and authorized limits.

Evaluate the hedge and its underlying invoice together against the objective set in advance. If the goal was to protect the budget exchange rate, a derivative loss can occur alongside a larger euro value on the invoice. Report the combined cash outcome, fees, remaining exposure and compliance with authority.

Theory and method

Treasury FX budget variance attribution

Volume variance · Unhedged rate variance · Execution slippage

A CFO reports a €100,000 FX budget miss. How would you identify whether the cause was lost orders, a deliberately open position or execution?

A budget assumes a quantity of foreign cash and a conversion rate. Actual sales can differ before treasury even trades. The hedge changes conversion of the covered portion, while uncovered receipts still move with spot. A variance bridge first restates the budget at actual commercial volume, then explains how actual execution rates and remaining spot exposure changed euros. Independent confirmation, delegated limits and bank reconciliation address the separate question of whether trades were authorized and settled correctly.

How the result is built

Actual minus budget = (A − Q)b + H(k − b) + (A − H)(x − b) − F

Q is budget foreign volume, A actual net foreign volume, H forward-sold amount, b budget rate, k actual forward rate, x settlement spot rate, all rates in euros per foreign unit, and F euro fees. The cash result is in euros.

  1. Begin with actual cash Hk + (A − H)x − F and subtract budget Qb. Add and subtract Ab and Hb to isolate volume, covered-rate and uncovered-rate effects.
  2. Budget US$1m at €0.92. Actual US$0.9m gives a −€92,000 volume change. US$0.6m covered at €0.94 adds €12,000 relative to budget. The US$0.3m open part at €0.88 loses €12,000.
  3. Deduct €3,000 fees. Total variance is −€95,000 and actual cash €825,000 against €920,000 plan. Relative to actual-volume budget €828,000, the residual treasury result is −€3,000.

When the reasoning holds

  • The budget and actual volumes must refer to the same net exposure and dates. A receivable delayed into a new period should be identified before labeling it a lost sale.
  • The covered-rate component includes market movement between budget and hedge execution. A separate executable quote at the trade timestamp is needed to identify dealer slippage.
Terms in this lesson
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Counterparty
The other party to a financial contract or trade.
Derivative
A contract whose value depends on something else, such as a currency, an interest rate or a share price.
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
First example

A company budgeted €900,000 for a dollar receipt. On settlement the receipt converts to €940,000 and its currency hedge loses €40,000. Ignoring fees, the combined amount is €900,000. The policy met its cash target even though the derivative alone had a loss.

Keep dealing authority separate from payment authority

Treasury staff can commit large amounts through a small number of trades. Separation of duties divides trade initiation, independent confirmation, settlement approval and reconciliation. If one individual can initiate a trade and redirect its settlement, a wrong amount or unauthorized counterparty change may pass without independent review.

Small organizations may have fewer people, but the same exposure can be controlled through separate authorization, bank-side dual release, independent call-back to a known counterparty contact and timely reconciliation. Controls should correspond to actual payment and market risks, not simply the existence of a signed policy.

Define performance before trading

A treasury team may be asked to protect a budget exchange rate, maintain a liquidity floor, reduce earnings variability or minimize funding cost subject to risk limits. These objectives imply different benchmarks. The quality of a hedge cannot be judged solely by whether the derivative made money in isolation.

For a budget comparison, measure underlying converted cash plus derivative settlement less fees against the target for the actual covered exposure. Separate volume or price changes in the commercial business from hedge execution or coverage decisions. An apparently poor result may be attributable to a smaller shipment beyond treasury’s control.

Independent confirmation and exception review

A back office compares the recorded trade with the counterparty’s independent confirmation, checking amount, direction, currency, dates and settlement instructions. A reconciler matches bank cash with the authorized trade and investigates differences. Independent price verification can reveal an incorrect mark even if the cash has not settled.

A policy breach and a financial loss are separate outcomes. A profitable oversized speculative trade can breach authority. A properly authorized hedge can produce a derivative loss while achieving its combined cash target. Report objectives, positions, cash performance, limits, exceptions and responsibility separately.

Method

Reconstruct a hedge review

  1. Anchor the mandate

    Identify the exposure, approved percentage, allowed instrument, date and combined benchmark documented before trading. Distinguish transaction risks from forecast volume and market execution.

  2. Build the performance bridge

    Show cash on the underlying, derivative, premiums or fees and resulting combined cash. Reconcile budget volume, actual volume and any rate effect so the comparison is meaningful.

  3. Check control evidence

    Match authorization, counterparty confirmation, payment and bank reconciliation. Escalate policy breaches and forecast changes even if the combined position happened to make money.

Worked case

A budget target and a volume change

A euro exporter budgets US$1m at €0.90 per dollar, targeting €900,000. It sells US$800,000 forward at €0.91. Actual sales are US$900,000, spot is €0.85 and fees are €3,000. The mandate permits up to 85% of the currently forecast net receipt.

Working

  1. Actual combined cash is US$800,000 × €0.91 + US$100,000 × €0.85 − €3,000 = €810,000. The original €900,000 target is missed by €90,000.
  2. At the budget rate, the actual US$900,000 volume is worth €810,000. On that volume-adjusted benchmark treasury exactly matches budget after fees. The €90,000 difference from the original plan is explained by lower sales volume.
  3. Original cover was 80% and within the 85% limit. After the forecast fell to US$900,000, the same forward covered 88.9%. Performance matches the adjusted budget but the policy exception requires review under this mandate.

Interpretation

Independent confirmation and an updated sales forecast address different controls. A correctly confirmed trade can become oversized after the underlying business changes.

A common mistake

Treating a profitable derivative as evidence both that the hedge policy succeeded and that the trade stayed within delegated authority.

How to correct it

Test combined cash against a volume-adjusted objective, then test limits and approvals separately. A good financial outcome cannot retroactively authorize a transaction.

Quick check
An exporter targeted €500,000. The shipment produces €470,000 after conversion, its hedge produces €35,000, and fees are €3,000. What is the appropriate combined outcome?

Combine €470,000 of underlying cash and €35,000 from the hedge, then subtract €3,000 of fees. This measures the budget objective on the full exposure.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

Treasury hedged US$400,000 at €0.92 when expected receipts were US$500,000. Actual receipts fall to US$450,000. Spot is €0.84, fees €2,000, and the budget rate is €0.90. Policy caps hedges at 85% of the latest forecast.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute combined cash and compare it with the volume-adjusted budget.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Assess policy compliance before and after the volume change and state an appropriate control response.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Combined cash is €368,000 + €42,000 − €2,000 = €408,000. Actual-volume budget is US$450,000 × €0.90 = €405,000. The result is €3,000 above budget.

Part B 3 marks

Original coverage was 80%, within the limit. Revised coverage is 400,000/450,000 = 88.9%, exceeding 85%. Report the exception, independently verify the forecast and seek the authorized adjustment or risk decision.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A CFO reports a €100,000 FX budget miss. How would you identify whether the cause was lost orders, a deliberately open position or execution?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would calculate actual combined cash, then bridge from the original budget using actual volume. Here fewer orders explain €92,000 of the €95,000 miss. Covered and uncovered exchange-rate effects offset, leaving €3,000 of fees. I would compare the trader’s rate with a contemporaneous executable benchmark to assess execution and independently verify policy compliance rather than attributing the sales shortfall to treasury.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The authorized executable forward quote was €0.945 per dollar when the desk filled at €0.940 on US$0.6m. What slippage can you identify?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The desk received €0.005 less per dollar than the comparison quote. On US$600,000 that is €3,000 adverse execution slippage. It is a breakdown of the existing covered-rate component, not an extra €3,000 to deduct again from actual cash. Verify the quote was executable for the same terms and size.
