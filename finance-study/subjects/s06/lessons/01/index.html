Cash positions

Reconcile bank and ledger balances by currency, entity and date. A consolidated balance may include money that cannot be transferred when needed.

The starting point

The ledger records the company’s view of its bank balance. The bank statement records transactions the bank has processed. Reconcile the two for a common date and currency, identifying payments still in transit, deposits awaiting credit and unrecorded fees.

For funding decisions use cash that can actually settle the obligation on time. Balances belonging to different subsidiaries, pledged cash or funds in a different currency may need approvals or a conversion before use. Consolidated cash can therefore exceed cash available for a particular payroll.

Theory and method

Bank reconciliation and value-dated cash positioning

Book balance · Available clearing balance

A group treasurer sees €1.2m on the ledger and €1.0m on the bank statement. Which can be committed to a payment this afternoon?

A retail company deposits cash and issues supplier payments around a bank cutoff. The books reflect instructions already issued while the statement reflects items the bank processed. An accountant first reconciles the two records to find genuine discrepancies. The treasurer then asks a different operational question about value-dated, transferable money by the new payment deadline. Cash can be correctly recognized in a ledger while unavailable to fund another transfer today.

How the result is built

Adjusted book = reported book + bank-only credits − bank-only debits. Adjusted bank = statement + deposits in transit − outstanding issued payments

All fields are euros for the same legal entity, bank account and timestamp. A bank-only item has cleared but is absent from the ledger. Transit and outstanding items explain reconciliation timing.

  1. A bank statement shows €1.00m, while €0.30m customer deposits are in transit and €0.12m issued supplier payments have not cleared. Adjusted bank is €1.18m.
  2. The ledger shows €1.20m but omits a €0.02m bank fee. Adjusted book is €1.18m as well. The reconciliation ties even though the bank cannot yet release the in-transit deposits.
  3. For a new same-day payment, reserve €0.12m for issued instructions and exclude €0.10m pledged cash from the €1m bank figure. The new available amount is €0.78m, assuming no other clearings or floors.

When the reasoning holds

  • The fee is already in the bank figure. Deducting it again in the availability schedule would understate cash.
  • Availability uses the paying entity and actual settlement deadline. A later deposit or a foreign subsidiary’s funds cannot solve the immediate shortfall until usable.
Terms in this lesson
Foreign exchange (FX)
The conversion of one currency into another. Always state how the rate is quoted.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
First example

A subsidiary’s bank shows €800,000. It has already issued €90,000 of payments that the bank has yet to process. A further €150,000 is pledged and unavailable. With no other adjustments, cash the treasurer can spend without using pledged funds or missing the issued payments is €560,000.

A balance has an owner, date and currency

Treasury needs to know what the paying entity can use before a settlement deadline. A positive amount in a foreign subsidiary’s bank account contributes to consolidated cash but does not pay a head-office supplier until a permitted transfer or conversion reaches the right account. Record the legal owner alongside every balance.

Distinguish the date a transaction was entered, booked by the bank and value-dated for available funds or interest. A bank may show a deposit that is still uncleared or a transfer already instructed that has yet to appear. Identify which convention the balance report and the payment deadline use.

Reconcile ledger and bank in both directions

Start from the bank statement. Deduct issued payments still outstanding and add deposits in transit to reconcile to adjusted book cash when appropriate. On the book side record missing bank charges or interest. Differences explained by a legitimate timing item need tracking until the bank eventually clears them.

Reconciliation and availability are different. A deposit in transit can help reconcile an accounting balance while still being unavailable today. A payment already debited by the bank should not be deducted again as an outstanding instruction. Identify who controls each adjustment and its evidence.

Translate for reporting without losing the operational view

Convert currency balances to one reporting currency for a group overview using explicit exchange quotations. Keep original-currency amounts as well. A group with euro-equivalent net cash can have a dollar payment shortage if FX trading or settlement has passed its cutoff.

Cash may be restricted by security pledges, regulatory requirements, customer ownership or agreement. Restrictions are specific facts, not universal rules for a country or currency. Mark dates for release, transfer approval and settlement to distinguish temporary inaccessibility from permanently excluded cash.

Method

Calculate spendable cash

  1. Anchor the ledger

    Reconcile bank and book balances at a common timestamp and legal entity. Track payments in transit, deposits in transit and unbooked bank-only entries separately.

  2. Test accessibility

    Identify amounts pledged, uncleared or outside the currency and account required. Count a transfer only if approvals, FX dealing and settlement can occur before the obligation.

  3. Forecast after settlement

    Subtract unavoidable outgoing payments and a minimum operating buffer. Show any remaining funding need even if consolidated cash translated into euros looks ample.

Worked case

A bilingual cash report misses payroll funding

A French entity’s bank shows €700,000. It has issued €90,000 of supplier payments not yet debited and €100,000 is pledged. A €70,000 customer deposit appears in its ledger but clears tomorrow. A US subsidiary has US$330,000 at US$1.10 per euro, inaccessible by today’s deadline.

Working

  1. Free French cash for new uses today is €700,000 − €90,000 − €100,000 = €510,000. The incoming €70,000 is excluded because it is uncleared by the deadline.
  2. The subsidiary’s balance is €300,000 on the reporting basis, but contributes zero to French settlement today. Do not add it to available payroll cash merely because group statements use euros.
  3. The payroll bill is €530,000 and treasury requires €20,000 after payment. Additional accessible funding must be €530,000 + €20,000 − €510,000 = €40,000.

Interpretation

Tomorrow’s clearing could help repay overnight funding if no other commitments intervene. The operational problem is a €40,000 same-day shortfall, not absence of money across the whole group.

A common mistake

Adding a ledger deposit as an available bank credit before its value date while ignoring an issued payment waiting to clear.

How to correct it

Reconcile both differences first, then prepare a separate availability measure for the specific obligation, currency, entity and deadline.

Quick check
Tomorrow’s French payroll is €400,000. The French entity has €300,000 free and another entity has €250,000 that cannot be transferred by tomorrow. What is the immediate payroll funding gap?

Only €300,000 is available to settle that entity’s €400,000 payroll by the deadline. The restriction changes timing even though both balances appear in the group accounts.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A bank shows €480,000 with €60,000 issued payments still outstanding and €50,000 pledged. A €40,000 deposit clears tomorrow. Today’s invoice is €390,000 and treasury must retain €25,000.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate free cash and funding needed today.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Does the €40,000 deposit change the answer if it clears before the invoice deadline instead?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Available cash is €480,000 − €60,000 − €50,000 = €370,000. Required resources are €415,000, so €45,000 additional same-day funding is needed.

Part B 3 marks

Yes. If verified as cleared, accessible cash becomes €410,000 and the gap falls to €5,000. Its entry in the ledger alone is insufficient evidence of that change.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A group treasurer sees €1.2m on the ledger and €1.0m on the bank statement. Which can be committed to a payment this afternoon?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would reconcile the records before deciding how much to commit. The bank and books both explain to €1.18m after timing and the bank fee. But only €0.78m is free for a new same-day transfer because €0.30m has not cleared, earlier payment instructions must still be honoured, and some cash is pledged. I would confirm the bank cutoff and any minimum operating buffer.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The bank confirms €0.20m of the deposit has now cleared and is value-dated today. The business must retain €0.05m. What can be committed?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Available new-payment cash rises from €0.78m to €0.98m when the €0.20m clears. Retaining the €0.05m operating floor leaves €0.93m. Check that the live bank balance reflects this clearing and that it has not also been entered as a forecast receipt.
