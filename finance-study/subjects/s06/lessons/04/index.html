Concentration and netting

Group cash pooling and net settlement can reduce external funding. Legal-entity restrictions, local rules and settlement timing can limit those benefits.

The starting point

Cash concentration moves or offsets balances across group entities so the group can reduce external borrowing. Payment netting offsets amounts that participants owe one another and settles only the remaining balances. One manages cash balances; the other reduces the number and size of payments.

Write down who owes whom, in which currency and on what date. Offsetting two invoices due on different dates can leave an interim funding gap. Transfers, pooling and set-off also depend on the contract, legal entities and local restrictions assumed in the case.

Theory and method

Cash-pool interest offset and legal-entity concentration

Physical cash sweep · Netting benefit · Intercompany funding

One subsidiary earns deposit interest while another pays on its overdraft. Is shifting the entire positive balance always valuable?

A subsidiary with cash might lend internally to another that borrows from the bank. On the amount eligible to offset, the group avoids the external borrowing rate but gives up deposit interest. The benefit stops once the deficit has been covered or transferable cash runs out. Physical pooling also creates intercompany claims requiring documentation and access on the withdrawal date. A consolidated net cash position alone cannot establish the amount the treasurer can mobilize.

How the result is built

Net saving = min(S,D) × (rᵦ − rᵈ) × t − F

S is legally transferable surplus after local buffer, D offsettable borrowing, rᵦ borrowing rate, rᵈ deposit rate, t the year fraction for which the offset persists, and F incremental cash-pool expenses. Amounts are euros.

  1. A subsidiary has €5m cash but €1m must stay for payroll, so S is €4m. Another affiliate borrows €3m at 7% while deposits earn 2%. The amount able to replace borrowing is €3m.
  2. Over six months the gross benefit is €3m × (7% − 2%) × 0.5 = €75,000. A €15,000 incremental fee leaves €60,000 group saving.
  3. Internal interest paid between affiliates reallocates that saving between them. It cancels before tax in the consolidated cash comparison, but local terms, taxes and legal obligations still require analysis.

When the reasoning holds

  • Surplus must be accessible in the appropriate currency by the borrowing deadline. Exchange, local restrictions or payout obligations may lower S.
  • Borrowing and deposit rates must be the marginal rates actually avoided or forgone. A fixed bank interest charge that survives repayment cannot be treated as saved.

The conversion of one currency into another. Always state how the rate is quoted.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Payable
Money a business owes a supplier for goods or services already received.
Counterparty
The other party to a financial contract or trade.
First example

On the same day A owes B €800,000 and B owes A €550,000. If their agreement permits bilateral netting in euros, A pays B €250,000. Gross transfers of €1.35m are replaced by one smaller payment. The underlying economic difference remains €250,000.

Payments and balances are different

Netting offsets amounts counterparties owe one another and settles the remaining obligations. A pooling arrangement mobilizes or offsets cash balances across group entities. The first can reduce transfers and bank charges even when it creates no new cash. The second can reduce external interest by matching one entity’s surplus with another’s deficit.

Bilateral netting offsets two counterparties’ eligible obligations. Multilateral netting combines a network of participating entities under agreed rules. Each entity’s net position is its total receivables from the network minus its payables to the network. Positive positions receive cash, negative positions supply it.

What makes a settlement eligible?

Amounts need compatible settlement dates, currencies or agreed conversion terms and enforceable rules for offset. A disputed invoice, restricted local payment or party outside the arrangement may need separate settlement. Do not offset across legal persons unilaterally merely because the ultimate group owns them.

If balances in different currencies are converted into a common settlement currency, specify the FX rate, timing and who bears the interval risk. If two invoices mature days apart, waiting to net can create interim funding, late-payment or counterparty risk. Consider those effects alongside lower transaction fees.

How cash pooling can change external cost

Physical pooling moves funds, often creating intercompany claims between participants and the pool leader. Notional arrangements can calculate interest on net balances without identical physical transfers if the bank and local arrangements permit. The legal and operational arrangements determine what is accessible in a stress.

Measure interest saved by replacing simultaneous expensive borrowing and low-yield deposits, then deduct bank, operational and tax costs supplied in the case. A local currency balance subject to restriction might still earn deposit interest without being usable against another entity’s loan in an emergency.

Method

Net a payment cycle

  1. Define the participants

    List each enforceable invoice with payer, receiver, currency and due date. Exclude disputed or ineligible items before calculating balances.

  2. Calculate net positions

    For every participant add eligible receipts and subtract eligible payments. Check that positive and negative positions sum to zero for the closed network.

  3. Verify settlement and savings

    Schedule actual transfers and compare aggregate gross amounts, fees and deadlines. For pooling, compute effective interest and check whether a physical transfer is possible when it matters.

Worked case

Three affiliate invoices

On the same permitted euro settlement date A owes B €100,000, B owes C €70,000 and C owes A €40,000. No invoices are disputed. The netting centre settles all eligible amounts and charges €150 per outgoing instruction in this case.

Working

  1. A’s net is €40,000 − €100,000 = −€60,000. B’s net is €100,000 − €70,000 = +€30,000. C’s net is €70,000 − €40,000 = +€30,000.
  2. A pays €60,000, allocated €30,000 each to B and C. Count two direct outgoing transfers versus three gross transfers. Gross direct transfers total €210,000. Direct net settlement transfers total €60,000.
  3. Under the stipulated direct payment and fee convention the outgoing instruction charge falls from €450 to €300, saving €150. A centre using a different payment architecture may produce a different instruction count.

Interpretation

Netting replaces reciprocal settlement flows, not the economic trade or the underlying accounting for sales. Do not describe the €150,000 lower payment volume as €150,000 of profit.

A common mistake

Adding all positive and negative net positions in absolute value to measure the cash paid, thereby counting each euro twice.

How to correct it

Total paid in the closed network equals the sum of positive receipts, equivalently the absolute sum of negative positions. Use one side for the transferred amount.

Quick check
Under enforceable same-day, same-currency netting, a branch owes a partner €90,000 and is owed €110,000 by it. What settlement clears both obligations?

The branch’s incoming claim exceeds its outgoing obligation by €20,000. Settlement of the net amount replaces the two gross transfers.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

On the same date X owes Y €80k, Y owes Z €50k and Z owes X €20k. All are eligible for direct multilateral settlement.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute each entity’s net and the amount of cash actually transferred.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    If the €20k Z-to-X invoice is not due until next week, what changes today?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

X is −€60k, Y +€30k and Z +€30k. X pays €60k in total, versus €150k of gross invoices. Receipts and payments reconcile.

Part B 3 marks

Exclude it today. X must pay €80k, Y receives a net €30k and Z €50k. Next week Z pays X €20k separately unless another eligible offset exists. The timing changes current funding needs.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

One subsidiary earns deposit interest while another pays on its overdraft. Is shifting the entire positive balance always valuable?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would identify the smaller of truly available surplus and the deficit that can be repaid. The group here can offset €3m and save a five-point annual spread for six months, giving €75,000 before pool costs and €60,000 after them. I would document the intercompany claim, local buffer and return arrangements before moving money, especially if the surplus entity needs it back soon.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A local restriction limits transferable money to €0.5m for the full period. The €15,000 pool fee remains. Is the initiative worthwhile on interest alone?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Gross saving is €0.5m × 5% × 0.5 = €12,500. After €15,000 fixed cost the group loses €2,500. It might still gain payment efficiency or resilience, but those benefits need separate evidence and valuation.
