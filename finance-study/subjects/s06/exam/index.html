Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

Free cash at a payment deadline

At the euro treasury entity the bank shows €600,000. €80,000 issued payments are not yet debited and €100,000 is pledged. A €70,000 deposit in transit clears tomorrow. Today’s payroll and invoices require €480,000, and the firm needs €40,000 afterward. A revolver is ready to draw in time. Compare two ways to fund exactly the required amount for 30 days. The €200,000 revolver charges 6% on the borrowing and 0.6% on the unused commitment. The alternative short loan costs 5% plus a flat fee of €200. Use a 360-day basis and assume the entire commitment can be avoided if the loan is chosen.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

The exporter’s bank document gap

An exporter invoices €300,000. Sixty-day open account would cost 6% annual funding on the full invoice, 360-day basis, with 2% chance of customer default and 50% loss if it defaults. A sight documentary credit costs €5,000. Credit terms require shipment by 12 May, presentation within seven calendar days after shipment and expiry 22 May. The exporter ships on 10 May and presents 18 May. No holiday adjustment applies. A factor offers an 80% advance if an eligible receivable is approved.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Hedge performance and an unmatched volume

A euro company forecasts US$1.4m receipts and US$0.4m payments on the same permitted settlement day. Budget rate is €0.88 per dollar. Policy permits selling up to 80% of current net receipts forward. The company sells US$0.8m forward at €0.90. Receipts later fall to US$1.1m, payments stay unchanged, spot is €0.82 and fees are €2,000. Assume the company buys any dollar shortfall at spot.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

A physical hedge creates a cash deadline

A copper exporter shorts futures on 4,000 tonnes. Futures rise €55 per tonne before shipment, while the physical cash price rises €48 per tonne. Variation margin is due today. The exporter has €190,000 free cash and €90,000 immediately drawable credit. Payroll is €75,000 today and the operating floor is €30,000. The physical sale settles next month. Assume no fees or prior margin amounts.

Case 1 · 10 marks

Free cash at a payment deadline

Part A 3 marks

Compute free cash and required draw.

€600,000 − €80,000 − €100,000 = €420,000 accessible (1). Today’s requirement is €520,000 including the floor (1). Draw €100,000. The €70,000 deposit is unavailable until tomorrow (1).

Part B 4 marks

Compare all-in 30-day funding costs.

Revolver interest €100,000 × 6% × 30/360 = €500 (1). Unused fee €100,000 × 0.6% × 30/360 = €50 (1). Total €550 (1). Short loan costs about €416.67 + €200 = €616.67, so the revolver leads by €66.67 (1).

Part C 3 marks

Tomorrow’s receipt is cancelled instead of delayed. How should the forecast and recommendation change?

Remove it from tomorrow and later forecasts until collection can be supported (1). Model how long the €100,000 needs financing, with interest and rollover beyond 30 days (1). Verify line conditions and timing for further payments. A temporary reimbursement plan is no longer supported by that deposit (1).

Case 2 · 10 marks

The exporter’s bank document gap

Part A 3 marks

Compare expected open-account and assumed compliant sight-credit net receipts before production costs.

Funding costs €3,000 (1). Expected credit loss equals €300,000 × 2% × 50% = €3,000 (1). Open account nets €294,000 versus €295,000 with a compliant credit, a €1,000 sight-credit advantage (1).

Part B 4 marks

Does this presentation meet the timing terms? A waiver delays payment by 30 days. Quantify the effect at 6% on €300,000.

Latest valid presentation was 17 May, before the 22 May expiry (1). It was one day late, despite on-time shipment. A waiver is not automatic (1). Thirty-day financing costs €1,500 (1). If a valid waiver obtains and this is the only extra cost, sight-credit net falls to €293,500, €500 below the open-account estimate (1).

Part C 3 marks

What can the factor supply initially, and what risk details must treasury verify?

If approved, advance is 80% × €300,000 = €240,000 (1). Verify whether the discrepancy makes the claim ineligible (1), and whether recourse, reserve release or remaining credit/dispute risk leaves the seller liable (1).

Case 3 · 10 marks

Hedge performance and an unmatched volume

Part A 3 marks

Find the original and revised commercial dollar exposure and hedge percentage.

Original net is US$1m and the hedge is 80%, exactly at the cap (1). Revised net is US$0.7m (1), so hedge coverage becomes 0.8/0.7 = 114.29%, outside policy (1).

Part B 4 marks

Calculate combined euro cash and compare with the budget at actual net volume.

The forward delivers €720,000 for US$800,000 (1). Buy the unmatched US$100,000 for €82,000 (1). Net is €720,000 − €82,000 − €2,000 = €636,000 (1). Actual-volume budget is €616,000, so cash is €20,000 ahead (1).

Part C 3 marks

Explain why the favourable result does not close the control issue and identify two separate controls.

A favourable spot move on an unmatched position does not authorize exceeding the cap (1). Update and independently validate the exposure forecast and escalate for an authorized adjustment (1). Independently confirm the derivative and verify settlement directions, amount and bank reconciliation (1).

Case 4 · 10 marks

A physical hedge creates a cash deadline

Part A 3 marks

Compute price effects on futures and physical stock and the remaining economic mismatch.

Short futures lose €220,000 (1). Physical stock gains €192,000 (1). Combined price result is −€28,000 because prices moved differently (1).

Part B 4 marks

Compute same-day liquidity needs and the shortfall. Why is it different from the economic mismatch?

Current need is €220,000 + €75,000 + €30,000 = €325,000 (1). Cash and confirmed credit total €280,000 (1). Shortfall is €45,000 (1). The physical value gain produces no cash by today’s margin deadline (1).

Part C 3 marks

Another €50,000 supplier bill is moved forward to today and shipment is delayed a week. Revise the gap and identify a practical decision.

The dated gap rises to €95,000 before other changes (1). Delay can defer repayment of bridging funds and permit further calls (1). Confirm an additional drawable line or negotiated payment timing before the deadline and stress further margin rather than counting next month’s sale as free cash (1).
