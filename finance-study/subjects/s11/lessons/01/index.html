Public-sector perimeter

Determine whether the figures cover central government or the wider public sector. Guarantees and state-owned companies can create exposures outside headline debt.

The starting point

When comparing two countries' debt, first ask whose borrowings appear in the number. A central-government figure may exclude municipalities, social security funds or public corporations. A wider perimeter includes more entities, and consolidation removes obligations owed between entities inside that perimeter.

Also distinguish gross debt from debt net of specified assets, and current obligations from guarantees that pay only if a trigger occurs. A guarantee can create fiscal exposure without yet becoming a recorded government loan. Note the date and units before comparing countries.

Theory and method

Deficit–debt adjustment

Stock–flow reconciliation · Public-sector consolidation

A government reports a deficit of 4bn but gross debt rises 11bn. What bridge would you request before claiming the accounts conflict?

A treasury can borrow money to finance public spending and also to buy a financial asset. Currency depreciation can revalue existing foreign debt without any new borrowing. Bringing an enterprise into the reporting perimeter can change the stock at the reporting date. A deficit–debt reconciliation starts with comparable consolidated opening and closing stock, then assigns the difference to the deficit and identified stock–flow adjustments. A large adjustment calls for explanation even when the arithmetic balances.

How the result is built

D₁ − D₀ = Def + A + V + O

D₀ and D₁ consolidated gross debt at comparable dates, Def overall deficit entered positive, A net financial-asset acquisition financed by debt, V debt valuation changes, and O remaining perimeter or other stock adjustments, in billions of local currency.

  1. Start at 90bn, add the reported deficit of 4bn, and identify 3bn new borrowing deposited in a state liquidity account. Gross debt rises while a financial asset also rises.
  2. A foreign principal revaluation adds 2bn. Classifying an entity within the perimeter adds another 2bn of external borrowing after removing reciprocal public claims.
  3. Closing debt is 90 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 2 = 101bn. The 7bn stock–flow adjustment reconciles the change without rewriting the reported spending deficit.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use a common perimeter and valuation basis for each flow, except where an explicit quantified perimeter adjustment bridges the change.
  • The meaning and sign of asset transactions depend on the deficit definition. Avoid adding an item already included in the published overall deficit.
Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
First example

A central government owes €90bn, including €5bn held by a municipality. The municipality owes outside lenders €20bn. Together these two entities have €105bn of consolidated debt to outside holders, calculated as €90bn + €20bn − €5bn.

A debt figure needs a boundary

Central government usually covers a narrower institutional group than general government, which may also include subnational entities and social funds. A still broader public-sector view can include certain public corporations. A debt claim can be outside one perimeter and inside another without its underlying contract changing.

Classification is about the institutional units and definition in the dataset. Ownership or a government guarantee alone does not automatically identify how a borrowing is recorded. Reconcile borrower, guarantor and current legal obligation before adding an exposure to a headline stock.

Consolidation and valuation

Combining units requires removing reciprocal holdings among them. If a public pension fund holds a central-government bond, one covered unit's liability is another's asset. It remains relevant to the individual units but cancels as an internal claim in a consolidated debt-to-outsiders measure.

Face value, market value and amounts payable at maturity can differ after market yields move. Compare values on the same basis. Net debt further deducts specified financial assets. An illiquid equity holding is distinct from immediately available cash and may not qualify under a chosen definition.

Guarantees and stock–flow adjustments

A contingent guarantee depends on a future trigger. The guaranteed face amount, expected payout, maximum payout and actual borrowing incurred after a trigger are separate quantities. Report potentially material guarantees alongside recorded debt when they are outside its definition.

A rise in debt beyond the deficit can reflect currency revaluation, financial asset purchases, recognition of old obligations or a newly consolidated enterprise. These stock–flow adjustments can be economically significant without representing current primary spending.

Method

Reconciling a public-sector figure

  1. Set a perimeter and unit

    State the entities, date, currency and valuation basis, then list the liabilities of each included unit.

  2. Remove internal claims

    Match who owes whom and remove claims completely internal to the selected group exactly once.

  3. Describe residual exposure

    Show outside guarantees, excluded entities and deductible liquid assets separately, indicating which would change the headline figure upon a trigger.

Worked case

A pension fund holds state bonds

Central government reports €90bn gross debt and municipalities €20bn. The included public pension fund holds €7bn of central bonds. Municipalities hold another €3bn of central bonds. Assume no other internal holdings and no debt issued by the pension fund itself.

Working

  1. Unconsolidated liabilities sum to €110bn. Removing €10bn held within the combined group leaves €100bn consolidated gross debt to outsiders.
  2. If the group owns €12bn eligible cash claims outside the perimeter, its specified net-debt measure is €88bn. These external cash claims are distinct from the already cancelled internal bonds.
  3. A public energy company's €9bn borrowing is outside this perimeter. The state guarantees €4bn of it. Reporting gross debt of €104bn by automatically adding the guarantee would mix recorded liabilities with conditional risk.

Interpretation

If the state assumes the €4bn and funds it through new debt, the stock can rise at that point. How much immediately enters debt depends on the accounting definition and financing action in the case.

A common mistake

Subtracting internal holdings and then subtracting those securities again when computing net debt reduces the same exposure twice.

How to correct it

Reconcile external assets available for deduction only after internal balances have been eliminated.

Quick check
Two public entities have 70 and 30 in outstanding debt. Of this total, 8 is owed from one to the other. What is their consolidated gross debt?

The 8 is an asset and a liability within the combined public group. Excluding the internal claim leaves 92 owed to outside holders.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A central treasury owes 140bn and provinces owe 40bn. Provinces hold 6bn of treasury bonds and a covered social fund holds another 9bn. The group has 18bn of eligible external cash. An excluded public firm has an uncalled 5bn state guarantee.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate consolidated gross and specified net debt.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    How should the 5bn guarantee be presented, and what would make it affect funding needs?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Gross is 140 + 40 − 6 − 9 = 165bn. Net deducts 18bn external cash, yielding 147bn.

Part B 3 marks

Show it as a contingent state exposure under the stated perimeter. A qualifying default and enforceable call can create a cash payment or assumption of debt requiring funding.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A government reports a deficit of 4bn but gross debt rises 11bn. What bridge would you request before claiming the accounts conflict?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would request matching opening and closing stocks, the deficit definition, financial transactions, currency marks and a perimeter reconciliation. Here a 4bn deficit plus 3bn deposited from borrowing, 2bn FX revaluation and 2bn of newly consolidated external debt explains the 11bn increase. I would still investigate whether the new asset is usable and whether the perimeter change reveals risks that existed earlier.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the 3bn deposit is liquid and eligible for net-debt deduction, how much of that specific transaction increases net debt?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Borrowing increases gross debt 3bn and the eligible cash asset increases 3bn, so this transaction alone initially leaves the specified net-debt measure unchanged. Interest carry, currency differences or later use of the deposit could change the net position afterward.
