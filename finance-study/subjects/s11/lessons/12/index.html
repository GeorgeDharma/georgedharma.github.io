Debt-office strategy, maturity and time to refixing

Calculate average time to maturity, average time to refixing and one-year exposure shares.

Theory and terminology

Public debt refixing and maturity risk

average time to maturity · average time to refixing

What you should be able to explain

  • Calculate average time to maturity, average time to refixing and one-year exposure shares.
  • Translate a rate shock into both first-year interest cash and a later annualized run rate.

Principal needs a maturity date, interest needs a reset date

Maturity is when principal must be repaid. A six-year floating-rate bond can reset its coupon next quarter, leaving the government exposed to market rates well before principal falls due. A fixed-rate bullet instrument usually refixes only if it is refinanced at maturity.

A bullet repays all principal at one date. For amortizing loans, split the principal into dated repayments and reflect their reset terms instead of assigning the entire balance to the final maturity. Use a stated currency conversion and stock value convention across instruments.

A weighted average needs a profile beside it

Average time to maturity weights each principal repayment date by its share of the debt stock. Average time to refixing similarly weights the next relevant rate-setting date. These averages summarize different risks and neither identifies a large individual maturity on its own.

Add a table showing the principal falling due and the principal subject to new rates within one year. Count both short fixed-rate maturities and outstanding floaters scheduled to reset. A short fixed bond may belong in both columns, so adding those totals would double count exposure.

Budget the timing

A two-point rate shock on principal resetting in nine months affects only the remaining three months of the first year. The same principal generates a full year of incremental cost after its first reset if the shock persists. Costs at a future annual rate and cash paid this year answer different budget questions.

Extending fixed-rate funding may reduce the amount needing renewal and the sensitivity of future interest bills. The quoted term premium increases regular cost. Compare rates, issuance capacity, fixed-versus-floating supply and fiscal risk appetite before selecting a strategy.

Method

Build the result

ATM = Σᵢ Dᵢmᵢ / Σᵢ Dᵢ. ATR = Σᵢ Dᵢrᵢ / Σᵢ Dᵢ. First-year additional interest ≈ Σᵢ DᵢΔyᵢ × fraction of year after reset.

Dᵢ is the face principal of instrument or repayment i, mᵢ years to principal maturity and rᵢ years to next rate refixing. ATM and ATR are years. Δyᵢ is the change in its annual coupon rate as a decimal. All D use the same currency. Σᵢ sums the portfolio.

  1. Map fixed and floating terms separately. Assign the next reset, the final maturity and any intervening principal repayment to each item.
  2. Weight both time measures by principal and extract the one-year shares using the same boundary convention.
  3. Apply the shock only when each coupon can change. A coupon that refixes at the very end of year one produces no additional accrued interest inside that year.
Worked case

The debt office considers extending a €20bn tranche

The €100bn portfolio contains €40bn fixed at 3%, maturing at the end of year one, €30bn fixed at 4%, maturing at year five, and €30bn floating at 5%, maturing at year six with its next reset in three months. Principal is bullet and refinanced at maturity. A permanent two-point rate shock applies at refixing. Use simple accrual. Alternatively replace €20bn of the one-year bond at issue with a five-year fixed tranche priced at 4.5%.

Working

  1. ATM is (40 × 1 + 30 × 5 + 30 × 6)/100 = 3.70 years. ATR is (40 × 1 + 30 × 5 + 30 × 0.25)/100 = 1.975 years.
  2. Within one year inclusive, €40bn matures but €70bn refixes. Baseline annual interest is 40 × 0.03 + 30 × 0.04 + 30 × 0.05 = €3.90bn.
  3. In year one the floating reset adds 30 × 0.02 × 0.75 = €0.45bn, giving €4.35bn. Immediately after the year-end refinancing, the annualized increase on the exposed €70bn is €1.40bn against unchanged rates.
  4. Replacing €20bn of one-year debt with five-year fixed increases both average times by 20 × 4/100 = 0.80 year. The refixing share drops to 50%, while first-year baseline cost rises €20bn × (4.5% − 3%) = €0.30bn.

Decision and interpretation

The alternative buys lower future repricing and rollover risk for higher initial interest. The first-year shock increment on the floating tranche remains €0.45bn under either strategy.

Quick check
A €20bn floater resets after nine months. A permanent two-point increase then applies. How much extra interest accrues inside the first year?

The new rate applies for one quarter. €20bn × 0.02 × 0.25 = €0.10bn, even though the full-year run-rate increase after resetting is €0.40bn.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A treasury has €60bn fixed with four years remaining and €40bn floating with three years remaining, next resetting in six months. A lasting 1.5 percentage point rise passes through on reset. No principal amortizes within year one. Find ATM, ATR and additional first-year interest.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. ATM is (60 × 4 + 40 × 3)/100 = 3.60 years.
  2. ATR is (60 × 4 + 40 × 0.5)/100 = 2.60 years. No principal matures in year one, but 40% of the debt refixes.
  3. The rate rises for the second half of the year on €40bn. The extra first-year accrual is 40 × 0.015 × 0.5 = €0.30bn. Its subsequent full-year annualized increase is €0.60bn if unchanged.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can a government with relatively long debt maturities still face a near-term jump in interest costs?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A long-dated floater can change coupon almost immediately. The worked portfolio matures in 3.70 years on average, but its average next refixing date is only 1.975 years. Seventy percent takes a new rate within one year even though only 40% of principal matures. I would show interest costs by payment period alongside both exposure measures when selecting fixed versus floating issuance.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the contract caps the floating rate increase at 0.5 point for this reset, does ATR change?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The contractual reset date is still six months away, so the timing metric does not change. The first-year additional cash accrual becomes €40bn × 0.005 × 0.5 = €0.10bn. A sensitivity measure captures a cap that an average date cannot show.
