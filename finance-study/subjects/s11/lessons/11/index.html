Sovereign exchange voting and collective action clauses

Reconcile eligible principal and consent across series under different voting structures.

Theory and terminology

Sovereign collective action clauses

CAC · exchange voting · aggregation

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile eligible principal and consent across series under different voting structures.
  • Explain why the voting threshold and the economic haircut require different evidence.

A contract addresses creditor coordination

One holder may prefer to retain the old bond while other investors accept a reduction that improves the sovereign’s capacity to pay. A collective action clause, or CAC, lets a specified majority amend defined terms in ways that bind affected holders under the contract. Which debt, holders and terms can be included depends on the documents.

A restructuring adviser first assembles a register by series, currency, principal outstanding and record date. The governing terms can exclude issuer-controlled holdings from voting. Abstentions may count against passage when the contract measures the threshold against all eligible outstanding principal.

Check each voting path separately

A series-by-series clause tests the threshold within each issue. A two-limb aggregated vote can require both an aggregate threshold and a minimum in every participating series. A single-limb vote may use one aggregate threshold with conditions on the offer’s treatment across holders.

A model single-limb safeguard can require an identical new instrument or the same menu for all affected holders. Different current bonds can suffer different economic losses from that same offer. Calculate voting compliance separately from value loss, using the actual documentation and legal process.

Build a decision-ready vote file

State exactly which procedure and series were designated before voting and whether thresholds are strict or inclusive. Validate denominators, currency conversion rules, evidence of control and valid voting instructions. In the example all series use the same currency and the rules are hypothetical.

A passing spreadsheet does not complete the transaction. Notices, documentation, agent certification, settlement and treatment of series outside the vote still matter. Opposing holders cannot be automatically treated as consenting merely because a proposal has political support.

Method

Build the result

Aggregate consent = Σᵢ Yᵢ / Σᵢ Eᵢ. Series consent for i = Yᵢ/Eᵢ. Under the example’s two-limb route, aggregate ≥ 2/3 and each series ≥ 1/2.

Eᵢ is eligible outstanding face amount for series i after contractual exclusions. Yᵢ is the eligible face voting yes. Σᵢ sums included issues. Percentages use principal, with the same currency and record date across series.

  1. Reconcile eligible face to total face for each issue and remove excluded holdings from both sides of the vote.
  2. Divide aggregate yes face by aggregate eligible face. Compare that principal-weighted result with the specified threshold. An unweighted series average would distort the voting power of differently sized issues.
  3. Apply series floors and any uniform-offer condition that the chosen route requires, then verify the date and procedural conditions with the voting agent.
Worked case

Three bond series and a difficult minority

Series A has €50m face, including €10m excluded issuer holdings. B has €30m and C €20m, all eligible. Eligible yes votes are A €32m, B €26m and C €9m. Assume contractual routes are designated as alternatives in advance. A uniform-offer single-limb vote requires at least 75% overall. A two-limb vote requires at least two thirds overall and at least half of each series. These teaching terms are not a jurisdictional rule.

Working

  1. Eligible principal is €40m + €30m + €20m = €90m. Confirmed yes face is €67m. Overall consent is 67/90 = 74.44%.
  2. Single-limb passage would need €67.5m. The proposal currently falls short by €0.5m even if it meets the uniform-offer and notice conditions.
  3. The two-limb aggregate passes because €67m exceeds two thirds of €90m (€60m). Series A is at 80%, B at 86.67% and C at 45%. The series floor fails in C by €1m.
  4. Before a properly authorized vote closes, an additional €1m yes in A would lift the aggregate to 75.56% but leave C at 45%. An additional €1m in C instead would lift C to 50% and the aggregate to 75.56%, satisfying both numerical alternatives.

Decision and interpretation

Tell the issuer how the chosen route applies. Do not choose a new procedure retroactively from the most favorable count. Uniform terms, disenfranchisement and procedural compliance need independent verification.

Quick check
Eligible series principal is €30m and €70m, with yes votes of €18m and €49m. A contract requires at least two thirds overall and half in each series. Does the vote clear its numerical tests?

Aggregate consent is (€18m + €49m)/€100m = 67%. The two series are at 60% and 70%, both above the stated series floor.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Two included series have eligible principal of €60m and €40m and yes principal of €51m and €16m. The same hypothetical inclusive thresholds apply. Both are offered the identical new bond. Test each route and calculate the minimum additional yes principal needed in series B for the two-limb route.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Total yes face is €67m out of €100m. It is below the €75m single-limb requirement and above the €66.667m approximate two-limb aggregate minimum.
  2. A consents at 85%, B at 40%, so B prevents two-limb passage.
  3. B must add €4m to reach €20m, or 50%. Total yes then reaches €71m. That passes the stated two-limb numbers but remains below the separate 75% threshold.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a sovereign exchange bind a small dissenting bond series when a majority of investors across all series supports the deal?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

It depends on the bond contracts and the voting route properly designated. Under the example’s two-limb clause, 74.44% overall passes the aggregate minimum but 45% in series C blocks passage. A uniform-offer single-limb mechanism can bind a small dissenting series once its separate aggregate and procedural requirements are met. I would reconcile eligible principal and each series before reporting whether the exchange can proceed.

Interviewer’s follow-up

One group is offered additional cash, and the chosen single-limb clause requires the same instrument or identical menu for everyone. Can 80% consent alone validate it?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. The uniform-offer condition is separate from the percentage. Examine the exact grouping, offer terms and notice. Change the proposal or use a contractually available alternative with its own procedures if appropriate. A high aggregate percentage cannot cure failure of the stated condition.
