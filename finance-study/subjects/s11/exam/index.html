Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A wider debt perimeter and a currency shock

Central government owes 150bn local-equivalent, including 30bn foreign-denominated. Municipalities owe 30bn local and hold 10bn of local treasury bonds. There are no other internal holdings. Consolidated external cash is 15bn local. GDP is 250bn. The local price of foreign currency rises 20% with no other changes.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A maturity wall and repricing

Opening debt is 80% of GDP, effective interest 5%, nominal GDP growth 2%, and primary surplus 1% of current GDP. There are no stock–flow adjustments. Independently, the cash schedule shows 25bn principal maturity, 5bn interest and 2bn primary surplus. Usable treasury cash is 4bn and 60% of principal is expected to roll.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A bank, a sovereign and a proposed exchange

A domestic bank holds 20bn of sovereign bonds marked to market with 6bn equity. Bond prices fall 10%, and the lending haircut on those bonds rises from 10% to 25%. Separately one sovereign creditor considers exchanging 100 due today for 110 in two years without coupons. Discount at 10%.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Who needs foreign currency this year?

Country P reports $30bn gross reserves with $5bn pledged. External debt due within a year includes $15bn issued short-term plus $10bn maturing long-term debt. Its current-account deficit is $4bn. The state has local-currency bonds held abroad and dollar bonds held by residents. Assume no duplication of the stated debt flows.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A wider debt perimeter and a currency shock

Part A 4 marks

Calculate initial consolidated gross and specified net debt, and each ratio.

Gross is 150 + 30 − 10 = 170bn, or 68% of GDP. Net is 155bn, or 62% of GDP after deducting eligible cash.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate post-shock gross debt and ratio, holding GDP fixed.

Foreign revaluation adds 6bn. Gross debt reaches 176bn and its ratio is 70.4%.

Part C 3 marks

Would adding a separately reported uncalled 8bn guarantee automatically be justified? What might happen if it is called?

Under the stated perimeter an uncalled contingent claim is separate. If the government assumes a liability or needs to borrow after a qualifying call, recorded debt or cash financing needs may increase depending on the action.

Case 2 · 10 marks

A maturity wall and repricing

Part A 4 marks

Calculate the closing ratio and the primary surplus that would stabilize debt at its opening ratio.

Inherited debt is 80 × 1.05/1.02 = 82.353%. Closing ratio is 81.353%. The stabilizing surplus would be 2.353% of current GDP.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate gross financing need and residual funding required after expected rollover and cash.

Need is 25 + 5 − 2 = 28bn. Rollover gives 15bn and cash provides 4bn, leaving 9bn to source.

Part C 3 marks

If rollover falls to 40%, recompute the gap and explain why a change in market yields would not fully reprice the inherited fixed-rate book in the same year.

Rollover is now 10bn and gap 14bn. Fixed coupon contracts keep promised payments until maturity. New borrowing, floating loans and repricing securities transmit higher yields more quickly.

Case 3 · 10 marks

A bank, a sovereign and a proposed exchange

Part A 4 marks

Calculate the bank's equity loss and change in collateral borrowing capacity.

Bond losses are 2bn, leaving 4bn equity. Funding capacity falls from 18bn to 0.75 × 18bn = 13.5bn, a 4.5bn drop.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate the exchanged claim's present value and the PV haircut versus the old claim.

PV is 110/1.10² = 90.91. Haircut relative to 100 is 9.09%, even though the future nominal amount exceeds the old amount.

Part C 3 marks

Explain a feedback channel from the bank to the sovereign and one reason a market yield does not measure default probability by itself.

A publicly funded recapitalization can increase state borrowing and affect sovereign yields, feeding back to bond values. Yields also reflect benchmark rates, recovery, liquidity and risk compensation.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Who needs foreign currency this year?

Part A 4 marks

Calculate usable reserve cover of remaining-maturity debt and residual aggregate need with zero rollover.

Usable reserves are $25bn against $25bn debt due, giving 1.0 times. Adding $4bn current deficit gives $29bn gross need and a $4bn gap before any other financing.

Part B 3 marks

If 40% of maturing debt rolls and the current-account deficit widens to $8bn, find funding need after rollover and available reserve cushion.

Renewal supplies $10bn. Need after rollover is 25 + 8 − 10 = $23bn, leaving $2bn of the defined usable reserves under complete accessibility.

Part C 3 marks

Distinguish the two bond types under residence and currency definitions and qualify the reserve result.

The local bonds held abroad are external by residence but locally denominated. Dollar bonds held by residents are domestic by residence yet create currency exposure. National reserves may not be available to all debtors or on every due date.
