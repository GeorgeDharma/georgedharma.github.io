Professional workshop · S11

A treasury financing plan with a dollar maturity wall

The Republic of Luma is a fictional commodity exporter. The minister needs a funded one-year borrowing plan, a downside in which the local currency weakens and lenders renew less debt, and an explanation of why a seemingly moderate debt ratio does not solve the next dollar payment. Prepare the budget committee's financing and risk memorandum using only the provided fiscal-year inputs. Local currency amounts are billions of lumens, abbreviated Lbn. US dollar figures are USD billions.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Sovereign debt-management office analyst briefing the finance minister
What to submit
Submit the annual borrowing requirement, cash and currency financing matrix, debt-stock roll-forward and contingency decision to the debt-management director. Identify confirmed resources separately from potential financing and central-bank reserves, and record the bank–sovereign exposure that should be monitored before increasing domestic issuance.

From inputs to decision

01

Verify the stocks, boundaries and timing

A central-government cash plan should never quietly use the central bank's assets or a private lender's holdings as though they were treasury cash.

  1. Opening debt is 60% of opening GDP. Of L600bn, USD20bn is L200bn at the baseline rate and the remaining L400bn is local principal. Opening cash is L40bn but L10bn must remain available for ordinary government payments.
  2. Keep the L15bn committed new facility separate from each rollover figure. Count any potential issuance only when describing how a gap might be filled, because neither indication is a binding receipt.

The authorized cash draw is L30bn. Build a gross financing calendar and a separate debt-stock reconciliation so maturing principal is not added twice.

02

Calculate baseline gross financing need

Gross need counts the cash falling due. The overall budget deficit can be reconstructed from interest and the primary balance.

  1. Maturing principal is L150bn local plus USD4bn × L10 = L40bn foreign, totaling L190bn. Interest of L30bn less a primary surplus of L5bn produces L25bn overall cash deficit.
  2. Gross financing need is L190bn + L25bn = L215bn. Known funding includes L120bn domestic rollover plus USD3bn × L10 = L30bn foreign rollover, L15bn committed new facility and L30bn cash draw.

Confirmed resources are L195bn. The ordinary plan therefore still needs L20bn of uncommitted funding before it can be described as executed.

03

Identify the foreign-currency payment inside the aggregate

A local-currency issue can cover the reported aggregate amount while leaving the treasury without dollars at settlement. The currency matrix identifies which conversions or foreign placements need approval.

  1. USD4bn matures with USD3bn of confirmed dollar rollover in the base. The treasury must source another USD1bn, equivalent to L10bn. This is part of the L20bn gap and should never be added to it as another need.
  2. One feasible target mix is USD1bn of external placement plus L10bn domestic syndication. Both fit indicated sizes. If foreign placement fails, domestic funds require a confirmed FX transaction settling before the dollar maturity.

Place responsibility for both the issue and the currency settlement on the borrowing calendar. Until both commit, the baseline remains L20bn short.

04

Build the joint downturn

A coherent downside links currency depreciation, weaker fiscal revenue, higher interest and reduced rollover. Hold the stipulated current-year principal and coupon definitions consistent.

  1. Foreign maturity now costs USD4bn × L12 = L48bn. With L150bn local maturity, total due principal is L198bn. Interest of L36bn plus a L4bn primary deficit creates L40bn overall deficit and gross financing need of L238bn.
  2. Confirmed funding is L100bn local rollover + USD2bn × L12 = L24bn dollar rollover + L15bn new facility + L30bn cash = L169bn. Remaining uncommitted need is L69bn.

The dollar shortfall inside that amount is USD2bn, or L24bn. Relative to baseline, gross need rises L23bn and confirmed sources fall L26bn in local terms. Together they explain the L49bn increase in the gap.

05

Test the contingency's capacity and currency coverage

The minister needs to know whether indicative sizes can close both the aggregate and the currency gap, and the remaining margin if any.

  1. At L12 per dollar, the maximum USD2.5bn placement provides L30bn equivalent. The L40bn domestic indication then gives up to L70bn in total versus the L69bn gap, only L1bn of size headroom before fees or failed subscriptions.
  2. One exact contingent mix is USD2.5bn plus L39bn domestic. After covering the USD2bn dollar shortfall, the additional USD0.5bn would need to be converted to L6bn for domestic uses. Secure a conversion and settlement date to make the L6bn proceeds available when domestic payments fall due.

If the treasury instead raises exactly USD2bn externally, L45bn would be needed domestically, exceeding the L40bn indicated ceiling by L5bn. Escalate the remaining L5bn and identify a lender able to settle in the required currency.

06

Reconcile closing debt and reserves

A cash need includes old principal being retired. Closing debt moves with net borrowing, budget flows and valuation. The cash draw finances part of the deficit using an asset already held.

  1. Assuming all gaps are successfully funded, baseline gross issuance is L215bn less the L30bn cash draw = L185bn. Retiring L190bn principal leaves closing debt L600bn − L5bn = L595bn. The ratio is 595/1,050 = 56.67%.
  2. In stress the opening dollar debt revalues by USD20bn × L2 = L40bn before servicing. New issuance is L238bn − L30bn = L208bn, while L198bn principal is retired. Closing debt is L600bn + L40bn + L208bn − L198bn = L650bn, or 66.33% of L980bn GDP.

The central bank has USD5bn above encumbrance and its own minimum. It belongs in the national resilience discussion. Zero is authorized for the treasury in the financing schedule. The L30bn cash draw also leaves the treasury exactly at its own L10bn operating floor.

07

Assess the domestic absorption and decide

A larger local syndication might push funding risk onto banks already exposed to sovereign yields. A mark-to-market estimate helps frame the trade-off without assuming a bank recapitalization.

  1. A 2-point yield increase gives approximate price decline 4 × 0.02 = 8% on L40bn marked bonds. The loss is L3.2bn, leaving L8.8bn from L12bn bank equity before other effects. That is about 26.7% of the initial banking equity in this simplified portfolio.
  2. Obtain bank-by-bank bids, collateral availability, maturity, cross-holdings and actual hedges before relying on the whole L40bn indication. Government intervention would require a distinct fiscal and legal decision and is excluded from the current stock figures.

Seek binding staged placements and confirm foreign settlement ahead of maturities. Do not label either scenario fully funded while the uncommitted amounts remain indications.

Completed document

Debt-management office financing recommendation

Borrowing mandate

Authorize negotiation of USD1bn and L10bn domestic in the baseline. With confirmed renewal and the L30bn cash draw this fills the L215bn financing need. Require currency settlement arrangements and dated evidence of the final commitments.

Stress action

Under the export and exchange-rate shock the gap rises to L69bn, including USD2bn in unmatched external principal. Target the indicative USD2.5bn external plus L39bn local combination and a conversion of USD0.5bn. The maximum combined indications provide only L1bn headroom.

Liquidity ownership

Preserve the L10bn treasury cash floor. No central-bank reserve transfer is authorized. Trigger escalation at the first settlement deadline at which confirmed local or dollar receipts fall short. Each payment depends on cash being available on its own settlement date.

Balance-sheet consequence

Fully funded closing debt would be L595bn in the base and L650bn under stress. The downside ratio rises to 66.33%, including L40bn translation on opening dollar debt. Gross maturities due are not all new debt.

Bank concentration

The illustrative domestic banking mark is L3.2bn, about 26.7% of opening bank equity. Obtain independent bid capacity and collateral data before relying on extended domestic rollover or an additional syndication.
