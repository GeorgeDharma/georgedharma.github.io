Asset-backed securities, reserve accounts and loss triggers

Separate asset losses, cash collection and credit protection.

Theory and terminology

Securitization waterfalls and credit enhancement

ABS · tranching · excess spread · reserve account

What you should be able to explain

  • Separate asset losses, cash collection and credit protection.
  • Apply a performance trigger and reconcile ending receivables, notes and overcollateralization.

Identify which balance belongs to whom

A securitization vehicle acquires receivables and issues notes serviced from their collections. Servicing, asset transfer and creditor rights depend on the legal documents. Several enhancements can coexist. A junior note stands behind a senior note. Overcollateralization means pool principal exceeds outstanding notes. A cash reserve is money held separately for designated uses.

Separate amounts already in the receivables balance from cash already in the bank account. A collateral write-off reduces the asset ledger. It does not put spendable money into the waterfall. Collections return cash and reduce receivables by the principal actually paid.

Read both the interest and principal rules

Interest collections usually have a specified order for items such as servicing, senior interest and junior interest. Positive interest remaining after contractual charges is excess spread. Documents may distribute it, retain it or require it to amortize notes against losses. Exhausting excess spread leaves other support exposed.

Principal may be allocated pro rata or entirely to senior notes. A performance trigger can cause a switch. Determine its measurement window, threshold, denominator and whether the documents permit cure. An illustrative trigger tied to one month’s net charge-offs will behave differently from a delinquency or rolling-average test.

Measure the remaining cushion

A senior investor asks how far collateral can fall before senior principal is impaired. Identify support still available after the remittance and distinguish accessible reserve cash from money already spent. Calculate the position at the date of interest.

This case defines overcollateralization from receivables alone. Including the cash reserve in that same calculation and then adding the reserve again would exaggerate support. Nor does positive overcollateralization guarantee timely coupon payment if collections arrive too late.

Method

Build the result

A₁ = A₀ − P − L. E = max(I − F − J, 0). D = min(L, E + B). OC₁ = A₁ − S₁ − U₁.

A₀ and A₁ are opening and ending receivable principal, P principal collected, L net charged-off principal, I interest received, F fees and J note interest due. E is positive excess interest, B opening usable cash reserve and D cash redirected against current losses under this contract. S₁ and U₁ are ending senior and junior principal. Amounts use one currency and observation period.

  1. Roll forward receivables using collected principal and credit losses. Verify the two movements against the servicer file.
  2. Apply interest to fees and all coupon amounts due. Under the case terms use excess first, then cash reserve, to restore principal distributions up to the loss.
  3. Test the trigger and send collected principal plus the restoration cash to the correct notes. Recalculate each ending note, overcollateralization and the unspent reserve.
Worked case

A performance trigger changes an auto trust’s remittance

Amounts are €m. Opening loans 100, senior notes 80, junior notes 15 and separate cash reserve 0.80. This month principal collections are 5 and net write-offs 4. Interest receipts are 1.35, fees 0.15, senior coupon 0.60 and junior coupon 0.15. In this trust, charge-offs above 3% of opening loans switch all distributable principal from pro rata to sequential senior payment. Excess interest and then reserve cash restore lost principal up to the loss.

Working

  1. Ending loans equal 100 − 5 − 4 = 91. Net write-offs are 4% of opening loans, so the sequential rule applies.
  2. Excess spread is 1.35 − 0.15 − 0.60 − 0.15 = 0.45. Add 0.80 from the reserve. That supplies 1.25 replacement cash against the loss of 4.
  3. Senior notes receive 5 + 1.25 = 6.25 principal. Senior balance falls to 73.75, junior stays at 15 and reserve ends at zero.
  4. Ending overcollateralization equals 91 − 73.75 − 15 = 2.25. It began at 5 and fell by the uncompensated 2.75. Ending pool principal exceeds senior notes by 17.25, including the junior layer.

Decision and interpretation

Continue weekly collection surveillance. The senior repayment is real, but €0.80m of previously liquid support has been used and cannot be counted in next month’s waterfall.

Quick check
A trustee reports a €1m loan write-off with no recovery cash. How should it enter the remittance?

An asset write-off reduces the amount collectible. Cash and loss allocation follow separate contract rules.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Another trust opens with €60m of loans, €45m senior and €12m junior notes, and €0.40m of reserve. Collections return €3m principal, net write-offs equal €2.40m and available excess interest is €0.25m. Apply the same 3% trigger and restoration terms.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Losses are €2.40m/€60m = 4%, activating sequential principal. Ending receivables are €54.60m.
  2. Senior receives €3m + €0.25m + €0.40m = €3.65m, leaving €41.35m senior. Junior remains €12m and reserve is exhausted.
  3. Ending overcollateralization is €54.60m − €41.35m − €12m = €1.25m. Its reduction from €3m equals the €1.75m portion of losses left uncovered by restoration cash.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can an auto-loan trust repay senior notes in cash while borrowers default, and how much loss protection remains?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Collections can repay senior notes during defaults because other borrowers still pay, and interest or reserve cash can restore part of the loss. The worked trust distributes €6.25m senior principal and ends with €2.25m overcollateralization plus €15m junior support. The reserve balance is zero after this distribution.

Interviewer’s follow-up

With balances frozen, no new collections or support, and junior and overcollateralization absorbing losses first, what would a further €14m loss do to senior principal?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Senior protection is €1.25m plus €12m, or €13.25m. Loss of €14m therefore erodes senior principal by €0.75m. A real loss projection must also date future collections and coupon shortfalls.
