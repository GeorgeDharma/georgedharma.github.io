Mortgage pass-throughs and prepayment speeds

Convert annualized prepayment speed into a monthly conditional rate.

Theory and terminology

Mortgage pass-through cash flows

MBS · conditional prepayment rate · single monthly mortality

What you should be able to explain

  • Convert annualized prepayment speed into a monthly conditional rate.
  • Reconcile interest, scheduled principal, prepayment and the remaining pool factor.

Follow a monthly remittance

A lender originates mortgages. A servicer collects household payments and administers the loans. A pass-through trust distributes eligible receipts to security holders under its contract. A borrower’s payment can contain both interest on the opening loan balance and repayment of principal.

The quoted mortgage rate may exceed the rate passed to investors because servicing and other contract fees are deducted. This example uses a constant fee spread and timely collection, and excludes defaults, advances and payment delays. Real remittance reports identify those items separately.

Apply prepayment to the surviving balance

Each month scheduled amortization retires a stated amount. The single monthly mortality rate, usually called SMM, is the fraction of the remaining balance voluntarily repaid in that month. Common transactions include refinancing, an early lump sum and a property sale.

The conditional prepayment rate, or CPR, annualizes a monthly speed by compounding survival. A 12% annual CPR therefore gives a monthly speed slightly above 1%. Applying 12% directly to one monthly balance grossly overstates the repayment. Portfolio composition also changes as the most rate-sensitive borrowers leave.

Interpret earlier return of capital

Prepayment shortens the time before principal comes back. A holder who paid a premium loses the opportunity to earn the high contractual coupon for as long as expected. A holder buying at a discount can recognize the return toward par sooner. Reinvestment opportunities affect both investors.

Falling rates can prompt prepayment precisely when attractive reinvestment rates disappear. Rising rates can slow refinancing and extend exposure. A curve-risk report should therefore recalculate the cash schedule under each rate path, allowing for borrower behaviour and differences between loan vintages.

Method

Build the result

s = 1 − (1 − c)^(1/12). U = s(B − A). Bnext = B − A − U. Cash = B × j/12 + A + U.

c is annual CPR, s monthly SMM, B beginning principal, A scheduled monthly principal, U unscheduled principal and Bnext ending principal. j is the annual investor pass-through coupon. Cash is the total month-end remittance before any further contractual adjustment.

  1. A constant monthly survival fraction repeated twelve times gives annual survival 1 − c. Take the twelfth root to obtain monthly survival.
  2. Apply monthly prepayment only after removing scheduled principal. Subtract the two distinct principal amounts to rebuild the ending balance.
  3. Calculate investor interest on beginning principal using the stated convention, add principal once and divide the new balance by original pool principal to obtain the pool factor.
Worked case

Reconcile a €10m residential pool

The pool begins at its original €10m balance. Gross mortgage coupon is 6% and the investor pass-through coupon is 5.4%. Scheduled principal is €20,000. Annual CPR is 11.3615%, equivalent to 1% monthly to rounding. Compare with a faster 2% monthly scenario.

Working

  1. Gross monthly interest is €50,000. The €5,000 fee spread leaves €45,000 for investors under the stated assumptions.
  2. Scheduled principal leaves €9.98m subject to prepayment. At 1% the prepayment is €99,800. Total returned principal is €119,800.
  3. Ending balance is €9,880,200, giving a pool factor of 0.98802. This month’s investor cash is €45,000 + €119,800 = €164,800.
  4. At 2% monthly prepayment, unscheduled principal is €199,600. Cash rises to €264,600 and ending balance falls to €9,780,400. The same €45,000 current interest is paid in both scenarios.

Decision and interpretation

The extra €99,800 is an earlier principal return. Next month’s interest-bearing base is correspondingly smaller. Full valuation requires a path of future speeds and discount rates.

Quick check
A €2m pool has €10,000 scheduled principal and 2% SMM. What unscheduled principal should the servicer report?

Apply 2% to €1,990,000 after scheduled principal. The €49,800 figure combines scheduled and unscheduled principal.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A second pool opens at €5m, with €40,000 scheduled principal, 0.6% SMM and a 4.8% investor coupon. All payments arrive and there are no other adjustments. Reconcile the remittance.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. The balance after scheduled amortization is €4.96m. Voluntary prepayment is 0.006 × €4.96m = €29,760.
  2. Total principal returned is €69,760. Closing principal is €4,930,240.
  3. Interest is €5m × 0.048/12 = €20,000. Total cash received is €89,760 and only €20,000 represents interest.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

What actually reaches a mortgage pass-through holder when refinancing accelerates?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The holder receives interest at the investor coupon plus two kinds of principal. In the €10m example, moving from 1% to 2% monthly prepayment raises cash by €99,800, all of it principal. Ending balance falls by exactly the same amount. Future coupon receipts therefore start from a smaller base.

Interviewer’s follow-up

An investor bought at 97 per 100 of face. How can faster prepayment affect that investor differently from a premium buyer?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Accelerated return of 100 can realize the discount sooner. The buyer also gives up future coupons and must reinvest earlier. Compare full discounted cash flows under both paths before determining the net value change.
