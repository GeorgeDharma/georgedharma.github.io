Bond promises

Identify principal, coupon, maturity, priority and currency. Read an example schedule before interpreting the quoted yield.

The starting point

A bond is a contract for future payments. Start with its face value, the amount on which interest is calculated. The coupon rate sets scheduled interest, and the maturity date identifies when the remaining face value is due. A plain bullet bond returns all principal at maturity.

Write each cash flow on a dated timeline in the promised currency. Frequency matters because a 6% annual coupon paid every six months produces two payments of 3% of face value. If the issuer fails, legal priority and available proceeds affect recovery. The promise and the amount eventually collected can differ.

Theory and method

Weighted average life of principal

WAL · Amortization schedule

A three-year amortizing note and a three-year bullet have the same stated coupon. How would you compare the actual timing of capital at risk?

A bank plans to reinvest principal as soon as the borrower returns it. The legal maturity tells it the final possible scheduled date, while weighted average life identifies the average date each euro of principal comes back. Coupons compensate for time and risk, but they do not repay principal unless the contract specifies capitalization or amortization. A dated schedule separates the cash available for reinvestment from the interest the lender earns while principal remains outstanding.

How the result is built

WAL = ΣtAₜ/P₀; couponₜ = cδₜPₜ₋₁

t is years from settlement to payment, Aₜ principal repaid in euros at t, P₀ original euro principal, c annual coupon rate, δₜ the period’s year fraction, and Pₜ₋₁ principal outstanding at the period start. The sum covers all scheduled principal payments.

  1. A €100 note pays principal of €20, €30 and €50 after one, two and three years. Each euro receives its repayment date as a weight.
  2. Compute WAL = (1 × 20 + 2 × 30 + 3 × 50)/100 = 2.30 years. A three-year bullet would have WAL of three years.
  3. At a 4% annual coupon and full-year periods, interest is €4.00, €3.20 and €2.00. Cash payments therefore total €24, €33.20 and €52, respectively.

When the reasoning holds

  • Every scheduled repayment must be included once and sum to original principal. Defaults, calls or prepayments require a scenario-dependent expected life.
  • WAL weights principal without discounting. For sensitivity to market interest rates, the present values of all payments including coupons are relevant.
Terms in this lesson
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Coupon
The interest payment specified in a bond’s terms.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
First example

A three-year €1,000 bullet bond with a 4% annual coupon pays €40 at the end of year one, €40 at year two and €1,040 at year three, assuming performance. A market quote of €960 changes what the buyer pays today while the contractual amounts stay fixed.

Read principal, coupon and ranking separately

Principal is the amount contractually repaid, with its dates determined by the amortization schedule. A coupon is interest calculated on the applicable principal. A bullet bond repays principal at maturity. An amortizing bond reduces principal earlier, which usually reduces later coupon amounts when the rate is fixed.

Seniority determines the order of claims on relevant assets if the issuer cannot perform. Currency determines which units must be tendered. A high fixed coupon does not protect against issuer failure or a fall in that currency’s purchasing power.

From the screen quote to settlement cash

Many bonds quote a clean price per 100 face value. Under an ordinary accrued-interest convention, the buyer pays the clean amount plus interest earned since the last coupon date. This dirty or full price is the amount used when recording the cash exchanged. Check local ex-coupon and day-count terms.

Accrued interest normally gives the seller credit for the elapsed coupon period when the buyer later receives the full next coupon. A changing dirty price between coupon dates can partly reflect that accrual even when market yields and the issuer’s credit have hardly moved.

Calendar, amount and ownership

For an annual coupon rate paid semiannually in equal ordinary periods, multiply face by the rate and divide by two. Irregular first or final periods may use the contract’s specified fraction of a year. Read which holder has the right to the next coupon at settlement.

A coupon date and a trading date are different events. Check holidays, payment currency and maturity principal before interpreting a quoted yield. Two instruments with the same coupon and maturity can have different economic timing because one repays principal in stages.

Method

Construct a claim register

  1. Contract

    List face value, outstanding principal, currency, ranking, coupon convention, payment frequency and redemption terms.

  2. Schedule

    For each date calculate interest on applicable principal, then add principal due. Track principal remaining afterwards.

  3. Settle

    Translate a per-100 quote into money and account for eligible accrued interest under the stated day-count convention.

Worked case

A mid-period quotation

Five bonds each have €1,000 face value, an 8% annual coupon paid semiannually and bullet repayment. The clean quote is 98.75 per 100 face. Settlement occurs 45 days into a 180-day coupon period. The buyer will receive the full next coupon.

Working

  1. A full semiannual coupon is €40 per bond. Accrued interest is €40 × 45/180 = €10. The clean price in euros is €987.50.
  2. Full settlement is €997.50 per bond or €4,987.50 for five. On the next ordinary coupon date the buyer receives €200 in total if the issuer pays.
  3. At final maturity an unchanged five-bond holding receives €5,000 principal plus its last €200 coupon. That principal is counted once on the final date.

Interpretation

A statement showing the €200 coupon as pure profit over the short holding period would overlook accrued interest paid at purchase and any intervening change in market price.

A common mistake

Multiplying the clean quote of 98.75 by five and treating the result as euros.

How to correct it

Interpret the quote as a percentage of face. Convert to full monetary settlement before comparing it with cash receipts.

Quick check
What is the final scheduled payment on a €2,000 bullet bond with a 6% annual coupon paid semiannually?

Each six-month coupon is €2,000 × 0.06/2 = €60. On the last payment date the holder also receives the €2,000 principal.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

A two-year €1,000 loan note pays annual 6% interest on beginning principal. It amortizes €400 at year one and the rest at year two.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Construct both total payments.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Would a bullet bond with the same rate have the same interest total?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Year one pays €60 interest plus €400 principal = €460. Remaining principal is €600, so year two pays €36 interest plus €600 = €636.

Part B 2 marks

No. The bullet pays €60 each year for €120 total interest. The amortizer pays €96 because €400 stops accruing after year one. The timing and interest-rate sensitivity also differ.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A three-year amortizing note and a three-year bullet have the same stated coupon. How would you compare the actual timing of capital at risk?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would state final maturity and WAL separately, show every principal payment, and calculate coupons on opening balances. Here 50% of principal comes back by year two, giving WAL 2.30 years. That helps funding and reinvestment planning. I would then calculate interest-rate duration from discounted coupons and principal if the risk manager wants a price sensitivity. A callable or prepayable contract needs several repayment paths.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If €20 due in year one moves to year three, what changes apart from nominal repayment dates?

Compare with the follow-up answer

WAL rises by €20 × two years/€100 = 0.40 year, to 2.70. The extra €20 also earns the 4% coupon for two additional annual periods under the stated contract, adding €1.60 nominal interest and increasing early funding exposure.
