Theory and terminology

Repurchase agreements and collateral margin

repo · reverse repo · haircut · margin call · secured financing

What you should be able to explain

  • Build the opening and closing cash and securities movements for both repo parties.
  • Distinguish repo interest, the initial haircut, and a subsequent call for cash or additional securities.

Describe one trade from each side

A dealer sells securities today and agrees to repurchase equivalent securities on a later date for an agreed cash price. The dealer calls this a repo. It obtains cash against securities and retains the economic risk of changes in their price. The cash investor calls the same trade a reverse repo. It provides cash, receives securities and agrees to deliver equivalent securities at the end.

On the opening date the parties exchange securities and cash, ideally through delivery versus payment. At the close the cash borrower pays principal plus repo interest and receives equivalent securities back. Check quantity, security identifier, settlement calendar, rate convention and any coupon passing through during the term. Our example has no intervening coupon.

Separate the haircut from the price of funding

The haircut is a fraction of the opening market value of collateral. A 5% haircut on €100 of bonds supports €95 cash. The extra €5 of collateral cushions adverse movements during closeout. The equivalent collateral-to-cash ratio is 100/95, or 105.263%. Confusing the percentage bases produces a different advance.

The repo rate applies to the cash borrowed. Under simple actual/360 accrual, one day earns one three-hundred-and-sixtieth of the annual rate on that cash principal. Market value movements in the bond do not reset the fixed repo rate. An overnight renewal can change the rate, the haircut or the amount available.

Revalue the collateral and the amount owed

Margin restores agreed protection during the transaction. Use the bond’s current dirty market value, including applicable bond accrued interest, and the repo repurchase obligation accrued to the margin date. Bond accrued interest and repo interest refer to different contracts.

The agreement determines valuation timing, thresholds, acceptable collateral, netting and treatment of excess margin. Here there is one trade, no threshold, no other margin already held and same-day delivery. A cash margin transfer is credited at 100%. Additional eligible securities receive the stated haircut. Margin held separately does not reduce the original repo principal.

Method

Build the result

C = (1 − h)V₀. Rₜ = C[1 + r × t/B]. M = max(0, Rₜ − (1 − h)Vₜ). Additional same-haircut securities = M/(1 − h).

C is opening cash, h the haircut as a fraction of collateral market value, V₀ original dirty market value, Vₜ current dirty market value, r annual simple repo rate, t actual days and B the day-count basis of 360. Rₜ is accrued repo cash exposure. M is extra required cash margin from the cash borrower under the stated rules. Amounts are in one currency.

  1. Apply the haircut once to collateral to find the opening cash advance. Record the reverse movements for the cash lender.
  2. Accrue interest on the original cash principal to the margin delivery date, then compare the accrued obligation with current eligible collateral value.
  3. If exposure is positive, post that amount as cash or gross it up for the haircut on newly delivered securities. Check that the post-call eligible collateral exactly equals the accrued obligation.
Worked case

A seven-day sovereign-bond financing

A dealer delivers €12m of government bonds at dirty market value. The haircut is 5%, the fixed annual repo rate is 3.60% actual/360, and the term is seven actual days. At the day-three margin calculation the original bonds are worth €11.82m. The dealer elects to post securities under the stated haircut. No coupon falls due.

Working

  1. Opening cash is 0.95 × €12m = €11.4m, received by the dealer and advanced by the reverse-repo investor. The €600,000 haircut is extra collateral value.
  2. Daily interest is €11.4m × 0.036/360 = €1,140. At day three the accrued repurchase exposure is €11,403,420.
  3. Eligible collateral is 0.95 × €11.82m = €11,229,000. The difference is €174,420 in cash margin terms. Posting bonds worth €174,420/0.95 = €183,600 closes it.
  4. Check 0.95 × (€11,820,000 + €183,600) = €11,403,420. At day seven the dealer owes €11,400,000 + 7 × €1,140 = €11,407,980 on the repo and recovers its equivalent securities and outstanding margin when all obligations settle.

Decision and interpretation

Fund the €7,980 term interest and plan collateral availability separately. An otherwise profitable financed position can still run out of securities available for a timely margin call.

Quick check
A reverse-repo investor faces a €76,000 cash-equivalent shortfall. Eligible additional securities receive a 5% haircut. What market value must the borrower deliver?

Only 95% of the new securities’ value is eligible. Dividing €76,000 by 0.95 gives €80,000 market value.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A ten-day repo starts against $8m in bonds with a 2% haircut and a fixed 4.5% actual/360 rate. On day four the bonds are worth $7.91m. Under the same haircut and margin rules, calculate the cash advance, cash-equivalent call, extra securities if used, and closing repo price.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Opening cash is $7.84m and daily interest is $7.84m × 0.045/360 = $980. Accrued exposure on day four is $7,843,920.
  2. Eligible collateral is $7.91m × 0.98 = $7,751,800. The lender can call $92,120 cash or $92,120/0.98 = $94,000 additional securities at market value.
  3. The ten-day price is $7,840,000 + $9,800 = $7,849,800. Margin posted separately is returned under the agreement when the trade closes.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A bond dealer’s repo has a fixed interest rate. Why can its lender call for additional collateral before maturity, and how should the desk calculate the call?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The fixed repo rate sets the interest on the cash borrowed. It does not prevent the bond from falling in value. I would compare the accrued repurchase obligation with today’s haircut-adjusted collateral, allowing for margin already delivered. At day three the resulting €174,420 shortfall can be met with €174,420 cash or €183,600 of bonds at the agreed haircut.

Interviewer’s follow-up

At maturity another lender will finance only the original bonds, still worth $7.91m, at a new 3% haircut. How much additional cash does the dealer need to repay the old repo?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The new advance is $7.91m × 0.97 = $7,672,700. Compared with $7,849,800 due, the dealer must supply $177,100. Funding returned margin on a separate transaction could change the answer if it is available in time.
