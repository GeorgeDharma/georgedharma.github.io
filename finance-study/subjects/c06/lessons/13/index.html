Inflation-linked bonds, indexed settlement and redemption floors

Reconcile an indexed bond’s quoted price with cash due at settlement.

Theory and terminology

Index-linked bond settlement and redemption

TIPS · index ratio · real yield

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile an indexed bond’s quoted price with cash due at settlement.
  • Distinguish indexed coupons and a contractual principal floor from the investor’s purchase cost.

Follow the inflation index into the contract

A capital-indexed bond specifies a reference price index and a base value at issuance. The ratio between the applicable reference index at a later date and the base changes the principal used to calculate cash flows. The reference series, indexation lag, interpolation and rounding belong to the instrument’s terms.

US Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities, or TIPS, pay a fixed coupon rate on inflation-adjusted principal every six months. The principal can fall as well as rise before maturity. At maturity the US Treasury repays at least the original principal. Other countries’ index-linked contracts can use different arrangements.

Read the units on the dealer quote

A real clean quote is expressed per 100 of original principal before the current index adjustment. Accrued interest compensates the seller for the part of the coupon period already earned. When both amounts are in real quote points, first add them, then apply the settlement index ratio once.

A price decline remains possible if market real yields rise, even during consumer price inflation. A buyer who pays above par plus accumulated indexation may receive less principal than the purchase cost if the price index subsequently falls.

Interpret the protection for a liability

A linker is useful when its index and payment dates resemble the obligation to be paid. A family’s rent, a pension promise linked to wages, or a construction budget can grow differently from consumer prices. That difference creates basis risk even if the security pays exactly as promised.

Matched nominal and real yields help measure market inflation compensation. Differences in trading liquidity, risk premia, tax treatment and the value of the deflation floor can also move the observed spread. A market break-even is therefore conditional on the comparison selected.

Method

Build the result

I = CPIref/CPIbase. Cashsettle = (F/100) × (P + A) × I. Coupon = F × It × c/2. Principal at maturity = F × max(IT, 1).

I is the applicable settlement index ratio, It the coupon-date ratio and IT the maturity ratio. CPIref and CPIbase are the applicable reference and original index levels. F is original face in dollars, P clean real quote per 100, A real accrued interest per 100 and c annual coupon as a decimal. The floor shown follows US TIPS terms.

  1. Recover real dirty price by adding accrued interest to the clean quote, ensuring both are expressed in the same units.
  2. Scale the price by original face in hundreds, then apply the current settlement ratio. Use the applicable coupon-date ratio for each later coupon.
  3. For final principal compare indexed face with original face. The market price paid for the bond does not enter the redemption-floor calculation.
Worked case

A secondary-market TIPS purchase

An investor buys $200,000 original face. The real clean quote is 103.20, real accrued interest is 0.40 per 100, the base reference index is 250 and the applicable settlement index is 265. Annual real coupon is 1.5%. Ignore taxes, rounding conventions and trading fees.

Working

  1. The settlement index ratio is 265/250 = 1.06. The real dirty quote is 103.60 per 100.
  2. Trading consideration equals 2,000 × 103.60 × 1.06 = $219,632, of which indexed accrued interest represents 2,000 × 0.40 × 1.06 = $848.
  3. At the next coupon, suppose the reference index is 272. Adjusted principal is $200,000 × 272/250 = $217,600. The coupon is $217,600 × 0.015/2 = $1,632.
  4. If the maturity index eventually falls to 245, indexed face is $196,000. The maturity principal floor raises the redemption component to $200,000.

Decision and interpretation

The floor covers original face. It cannot alone recover the $219,632 invoice. Evaluate all intervening coupons and timing when computing the investor’s realized return.

Quick check
Which amount is the correct floor reference for the investor who paid $219,632?

The US TIPS maturity floor compares maturity-indexed face with original face. The later purchase invoice is a secondary-market transaction.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A desk buys $100,000 original face at a real clean quote of 98.80 and real accrued interest of 0.30 per 100. The settlement ratio is 1.04. The annual coupon is 2%. Calculate the invoice, and next coupon if its ratio is 1.07.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Add the two real quote components to obtain 99.10 per 100. Convert $100,000 face into 1,000 units of 100.
  2. The invoice is 1,000 × 99.10 × 1.04 = $103,064. Of this, indexed accrued interest is $312.
  3. Next coupon is $100,000 × 1.07 × 0.02/2 = $1,070. Using the settlement ratio for that later payment would give the wrong amount.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How much cash changes hands for an inflation-protected bond, and what amount is actually protected?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would add real accrued interest to the real clean quote and apply original face and the published index ratio. Here that produces $219,632 cash. The TIPS maturity floor protects $200,000 original principal. It does not reimburse the purchase premium or the index adjustment the buyer paid, and interim prices are exposed to real yields.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If market real yields rise while that index ratio stays at 1.04, must the investor still be able to sell for the invoice?

Compare with the follow-up answer

No. Higher required real yields reduce the present value of future real payments, other things equal. The principal floor applies at maturity. It does not guarantee an interim resale price or refund previously paid accrued interest on demand.
