Curve risk and immunization

Match sensitivity of assets and liabilities. A duration match may leave exposure to a twist in the curve or a changing credit spread.

The starting point

A pension fund can aim to protect its ability to make promised payments when interest rates change. First compare the present value of assets and liabilities. Then compare how much money each side gains or loses for a small shift in the relevant curve. This is the starting point for immunization.

Equal present values and equal durations can match the first-order effect of a small parallel shift. A twist, where short and long yields move differently, can still hurt. Key-rate duration examines sensitivity near individual maturities. Credit deterioration and the passage of time also make rechecking the hedge necessary.

Theory and method

Key-rate duration hedging as simultaneous equations

Bucket DV01 matching · Liability-driven investment

Two swaps both reduce the total duration gap. How would you choose the amounts to hedge a two-year and five-year liability exposure independently?

A pension plan may be underexposed at both curve maturities, but its available hedge instruments often affect more than one date. Buying enough of one to fix the long bucket can overshoot the short bucket. Construct one equation per relevant maturity using signed euro changes for a one-basis-point rise. Solving the equations exposes whether the available instruments span the required pattern and how much leverage or collateral the solution would entail.

How the result is built

H n = g, with g = DV01liability − DV01assets

g lists euro sensitivity gaps per one-basis-point parallel move within each specified maturity bucket. H gives each hedge’s euro DV01 contribution per €1m traded, n lists notionals in millions of euros. Positive DV01 here denotes the magnitude of loss on a rate rise.

  1. The liability exceeds asset DV01 by €1,000 per basis point at two years and €2,000 at five years. Hedge A supplies (200,100) per €1m and B supplies (50,250).
  2. The two equations are 200A + 50B = 1,000 and 100A + 250B = 2,000. The first implies B = 20 − 4A.
  3. Substitute into the second to obtain −900A = −3,000. Choose A = 3.333m and B = 6.667m, then recheck both exposures using unrounded amounts.

When the reasoning holds

  • Key-rate sensitivities must be measured under the same curve interpolation, signed unit and valuation time for assets, liabilities and hedges.
  • The columns need different relative maturity exposures. Nearly proportional columns can yield unstable large notionals and outsized implementation risk.

Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Duration
A measure used to estimate how strongly a bond price responds to a small interest-rate change.
Credit spread
Extra yield over a chosen reference yield. Credit risk, liquidity and market conditions can all affect it.
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
How to read the notation
Basis points and percentage points
A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises by one percentage point, or 100 basis points. Its increase relative to the original rate is (5 − 4) ÷ 4 = 25%.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

Assets of €10m with duration 6 and liabilities of €12m with duration 5 each have a sensitivity measure of €60m-years. A one percentage-point parallel rise reduces each present value by roughly €0.60m. The initial €2m shortfall remains.

Define the liability before choosing an asset

A pension fund, insurer or corporate treasury should specify amount, currency, date and possible indexation of the obligation it wants to protect. A rise in reference yields can reduce the present value of future liabilities as well as its bond holdings. Evaluate the surplus, assets minus liabilities, rather than asset return alone.

A cash-flow match purchases securities whose dated payments cover each liability. It is powerful when suitable securities and reliable payers exist. Duration immunization instead aligns first-order sensitivities and requires assumptions about curve shifts, credit and how often the hedge is rebalanced.

Use money sensitivity for unequal values

An asset portfolio of €8m with duration 5 and liabilities of €10m with duration 4 each have €40m-years of parallel exposure. Their present values are different, but their first-order money responses to a small parallel shock can match. Conversely, equal durations on unequal values leave an unmatched money exposure.

Classical immunization of a fully funded position commonly starts with equal present values and equal duration. Convexity and the horizon of the obligation also matter for larger changes. Matching sensitivity today does not fill an existing funding shortfall or finance future contributions.

Decompose sensitivity by maturity

Key-rate duration changes the curve near one maturity at a time while applying a specified interpolation elsewhere. The set of sensitivities shows whether the assets concentrate rate exposure at the short end while the liability responds mainly to the long end. A twist can produce a surplus loss even when totals match.

Nonparallel changes, inflation indexation, embedded options, credit spreads and derivative collateral can remain after a rates hedge. Recalculate after payments and as maturity approaches because weight and duration drift over time.

Method

Build an immunization report

  1. Measure

    Show current asset value, liability value, initial surplus and signed money sensitivity to a one-basis-point change in each relevant maturity.

  2. Match

    Select hedges to align the required sensitivities. Distinguish an improvement in exposure from an improvement in the opening funded status.

  3. Test

    Apply a parallel rise, a steepening and a spread shock. Identify the residual, the assumption responsible and a practicable rebalancing trigger.

Worked case

Matched totals hide a twist

A plan has €10m assets and €10m liabilities. Asset short- and long-key durations are 1 and 4. Liability key durations are 3 and 2. Assume the two bucket changes approximate the price effect additively.

Working

  1. Each side has total duration 5. A parallel 10-basis-point rise lowers each by about €50,000 and initially leaves surplus unchanged.
  2. Now let short yields rise 20 basis points and long yields fall 10. Asset change ≈ −€10m × [1 × 0.002 + 4 × (−0.001)] = +€20,000.
  3. Liability change ≈ −€10m × [3 × 0.002 + 2 × (−0.001)] = −€40,000. Surplus increases by approximately €60,000. The opposite twist would reverse the sign.

Interpretation

The exposure is to opposing curve movements rather than the overall level. An additional hedge that simply reduces all duration would alter both the parallel and twist results.

A common mistake

Subtracting liability price changes with the same sign used for assets when estimating surplus.

How to correct it

Write change in surplus as change in assets minus change in liabilities. A fall in liability value can offset or exceed an asset loss.

Quick check
Which action addresses exposure to a steepening curve that a single-duration comparison misses?

Separate maturity sensitivities reveal where a twist can produce unequal price changes even when the total duration exposure matches.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

Liabilities worth €12m have duration 6. Assets worth €9m have duration 6 before any hedge.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find the approximate surplus change for a parallel one-percentage-point yield rise.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    What asset duration would match the initial money sensitivity without changing asset value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Asset value falls about €0.54m, liabilities about €0.72m. Surplus improves by about €0.18m from an initial −€3m. Equal durations do not match euro sensitivity on unequal values.

Part B 2 marks

Required asset duration is €12m × 6/€9m = 8. That matches local parallel sensitivity while leaving the initial €3m deficit and other risks unresolved.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two swaps both reduce the total duration gap. How would you choose the amounts to hedge a two-year and five-year liability exposure independently?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would define the sign and curve buckets, then solve the two exposure equations rather than selecting whichever swap has the largest total DV01. The result here is €3.333m of A and €6.667m of B. I would reprice the whole balance sheet under twists and large shocks, check collateral and basis risk, then decide how often to rebalance as payments shorten the liabilities.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose hedge B supplies exactly twice A’s exposures in every bucket. Can this pair still reproduce the stated gap?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Both instruments would then supply short-to-long sensitivity in the same 2:1 ratio as A. The required gap has a 1:2 ratio. No linear combination can produce it. I would source a distinct maturity exposure or quantify the residual under an explicitly chosen best-fit hedge.
