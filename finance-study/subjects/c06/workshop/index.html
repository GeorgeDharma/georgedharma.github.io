Professional workshop · C06

A two-date euro annuity book and an executable cash-flow match

A fictional insurer has ring-fenced €9.00m against guaranteed euro payments of €4.00m in two years and €6.00m in five years. Its risk committee wants protection against a twist in the euro curve, and its treasury requires at least €140,000 in immediately available cash after trading. Submit a same-day trade proposal, showing the difference between mid-market valuation and executable proceeds. Assume no default, optionality, tax, accrued interest or market impact beyond the displayed bid and ask. Cash has no modeled curve exposure over today’s shock.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Fixed-income asset–liability portfolio manager
What to submit
Deliver an asset–liability management trade ticket to dealing and a signed-off surplus-risk reconciliation to the asset–liability committee. Use the liability dates, legal currency and settlement convention consistently so the insurer can actually make the two promised payments.

From inputs to decision

01

Reconstruct the starting funded position

The risk committee needs a defensible starting surplus before seeing sensitivity numbers. Every claim must be valued on the same curve and in the same euros.

  1. Discount the two liability payments. The short obligation is worth €4m × 0.95 = €3.80m and the long €6m × 0.84 = €5.04m, together €8.84m.
  2. Value the assets at mid. The two-year zero is €4.94m, the five-year zero €4.032m and cash €0.028m. Total €9.00m exceeds liability present value by €160,000.

Record the difference between €10m future nominal liabilities and €8.84m present liabilities. Subtracting future nominal payments from current assets would mix dates.

02

Measure the mismatch in actual maturity cash

Both the trade direction and the impact of unexpected rate moves come from a surplus at the short date and a deficit at the long one.

  1. At year two, existing face exceeds the benefit by €5.2m − €4.0m = €1.2m. At year five, existing face falls short by €6.0m − €4.8m = €1.2m. Equal face gaps do not offset for an insurer unable to travel forward or backward through time.
  2. Keeping that year-two excess would require reinvesting it for three years at an as-yet unknown rate to fund the long shortfall. A nominally balanced €10m bond face therefore retains future reinvestment risk.

Propose selling €1.2m two-year face and buying €1.2m five-year face to place the money at the required maturity.

03

Compute key-rate DV01 and surplus risk

A single portfolio duration could conceal the two opposing exposures. Each bucket has a different sign in the current surplus.

  1. Asset DV01 is €4.94m × 2 × 0.0001 = €988 at the two-year point and €4.032m × 5 × 0.0001 = €2,016 at the five-year point. Liability DV01 is €760 and €2,520 respectively.
  2. Asset less liability DV01 is +€228 at two years and −€504 at five. A short-end rise hurts surplus, while a long-end rise benefits it. A parallel one-basis-point rise improves surplus by €276 on the first-order convention.

Under the specified twist the first-order surplus change is −€228 × 25 − (−€504) × (−15) = −€13,260. Both components hurt although the parallel exposure would suggest a gain for rising rates.

04

Independently reprice the twist

The linear measure approximates a change at today’s curve. The full exponential repricing verifies the sign and reveals curvature effects.

  1. The net short-date surplus face is +€1.2m and long-date surplus face −€1.2m. Under a spot shift a discount factor scales by exp(−maturity × decimal change).
  2. The exact surplus change is €1.2m × 0.95 × [exp(−2 × 0.0025) − 1] − €1.2m × 0.84 × [exp(5 × 0.0015) − 1]. The result is approximately −€13,274, close to the linear loss. Cash is unchanged in this instantaneous scenario.

Retain the full-value calculation in the risk pack. An exact match of both liability cash flows after the trade removes sensitivity to even large moves in these two discount factors under the stated certainty assumptions.

05

Cost the orders at executable sides

The insurer sells the shorter zero at the dealer’s bid and buys the longer one at the ask. Using mid prices for both would overstate closing cash.

  1. Selling €1.2m face at 94.90 gives €1,138,800 cash. Buying €1.2m at 84.20 costs €1,010,400. Net cash raised is €128,400.
  2. Add opening cash €28,000 to obtain €156,400 after matched settlement. At mids the same trades would have released €132,000. The €3,600 shortfall is combined crossing cost, consisting of €1,200 on the sale and €2,400 on the purchase.

The treasury floor of €140,000 is met by €16,400. Verify simultaneous settlement and minimum executable price conditions before releasing either ticket.

06

Set a price limit that protects treasury liquidity

A trade instruction that names only quantities could breach the cash floor if the long security reprices before execution.

  1. Holding the quoted two-year bid fixed, maximum affordable five-year purchase cash is €28,000 + €1,138,800 − €140,000 = €1,026,800. On €1.2m face this equals 85.5667 per €100.
  2. Use the current 84.20 ask for the proposed execution, with a maximum aggregate settlement cost consistent with the €140,000 floor. If the short bid moves, recompute the combined constraint rather than relying on the old isolated long limit.

If either leg fails, keep the residual exposure visible and refer the replacement quote. A temporary cash surplus from an unmatched sale does not establish a completed hedge.

07

Reconcile after execution and define the remaining exposures

The committee needs to know exactly what has been removed and what remains if the real liability differs from its simplified modeling assumptions.

  1. Post-trade face is €4.0m at year two and €6.0m at year five. Their full proceeds meet the exact euro benefits at the corresponding dates under the stated contract. Mid-valued bonds are €8.84m and cash €156,400, giving €8,996,400 total assets and €156,400 funded surplus.
  2. Post-trade asset DV01 is €760 and €2,520, matching liability DV01 in each bucket. Under the exact twist both assets and liabilities change identically at each date. The €156,400 cash surplus is unchanged in the instantaneous scenario.

Monitor custodial settlement, any benefit option or revised payment date, credit quality of the instruments, and treasury cash use. Report a quantity, timing or currency change to the committee before carrying a zero residual-risk claim forward.

Completed document

Asset–liability committee execution note · guaranteed euro benefits

Order requested

Sell €1.20m face of the two-year zero at the displayed 94.90 bid and buy €1.20m face of the five-year zero at 84.20 ask. Link settlement of the two instructions. The resulting €4m and €6m maturities match guaranteed benefits in amount, currency and date.

Risk rationale

Before the orders our curve exposure relative to liabilities is +€228 per basis point at two years and −€504 at five. The specified short-end rise and long-end rally cost about €13,260 of surplus on a first-order basis. Following the trade each dated asset equals the corresponding claim, eliminating this modeled curve mismatch.

Execution economics

Closing cash is €156,400, €16,400 above the treasury minimum. Marked surplus falls from €160,000 by the €3,600 bid–ask cost. With the sale price fixed at 94.90, a purchase beyond 85.5667 per €100 face would breach the required cash floor and needs revised instructions.

Controls and exceptions

Operations must confirm delivery and receipt of both zero positions and record the actual fills. Asset–liability management must rerun the hedge if beneficiaries can accelerate payment, if a liability becomes inflation-linked, or if settlement or issuer risk threatens the promised dates. A failed leg is reported as an open position.

Task 1

If the five-year ask rises to 85.00 and the two-year bid remains 94.90, what euro cash remains after the approved face trades?

See the full solution

The sale still raises €1,138,800. Buying €1.2m at 85.00 costs €1,020,000. Adding €28,000 opening cash leaves €146,800, still €6,800 above the floor.

Task 2

If the insurer holds its starting positions and short rates fall 10 basis points while five-year rates rise 20, what is the first-order surplus gain in euros?

See the full solution

Apply the pre-trade net sensitivities. Change is −€228 × (−10) − (−€504) × 20 = €2,280 + €10,080 = €12,360. Repricing would produce a slightly different exact result.

Task 3

Following the trade, an additional €100,000 benefit becomes due at year five. What new five-year surplus DV01 gap results before rebalancing, using the same 0.84 discount factor? Enter the absolute magnitude.

See the full solution

The new liability has present value €84,000 and DV01 €84,000 × 5 × 0.0001 = €42 per basis point. The signed asset-minus-liability gap is −€42, meaning a five-year rate fall now hurts surplus.

Task 4

An underwriter says actual benefits may be paid one year early. Does the matched face still guarantee the projected liquidity?

No. Payment timing is part of the match. Build the alternate schedule, identify the premature cash requirements and model the sale or funding needed before the zero matures. Reassess issuer, liquidity and rate exposure and compare the cost of earlier cash or a suitably timed instrument.
