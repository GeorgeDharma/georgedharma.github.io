Strip the curve and price a forward commitment

A one-year €100 zero costs €96. A two-year annual 4% coupon bond at €100 face costs €99.52. A corporate treasurer can agree today to invest €2m for one year beginning at the end of year one, under the same reference curve.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

From a screen quote to a risk limit

A desk buys €2m face of an annual 6% bullet at a clean quote of 99.50. Half of the 360-day coupon period has elapsed and the buyer receives the full next coupon. On its full market value, modified duration is 4 and convexity 30.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A callable borrower and an illiquid pool

A €100m pool funds €70m senior and €30m junior, with collections paying senior principal first and losses charged against junior first. Borrowers prepay €18m, followed by €35m in unrecovered principal. A separate one-year credit claim promises €106 per €100 advanced and pays €40 on default. A comparable safe investment pays €103.

A pension promise exposed to a twist

Assets worth €15m have short and long key-rate durations 2 and 3. Liabilities worth €12m have corresponding durations 1.25 and 5. Assume first-order bucket approximation. A stress raises short yields 30 basis points and reduces long yields 10 basis points.

Case 1 · 10 marks

Strip the curve and price a forward commitment

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the two discount factors and the two-year annual spot rate.

d1=0.96. From 4×0.96 + 104d2 = 99.52, d2=0.92. The two-year spot rate is √(1/0.92)−1≈4.26%. Award one mark per result.

Part B 4 marks

Calculate the annual forward rate and its promised future euros on €2m.

The forward growth factor is 0.96/0.92 = 1.043478, rate 4.3478%. End-of-year-two payment for €2m invested at year one is €2,086,956.52. Award for matching dates, ratio, rate and amount.

Part C 3 marks

If a survey expects a 3% future short rate, describe why it can differ and what the treasurer has secured.

Market-implied rate sets prices consistent today. Future short rates are uncertain. Term premiums or differing estimates can create a gap. The agreed investment fixes an outcome subject to contract and counterparty performance. Award one mark per distinct pertinent point up to three.

Case 2 · 10 marks

From a screen quote to a risk limit

Part A 3 marks

Find accrued interest, clean price in euros, and total settlement.

Annual coupon is €120,000. Half accrued is €60,000. Clean price is €1,990,000 and full settlement is €2,050,000. Award one mark per required result.

Part B 4 marks

Estimate the euro price change on a 75-basis-point rise with duration and convexity.

Δy=0.0075. Duration gives −3%. Convexity adds 0.5×30×0.0075²=0.00084375 or 0.084375 percentage point. Net −2.915625% times €2,050,000 gives approximately −€59,770. Award one mark per element.

Part C 3 marks

Explain why a clean-price-only cost and an unqualified realized-return claim would both be misleading.

Accrued cash is paid at settlement. Clean alone understates purchase outlay. The rate shock estimates an instantaneous model price change. Realized return may also include coupon accrual, reinvestment, changes in spreads or sale timing. Award marks for settlement, shock interpretation and an additional driver.

Case 3 · 10 marks

A callable borrower and an illiquid pool

Part A 4 marks

Show the pool waterfall and the loss ultimately borne by senior holders.

After €18m principal, balances are €52m senior and €30m junior. Losses consume all €30m junior and €5m senior, leaving €47m senior. Seniors already received €18m, total €65m versus initial €70m. Award one mark per stage and final loss, up to four.

Part B 3 marks

Infer the credit claim’s actuarial break-even default probability assuming no extra risk compensation and year-end payments.

106(1−p)+40p=103, giving p=3/66≈4.55%. Award for expected-cash equation, algebra and probability.

Part C 3 marks

Give three reasons a quoted 3-point yield spread need not uniquely identify physical default probability.

Uncertain recovery and timing change loss conditional on default. Priced bad-state risk or liquidity compensation can contribute to the spread. Options and differing reference curves can alter quoted yields. Award one mark for each distinct mechanism.

Case 4 · 10 marks

A pension promise exposed to a twist

Part A 3 marks

Show current surplus and compare total money durations.

Initial surplus €3m. Asset money duration is €15m×5=€75m-years. Liability money duration is €12m×6.25=€75m-years. Thus local parallel exposure matches. Award one mark for each monetary result.

Part B 4 marks

Compute the separate values’ changes and the new approximate surplus.

Assets change −€15m×(2×0.003−3×0.001)=−€45,000. Liabilities change −€12m×(1.25×0.003−5×0.001)=+€15,000. Surplus falls €60,000 to about €2.94m. Award one mark per result including direction and new balance.

Part C 3 marks

Specify a hedge adjustment and two residual risks.

Reduce relative short-key exposure and increase long-key exposure to approach liability sensitivities while maintaining parallel money duration. Residual risks include convexity, spread movements, liability inflation or changes in the schedule. Award one mark for the direction and one for each distinct residual.
