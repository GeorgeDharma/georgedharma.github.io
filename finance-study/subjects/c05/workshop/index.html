Professional workshop · C05

A liquidity reserve and a global equity allocation for a university endowment

A fictional euro-denominated endowment has €12.0m and must pay €1.20m in committed grants in twelve months. The investment committee requests a strategic allocation and foreign-currency overlay with a one-year implementation and review note. Work before grant payment, before fees, and in euros. The grant reserve is contractually segregated. No borrowing is allowed to buy securities. All capital-market assumptions and scenarios below are teaching inputs.

Your role
Institutional investment analyst
What to submit
Submit an investment policy implementation memorandum to trustees, with investable euro notionals, an explicit currency trade, a risk budget and an executable review dashboard. The custodian needs clear distinction between cash held for grants and resources available for collateral.

From inputs to decision

01

Reserve the grant before optimizing risk

Trustees cannot meet a fixed-date grant with a statistical average return. Economic availability by the actual due date is a hard constraint.

  1. Instruct the custodian to ring-fence €1.20m, 10% of €12m, in the specified euro maturity. That leaves €10.80m for bonds and equity. Keep this reserve separate from collateral for derivatives.
  2. The cash position contributes 0.10 × 2% = 0.20 percentage point to expected total return. The remaining 90% consequently needs to generate 5.80 points of total portfolio return, or 6.444% on its own capital.

Carry weights relative to the full €12m throughout. Quotes in the risky sleeve must be translated to total portfolio weights before testing committee limits.

02

Determine the smallest compliant equity allocation

With fixed cash, the 9%-return equity replaces a 4%-return bond euro for euro. The target therefore directly determines the minimum equity fraction.

  1. Let w be equity as a fraction of total capital. Bonds receive 0.90 − w. Expected return equals 0.10 × 2% + (0.90 − w) × 4% + w × 9% = 3.80% + 5% × w.
  2. Set this to 6% and obtain w = 0.44. Approve €5.28m global equities, €5.52m euro bonds and €1.20m cash. Expected annual income and appreciation in the model sum to €720,000 before grants and costs.

The return target binds. No novel estimate of optimality is required to observe this implication of the committee’s minimum-equity instruction.

03

Measure portfolio variance before hedging

The committee needs the combined exposure in the same currency and horizon. Adding standalone volatilities would incorrectly assume perfect positive co-movement.

  1. Weighted bond and equity volatilities are 0.46 × 0.06 = 0.0276 and 0.44 × 0.16 = 0.0704. Variance equals 0.0276² + 0.0704² + 2 × 0.20 × 0.0276 × 0.0704 = 0.006495136.
  2. Annual volatility is about 8.06%, within the 9% base ceiling. The equity contribution to variance is 0.0704² + 0.20 × 0.0276 × 0.0704 = 0.005344768, or about 82.29% of total risk on the additive Euler basis.

Report both 44% equity capital and 82.29% pre-overlay risk contribution. The latter is a concentration measure, not a forecast equity loss.

04

Test joint stress rather than isolated asset shocks

Portfolio diversification can weaken exactly when asset volatility rises. The mandate requires the stress covariance test before any overlay benefit is assumed.

  1. With stressed bond and equity volatilities the weighted figures become 0.0368 and 0.1012. Using correlation 0.60 gives stress volatility √(0.0368² + 0.1012² + 2 × 0.60 × 0.0368 × 0.1012) ≈ 12.67%.
  2. The 13% cap passes by only 0.33 percentage point. For the separate named shock the unhedged return is 0.10 × 2% + 0.46 × (−5%) + 0.44 × (−18%) = −10.02%, or −€1,202,400.

Label 12.67% as a stressed spread measure and −10.02% as one realized-scenario return. They should not be directly subtracted or treated as probabilities.

05

Size the currency overlay against the whole portfolio

A one-for-one hedge of the equity sleeve ignores correlation with its underlying business and the restriction on margin support.

  1. Covariance of total euro return with the long-dollar forward is 0.44 × 0.50 × 0.16 × 0.10 = 0.00352. Divide by forward variance 0.01 to obtain unconstrained short hedge weight 0.352, or €4.224m notional.
  2. At 5% collateral this would require €211,200, above €180,000. The executable cap is €180,000/0.05 = €3.60m or 30% of total capital. At €0.90 per dollar this corresponds to selling US$4.0m forward. It is below the equity sleeve’s €5.28m exposure.

Use €3.60m as trade notional. The 5% modeled reserve uses the full authorized collateral line, so additional margin needs require treasury escalation rather than use of grant cash.

06

Reprice residual currency and scenario risk

Derivatives change both market variation and cash requirements. The board must see the effect in the fund’s own reporting currency and actual cash availability.

  1. At hedge weight 0.30, base variance becomes 0.006495136 − 2 × 0.30 × 0.00352 + 0.30² × 0.01 = 0.005283136. The result is about 7.27% annual volatility. Modeled expected return remains 6% under zero expected forward gain.
  2. The named shock’s −4% long-dollar forward return generates a gain of 0.30 × 4% = 1.20% of total portfolio for the short. Portfolio shock return improves to −8.82% or −€1,058,400. A different dollar move could make the overlay lose cash even when longer-dated assets rise.

Record how actual futures or forward collateral is managed, the counterparty and settlement terms. The 5% policy amount is a sizing convention, not a guaranteed maximum market call.

07

Set executable rebalance and reporting instructions

A plan should tell the custodian what to trade now and the committee which assumptions may force a new decision.

  1. Instruct purchase of the euro cash reserve and bonds, and fund the equity holding without leverage. Obtain an executable forward quote, check counterparty limits, and record opening euro value, dollar notional, maturity, collateral account and authorized line.
  2. Report monthly time-weighted euro performance, the overlay contribution separately, projected grant liquidity, actual collateral, risk contributions and both volatility tests. Reassess before increasing notional if grant cash is needed earlier or the stressed pre-overlay risk exceeds 13%. The committee must approve funding, objective or asset-set changes.

Adopt a quarterly strategic review and an immediate review on projected collateral use above €180,000, revised grant timing or a breached risk limit.

Completed document

Investment committee memorandum · annual grant portfolio

Recommendation

Approve €1.20m in grant-matched euro cash, €5.52m euro bonds and €5.28m global equities. Authorize sale of US$4.00m forward against the euro at an executable market quote for the specified one-year hedge. Preserve the grant cash and use only the existing separate collateral authorization for the overlay.

Investment rationale

The 44% equity weight is the smallest satisfying the 6% expected-return objective under the approved assumptions. Pre-overlay volatility is 8.06% base and 12.67% stress. The equity sleeve carries 82.29% of modeled pre-overlay risk despite using 44% of capital. Trustees should expect an equity drawdown to dominate performance.

Currency and liquidity limits

The unconstrained minimum-variance short is €4.224m equivalent, which exceeds collateral capacity. The €3.60m authorized short uses €180,000 at the policy reserve rate and brings base volatility to about 7.27%. A collateral call exceeding the facility triggers treasury and trustee review before any new trade.

Performance and review

Use a euro benchmark matching strategic sleeve weights and show the overlay separately. At the stated joint market shock the expected mark is a loss of €1.0584m, with the grant reserve intact under its assumptions. Review monthly and escalate a revised cash need, an actual margin shortfall or a breach of the 13% pre-overlay stress ceiling.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

If expected global equity return rises to 10%, with cash and bond assumptions fixed, what minimum total equity weight now reaches the 6% target?

See the full solution

Expected return becomes 3.8% + (10% − 4%)w. Solve w = (6 − 3.8)/6 = 0.366667, or 36.67% of total capital. New bonds receive 53.33%, cash stays 10%, and risk and hedge sizing require fresh calculation.

Task 2

If policy collateral increases from 5% to 8% with the €180,000 facility unchanged, what maximum forward position can the custodian sell at the same spot equivalent? Enter USD millions.

See the full solution

Maximum euro-equivalent notional becomes €180,000/0.08 = €2.25m. Divide by €0.90 per US dollar to obtain US$2.50m. This is below the earlier unconstrained target.

Task 3

If the named market shock has the same security returns but a positive 5% return on the long-dollar forward, what is the approved portfolio’s net euro loss? Enter a positive number in EUR thousands.

See the full solution

Unhedged loss is €1,202,400. The €3.60m short loses 5%, or €180,000. Total loss is €1,382,400, equivalent to 11.52% of the initial €12m.

Task 4
