Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

Currency and household wealth

A household invests €90,000 in US$100,000 of shares at €0.90 per dollar. After one year the holding is US$110,000 and spot is €0.84. It sold US$80,000 forward at €0.92 per dollar. There are no costs. Consumer prices in euros rise by 3%.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A constrained investment committee

Assets A and B have expected returns of 5% and 11%, volatilities 10% and 20%, correlation zero and market betas 0.4 and 1.2. No shorting is allowed. The committee targets risky-portfolio expected return of 8%. A one-year cash investment yields 2%.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

State claims and missing risk

In bad and good states a share worth €90 pays €60 or €120. A bond costing €0.96 pays €1 in both states. A fund worth €1m additionally has market loading 1.1 and industry loading 0.4. Pure market and pure industry trackers each have unit loading on their own factor.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Evaluating an alleged anomaly

A signal appears to beat a benchmark by 3.2 percentage points annually before costs. Omitted risk exposure explains 1.1 points. Gross one-way traded notional equals five times initial equity per year, costing 0.30% per traded euro. A researcher tested 200 variants and reports the winner. Investors sold 40 of 120 gain opportunities and 20 of 100 loss opportunities.

Case 1 · 10 marks

Currency and household wealth

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the unhedged euro return and decompose its two drivers.

Local growth is 1.10. Dollar growth in euros is 0.84/0.90 = 0.93333. The product is 1.02667 for a 2.67% euro return, or €92,400 final value. Award a mark each for local, currency and combined result.

Part B 4 marks

Calculate actual hedged nominal and real euro returns.

US$80,000 delivers €73,600 and the US$30,000 remainder gives €25,200. Terminal €98,800 implies 9.78% nominal. Real return is 1.09778/1.03 − 1 ≈ 6.58%. Award a mark per step.

Part C 3 marks

Explain why the hedge was incomplete and give two reasons a different household could choose another size.

The initial US$100,000 position was only partly hedged and the € value of subsequent share gains was uncertain. A dollar spending liability can justify leaving dollars open. Available funding or hedge adjustment costs may constrain size. Award one mark per distinct point.

Case 2 · 10 marks

A constrained investment committee

Part A 3 marks

Size the risky mix and find its volatility.

The target implies equal weights. Variance is 0.5² × 0.1² + 0.5² × 0.2² = 0.0125. Volatility is 11.18%. Award one mark each for weights, variance and volatility.

Part B 4 marks

Find the minimum-variance mix and compare its mean and risk with the target mix.

Weight in A is 0.04/(0.01 + 0.04) = 0.8. Mean is 6.2%. Risk is √0.008 = 8.94%. It trades lower risk for lower return so neither of these two choices dominates the other. Award one mark per step and comparison.

Part C 3 marks

If half the capital is held in cash and half in the target risky mix, find beta, expected return and volatility.

Risky beta is 0.5×0.4 + 0.5×1.2 = 0.8, giving total beta 0.4. Expected return is 5% and volatility is about 5.59%. Award one mark per final result.

Case 3 · 10 marks

State claims and missing risk

Part A 4 marks

Infer state prices and the price of €100 downside protection paid only in the bad state.

Let b+g=0.96 and 60b+120g=90. Substitution yields g=0.54, b=0.42. Protection costs €42. Award one mark per equation setup, each state solution and protection price, up to four.

Part B 3 marks

If the physical probability of the bad state is 30%, compare state price per probability across outcomes and explain.

Bad is 0.42/0.30=1.40, good is 0.54/0.70≈0.771. A euro has greater discounted marginal value in the bad state under these prices. Award one mark per calculation and interpretation.

Part C 3 marks

Hedge the fund’s two exposures and identify what could still go wrong.

Short €1.1m of pure market tracker and €0.4m of pure industry tracker. Company-specific losses, unstable loadings, short costs or collateral calls can remain. Award one mark for each hedge and a specific residual exposure.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Evaluating an alleged anomaly

Part A 3 marks

Calculate risk-adjusted performance after trading and before any other fees.

Trading costs use five times capital, so the drag is 1.50 percentage points. Adjusted performance is 3.20 − 1.10 − 1.50 = 0.60 point. Award marks for basis, calculation and result.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate both sale propensities and assess their relevance to the price claim.

Gain propensity is 33.33%, loss propensity 20%, a 13.33 point gap. This is consistent with a disposition pattern in these data but does not by itself establish a cause or a profitable price anomaly. Award a mark for each rate and inference.

Part C 4 marks

Design a reproducible test of the alleged abnormal return.

Fix one rule and risk benchmark before evaluating an untouched period. Use point-in-time data including delistings. Deduct realistic execution costs and funding. Quantify sampling uncertainty and the effect of trying many rules. Award one mark for each distinct design element.
