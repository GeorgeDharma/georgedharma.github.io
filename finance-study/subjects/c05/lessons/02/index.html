Portfolio variance

Combine individual variances with covariances. An asset can reduce portfolio risk even when its standalone volatility is high.

The starting point

Imagine owning an umbrella shop and an ice-cream stand. If rainy weather helps one business and hurts the other, combining them can smooth earnings. Portfolio variance measures the spread of the combined returns. Covariance measures whether two assets tend to move together around their average returns.

For two assets, weight each variance by the square of its portfolio weight, then add twice the product of the weights and the covariance. To get volatility, take the square root. Covariance equals correlation multiplied by the two volatilities. A low correlation can help even if both assets fluctuate sharply on their own.

Theory and method

Markowitz portfolio variance and Euler risk contributions

Component volatility · Marginal contribution to risk

Sixty percent of a portfolio’s capital sits in an equity fund. How can that fund account for nearly 85% of its measured risk?

A €100 portfolio allocates €60 to an equity fund and €40 to a defensive fund. The positions do not bring the same volatility, and each can rise or fall with the rest of the portfolio. Markowitz’s covariance model measures their joint variation. Euler decomposition asks how much of total volatility comes from each weighted asset’s co-movement with the entire portfolio. It makes a risk budget comparable across positions with very different standalone volatilities.

How the result is built

RCᵢ = wᵢ Cov(Rᵢ,Rₚ)/σₚ, with ΣRCᵢ = σₚ

RCᵢ is asset i’s contribution in return-volatility units, wᵢ is its portfolio weight, Rᵢ and Rₚ are aligned asset and portfolio returns, Cov denotes covariance, and σₚ is portfolio volatility. Divide each contribution by σₚ to express percentage share of risk.

  1. Assume volatilities of 20% and 10% and correlation 0.25. Their covariance is 0.20 × 0.10 × 0.25 = 0.005.
  2. The equity covariance with the portfolio is 0.6 × 0.04 + 0.4 × 0.005 = 0.026. For the defensive fund it is 0.6 × 0.005 + 0.4 × 0.01 = 0.007.
  3. Weighted contributions to variance are 0.0156 and 0.0028. Total variance is 0.0184. Divide each by total variance to obtain volatility shares of 84.78% and 15.22%.

When the reasoning holds

  • Compute covariance from returns measured in the investor’s relevant currency and at matching times. Stale prices can depress measured co-movement.
  • The decomposition applies to the current position and covariance matrix. Liquidating the entire fund changes both portfolio volatility and the remaining positions’ shares.

Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
Correlation
A measure of how closely two series move together in a linear pattern.
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Diversification
Combining exposures that do not always move together to reduce dependence on a single outcome.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.

All notation examples

First example

Two equally weighted funds each have 20% volatility. With zero correlation, variance is 0.5² × 0.2² + 0.5² × 0.2² = 0.02. The portfolio’s volatility is about 14.1%.

Derive the covariance term

For two assets held throughout one period, portfolio return is w times the first return plus (1 − w) times the second. Subtract expected return and square. Expanding that square produces two squared deviations and two copies of their cross-product. Taking expectations gives the familiar two-asset variance expression.

The cross-product is positive when the two returns move to the same side of their means. Covariance reports its average in squared-return units. Correlation divides that covariance by both volatilities to express co-movement on a scale from −1 to +1. Keep decimal and percentage units consistent.

When risk can disappear in a classroom model

With exactly opposite standardized movements, one asset’s surprise can cancel the other’s. Matching weights requires matching the sizes of the weighted volatilities. Equal weights cancel only when the underlying volatilities are equal. Such an exact hedge depends on stable correlation and trading weights.

With positive correlation less than one, diversification can still lower risk relative to the average weighted volatility. The incremental effect of adding an asset depends on its covariance with the existing portfolio. An especially volatile addition can reduce total risk if the exposure it offsets is large enough.

What a covariance estimate represents

Historical covariance estimates describe co-movement in the observations sampled. Monthly returns, local currencies and stale bond prices can change the apparent result. A covariance measured in different currencies from the intended investment does not describe its final spending risk.

Asset weights drift when market values move. Rebalancing restores intended weights but creates trades and costs. Simulate high-correlation stress years as well as normal years when severe common shocks are the concern.

Method

Build risk from exposures

  1. Align

    Use returns measured for the same dates, periods, currency and reinvestment convention. Set value weights that sum to one for a fully invested unlevered portfolio.

  2. Calculate

    Convert correlation to covariance, add each weighted variance and twice the weighted covariance, then take the square root.

  3. Challenge

    Recompute after a plausible correlation shift or after one holding’s weight increases. State what risk the hedge actually removes.

Worked case

Two businesses, different amplitudes

An investor assigns two thirds to a business with annual volatility 15% and one third to another with volatility 30%. Their correlation is initially −1. Both assets are valued in the same currency, with fixed starting weights.

Working

  1. Weighted volatilities are (2/3) × 0.15 = 0.10 and (1/3) × 0.30 = 0.10. Variance at correlation −1 is 0.01 + 0.01 − 0.02 = 0.
  2. If correlation becomes −0.5, variance is 0.01 + 0.01 − 0.01 = 0.01. Underlying volatilities are identical to the initial assumptions, yet portfolio volatility becomes 10%.
  3. At correlation +1 the same portfolio has 20% volatility. This is a conditional stress calculation, not a forecast of a sudden realized loss of exactly 20%.

Interpretation

An exactly zero modeled variance makes dependence on a strong correlation assumption especially visible. Changing weights or exchange-rate exposure would also remove exact cancellation.

A common mistake

Adding portfolio volatilities instead of variances and covariances.

How to correct it

Add weighted volatilities directly only in the special case of perfectly positive correlation and nonnegative weights. For general co-movement, expand the squared deviations first.

Quick check
What would volatility be if their correlation rose to +1, with everything else unchanged?

Their fluctuations now move in step. The two equal positions each contribute half of 20%, adding to 20% volatility.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

Two equally weighted assets have volatilities 10% and 20% and correlation 0.25.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find the portfolio variance and volatility.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    If correlation were −1, what would the same weights achieve and what weights would cancel risk?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Weighted volatilities are 0.05 and 0.10. Variance is 0.0025 + 0.01 + 2 × 0.05 × 0.10 × 0.25 = 0.015. Volatility is √0.015 ≈ 12.25%.

Part B 2 marks

The residual volatility would be |0.05 − 0.10| = 5%. Cancellation requires w × 10% = (1 − w) × 20%, so assign two thirds to the 10%-volatility asset.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Sixty percent of a portfolio’s capital sits in an equity fund. How can that fund account for nearly 85% of its measured risk?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would show the committee both capital and risk weights. On these estimates, the equity fund contributes 0.0156 of the total 0.0184 variance, or almost 85% of volatility on an additive basis. If the mandate controls risk concentration, allocating only 60% of cash to equity does not establish compliance. I would repeat the analysis under stressed correlation and check the currency of the returns.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Can a long holding have a negative component risk contribution?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Yes. If its covariance with the whole portfolio is negative, its marginal addition reduces modeled volatility, producing a negative contribution at that position. Other assets’ positive contributions can then sum to more than 100% before subtracting this hedge contribution.
