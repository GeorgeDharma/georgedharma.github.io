Fund NAV and the ETF creation and redemption mechanism

Compute per-share net asset value and an exchange premium.

Theory and terminology

ETF creation and redemption arbitrage

authorized participant · net asset value · premium and discount

What you should be able to explain

  • Compute per-share net asset value and an exchange premium.
  • Test a creation or redemption using executable prices, the permitted basket and costs.

Identify the claim behind one share

A fund pools securities and cash on behalf of investors. Its net asset value, or NAV, is marked assets less liabilities. Divide by outstanding shares to obtain a reference value per share. Fees owed by the fund are liabilities even before cash is paid. Use one valuation time and currency across all holdings.

In a conventional open-end mutual fund, investors generally transact with the fund at a NAV determined under the fund’s dealing rules. An exchange-traded fund also has listed shares that change hands at bid and ask prices during the trading day. A buyer’s exchange purchase normally transfers an existing share between investors.

Connect primary and secondary markets

An authorized participant under an agreement with the ETF can create a designated block by delivering the prescribed securities and cash. It receives ETF shares and may sell or lend them. In a redemption it delivers a block of ETF shares and receives the specified basket or cash. Some funds use cash transactions or representative baskets that differ from the full index.

When ETF shares look expensive, a dealer evaluates buying the creation basket and selling ETF shares. When they look cheap, it evaluates the reverse. These trades can increase or decrease shares outstanding. Ordinary secondary trades typically leave the fund’s share count unchanged.

Price the actual transaction

The offer for securities to be purchased and the bid for those to be sold determine achievable gross proceeds. Include creation or redemption charges, financing, hedge slippage and any delivery or settlement delays. A gap from the last official closing NAV can merely reflect new information while underlying markets are closed.

Access to the basket, usable financing and the possibility of executing both legs matter. In a market disruption, the gap may persist because inventories are scarce or the dealer’s trading risk rises. Report both the accounting reference and the estimated all-in tradable value.

Method

Build the result

NAV = (A − L)/Q. Premium = P/NAV − 1. Creation profit = q × bidETF − Cbasket − Cother.

A is the marked value of all assets, L fund liabilities, Q outstanding shares, and NAV currency per share. P is the chosen contemporaneous exchange reference price. q is the creation-unit size, bidETF the executable per-share sale price, Cbasket the cash cost of delivering the required basket and Cother incremental expenses for that trade.

  1. Subtract all liabilities from assets before dividing by the existing share count. Specify whether the quoted premium uses the midpoint, bid or ask.
  2. For creation, estimate cash paid for the actual basket, including any required balancing cash. Use the ETF bid if selling immediately.
  3. Compare total sale proceeds with every incremental cost on the same block. Reverse the trading sides and values for a redemption.
Worked case

An authorized participant tests a creation

An ETF holds €50m of securities and €1.4m of cash, owes €0.9m and has 1m shares. A 50,000-share block can be created. The contemporaneous exchange quote is €50.63 bid and €50.69 ask. The executable creation basket plus balancing cash costs €2,527,000. Additional fees and funding are €1,500. Assume the quotes cover the full block.

Working

  1. Net assets are €50.5m and NAV is €50.50. The €50.66 exchange midpoint represents a 0.317% premium to NAV.
  2. Selling 50,000 ETF shares at the bid raises €2,531,500. The midpoint would overstate proceeds by €1,500.
  3. Total creation cost is €2,528,500. The minimum sale price to cover it is €50.57 per share.
  4. The modeled profit is €3,000, or €0.06 per share, after the stated costs. Creating additional shares increases supply if the trade completes.

Decision and interpretation

Proceed only at mutually executable prices and size under the agreement. A six-cent adverse difference per share consumes the modeled profit. An investor who cannot transact in the creation unit cannot assume the same economics.

Quick check
An investor buys 2,000 existing ETF shares on the exchange from another investor. What usually changes in the fund itself solely because of that trade?

An exchange trade transfers ownership of existing shares. A separate creation or redemption with the fund changes its shares outstanding.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A dealer can buy 40,000 ETF shares at the executable ask of $24.82 for one redemption unit. Liquidating the delivered basket would return $994,400 including balancing cash. Additional fees and finance cost $1,000. Evaluate the redemption.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The ETF shares cost 40,000 × $24.82 = $992,800.
  2. The delivered assets generate $994,400 − $1,000 = $993,400 net. Expected surplus is $600, or 1.5 cents per share.
  3. A worsening of basket liquidation price by more than $0.015 per ETF share consumes the surplus. The dealer should confirm the quoted size and settlement timing.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

When can a professional dealer profitably close a gap between an ETF’s exchange price and its holdings?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A dealer can exploit the gap only if the authorized transaction and exchange trade are executable together at prices covering the whole block. In the creation example the basket plus costs works out to €50.57 per share. Selling at the €50.63 bid leaves six cents. An official NAV of €50.50 alone cannot establish that profit.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Additional market impact is estimated at two cents per ETF share. Does the trade still close the discount profitably?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Impact costs another $800 on 40,000 shares, leaving a $200 loss. The headline discount can persist because the marginal dealer cannot realize enough cash under those conditions.
