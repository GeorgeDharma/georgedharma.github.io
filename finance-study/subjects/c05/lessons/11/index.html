Building an equity index and maintaining its divisor

Calculate price-weighted, equal-weighted and free-float capitalization-weighted returns from identical stocks.

Theory and terminology

Index weighting and divisor continuity

price weighting · capitalization weighting · index divisor

What you should be able to explain

  • Calculate price-weighted, equal-weighted and free-float capitalization-weighted returns from identical stocks.
  • Adjust a price-weighted index divisor so a stock split creates no artificial return.

Choose what one unit of the benchmark owns

In a price-weighted index, a one-dollar movement by either constituent has the same initial effect on the index level. A company with a higher share price consequently carries more weight at the start of a measurement interval. Its shares can be expensive per share simply because the company chose to issue fewer shares.

A float-capitalization-weighted index allocates weight to the market value available for public investment. Multiply each price by eligible public shares and divide by their total value. An equal-weighted index begins the interval with the same capital in each name. Its weights drift with relative returns until the methodology next rebalances.

Construct the return before selecting a comparison

For one interval without cash contributions, portfolio return is the sum of starting weights times each constituent return. A price-return index ignores ordinary dividends under the assumption here. A total-return version credits reinvested distributions according to a specified convention. Align currency, dividends and observation dates when comparing index figures.

Changes in constituents are reconstitution. Restoring target weights among existing constituents is rebalancing. A rebalance can generate real trades and costs for a fund mirroring a benchmark. The index itself generally follows a published calculation rule that may exclude those trading costs.

Keep corporate actions economically neutral

A two-for-one split doubles the share count and halves the price per share. The holder has the same wealth immediately on either side of the split. A capitalization calculation with updated shares handles that mechanical change directly.

A price index based on the sum of constituent prices needs a divisor adjustment. Solve for the new divisor using the prior closing index and the comparable post-action price sum. Future price sensitivity can change even though the level at the transition stays constant.

Method

Build the result

wᵢ = PᵢQᵢ/Σ(PⱼQⱼ). R = Σwᵢrᵢ. Dnew = ΣPafter / Ibefore for a price-weighted split adjustment.

Pᵢ is the starting price, Qᵢ eligible free-float shares, wᵢ starting market-value weight and rᵢ the interval’s stock return. j ranges across stocks in the sum. R is the index return. ΣPafter is the sum of comparable prices immediately after the corporate action, Ibefore the prior index level and Dnew its replacement divisor.

  1. Specify the universe and whether public float or all shares enter market value. Starting market weights must sum to one.
  2. For price weighting use starting prices as the numerator instead of starting market values. For equal weighting divide one by the starting number of constituents.
  3. Apply individual returns to the chosen starting weights. Separately enforce index continuity at a purely mechanical split whose prices contain no market movement.
Worked case

Opposite returns on the same two shares

A trades at €80 with 4m shares, half freely traded. B trades at €20 with 15m shares, 80% freely traded. A falls to €76 and B rises to €22. There are no dividends. A price index starts at 100 with divisor 1. After the close A splits two for one.

Working

  1. Free float is 2m shares of A and 12m of B. Starting eligible market values are €160m and €240m, so weights are 40% and 60%.
  2. A returns −5% and B +10%. Float-weighted return is 0.40 × (−5%) + 0.60 × 10% = +4%. Ending float value confirms €416m, up from €400m.
  3. The price-weighted index falls from 100 to 76 + 22 = 98, returning −2%. Equal weight returns 0.5 × (−5% + 10%) = +2.5%.
  4. The split changes A’s comparison price to €38. Keep the index at 98 by setting the new divisor to (38 + 22)/98 = 0.612245. Updated shares keep A’s float market value at €152m.

Decision and interpretation

Identify the benchmark rule in the investment mandate. A 4% portfolio return could perfectly match one published index and beat another by six percentage points on this day.

Quick check
After a pure split, the comparable sum of prices is 90 and the intended continuing price-index level is 120. What divisor preserves continuity?

Index level equals price sum divided by the divisor, so the divisor must be 90/120 = 0.75.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

Two shares start at $40 and $60 with free floats of 8m and 2m. The first rises 5% and the second falls 10%. A price-weighted index starts at 100 using divisor 1. The second share splits two for one immediately after the close. Compute both index returns and the new divisor.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Eligible values are $320m and $120m. Float-weighted return is (320/440) × 5% + (120/440) × (−10%) = +0.9091%.
  2. Closing prices are $42 and $54, so the price index closes at 96 and returns −4%.
  3. After the split use $42 and $27. A divisor of 69/96 = 0.71875 preserves the closing index.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can two accurate stock indices move in opposite directions on the same day?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. The index defines a portfolio. The high-priced stock has 80% initial weight in the price index and only 40% of eligible float value. Its decline dominates the first index. Gains by the other stock dominate the second. With identical prices the price index loses 2% and the float index gains 4%.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the first stock also pays a cash dividend, does the same comparison describe an investor’s full wealth change?

Compare with the follow-up answer

A price-only index omits the distribution. Recalculate with dividends in each constituent’s total return and follow the benchmark’s reinvestment policy. The choice of initial weights still changes each stock’s contribution.
