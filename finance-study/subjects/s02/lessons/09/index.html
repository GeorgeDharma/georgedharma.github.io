Contract design and monitoring

Select maturity, amortization, security, covenants and reporting frequency. Explain which incentive or information problem each provision is meant to address.

The starting point

Loan terms shape incentives and determine when the lender receives information. A covenant is a contractual condition, such as maintaining enough cash coverage or limiting new secured borrowing. Reporting deadlines let a lender test the condition and investigate deterioration. When a breach occurs, the contract specifies rights and available responses.

A borrowing base links the permissible drawing to eligible assets using advance rates. Eligibility rules can exclude disputed, old or concentrated receivables because their apparent value may not be collected. A limit can control availability without guaranteeing that the borrower generates enough cash to repay.

Theory and method

Maintenance and incurrence covenants

Net leverage headroom · Equity cure

A borrower is inside its leverage limit today. Why can a quarterly maintenance covenant give the lender rights sooner than an incurrence covenant with the same threshold?

Management can become more leveraged even without borrowing another euro if earnings fall. A maintenance covenant measures the balance-sheet ratio periodically. An incurrence covenant can test it only when the firm takes a defined action, such as adding debt or paying a dividend. Two documents with the same printed number can therefore produce different lender rights after the same contraction in earnings. A cure changes the outcome only as permitted by its actual definition.

How the result is built

Leverage = N/E; minimum compliant E = N/L; maximum compliant N = LE

N is eligible net debt in euros, E is contractually accepted earnings in euros, and L is the maximum leverage multiple. The case excludes new add-backs and uses the testing-date figures.

  1. At €96m debt, €32m earnings and a 3.5 multiple ceiling, actual leverage is 3.0. Earnings can fall to €27.43m before the maintenance ceiling is reached.
  2. If earnings fall to €25m with unchanged debt, leverage rises to 3.84. Reducing net debt to 3.5 × €25m = €87.5m requires an €8.5m paydown.
  3. A permitted cure credited solely to denominator earnings requires €96m/3.5 − €25m ≈ €2.43m of deemed earnings. The legal effect differs from true debt reduction.

When the reasoning holds

  • The maintenance test has an agreed quarterly measurement date, calculation, notice and cure period. Rights do not automatically arise on every intraday movement.
  • Cash injections counted as deemed earnings can be restricted in size or frequency and may prohibit double counting as reduced debt. Treat the case definition as binding.
Terms in this lesson
Covenant
A promise or condition in a financing agreement, such as maintaining an agreed level of cash.
Borrowing base
A lending limit calculated from eligible collateral after applying agreed advance rates and deductions.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
First example

An importer has €200,000 of invoices, including €40,000 that are ineligible. At a 75% advance rate, the borrowing base is €120,000. Against an existing drawing of €100,000, further availability is €20,000, assuming the facility limit is above the borrowing base.

Write a term for a specific economic risk

A maturity date answers how long the bank is exposed. Amortization determines how fast principal declines. Security addresses losses if repayment fails. Covenants can restrict behaviour or give early warning. Requiring a report is worthwhile when someone can obtain it, verify it and act on it in time.

A maintenance covenant tests an agreed threshold regularly. An incurrence covenant may test a threshold only when a specified action occurs, such as raising more debt. A borrower can pass an incurrence test at origination but weaken between transactions. Contract definitions and test dates decide which right a lender actually has.

Compute and interpret headroom

For a minimum coverage ratio, headroom may be expressed as actual coverage minus the minimum or as how far sustainable cash could fall before breach. For a maximum leverage covenant, lower debt or higher earnings generally create room. State the unit of headroom, since ratio points and euros mean different things.

Management may change reporting choices, postpone expenses or add back costs in adjusted earnings. Define permissible adjustments, cross-check statements, and test whether the ratio still represents cash risk. An overly tight term can generate repeated waivers for ordinary seasonality.

Monitor asset-based lending with discipline

Receivable eligibility, advance rates, overall commitment size and sublimits jointly determine availability. Outstanding drawings reduce what can still be borrowed. Concentration caps can leave a large customer invoice partially excluded even when it is current. Know the order in which the contract applies eligibility and caps.

A falling base can leave existing debt above the allowed borrowing amount. That overadvance calls for a response under the agreement, rather than assuming the borrower can use remaining headline commitment. Ongoing reporting should detect disputed invoices, aging or related-party sales.

Method

Translate risks into terms

  1. Identify

    Match each risk to an observable indicator and specify who reports it, when and with what reconciliation.

  2. Calculate

    Apply contractual definitions, eligibility restrictions and caps in the stated order, then compare calculated availability with drawings and commitment.

  3. Respond

    Set a meaningful reporting trigger and verify possible remedies, such as curtailing further draws or requesting a cure under the contract.

Worked case

A cap hidden beneath the headline line

A €500,000 committed facility lends against eligible invoices. From €650,000 face, €70,000 is disputed and €80,000 is overdue, with no overlap. A contractual concentration cap removes another €50,000. The advance rate is 80% and €330,000 is drawn.

Working

  1. Eligible collateral is €650,000 − €70,000 − €80,000 − €50,000 = €450,000. Its advance supports €360,000, below the €500,000 commitment.
  2. Remaining headroom is €30,000. If a further €60,000 previously eligible customer invoice is disputed, the base becomes €390,000 × 0.8 = €312,000 and drawings exceed it by €18,000.
  3. Review dispute correspondence and subsequent collections, stop assuming new availability, and examine the cure period and cash sources specified by the agreement.

Interpretation

The credit memorandum should distinguish a collateral shortfall, a missed payment and an event of default. One may lead to another, depending on the precise terms and timing of any remedy.

A common mistake

Assuming ‘€500,000 committed’ always means €170,000 undrawn and available.

How to correct it

Take the lower of the borrowing base and the commitment, then subtract drawings and any separately reserved amounts before describing available cash.

Quick check
A further €20,000 of previously eligible invoices becomes disputed. What headroom remains?

Eligible invoices fall to €140,000. At 75% the base is €105,000, leaving €5,000 over the existing €100,000 drawing.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

A loan requires net debt divided by accepted EBITDA at or below 3.0 times. Net debt is €9m and reported EBITDA is €3.5m, including €0.7m of adjustments prohibited under the agreement.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute covenant leverage and explain compliance.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    How much net debt reduction restores the threshold with the same accepted EBITDA?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Accepted EBITDA is €2.8m. Leverage is €9m/€2.8m ≈ 3.21, above the 3.0 ceiling. Reported adjusted EBITDA would give a misleading pass.

Part B 2 marks

Maximum net debt is 3.0 × €2.8m = €8.4m. Reduce net debt by €0.6m using a source that actually reduces the defined net balance. Review timing and cure rights.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A borrower is inside its leverage limit today. Why can a quarterly maintenance covenant give the lender rights sooner than an incurrence covenant with the same threshold?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask when the ratio is tested and what action it governs. With €96m debt and €25m accepted earnings the ratio is 3.84. A maintenance test can create a breach on its measurement date, while an incurrence test may only block the next restricted financing. I would verify notice and cure rights, then distinguish any technical cure from an enduring improvement in cash capacity.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The sponsor says it can contribute €3m only once and the contract permits only debt reduction. Does that cure the breach?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Debt falls to €93m, and €93m/€25m = 3.72, above 3.5. A further €5.5m paydown, eligible earnings improvement, or negotiated waiver would be needed under the stated test. The sponsor cannot call the €3m a denominator adjustment under this contract.
