Capacity and debt service

Compare cash available for debt service with scheduled principal and interest. Test a difficult period because an acceptable annual average can conceal a missed instalment.

The starting point

The debt-service coverage ratio divides cash available for debt service by the interest and scheduled principal payable over the same period. A ratio of 1.2 means €1.20 is available per €1 due. Analysts specify precisely which cash items and obligations the calculation includes.

Calculate coverage on actual payment dates as well as annual totals. A seasonal company can have enough cash for the whole year and still be unable to make a spring payment. Reserve balances and reliable unused facilities may bridge a timing gap if they are accessible when needed.

Theory and method

Loan life coverage ratio and debt-service sculpting

LLCR · Debt service coverage ratio

Can a two-year loan exceed its cash-flow capacity over its life even though a particular annual coverage test passes?

An infrastructure lender is paid out of future project cash. One year’s coverage ratio compares that year’s cash with its scheduled payment. The loan life coverage ratio puts all cash before maturity on a common valuation date and compares that value with outstanding principal. A long grace period can make early annual coverage look generous while leaving a terminal balloon too large. Conversely, strong lifetime value can coexist with a missed payment in an early dry season.

How the result is built

LLCR = Σ CFADSₜ/(1 + r)ᵗ ÷ D₀; sculpted paymentₜ = CFADSₜ/k

t indexes year-end dates through loan maturity. CFADSₜ is euros available before debt service in year t. r is the case discount rate per year, D₀ is initial euro principal, k is required annual coverage, and Σ adds all dated discounted amounts.

  1. Two annual €90,000 receipts discounted at 5% are worth €85,714.29 and €81,632.65, together €167,346.94. A €150,000 loan has LLCR about 1.116 times.
  2. At required annual coverage of 1.20, each payment can reach €90,000/1.20 = €75,000. Discount both affordable payments at the contractual 5% loan rate.
  3. Their present value is about €139,455.78. This is maximum fully amortizing initial principal under the stated assumptions. The €150,000 request requires more cash, a different term or a residual payment.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use only cash available before the specified maturity. Post-maturity cash requires an enforceable extension or refinancing assumption.
  • Discounting at the loan rate produces the initial principal for this fixed-rate loan with normal interest accrual. Reserve balances and distributions are excluded unless the definition permits them.
Terms in this lesson
Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
Cash available to meet scheduled debt payments divided by the principal and interest due for the period.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Coupon
The interest payment specified in a bond’s terms.
How to read the notation
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

A borrower generates €35,000 in the first half and €95,000 in the second. It owes €50,000 each half. Annual coverage is €130,000/€100,000 = 1.30, while first-half coverage is 0.70. With no opening cash, the first payment is €15,000 short.

Coverage and capacity are separate calculations

Coverage describes a proposed payment schedule. Divide cash available by debt service over the same period. Capacity asks the reverse question, how much payment can cash support under a required coverage floor? Divide cash by the floor, then subtract payments already promised to other lenders.

A coverage threshold such as 1.25 times belongs to the stated contract or credit policy. It is not a universal safe level. Volatile margins, customer concentration and bullet maturities can justify different sizing decisions for borrowers with the same current ratio.

Turn a service limit into a loan amount

A service limit is a payment, not a principal balance. For an amortizing fixed-rate loan, price the series of affordable payments. If each end-of-year payment is A, rate r and term n, the principal is A times [1 − (1 + r) to the power −n] divided by r. Check that the last payment clears the balance.

A grace period that delays principal may increase early coverage yet leave a larger later burden. Evaluate peak service and terminal debt. A cash reserve can fund a temporary mismatch but does not produce recurring earnings. Specify whether its release is permitted under the lender’s terms.

Test the dates before approving

A six-month crop cycle can leave an exporter cash-poor at an interim coupon date even with excellent annual coverage. Use an opening balance and sequential cash roll-forward. Draw on a reserve only once and carry its remaining balance forward.

A projected balance at a date before collection should exclude customer money that arrives afterwards. Stress the dates as well as the totals, for instance a late shipment or currency conversion, then examine whether a committed bridge or a revised repayment date is economically reasonable.

Method

Size a proposed facility

  1. Set the service ceiling

    Apply the required coverage floor to sustainable cash under base and downside projections. Subtract existing debt service to obtain capacity for the new contract.

  2. Price the schedule

    Translate annual or quarterly affordable payments into principal using the matching rate and dates. Assess a balloon separately.

  3. Check liquidity

    Roll balances through actual dues and collections, including opening unrestricted cash and permitted reserve use.

Worked case

A three-year amortizing offer

A firm expects €360,000 annual cash available and owes existing lenders €150,000 a year. Policy requires 1.20 times coverage. The new loan charges 5% annually, with three equal year-end payments and no fees.

Working

  1. Maximum total service is €360,000/1.20 = €300,000. The new facility can use €150,000 annual service. The three-year annuity factor at 5% is about 2.72325.
  2. The implied maximum principal is €150,000 × 2.72325 ≈ €408,487. A €450,000 request would need about €165,243 annually and exceed the base ceiling.
  3. If cash falls 20% to €288,000, the service cap is €240,000 and the new loan has room for only €90,000 annually, supporting about €245,092.

Interpretation

This is a scenario-dependent sizing range. Checking the dates, customer concentration and recovery support remains necessary before a credit decision.

At the board

An annual coverage pass can conceal a missed instalment

The exporter starts with €0.20m cash. Cash available for debt service varies by quarter and scheduled principal plus interest is €0.60m each quarter. No overdraft has been arranged. The last column shows the theoretical balance if every payment could be made.

Annual debt-service coverage is 3.00 / 2.40 = 1.25×. The first due date is short by €0.10m, which must be funded or renegotiated before the borrower can follow the schedule. Delaying €0.10m of Q1 principal until Q2 removes that specific gap in this simple case, provided the lender agrees and interest costs do not change. A forecast with customer-by-customer collection dates is the next check.

A common mistake

Reporting €150,000 of affordable annual service as the maximum loan balance.

How to correct it

Cash capacity and loan principal have different units through time. State the interest rate, maturity and repayment profile before converting one into the other.

Quick check
How much opening cash would allow the borrower to meet both payments on time, assuming no other cash flows?

€35,000 of first-half cash plus €15,000 covers the first €50,000. The €95,000 arriving in the second half covers the remaining €50,000.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

A borrower generates €210,000 annually. Existing service is €100,000 and the lender requires 1.4 times coverage. A two-year loan has equal year-end payments at 0% for this exercise.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Size the new principal and give the payment path.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    If first-year available cash falls to €168,000, how much extra annual service fits the floor then?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Total permitted service is €210,000/1.4 = €150,000, leaving €50,000 annually. At 0%, two €50,000 repayments support €100,000 principal.

Part B 2 marks

The first-year cap falls to €120,000. Existing service leaves €20,000 for the new facility, €30,000 below the proposed payment. The original plan would need redesign or verified support acceptable under the policy.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a two-year loan exceed its cash-flow capacity over its life even though a particular annual coverage test passes?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would test both the scheduled dates and the entire loan life. Here annual payments must stay under €75,000 to preserve 1.20 times coverage. Discounting that schedule supports €139,456, below the requested €150,000 despite €180,000 nominal operating cash. I would show the shortfall and test the downside rather than accepting a balloon solely because the first year’s instalment looks affordable.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If all €180,000 arrives in year two and there is no opening cash, what maximum principal fits the same sculpting rule?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Year one supports zero cash payment and year two supports €150,000. Discounting €150,000 over two years at 5% gives €136,054.42. This requires a contract that permits interest to accrue through year one. A mandatory first-year cash coupon would require another source.
