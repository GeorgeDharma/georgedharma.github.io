Borrower and purpose

Distinguish commercial, household and private-bank lending. A home mortgage, an inventory line and a securities-backed loan have different primary repayment sources.

The starting point

Start a credit file with two questions. What will the borrower buy or pay, and what cash will repay the bank at each due date? Purpose, term and repayment source should fit. A seasonal importer may repay when customers pay invoices. A household usually services a home loan from recurring income.

A loan against a portfolio of securities may be repaid from other resources or by selling investments. Market changes can trigger a demand for extra collateral before scheduled maturity. In each case, identify the primary source, any fallback, and the events that could damage both at once.

Theory and method

The five Cs of credit

Character, capacity, capital, collateral and conditions · Primary and secondary repayment

A prosperous founder guarantees the debt of a loss-making trading company. How do the five Cs change your approval decision?

A distributor wants a bank to fund a €120,000 delivery. The founder promises help if customers pay late. A credit officer has to establish which cash is available on the due date and what the founder can still contribute in that same bad state. The five Cs organize distinct pieces of evidence. Character concerns willingness and reporting reliability, capacity concerns cash service, capital concerns the owner’s stake, collateral concerns recoveries, and conditions concern the business environment and purpose.

How the result is built

Payment gap on date t = amount due on t − verified cash usable on t

The date t identifies the contractual deadline. All amounts are euros at the borrowing entity or transferred to it in time. A positive gap is the amount still requiring an enforceable source.

  1. Map trade collection dates and the account receiving each payment. €85,000 collected before a €120,000 bullet leaves €35,000 uncovered.
  2. Verify the founder’s independent resources after personal debt and any other guarantees. An enforceable €20,000 available that day still leaves €15,000.
  3. Evaluate collateral that can be sold later in a separate recovery analysis. A €50,000 forced sale three months later may reduce eventual loss but cannot settle the current €15,000 gap.

When the reasoning holds

  • A guarantee changes the funding ladder only if it can be called and collected by the due date.
  • Sales to related companies or customers sharing the same source of finance can make apparently independent evidence of capacity and collateral deteriorate together.
Terms in this lesson
Primary repayment source
The cash a lender expects will repay a loan in the normal course, such as operating receipts or household income.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Working capital
Money tied up in day-to-day operations. For a deal, agree which receivables, stock and payables count.
Margin
In business accounts, profit expressed relative to sales. In derivatives trading, margin is collateral required to support a position.
How to read the notation
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.

All notation examples

First example

A bakery wants €100,000 for an oven with an eight-year useful life and €30,000 for holiday ingredients. The oven could be financed with installments sized to its recurring operating cash. A seasonal line can finance ingredients and be repaid from holiday receipts.

Identify the economic borrower

The name signing the contract is the legal borrower. The business, person or property expected to supply repayment may be different. A holding company borrowing to buy a subsidiary relies on dividends or asset sales upstreamed from that subsidiary. A profitable subsidiary can still be unable to distribute cash when its own obligations are due.

Draw the path from the external customer to the account that repays the lender. At each transfer ask who controls the money, which commitments come first, whether the currency changes and when it can legally move. A guarantee creates another potential claim with its own conditions.

Match the instrument to the purchase

Working capital buys stock or funds customer invoices. On completion of a cycle, collections can repay a revolving facility that becomes available for another draw. A productive machine typically releases its cost through years of operating cash. An instalment loan can spread repayment across those years.

A mortgage on a home commonly depends on household income for scheduled payments. A private banking facility secured by traded assets can have a rapid margin call when asset prices fall. Similar collateral values across the two cases conceal different payment calendars and liquidation risks.

Distinguish repayment from refinancing

A bullet loan can appear affordable because the borrower pays only interest until a large terminal payment. Identify whether final proceeds come from a contracted sale, accumulated cash or a fresh lender. If a new financing market has to be open on one particular date, refinancing risk is material.

A stated purpose can change after funds leave the bank. Draw conditions, supplier invoices and cash controls can provide evidence of how money is used. The value of each control depends on what it verifies and how early it alerts the lender to a change.

Method

Form the approval hypothesis

  1. Purpose

    Identify the purchase, which entity receives it and what changes if the bank refuses the requested amount.

  2. Repayment

    Build dated primary and fallback cash streams and compare their timing with interest and principal.

  3. Structure

    Choose maturity, amortization and monitoring that follow those streams. Write down the condition that would invalidate approval.

Worked case

One customer asks for a single bullet

A wholesaler requests €240,000 due in six months. €80,000 pays for export inventory that a reliable buyer is expected to pay for in four months. €160,000 fits out a warehouse and is expected to generate €50,000 annual cash after operating costs. Ignore interest initially.

Working

  1. A four-month collection of €80,000 can repay the stock advance if goods are delivered and payment reaches the pledged account. Verify delivery, buyer acceptance and customer concentration.
  2. At most about half of an evenly earned €50,000 reaches the account over six months. That leaves €135,000 of the fit-out principal unfunded at maturity even if expectations hold.
  3. Separating a seasonal advance from a suitably sized term loan makes the weaknesses visible. Four annual principal instalments of €40,000 use most of the annual cash before interest and stress.

Interpretation

A first claim on warehouse equipment could improve recovery after default. It does little to resolve the timing gap in the original bullet request.

A common mistake

Calling a long-lived purchase a working-capital need because the supplier invoice is due this month.

How to correct it

Trace how the money spent is recovered. The invoice deadline determines when the business needs funds. The useful life and resulting cash determine when it can repay.

Quick check
What is the main weakness of placing the entire €130,000 on a facility due immediately after the holiday?

The two purchases generate cash at different speeds. A short repayment deadline could force refinancing of the equipment even when it is economically useful.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

A parent asks for €300,000 to be repaid in one year. Its only asset is a subsidiary expected to produce €420,000 of cash. Senior subsidiary obligations absorb €250,000 before any dividend and the parent has no other cash.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find the maximum cash reaching the parent under these assumptions.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Identify one document and one structure change that bear directly on the weakness.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

At most €420,000 − €250,000 = €170,000 reaches the parent, subject to permission to distribute. This leaves at least €130,000 principal unpaid before interest.

Part B 2 marks

Inspect subsidiary debt terms or the distribution covenant to verify dividend capacity. Reduce or extend the parent debt to the amounts and dates of lawful transfers, or obtain separately verified repayment resources.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A prosperous founder guarantees the debt of a loss-making trading company. How do the five Cs change your approval decision?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would lead with the cash path at the company and document the purpose of the borrowing. Here the founder helps, but even after verified support the due date lacks €15,000. I would test whether a shorter operating cycle, smaller advance or scheduled repayment tied to customer collections closes it. I would then examine the guarantee’s enforceability and independent value as protection if the base plan fails.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the founder’s entire guarantee comes from selling shares in the borrower. What does that change?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The guarantee now depends heavily on the same enterprise that is already unable to repay. I would stress share value and the time required to sell under distress, and obtain evidence of separate assets or committed third-party funds before giving the guarantee independent credit.
