Statements and cash available

Reconcile reported earnings to cash after taxes, maintenance investment and changes in operating working capital. Document the definition of available cash in the case.

The starting point

Accounting profit recognizes transactions when earned or incurred. A lender needs cash that can actually reach the loan payment date. In a simple operating model, start with earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization, commonly called EBITDA. Then deduct relevant cash taxes, necessary investment and the cash absorbed by operating working capital.

Define each adjustment. A rise in unpaid customer invoices consumes cash. An extension of supplier credit can release it. Financial statements, covenants and project agreements can use different definitions of cash available for debt service. Avoid deducting interest twice if the number will be compared against both principal and interest.

Theory and method

Cash flow available for debt service

CFADS bridge · Cash conversion of EBITDA

Management pays most of its suppliers by selling receivables to a factor. Where might a reported improvement in cash conversion overstate repayment capacity?

A wholesaler sells €100 of invoices just before year-end and receives €95 immediately. The collection improves its bank balance, but the outstanding invoices will not deliver another €100 to the lender next month. With recourse, the wholesaler can also be asked to pay the factor when customers default. Analysts reconcile earnings, underlying operating working capital and financing-like receipts so they can separate repeatable cash generation from a one-time acceleration of collections.

How the result is built

CFADS = EBITDA − T − M − ΔW + J

CFADS is operating cash available before interest and principal. EBITDA is operating earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization. T is cash tax, M is maintenance investment, ΔW is the increase in underlying operating working capital, and J includes explicitly specified operating cash adjustments. All amounts cover one entity and period in euros.

  1. Start with €430,000 recurring EBITDA, €40,000 cash tax and €70,000 maintenance investment. This leaves €320,000 before changes in working capital.
  2. An underlying increase in inventories and receivables net of trade payables absorbs €90,000. Sustainable CFADS under the case definition is €230,000.
  3. A new €95,000 factor advance raises current cash availability to €325,000 before financing payments. Model its subsequent settlement or withheld collections separately so the same invoices do not support two periods.

When the reasoning holds

  • The case includes the full underlying receivable change in ΔW. Using a balance already reduced by the transaction would require reconstructing the gross balance first.
  • Contracts differ over recourse, fees, reserves and treatment of advances. Read the factor’s waterfall and loan covenant definitions before classifying its cash flows.

Terms in this lesson
Working capital
Money tied up in day-to-day operations. For a deal, agree which receivables, stock and payables count.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Check how the seller calculates and adjusts this measure.
Covenant
A promise or condition in a financing agreement, such as maintaining an agreed level of cash.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
First example

A borrower reports €900,000 EBITDA. Cash tax is €100,000, upkeep investment €150,000 and additional operating working capital €250,000. Assume no other cash flows. Cash available before principal and interest is €400,000.

Understand the bridge from earnings to cash

A lender’s starting number determines the adjustments required. Starting from EBITDA avoids reversing interest and ordinary depreciation a second time. Starting from net income requires addressing tax, interest and noncash expenses already included in that number. Label the starting subtotal before touching the calculator.

Earnings generated by an unpaid sale can increase profit immediately. If an invoice remains uncollected, the increase in operating receivables generally reduces cash relative to that profit. Conversely, an increase in trade payables may preserve cash during the period. Repaying overdue suppliers may produce the opposite effect later.

Measure investment and recurring earnings

A factory needs upkeep to produce future cash. Deduct maintenance capital expenditure even if management could briefly postpone it. Expansion investment may be discretionary or contractually unavoidable. Identify the case’s definition and timing before deciding how to treat it in a coverage analysis.

A one-time cash grant, unpaid legal claim or profit on an asset sale changes the quality of the reported subtotal. Reconcile actual cash with a sustainable operating forecast. Identify which costs return next year. Sale proceeds themselves may be available for a one-off debt reduction even if recurring operating capacity is weak.

Keep periods and claimholders aligned

Cash flow available for debt service should be measured before the interest and principal in the denominator when both are treated as payment obligations. If interest has already reduced the cash numerator, adding the same interest again to the denominator understates capacity.

Cash held in an entity restricted from transferring it may be unavailable to the particular borrower. Include only amounts available in the stated period and currency. A consolidated cash-flow statement can conceal restricted balances or financing provided by selling invoices with recourse.

Method

Build a reconcilable schedule

  1. Start

    Label the earnings measure, the entity, the period, and which party’s cash will be assessed.

  2. Adjust

    Show taxes paid, cash investment and operating working-capital movements separately. Explain any nonrecurring gain or cash charge.

  3. Cross-check

    Compare opening and closing cash, reconcile financing and extraordinary receipts, and identify any amount counted twice in debt service.

Worked case

A distributor’s forecast under scrutiny

EBITDA of €1.40m includes €0.12m of temporary grant income. Cash taxes are €0.18m. Necessary equipment replacement costs €0.24m. Receivables and inventory rise €0.19m and €0.08m, while trade payables rise €0.05m. The grant was collected this year.

Working

  1. Operating working capital absorbs €0.19m + €0.08m − €0.05m = €0.22m. On the stated terms current cash before debt service is €1.40m − €0.18m − €0.24m − €0.22m = €0.76m.
  2. For a forward year with identical other assumptions but no grant, normalized cash is €0.64m. Label this difference so a reader can reconcile actual receipts to forecast capacity.
  3. Interest and principal total €0.60m. Coverage is roughly 1.27 times with the grant and 1.07 times excluding it. The latter leaves €0.04m before any further downside.

Interpretation

A larger receivable balance might represent higher sound sales or slow debt collection. Invoice ages and subsequent receipts can distinguish these causes.

A common mistake

Adding depreciation to EBITDA again because depreciation is noncash.

How to correct it

EBITDA already excludes depreciation. Trace every adjustment against the specific subtotal and reserve room for the cash actually spent maintaining productive assets.

Quick check
If operating working capital instead releases €50,000, what cash is available using the same definition?

The release adds cash. €900,000 − €100,000 − €150,000 + €50,000 = €700,000.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

A borrower reports €820,000 EBITDA before all debt service. Taxes paid are €90,000, upkeep is €110,000, inventory falls €30,000 and trade payables fall €50,000. Other operating balances are constant.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate cash before debt service.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Scheduled principal is €400,000 and interest is €100,000. Find coverage and interpret it.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The stock decrease releases €30,000 and the payable decrease consumes €50,000. Available cash is €820,000 − €90,000 − €110,000 + €30,000 − €50,000 = €600,000.

Part B 2 marks

€600,000/€500,000 = 1.20 times, leaving €100,000 over the period. A due-date cash schedule is needed to establish whether that cushion exists when each payment is due.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Management pays most of its suppliers by selling receivables to a factor. Where might a reported improvement in cash conversion overstate repayment capacity?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would rebuild a gross receivables roll-forward and match the factor receipts, fees and customer payments by date. On the specified inputs the business produces €230,000 before debt service. Current cash rises to €325,000 with factoring, which can legitimately solve a short timing problem. I would base recurring capacity on underlying collections and test obligations to the factor separately, especially if the borrower guarantees customer performance.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The factoring agreement has no recourse and is renewed each month at constant volume. Can all proceeds be excluded from annual operating economics?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The recurring arrangement changes the timing and net amount of genuine customer cash. A steady-state forecast can reflect its fee and collection pattern. I would distinguish the initial step-up in financing from recurring settlements and avoid excluding valid collections or including a permanent new advance every period.
