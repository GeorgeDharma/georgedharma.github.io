Collateral, guarantees and priority

Estimate realizable proceeds after costs and delay. Check collateral eligibility, seniority and concentration. A guarantee depends on the guarantor’s capacity when called.

The starting point

Collateral is an asset a lender may be able to sell after default. Its invoice or appraised value can exceed the cash the lender receives. Estimate eligibility, how quickly the asset can be sold, disposal costs and who gets paid ahead of the bank. Discount delayed proceeds when comparing them with money today.

A guarantee adds a claim on another party. Investigate its ability to pay precisely when the borrower needs help. A parent heavily exposed to the same customers may offer weak protection in an industry slump. Loan documents and local law determine whether a claim is enforceable.

Theory and method

Pari passu recovery waterfall

Intercreditor priority · Capped deficiency guarantee

Two banks share security equally in rank but have different claim sizes. How should one value a guarantee covering its residual shortfall?

An auction raises cash for a group of creditors. Senior expenses come out first. The two banks share the remaining pot in proportion to their admissible claims when their intercreditor agreement gives them equal priority. A guarantee limited to one bank’s deficiency can pay only after that bank’s own secured recovery is calculated. Mixing the two stages may assign one euro of sale proceeds to both lenders or claim more than the guarantee allows.

How the result is built

R₁ = min(C₁, V × C₁/(C₁ + C₂)); G = min(H, a(C₁ − R₁))

V is euro sale cash left after higher-ranking amounts, C₁ and C₂ are the banks’ admissible euro claims, R₁ is bank one’s recovery from collateral, a is the guaranteed fraction of its shortfall, H is the euro guarantee cap, and G is the additional euro guarantee payment.

  1. Sale proceeds of €500,000 after costs first pay €100,000 senior debt. The two bank claims are €300,000 and €200,000, so the first bank takes 60% of the remaining €400,000, or €240,000.
  2. Its shortfall is €60,000. A 50% guarantee capped at €40,000 supplies €30,000, leaving this bank with €270,000 and a €30,000 loss.
  3. A €100,000 lower sale gives bank one €180,000, leaving €120,000. Half would be €60,000, but the cap cuts it to €40,000 and total recovery falls to €220,000.

When the reasoning holds

  • The equal-ranking agreement allocates pro rata by admitted claim. A first-out tranche or different security pool changes the allocation.
  • The guarantee is collectible at the same date here, with no recoverable interest or subrogation redistribution. Timing or correlated guarantor failure requires a separate adjustment.
Terms in this lesson
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
First example

Inventory appraised at €200,000 is expected to sell for 70% of appraisal. Selling costs are €20,000, and a senior creditor receives the first €30,000. Before discounting any delay, €200,000 × 0.70 − €20,000 − €30,000 = €90,000 remains for the bank.

From an appraisal to distributable cash

An appraisal answers a question about value at a particular date and under specified sale conditions. A lender’s recovery depends on a potentially different sale. Check asset ownership, an enforceable security interest, prior claims, insurance, currency and time needed to take control. An invoice face amount is just the opening input.

Eligible receivables may exclude disputes, related parties, very late invoices and concentrations beyond contractual limits. A lender advances a fraction against eligible amounts to allow for collection risk and costs. That advance rate is a lending rule. Actual liquidation proceeds may fall above or below it.

Work through priority one claim at a time

Prepare a waterfall listing which claims receive available proceeds and what remains afterwards. Distinguish the proceeds of this specific asset from cash distributable across the wider borrower estate. Two secured creditors can have different ranks or share pro rata under the case terms.

A guarantee introduces a separate potential payer. Understand limits, expiry, conditions to demand payment and the guarantor’s own financial resources when the borrower is distressed. A parent and its subsidiary may fail together. A guarantee on a shortfall cannot generate more than the remaining valid claim under the assumed terms.

Time, currency and wrong-way risk

Use one valuation date to compare near and distant recoveries. A sale worth €1m in two years buys less today than €1m now at a positive discount rate. Also model how a pledged asset correlates with the business. Shares in the borrower itself may collapse precisely when the lender needs to sell them.

Valuation sensitivity informs monitoring design. Securities with frequent prices permit regular margin tests but may gap overnight. Special-purpose machinery can have a thin resale market. Liquidating it may depend on the very industry experiencing distress.

Method

Estimate an economic recovery

  1. Establish rights

    Verify ownership, enforceability, ranking and scope of any guarantee under the specified case terms.

  2. Reduce proceeds

    Apply a supportable sale scenario, costs and senior claims. Cap the claim at the amount owed before adding any guarantee recovery.

  3. Compare dates

    Discount cash collections to the same date. Run a scenario in which collateral prices and borrower cash flows fall together.

Worked case

A machine and a limited guarantee

A lender is owed €600,000 today. A machine sells for €560,000 in one year under the assumed enforceable terms. Auction and enforcement costs are €40,000 and a senior claim takes €120,000 from sale proceeds. A separate solvent guarantor pays half the remaining nominal shortfall, capped at €80,000, on that same date. Ignore further interest.

Working

  1. Machine proceeds for the lender are €560,000 − €40,000 − €120,000 = €400,000. The nominal shortfall is €200,000.
  2. Half the shortfall is €100,000, so the €80,000 cap binds. The lender receives €480,000 after one year, worth about €436,364 today at 10%.
  3. Economic loss against €600,000 is about €163,636, or 27.27%. If the guarantor fails at the same time, present recovery falls to about €363,636.

Interpretation

The waterfall shows where a lower auction price, extra sale delay or a common shock to the guarantor enters the estimate. A different rank would change the answer even at the same sale price.

A common mistake

Adding a €600,000 guarantee limit to a €400,000 collateral estimate on a €600,000 claim.

How to correct it

Apply guarantee conditions to the residual claim after collateral and account for guarantor capacity, collection timing and any contractual cap.

Quick check
If repayment is delayed one year and the case specifies a 10% discount rate, what is the present value of the bank’s €90,000 recovery?

Discount the future receipt, €90,000/1.10 ≈ €81,818. A sale-price haircut and a time discount address different reductions in value.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

A pledged asset sells immediately for €320,000. Costs are €20,000 and a senior creditor takes €90,000. Your bank is owed €250,000. A solvent guarantor covers 50% of your residual shortfall.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find recovery from each source and overall loss.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Identify the largest potential oversight if borrower and guarantor sell to the same distressed customer.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Net collateral after priority is €210,000. The €40,000 shortfall brings €20,000 from the guarantor. Total recovery is €230,000 and loss €20,000.

Part B 2 marks

Their cash flows can fail together. A separate legal claim provides less protection if the guarantor loses its ability to pay in the very default scenario being considered. Stress its assets and collection timing independently.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two banks share security equally in rank but have different claim sizes. How should one value a guarantee covering its residual shortfall?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would first identify the collateral pool and enforceable ranks. Equal rank means the two lenders share the distributable cash pro rata to their claims, giving our €300,000 claim €240,000 here. Only the €60,000 deficiency triggers our guarantee. With 50% coverage we gain another €30,000. I would obtain evidence of the guarantor’s capacity under the same adverse scenario as collateral liquidation.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The guarantor charges a secured reimbursement claim ranking ahead of the banks for a separate existing €50,000 advance. What changes at the original sale price?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The pre-bank pot falls from €400,000 to €350,000. Our 60% share is €210,000, leaving €90,000 deficiency. The 50% guarantee now binds at €40,000, giving €250,000 total recovery. This assumes the separate senior advance is independently valid and enforceable.
