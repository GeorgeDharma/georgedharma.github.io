Underwriting an income-producing property

Construct recurring property income and cash for a mortgage from operating evidence.

Theory and terminology

Commercial mortgage sizing

debt yield · debt service coverage · capitalization rate

What you should be able to explain

  • Construct recurring property income and cash for a mortgage from operating evidence.
  • Size and stress a commercial mortgage using loan-to-value, debt-service coverage and debt yield.

Read the leases before the appraisal

Gross potential rent assumes the relevant space earns its scheduled rent. Vacancy, tenant defaults, rent-free periods and leases expiring next year can pull collections below this figure. Other recurring property income may include parking. Subtract ordinary operating expenses such as insurance, repairs and property taxes to obtain net operating income, or NOI.

Review a rent roll by tenant and lease expiry, then reconcile billed rent with cash receipts. A recently signed short lease at an unusually high rent gives weaker evidence of sustainable income than several occupied years. Exclude mortgage interest from NOI because different capital structures can fund the same building.

Translate income into value and payment capacity

Direct capitalization divides sustainable annual NOI by an independently supported market capitalization rate. A higher rate lowers value for unchanged income. Market rents, comparable property sales and differences in location or condition matter when choosing that rate.

A coverage test divides cash available for debt service by annual principal and interest. This lender deducts a replacement reserve from NOI when measuring cash available. A debt-yield test divides NOI by original principal. It gives the lender an income comparison independent of the chosen amortization term. Document numerator conventions for both tests.

Recognize a maturity problem early

Loan-to-value limits exposure against market value. Debt coverage limits pressure on annual cash. Debt yield compared with market standards provides a further check if a low interest-only payment makes coverage look strong.

For a balloon loan, calculate the balance due at maturity and compare it with potential future lending capacity. A building can make every interest payment yet require an owner contribution at refinancing. Tenants, borrowing rates and comparable sale prices can deteriorate simultaneously.

Method

Build the result

V = NOI/c. Lmax = min(vV, (NOI − R)/(k × a), NOI/y).

V is estimated property value, NOI annual net operating income, c the market capitalization rate, v the maximum loan-to-value fraction and R annual replacement reserve. k is minimum coverage, a annual debt service per unit borrowed under the proposed payment schedule and y the minimum annual debt yield. Lmax is currency principal.

  1. Capitalizing one stable year gives a benchmark value. Multiply by the permitted loan fraction to obtain a collateral ceiling.
  2. Annual cash divided by required coverage gives maximum annual service. Divide by the annual payment per unit of loan to obtain the cash-based limit.
  3. A minimum debt yield requires principal to stay below NOI divided by the required yield. The smallest of the three limits governs under these rules.
Worked case

A warehouse refinancing request

A lender receives a €6m request. Potential rent is €1.20m yearly, economic occupancy 90%, other recurring income €60,000 and operating expenses €420,000. Annual replacement reserve is €60,000. The appraisal cap rate is 8%. Policy permits 65% loan-to-value, requires 1.35 times coverage and 11.5% debt yield. Annual loan payments equal 8% of original principal.

Working

  1. Rent collected is €1.08m. Add €60,000 and deduct €420,000 to obtain €720,000 NOI. Annual cash for debt service is €660,000 after reserves.
  2. Value is €720,000/0.08 = €9m. The collateral ceiling is €5.85m.
  3. The coverage ceiling is €660,000/(1.35 × 0.08) = €6.111m. The debt-yield ceiling is €720,000/0.115 = €6.261m. Even the base case falls €150,000 short of the request.
  4. At 80% occupancy and a 9% cap rate, NOI falls to €600,000. The collateral ceiling becomes €4.333m. Annual cash of €540,000 supports €5m under coverage. Debt yield permits €5.217m.

Decision and interpretation

Use €5.85m as the base upper bound and document the much lower stressed capacity. A term, amortization or equity adjustment depends on whether the bank requires the stress to pass at origination or uses it to set a refinancing cushion.

Quick check
The warehouse owner negotiates lower annual debt service while income, reserve, capitalization rate and property policy stay fixed. Which base ceiling rises directly?

A lower annual payment per euro of debt lets the same available income cover more principal. The debt-yield and value calculations do not use the loan payment.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

An office has €880,000 sustainable NOI and €80,000 annual reserves. The cap rate is 7.5%, maximum loan-to-value 70%, minimum coverage 1.40 and minimum debt yield 10%. Annual service equals 9% of principal. What amount can the lender support?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Capitalized value is €11.733m. Applying 70% gives €8.213m of collateral capacity.
  2. Cash for debt is €800,000. Coverage supports €800,000/(1.40 × 0.09) = €6.349m.
  3. Debt yield supports €8.8m. Coverage binds at €6.349m. A proposal rounded to whole thousands should stay below the exact limit.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How much could a lender advance on a leased building if both vacancy and required investor yields rise?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would present the three separate limits to committee and identify the lowest in each scenario. The warehouse supports €5.85m in the base case and €4.333m under joint vacancy and capitalization-rate stress. Collateral binds both times. The €6m proposal exceeds even the base limit, so it requires more equity or a revised financing amount.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The bank offers a longer amortization with annual service of 6.5% of principal. Which test governs now?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Coverage permits €800,000/(1.40 × 0.065) = €8.791m. The unchanged collateral ceiling of €8.213m becomes binding. Compare the new balance at maturity with refinance capacity before lengthening the term.
