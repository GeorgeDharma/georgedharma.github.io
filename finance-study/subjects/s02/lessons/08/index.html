Pricing the loan

Build a price from funding, operating costs, expected loss and a return for capital at risk. Distinguish a simple spread approximation from a full expected-value calculation.

The starting point

A loan quote has to cover funding, administration and possible credit losses. A bank may also require payment for the capital committed to unexpected losses. For a simple one-period calculation, write the bank’s receipts separately for repayment and default and set their probability-weighted average equal to the required end-of-period amount.

This method makes assumptions visible. If interest is earned only when the borrower repays, increasing the rate adds revenue only in that state. A quick addition of expected loss to funding cost can therefore differ from an exact break-even rate. Real contracts add fees, interim payments and exposure changes.

Theory and method

Risk-adjusted return on capital

RAROC hurdle pricing · Survival-weighted loan margin

A relationship manager says a 6% loan earns a three-point spread over funding. Can it meet a 10% capital hurdle?

A bank quotes annual interest on a €1m loan but receives the full coupon only if the borrower performs. The expected principal loss, operating expense and funding expense absorb some revenue. The bank also dedicates equity capacity to unexpected losses. A capital hurdle asks for enough expected earnings after these costs to justify that allocation. It produces a break-even quote conditional on credit estimates and market terms. The customer’s ability to service that rate needs its own cash forecast.

How the result is built

RAROC = [(1 − p)cP − pLP − F − O]/K

p is one-year default probability, c is contractual annual interest rate, P is euro principal, L is fractional principal loss on default, F and O are euro funding and operating expense, K is euro economic capital, and RAROC is expected profit divided by allocated capital.

  1. Use €1,000,000 principal, 2% default probability, 50% severity, €30,000 funding, €7,000 expense and €120,000 capital. Expected principal loss is €10,000.
  2. At 6% promised interest, survival-weighted coupon is €58,800. Net expected profit is €58,800 − €10,000 − €30,000 − €7,000 = €11,800, giving 9.83% RAROC.
  3. A 10% hurdle needs €12,000 profit. Solve 0.98 × c × €1m = €59,000 to obtain c ≈ 6.0204%.

When the reasoning holds

  • There is zero interest recovery on default, costs are deterministic and no fee has already reduced funding. Other conventions change both numerator and break-even rate.
  • The risk estimate and capital allocation must remain valid at the new quote. Repricing may affect borrower take-up, affordability or capital needs.

Terms in this lesson
Probability of default (PD)
The chance that the borrower meets the specified default definition over the stated horizon.
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
First example

Lend €100 for one year. Required receipts to cover capital advanced and stated costs are €104. Default probability is 4% and recovery in default is €50. Solve 0.96 × promised payment + 0.04 × €50 = €104. Promised payment is €106.25, a 6.25% rate.

Build the lender’s economic account

A lender parts with cash at origination, incurs funding and servicing expenses, and receives contractual or recovery cash later. Put all flows at the same date. A fee collected upfront reduces the amount actually funded. A fee payable only when the borrower survives belongs on the performing branch.

Separate economic break-even from a target return. A lender charging for scarce equity capital may require proceeds above funding and expected operating cost. State whether a required capital return has already been incorporated in the hurdle to avoid adding it twice.

Survival-weight the contractual payment

For principal P, one-period default probability p, recovery R and promised rate r, expected receipts are (1 − p) × P × (1 + r) + p × R. Set this equal to the required terminal proceeds. This form assumes no interim payments and no interest in the default recovery beyond the specified R.

For a multi-period amortizing loan, reduce outstanding principal after every payment and distinguish conditional default probability from survival to that date. Costs, late fees and recovery lags also have dates. Computing a single year’s spread only at origination need not price the full schedule.

Decide which extra price is feasible

A rate that clears the lender’s arithmetic hurdle may still make the borrower unable to pay, change the applicant pool or exceed what comparable facilities command. Apply a repayment-capacity test at the proposed contract rate and check that fees, maturity and collateral resemble the alternatives used as comparables.

If the viable price range is empty, change structure or decline. Reducing exposure can lower risk concentration. Better reporting may reduce information costs. A guarantee can improve recovery but generally has its own costs and credit exposure.

Method

Quote from a common date

  1. Funding

    Reduce the amount advanced by any collected fee and grow funding and costs to the agreed comparison date.

  2. Branch payoffs

    Write separate receipts for survival and default. Solve for the price using realistic recovery timing and conditional payment terms.

  3. Feasibility

    Recalculate borrower cash coverage at the solved rate and stress PD and recovery. Compare proposed terms with credible alternatives.

Worked case

An upfront fee changes the rate

A lender advances a face amount of €100,000 and immediately receives a €2,000 fee. Its funding rate on the net cash advanced is 4%. Servicing costs of €1,000 are due at year-end. One-year PD is 3% and default recovery is €40,000 at year-end. No interim payments occur.

Working

  1. Net funding is €98,000. Required terminal cash for break-even is €98,000 × 1.04 + €1,000 = €102,920.
  2. Survival-weighted promised payment plus default receipts must equal the hurdle. Solving 0.97 × payment + 0.03 × €40,000 = €102,920 gives payment about €104,866 and contractual interest about 4.87% of face.
  3. Without the fee, funding grows from €100,000 and the hurdle becomes €105,000. Payment then rises to about €107,010, or 7.01% contractual interest.

Interpretation

The quoted rate alone cannot compare the offers from a borrower’s perspective. Calculate its effective cost using the cash it actually receives and the payments it contractually owes.

A common mistake

Adding the fee to future performing receipts after already subtracting it from the initial advance.

How to correct it

Place each cash flow only once on its actual date. The effect of an upfront fee persists through the funding saved on it.

Quick check
If default recovery falls to zero and the other assumptions are unchanged, what break-even rate follows?

Now 0.96 × promised payment = €104. The promised payment must be about €108.33 per €100 lent.

Written practice · 5 marks

Independent exam problem

A bank advances €50,000 with no fee. Required year-end receipts total €52,000. Default probability is 5%, with €10,000 recovered at year-end.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Solve the contractual interest rate.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Why might a bank decline even at a contract rate of 8.42%?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

0.95 × payment + 0.05 × €10,000 = €52,000. Payment = €51,500/0.95 ≈ €54,210.53, implying 8.42% interest.

Part B 2 marks

The calculation assumes the 5% PD and recovery still apply. If the higher payment exceeds the customer’s cash capacity or deters safer customers, expected receipts may fall below the hurdle. Concentration limits can also bind.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A relationship manager says a 6% loan earns a three-point spread over funding. Can it meet a 10% capital hurdle?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would not recommend the 6% rate solely from the quoted spread. After €10,000 expected principal loss, €37,000 funding and administration, and survival weighting of interest, the bank earns €11,800 on €120,000 capital. That is about 9.83%. A quote just above 6.02% reaches the model hurdle, conditional on unchanged default risk, and I would separately test repayment capacity at the proposed terms.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the bank earns a €3,000 end-of-year servicing fee in both states, how much could the contractual rate fall while preserving the hurdle?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Required survival-weighted interest falls by €3,000. The rate reduction is €3,000/(0.98 × €1m) ≈ 0.3061 percentage point, bringing the hurdle quote to about 5.7143%. A fee due only from surviving borrowers would give a different adjustment.
