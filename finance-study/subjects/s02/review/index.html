New and expanded lessons

S02.13

Residential mortgages and household affordability

The credit-purpose and coverage lessons discuss collateral and loan capacity for businesses. The learner had yet to qualify a household from documented monthly income, a full housing payment, property value and cash to close.

  • Calculate the payment ceiling and maximum residential loan under income and valuation limits.
  • Reconcile savings, closing costs and post-closing reserves, including a reset-rate stress.

S02.14

Underwriting an income-producing property

Commercial lending already uses cash flow, collateral and repayment priority. No worked property file combined a rent roll, normalized vacancy, an independent capitalization rate, replacement reserves and three concurrent loan-sizing tests.

  • Construct recurring property income and cash for a mortgage from operating evidence.
  • Size and stress a commercial mortgage using loan-to-value, debt-service coverage and debt yield.

S02.15

Underwriting, distributing and pricing a syndicated loan

Individual loan pricing and revolving liquidity are already worked. This chapter follows an underwritten commitment from the mandate to each bank’s retained hold, then reconciles the borrower’s interest, recurring charges and arrangement fees.

  • Distinguish the amount an arranger underwrites, aims to retain, actually distributes and must fund on drawdown.
  • Calculate borrower cash charges and arranger fee income, including a utilization trigger and an agreed pricing flex.

Public benchmarks

University of British Columbia, Sauder School of Business

BUSI 221 – Real Estate Finance in a Canadian Context

The sequence distinguishes household qualification from commercial mortgage underwriting, then examines repayment plans. The course also connects mortgages to their property security.

University of British Columbia, Sauder School of Business

BUSI 221 learning objectives

The residential unit asks students to calculate debt-service ratios. The commercial unit asks them to determine lending value, loan amount and the information needed to assess borrowers.

Fannie Mae, United States

Selling Guide B3-6-02, Debt-to-Income Ratios

The guide includes housing payments and recurring liabilities when assessing monthly obligations against qualifying income. Its own product and underwriting restrictions have specific scopes.

Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, United States

Comptroller’s Handbook: Commercial Real Estate Lending

The handbook distinguishes debt-service coverage, debt yield and loan-to-value, and discusses property cash flow, vacancy, appraisal and refinance risk.

University of London, with academic direction from UCL and Queen Mary University of London

Law of international finance: syndicated loans

The postgraduate module covers term sheets, mandates, commitment letters, draw requests, conditions precedent, and the different duties of arrangers and agents. Its final module examines syndicate management and transfer of loan rights and obligations.

Central Bank of the Republic of Kosovo

Guide to Syndicated Loans, December 2024

The supervisory advisory guide distinguishes underwritten and best-efforts mandates, targets for individual lenders’ commitments, syndication and allocation, revolving commitments, front-end fees and fees on undrawn balances.

Federal Reserve Board Finance and Economics Discussion Series

Pipeline Risk in Leveraged Loan Syndication

Bruche, Malherbe and Meisenzahl examine lender demand, adjustments to spreads and discounts during placement, and the inventory risk that arrangers bear when an underwritten loan is difficult to distribute.

Visa Inc., filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission

Five-Year Revolving Credit Agreement, February 15, 2008, utilization fee provision

The executed agreement provides a practical example of a daily utilization threshold above 50% that triggers an annualized fee on outstanding loans. It demonstrates why the threshold, fee base and measurement period need to be read separately.
