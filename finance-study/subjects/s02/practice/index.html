Theory midterm · 10 marks

Credit rationing when the price of a loan changes the pool

After lessons 1–6, allow 20 minutes. Explain the reasoning behind your answer.

A one-year loan advances 100. A safe borrower repays in full with probability 0.96 and a riskier one with probability 0.75. Recovery at default is 40 in either group. At an 8% rate both borrow and the pool is half of each. At 15% only risky borrowers apply.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Compare expected receipts per loan under the two rates.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Why can a higher contractual rate lower the return? Distinguish two mechanisms.

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    Which information would change a real credit decision?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the midterm marking guide

Part A · 4 marks

At 8%, average repayment probability is 0.855. Expected receipts = 0.855 × 108 + 0.145 × 40 = 98.14. At 15%, receipts = 0.75 × 115 + 0.25 × 40 = 96.25. Credit both probability setups and results.

Part B · 3 marks

Here higher price drives safer applicants away, changing the pool through adverse selection. If an existing borrower switched to a riskier project after contracting, that would be moral hazard. Only the first mechanism is built into the numbers.

Part C · 3 marks

Test the cash available at each repayment date, verify its source and examine downside recovery. Compare expected receipts with funding, operating and capital costs. Even the higher receipts in this example are below the 100 advanced.

Problem 1

A one-year exposure is €2m, default probability is 3% and loss given default is 40%. Estimate expected credit loss.

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

0.03 × €2m × 0.40 = €24,000 for the year. This simplified estimate assumes a fixed exposure and severity conditional on default.

Problem 2

A borrower offers more collateral but has a recurring operating cash deficit. What should the lender examine?

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

Test whether cash can return to sustainable coverage, when asset proceeds would arrive and who has priority. Collateral can reduce loss severity while repayment capacity remains weak.

Suggested academic marking scheme

Use these weights when assessing the complete subject. The four problem sets collect the written problems from individual lectures. You can use the 10-mark midterm and the 40-mark subject paper with the weights below.

Problem sets 25%
Four sets per standard subject. Show reasoning, calculations, units and checks. The two practice problems in each subject are worked samples for these sets.
Theory midterm 20%
After the first half of the subject. Explain or derive a model, identify assumptions and state what would change if one failed.
Seminar paper or laboratory 25%
During the second half. Apply the theory to a case, dataset or implementation. Address an alternative explanation and document how to reproduce the result.
Individual final examination 30%
At the end of the subject. Solve an unfamiliar problem, interpret the result and critique a claim. M&A and lending each include a 30-mark examination question with a marking guide.
