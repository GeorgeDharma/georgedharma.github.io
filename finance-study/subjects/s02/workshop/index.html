Professional workshop · S02

Credit committee submission for an exporter’s equipment purchase and seasonal line

It is 15 January in a hypothetical euro lending market. Aster Foods, a profitable independent exporter, asks the bank to fund a €600,000 packing line and renew its revolving facility at a €450,000 limit. The customer wants a decision before its February purchase orders. Recommend size, terms, owner contribution, conditions to first draw and review triggers. All euro amounts below are in thousands unless specifically stated. Assume enforceable security as described, no VAT, fees or currency mismatch, and no cash interest on the owners’ equity.

InputsWalkthroughReconciled exhibitCompleted documentYour turn
Your role
Corporate credit analyst
What to submit
Submit a completed sanction memorandum and a reconciled capacity and collateral exhibit to the delegated credit committee. The relationship manager will use its conditions in the offer, and loan operations will use the same figures to determine funds available.

From inputs to decision

01

Separate the equipment and operating cycle

The two uses have different repayment sources, and the same euro of equipment cash cannot also finance seasonal inventories.

  1. Match the €600 packing machine with a term facility amortizing from recurring operating cash. Verify the supplier invoice and have the bank pay the supplier directly once owner funds arrive.
  2. Map the revolver to purchases, sale invoices and April collections. Confirm the €105 annual working-capital growth measures a permanent change, while the monthly seasonal funding changes are temporary additional peaks. Using both as expenses in the annual coverage calculation would count the peaks twice.

Take two facilities to committee with separate purposes. Treat the opening €280 revolving draw as outstanding debt when sizing further availability.

02

Underwrite recurring cash before debt service

The lender must establish what cash reaches debt holders in the base plan and after an operating setback.

  1. Calculate base annual cash available as 760 − 90 − 125 − 105 = 440. Request the customer ledger, tax payment schedule and machine-maintenance budgets that support those numbers.
  2. For the downside, use 650 − 70 − 125 − 85 = 370. Avoid improving downside cash further by adding the owner injection or new borrowings, which are financing sources. Existing annual debt service of 145 is payable in both scenarios.

Carry 440 and 370 into the separate service tests. A covenant should specify permissible adjustments and eligible unrestricted cash.

03

Solve for the most the term loan can carry

The first year is the heaviest for four equal principal instalments because interest is calculated on the largest opening balance.

  1. For term principal T, first-year service is T/4 + 0.06T = 0.31T. Revolving interest is 300 × 6.5% = 19.50 in base and 350 × 7.5% = 26.25 in downside.
  2. Under downside the aggregate service ceiling is 370/1.25 = 296. Deduct existing service 145 and revolving interest 26.25, leaving 124.75 for term service. The mathematical principal ceiling is 124.75/0.31 = 402.42. Approve a rounded 400 with a 200 owner equipment contribution.

At 400, term service is 124, aggregate service is 288.50 and 295.25, and coverage is 1.525 and 1.253. A full 600 term loan would push downside service to 357.25 and coverage to 1.036.

04

Reconstruct available security and borrowing capacity

A committed €450 line is drawable only to the extent permitted by collateral eligibility and the contractual bank limit.

  1. Receivables eligible at origination are 700 − 60 − 40 − 60 = 540, supporting 405. Eligible stock of 300 supports 120. The base of 525 exceeds commitment, leaving maximum allowed drawings of 450.
  2. At the joint downside the receivable contribution is (540 − 100) × 0.75 = 330 and stock contributes (300 − 90) × 0.40 = 84. Stressed capacity is 414. Ask for an invoice-age reconciliation, customer concentration test and independent inventory verification.

Opening availability is 450 − 280 = 170. A collateral shock can turn an apparently available nominal commitment into a real borrowing limit.

05

Fund the monthly peak and a downside buffer

Annual cash coverage does not establish that money is available on the highest seasonal purchase date.

  1. Without additional seasonal equity, drawings would reach 280 + 180 = 460 in March. The initial owner cash of 30 applied to the revolver brings prospective month-end drawings to 410 in February, 430 in March and 260 in April.
  2. Base March headroom becomes 450 − 430 = 20. On stressed collateral, the same peak gives 414 − 430 = −16. To carry the additional required 20 cushion requires at least 36 more equity at that point. Require the proposed 40 available in escrow. Drawing it cuts the peak to 390 and leaves 24 headroom.

Require the 200 equipment contribution and 30 initial seasonal contribution before first use. Confirm the separate 40 standby cash can reach the borrower before the stressed borrowing-base test.

06

Check the line quote against credit economics

The headline coupon must pay for losses and funding and should not be used to compensate for an unaffordable term loan.

  1. Per €100 advanced, required terminal receipts are €100 + €3.20 + €0.55 + €0.80 return on allocated capital = €104.55. The bank receives the promised amount with 98% probability and €50 with 2% probability.
  2. Solve 0.98 × promised payment + 0.02 × €50 = €104.55. The promised payment is about €105.6633, so the simple hurdle rate is 5.66%. At 6.5%, expected receipts are €105.37 and exceed this hurdle by €0.82 per €100.

The quoted 6.5% covers the simplified unit economics at the assumed PD and recovery. Validate those inputs before sanction. Additional aggregate exposures and liquidity costs can alter the pricing conclusion.

07

Convert the calculation into enforceable controls

The downside term cushion is thin enough that a late eligibility dispute or modest cash miss could trigger a breach.

  1. Close only after documenting the 200 owner equipment contribution, 30 seasonal payment and verified 40 standby equity. Maintenance covenant is annual debt-service coverage of at least 1.25 under a precisely reconciled definition, with monthly management reporting and a rolling thirteen-week cash forecast during the season.
  2. Require a monthly borrowing-base certificate and weekly reporting in February and March. Flag unpaid customer invoices, new disputes or erosion of eligible collateral. Escalate to the credit officer before approving further draws if actual or projected headroom falls below 20. Stop new drawings above the applicable allowed base.

Underwrite to the downside annual cash level of 370. Breach of the signed coverage or availability conditions requires review of contractual remedies and a fresh forecast rather than automatic rollover.

Completed document

Delegated sanction memorandum · Aster Foods

Decision requested

Approve a €400,000 four-year equipment loan at 6% fixed with four equal annual principal repayments. Renew the €450,000 revolver subject to the lower of the commitment and monthly borrowing base. The €600,000 equipment request exceeds stressed capacity. The owner must put €200,000 into the machine purchase.

Basis of repayment

Recurring cash after taxes, upkeep and permanent inventory and receivable investment is €440,000 base and €370,000 downside. Combined first-year service is €288,500 base and €295,250 downside. Downside coverage of 1.253 times exceeds 1.25 by only about €938 of annual eligible cash, since €295,250 × 1.25 = €369,062.50. Any unfavorable revision goes back to committee.

Peak funding and collateral

Obtain €30,000 seasonal equity before first draw and a legally accessible €40,000 standby contribution. At the March stress peak, verified eligibility falls to €414,000. Following both cash contributions projected drawing is €390,000, leaving €24,000 available. Confirm no overlap among overdue, disputed and concentration exclusions and verify collateral ranking before closing.

Pricing and monitoring

A minimum 5.66% revolver coupon covers the stated per-unit hurdle. The 6.5% base quote is acceptable at the modeled loss rate. The relationship manager must verify forecast orders and customer payment terms, and loan operations must monitor weekly certificates at the seasonal peak. Escalate any projected headroom below €20,000 or coverage below 1.25 times.

Residual risk

A simultaneous deterioration in the major customer, export collections and inventory valuation could exceed the illustrated downside. Restrict dividends while coverage or peak availability is below policy and require a revised cash forecast before any waiver. The owner’s standalone capacity under the same customer shock must be verified.

Task 1

If stressed annual cash available falls to €350,000 and all other stressed annual inputs remain fixed, what maximum equipment principal satisfies 1.25 times coverage? Enter EUR thousands.

The service ceiling is 350/1.25 = 280. Deduct 145 and 26.25, leaving 108.75 for the new loan. Divide by 0.31 to obtain 350.806 thousand, so a rounded approval should not exceed about €350,800 under this sizing test.

Task 2

At the March peak before standby equity, another €40,000 of previously eligible receivables becomes excluded on top of the original downside. How much standby cash in total would restore €20,000 headroom? Enter EUR thousands.

See the full solution

The further eligibility loss cuts availability by 40 × 75% = 30, from 414 to 384. Peak draw is 430 before standby. Target draw is 384 − 20 = 364, requiring 430 − 364 = 66 of standby cash in total. The current 40 would leave a 26 shortfall to target.

Task 3

If default recovery on the €100 pricing tranche falls from €50 to €20, with all other costs and PD fixed, what is the exact hurdle coupon to two decimal places?

See the full solution

Set 0.98 × payment + 0.02 × €20 = €104.55. Payment equals €104.15/0.98 ≈ €106.2755. The hurdle coupon rises to 6.28%, narrowing room below the 6.5% quote.

Task 4

The owner offers an unsecured letter of intention for the €40,000 standby. What decision should the analyst record?

Maintain the funding condition. An intention does not establish timely cash under joint stress.
