Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

What lies beyond an identical percentile?

A desk forecasts a one-day positive-loss distribution of €0m (94%), €5m (4%) and €25m (2%). In a stress calibration the loss in the last state rises to €40m with probabilities unchanged. Use the first cumulative probability reaching 95% for VaR.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

Collateral and default in the same storm

A firm holds positive counterparty exposure of €2m if the market is calm (75% probability) and €8m in stress (25%). These figures are already net of realizable collateral. Conditional default probabilities are 1% and 12%, respectively. Loss severity given default is 50%.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Solvency and liquidity on different clocks

A bank has €14m eligible capital and €110m risk-weighted assets. After a loss it has €12m capital and €125m risk-weighted assets. Its internal capital floor is 10%. It has €3m liquid cash and €10m unpledged collateral, which falls 20% in stress. Same-day secured lending applies a 25% haircut to the stressed value. It owes €10m today.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Curve shocks and a suspicious sequence

A rates desk gains €2,000 per 1 bp rise in the two-year yield and loses €2,000 per 1 bp rise in the ten-year yield. Treat sensitivities as linear for the stated shocks. Its daily 99% VaR forecasts over 300 days produced four exceptions, including three in consecutive days.

Case 1 · 10 marks

What lies beyond an identical percentile?

Part A 4 marks

Calculate original 95% VaR and 95% expected shortfall.

VaR is €5m. The worst 5% includes all 2% of the €25m state plus 3% of the €5m state. Expected shortfall is (0.02 × 25 + 0.03 × 5)/0.05 = €13m.

Part B 3 marks

Recompute under the revised state and interpret the disagreement.

VaR remains €5m. Expected shortfall rises to (0.02 × 40 + 0.03 × 5)/0.05 = €19m. Larger severity within the tail changes average tail loss without moving the boundary.

Part C 3 marks

Can this one-day distribution size tomorrow's cash needs by itself? Specify two missing items.

Revaluations need not settle tomorrow. Record the due dates of payments and margin calls, drawable funding, or cash actually available by the relevant cutoff.

Case 2 · 10 marks

Collateral and default in the same storm

Part A 4 marks

Calculate expected credit loss by state and in total.

Calm contributes 0.75 × 0.01 × €2m × 0.5 = €7,500. Stress contributes 0.25 × 0.12 × €8m × 0.5 = €120,000. Total is €127,500.

Part B 3 marks

Calculate the product of average exposure, average probability of default and severity. Diagnose the difference.

Average exposure is €3.5m and default probability is 3.75%. Product estimate is €65,625. The €61,875 understatement reflects exposure rising in the state where default is more likely.

Part C 3 marks

Propose one independent validation test and one further coherent stress.

Compare observed exposures and defaults across market states on data kept outside development. A coherent stress combines a weaker counterparty, lower collateral values and a longer time to close out after default.

Case 3 · 10 marks

Solvency and liquidity on different clocks

Part A 4 marks

Find the capital ratios before and after stress and the additional eligible capital needed at constant post-stress risk-weighted assets.

Original ratio is 12.73%. Post-stress ratio is 9.6%. Required capital is €12.5m, so the gap is €0.5m.

Part B 3 marks

Find post-stress same-day cash and the cash gap.

Collateral value falls to €8m. It raises €6m after haircut. Adding €3m cash gives €9m available and a €1m shortfall.

Part C 3 marks

The bank expects a €2m customer repayment tomorrow. Explain which calculations it can affect and why it does not automatically solve either stated gap today.

Tomorrow's collection arrives after today's cash cutoff. Collection of an existing good asset exchanges a receivable for cash and does not by itself add €0.5m equity. Check actual settlement and prudential treatment separately.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Curve shocks and a suspicious sequence

Part A 4 marks

Calculate P&L for a parallel 3 bp rise and for a twist where the two-year yield falls 2 bp and the ten-year rises 4 bp.

Parallel change gives +€6,000 − €6,000 = zero. The twist gives −€4,000 − €8,000 = −€12,000.

Part B 3 marks

Find the expected number of exceptions and identify two questions the count alone cannot answer.

Expected exceptions are 300 × 1% = 3. The count does not establish independence or how much loss exceeded VaR. The three consecutive days warrant investigation.

Part C 3 marks

Suggest a specific diagnostic and an exposure measure that could reveal the vulnerability.

Compare frozen-position losses, curve shapes and time-stamped forecasts around the three dates. Monitor signed two-year and ten-year sensitivities alongside parallel DV01 and a curve-twist stress.
