Capital and supervision

Compare accounting equity, regulatory capital and risk-weighted assets. Identify the prudential text and local implementation before interpreting an actual threshold.

The starting point

Accounting equity is the residual after subtracting liabilities from assets. A supervisor may define eligible regulatory capital differently, for example by deducting particular assets or admitting only loss-absorbing instruments. Begin with the numerator that the stated rule actually permits.

A capital ratio often divides eligible capital by risk-weighted assets. The risk weights mean two equally large balance sheets can have different denominators. Compare definitions, the consolidation perimeter and the applicable local rules before drawing a conclusion about a real bank.

Theory and method

Risk-based and leverage capital constraints

Tier 1 leverage ratio · Binding capital requirement

Which constraint determines how much capital a low-risk-weight balance sheet must raise?

A bank can accumulate a very large holding of assets with low risk weights. A risk-based ratio charges capital against weighted exposures. A parallel leverage constraint compares eligible capital with a broadly unweighted exposure measure. Raising the same eligible capital can improve both, but the binding requirement is whichever demands the larger amount, after accounting for differences in definition. For a teaching example using one eligible numerator, the combined minimum is a maximum of the two calculated monetary requirements.

How the result is built

Kmin = max(rW, ℓE); headroom = K − Kmin

K is existing capital eligible for both illustrative internal tests, W risk-weighted assets, E leverage exposure measure, r and ℓ their respective minimum fractions, and Kmin required monetary capital. All amounts use euros.

  1. For W = €60m, E = €200m, risk minimum 10% and leverage minimum 4%, required amounts are €6m and €8m.
  2. The leverage-based test binds. With €9m capital the firm has €1m common headroom even though it appears €3m above its risk-based minimum.
  3. The binding point occurs where rW = ℓE. Here W/E = 40% is the switching density. The current 30% density makes leverage more demanding.

When the reasoning holds

  • The exercise uses the same qualifying capital for both constraints. Real ratio definitions and eligible capital tiers must be reconciled separately.
  • Balances, local thresholds and exposure definitions are stipulated internal teaching assumptions. A disposal or hedge may change both W and E.
Terms in this lesson
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
How to read the notation
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.

First example

Suppose an exercise gives €12m accounting equity, €2m of specified regulatory deductions and €100m risk-weighted assets. Eligible capital is €10m and the ratio is 10%. The calculation alone says nothing about another bank's legal minimum.

The two sides of a capital ratio

A prudential ratio has a numerator of eligible loss-absorbing resources and a denominator that reflects defined exposures. Regulatory deductions, instrument eligibility and consolidation can make the numerator differ materially from ordinary equity. The applicable jurisdiction and reporting date determine the real definitions.

Risk-weighted assets reflect supervisory or approved-model treatment across exposures. A firm can increase its ratio by raising eligible capital, reducing risky assets or changing the composition of exposures. The same reported ratio can conceal very different absolute balance sheets and leverage.

How losses feed back into both sides

A credit loss reduces earnings and may reduce eligible capital. If credit quality worsens, the risk weights applied to remaining assets may rise at the same time. Computing a loss against the old denominator can overstate the post-stress ratio.

Some transactions change the denominator without reducing the entire economic risk. Selling a recognized exposure with a guarantee, for example, may leave a residual obligation. Inspect risk-transfer terms and accounting treatment before interpreting movements in the headline figure.

Risk-based and unweighted views

A leverage measure compares a defined capital amount with a broadly unweighted exposure measure. It can complement risk-based ratios when model weights are low or uncertain. Large nominal holdings can generate funding and concentration concerns even with low measured risk weights.

Buffers and supervisory expectations can affect distributions or intervention before legal insolvency. Do not infer an actual legal minimum from a classroom threshold. The decision also depends on supervisory definitions, available instruments, currency and the time needed to raise funding.

Method

Analysing a capital action

  1. Reconcile eligible funds

    Start from reported equity and apply only the deductions and additions allowed for the chosen capital definition.

  2. Rebuild the denominator

    Apply the stated weights to each exposure and incorporate any post-stress changes. Keep currency and perimeter consistent.

  3. Quantify the remedy

    Solve either for new capital at fixed risk-weighted assets or reduced risk-weighted assets at fixed capital, stating any gain or loss on the action.

Worked case

A downgrade changes the denominator

A teaching balance sheet has €12m of eligible capital. It holds €80m assets assigned 50% weight and €50m assigned 100%. An illustrative internal requirement is 11%, with no claim about a legal minimum.

Working

  1. Initially risk-weighted assets are €40m + €50m = €90m and the ratio is 13.33%. The unit of the denominator is money, after applying weights.
  2. A €2m loss reduces eligible capital to €10m. Assume the surviving exposure schedule separately gives post-stress risk-weighted assets of €105m. The ratio becomes 9.52%.
  3. To regain 11% at that denominator requires €11.55m eligible capital, an addition of €1.55m. At unchanged capital the maximum denominator would be €10m/0.11 = €90.91m.

Interpretation

Reducing risk-weighted assets by €14.09m need not mean selling €14.09m of securities. The required nominal disposal depends on the weight and on any disposal loss.

A common mistake

Comparing capital divided by gross assets with a risk-based threshold substitutes a different denominator.

How to correct it

Write both numerator and denominator definitions beside every reported ratio and rebuild the applicable threshold amount in euros.

Quick check
A hypothetical rule allows €15m capital and assigns €120m of risk-weighted assets. Total accounting assets are €200m. What is the rule's capital ratio?

Use the denominator specified by the rule. €15m divided by €120m is 12.5%. Dividing by total assets answers a different balance-sheet question.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

An exercise gives €18m accounting equity, €3m deductions, €60m assets at 50% risk weight and €90m at 100%. Assume the exercise requires 12% capital.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate eligible capital, risk-weighted assets, ratio and monetary headroom.

  2. Part B · 2 marks

    Following stress, eligible capital is €13m and risk-weighted assets €125m. How much additional eligible capital restores 12%?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Eligible capital is €15m. The denominator is €30m + €90m = €120m. The ratio is 12.5%, and headroom is €15m − €14.4m = €0.6m.

Part B 2 marks

The requirement in money is €15m. Additional eligible capital of €2m restores it if the denominator and other adjustments stay fixed.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Which constraint determines how much capital a low-risk-weight balance sheet must raise?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

With the same capital qualifying for both tests, I first convert both ratios into euro requirements. This book needs €6m on risk-weighted assets and €8m on leverage exposure. Its €9m capital therefore has €1m usable headroom under the pair. I would inspect the actual capital definitions, business changes and supervisory buffers before applying this teaching threshold to a real bank.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A new low-weight asset adds €50m exposure and €5m risk-weighted assets without changing capital. What is the shortfall?

Compare with the follow-up answer

E rises to €250m and W to €65m. The two requirements become €10m and €6.5m. With €9m eligible capital, at least €1m more is needed if all other amounts remain fixed.
