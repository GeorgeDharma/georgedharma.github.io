Market risk sensitivities

Decompose risk into rates, equity, currency, volatility and credit spread. A sensitivity to one factor is local to the assumed starting market.

The starting point

A bond trader can ask how much a position would lose if interest rates rose slightly while other market prices held steady. A sensitivity measures that local response to one risk factor. One basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point.

State whether the sensitivity is signed and what move it describes. Then apply it to small shocks. A large shock may change the sensitivity itself. Options, for example, can gain or lose value nonlinearly as the underlying price moves.

Theory and method

Key-rate duration and cross-hedging

Partial duration · Curve twist risk

Two bonds have exactly offsetting parallel DV01. Which rate movement can still hurt the book?

A funding book contains a short-dated bond and a long-dated hedge. A parallel rate shock changes both yields in the same direction, so gains on one maturity can offset losses at the other. Actual curves can steepen, flatten or pivot. Key-rate sensitivities revalue exposure to small changes around selected maturities and retain the direction of each result. Testing a twist asks how the hedge behaves when the two rates cease to move together.

How the result is built

ΔV ≈ b₂Δy₂ + b₁₀Δy₁₀

ΔV is the change in euro value. The coefficients b₂ and b₁₀ are signed euro value changes per one-basis-point rise at the two- and ten-year nodes. The changes Δy₂ and Δy₁₀ are yield changes in basis points.

  1. Reprice with a small two-year node rise, holding the ten-year node fixed under the chosen interpolation, then repeat at the ten-year node.
  2. Suppose the book gains €900 for a 1 bp two-year rise and loses €900 for a 1 bp ten-year rise. Their parallel sum is zero.
  3. Under a curve steepening with the short point down 3 bp and long up 5 bp, P&L is €900 × (−3) − €900 × 5 = −€7,200.

When the reasoning holds

  • The approximation assumes changes small enough for sensitivities to stay stable. Large moves or embedded options require nonlinear revaluation.
  • The curve construction used for each node bump must match the convention for the hedge. A different interpolation can shift reported bucket sensitivities.
Terms in this lesson
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
Credit spread
Extra yield over a chosen reference yield. Credit risk, liquidity and market conditions can all affect it.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
Risk factor
A common source of variation in returns, such as market movements or a change in interest rates.
How to read the notation
Basis points and percentage points
A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises by one percentage point, or 100 basis points. Its increase relative to the original rate is (5 − 4) ÷ 4 = 25%.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

A bond desk estimates that a one-basis-point rise reduces its bond value by €2,800. A hedge gains €900 on the same rise. For a parallel five-basis-point rise, net estimated loss is (€2,800 − €900) × 5 = €9,500.

First-order risk in money units

A local sensitivity estimates how much value changes for a small movement in one risk factor. Equity delta gives exposure to the share price. A rate sensitivity may be quoted per basis point, while currency exposure may be quoted per unit of the exchange rate.

Translate sensitivities to a common direction before adding them. A positive DV01 is often quoted as the price increase when yields fall by one basis point. An explicitly signed value change for a yield rise would have the opposite sign on the same long bond.

Curvature and joint changes

One linear approximation uses change in value approximately equal to delta times the factor change. A second-order approximation adds one half of gamma times the squared change. Gamma measures how quickly the first sensitivity itself changes as the underlying price moves.

This matters for options and positions with embedded choices. A hedge can remove the first-order exposure around today's price while leaving curvature, volatility or time exposure. Simultaneous large shocks can also have interaction effects between the factors.

Shape risk within a curve

A parallel yield shift assumes every maturity changes equally. Key-rate sensitivities instead describe response to movements around particular maturities. Matching total exposure at one parallel shock can leave substantial losses if long-term yields rise while short-term yields fall.

State what else the calculation holds fixed, such as credit spreads and volatility. A historical correlation does not make a two-factor hedge perfect in every market. Revalue the combined instruments under changes that break that historical relationship.

Method

Designing a local hedge

  1. Specify the shock unit

    Record signed P&L for a stated one-basis-point, one-currency-unit or one-share-price move.

  2. Solve the hedge ratio

    Divide unwanted exposure by the opposite signed sensitivity of one hedge contract, retaining feasible whole-contract constraints.

  3. Check other directions

    Test a larger move, a curve twist and any material remaining gamma or basis exposure with full valuation where possible.

Worked case

A neutral parallel exposure still loses

A book loses €1,500 for a 1 bp rise at the two-year point and gains €1,500 for a 1 bp rise at ten years. Treat the two sensitivities as linear over the small moves specified.

Working

  1. For a parallel 4 bp rise the book loses €6,000 on the short point and gains €6,000 on the long point, for zero net change.
  2. For a twist in which the two-year point rises 4 bp while the ten-year point falls 3 bp, the changes are minus €6,000 and minus €4,500. Net loss is €10,500.
  3. Matching the sum of the sensitivities eliminated one pattern of yield change. Removing the twist risk would require an offset at the relevant individual points, subject to hedge costs and residual basis.

Interpretation

A governance report that shows the two signed buckets alongside the aggregate can reveal the issue before the first large curve twist.

A common mistake

Adding a quoted positive DV01 directly to a signed P&L per rate rise can reverse the hedge.

How to correct it

Write the money gain or loss on the same small upward shock for each instrument before combining exposures.

Quick check
Two positions respectively lose €1,200 and gain €700 for each basis-point rise. What does a small parallel rise of four basis points imply if the sensitivities stay fixed?

The hedge partly offsets the exposure. The net loss per basis point is €500, hence €2,000 for four. A nonparallel shift in the yield curve would require more detailed sensitivities.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

An option position has delta of €80 per €1 price rise and gamma of €12 per squared euro. A hedge removes €80 of delta and has zero gamma. Ignore volatility, time and higher terms.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Estimate the option's change for a €2 rise before hedging.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Estimate hedged P&L for both a €2 rise and a €2 fall. Why can both have the same sign?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

First-order gain is €160 and curvature adds ½ × 12 × 2² = €24. Approximate total gain is €184.

Part B 3 marks

The hedge removes the linear term in either direction. The remaining quadratic term is +€24 in both cases because squared changes are positive and gamma is positive.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two bonds have exactly offsetting parallel DV01. Which rate movement can still hurt the book?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A parallel hedge only neutralizes the sum of the signed rate sensitivities. Our two-year gain of €900 per rising basis point offsets a ten-year loss of €900 under a common move. With the short yield falling and the long yield rising, both legs lose. I would hedge the specific node pattern or disclose a twist limit and test the full book on larger curves.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A ten-year hedge adds €600 for each 1 bp rise at the long node only. What is the new steepening result?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Long sensitivity becomes minus €300 per basis point. The short leg loses €2,700 and the long leg loses €1,500, for a €4,200 loss. Parallel exposure is now positive €600 per basis point, so the improvement carries a different risk.
