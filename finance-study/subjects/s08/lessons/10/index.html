Backtesting and governance

Compare observed exceptions with the model’s prediction. Investigate clustering, changing exposures and how management acts when a limit is reached.

The starting point

A forecast of risk should be recorded before the result arrives. Backtesting compares those saved forecasts with later profit and loss measured on a compatible basis. A day on which loss exceeds the earlier VaR forecast is an exception.

Test how often exceptions occur and whether they cluster. An approximately correct count over a full year may still hide a model that misses risk for weeks during volatile markets. Investigate new positions, stale data and changes in the market before deciding how to change limits.

Theory and method

Kupiec unconditional coverage test

Proportion-of-failures test · VaR backtesting

What exactly does a Kupiec test examine, and which model failure could pass it?

A trading desk predicts that 1% of days will exceed its daily loss limit. The data could have arisen under that stated exception frequency or under the observed frequency that best fits the sample. Kupiec compares the two binomial likelihoods. A sufficiently large difference challenges unconditional coverage. The test uses the number of exceptions, so the same result can come from isolated bad days or a string of exceptions during a crisis. Their order needs separate examination.

How the result is built

LR = 2[x ln(p̂/p) + (n − x) ln((1 − p̂)/(1 − p))], with p̂ = x/n

LR is the likelihood-ratio statistic, x exception count, n observations, p stated exception probability, p̂ observed frequency, and ln natural logarithm. Interpret 0 ln 0 by its limiting value.

  1. Under a constant exception probability p, ignoring terms that cancel, the likelihood is pˣ(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ˣ.
  2. The unrestricted likelihood is largest at p̂ = x/n. Subtract the restricted log likelihood from that maximum and double the difference.
  3. In 100 days with p = 0.01 and x = 5, p̂ = 0.05 and LR ≈ 8.26. The usual approximate 5% chi-square reference with one degree of freedom is 3.84.

When the reasoning holds

  • The usual chi-square interpretation is asymptotic. Rare exceptions in a short window call for exact binomial checks and cautious judgment.
  • Forecasts and realized P&L must use compatible pre-recorded portfolios and horizons. A change of position definition can look like calibration failure.
Terms in this lesson
Value at risk (VaR)
For a chosen horizon and probability, a threshold in the loss distribution. Larger losses remain possible.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
Backtest
A historical simulation of decisions made under an explicit rule, using information available at each date.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.

First example

At a 99% daily confidence level, 250 trading days imply 2.5 exceptions on average under a well-calibrated model. Three exceptions during one turbulent week raise a different question from three spread through the year. The expected count is an average across possible samples.

Exceptions are observations, not verdicts

Save the risk forecast before the trading day, then compare it with the realized loss on the same portfolio and horizon. A sufficiently high loss is an exception. A model with confidence c expects approximately n × (1 − c) exceptions over n days if forecasts are calibrated.

Random variation means a small sample need not hit that exact count. Under independent days with a constant exception probability p, binomial variance is n × p × (1 − p). A count near the expected number can still conceal persistent exposure to particular markets.

Frequency and independence

Unconditional coverage asks whether the overall exception frequency matches the intended tail probability. Independence asks whether one exception makes another unusually likely. Comparing exceptions immediately following an exception with exceptions on other days helps diagnose delayed response to a volatility regime shift.

Actual trading P&L may include intraday trades, fees and new positions. A hypothetical P&L on frozen starting positions better isolates the model's revaluation forecast. Both comparisons have value when the question and the adjustments are stated.

What governance does with an exception

First verify that positions, prices and forecast dates align. Separate a real stress movement from a corrupted price or a scope mismatch. Then identify whether the discrepancy is isolated, a recurring underestimation or a change in desk behaviour.

Determine who can change model parameters or limits, how the decision is reviewed and what temporary control operates while the issue is unresolved. Deleting inconvenient observations to improve backtest results destroys the evidence that the control needs.

Method

Investigating an exception cluster

  1. Align the records

    Pair each dated ex-ante forecast with compatible actual or hypothetical loss using consistent signs and fees.

  2. Examine time and market context

    Count exceptions and inspect their spacing, affected factors, hedges, position changes and distribution of losses beyond the threshold.

  3. Decide an action

    Assign an owner for recalibration, corrected data or limit review and verify performance again on genuinely later observations.

Worked case

Two desks with six exceptions each

Over 250 days two desks use one-day 98% VaR. Each records six exceptions. One desk's six occur in a row during a funding shock while the other's are separated by at least twenty trading days.

Working

  1. Expected exceptions are 250 × 0.02 = 5. Under an independent constant-probability model the count's variance is 250 × 0.02 × 0.98 = 4.9 and standard deviation about 2.21.
  2. A count of six alone is not especially distant from five. The consecutive events motivate an additional investigation into changing volatility, concentration or a model that uses too long a calm window.
  3. Rebuild each calculation on frozen positions, compare with the time-stamped actual desk trades and examine whether delayed marks preceded the cluster. A comparison of counts alone cannot distinguish those mechanisms.

Interpretation

A manager can hold current limits temporarily while requesting an independent revaluation of the affected factor. A model change should also be tested on fresh observations.

A common mistake

Fitting a historical window until it removes last week's exceptions gives an optimistic view of future performance.

How to correct it

Preserve a genuinely later validation period and retain the original forecasts for comparison.

Quick check
A desk with 100 days of 95% VaR forecasts sees five exceptions, all on consecutive days. What should a reviewer examine?

Five is the expected total across 100 days, but the timing suggests dependence. Examine forecasts, positions and realised losses around the cluster and document what prompted action.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A desk records eight exceptions in 400 days with a 98% VaR. Seven occur within one ten-day interval. An analyst concludes the model is fully validated because the count is exactly as forecast.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find the expected count and explain why the conclusion is incomplete.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Specify two checks that would distinguish model lag from an accounting mismatch.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Expected count is 400 × 0.02 = 8. Overall coverage matches, but the tight cluster raises an independence or conditional-calibration question and exception severity is still unknown.

Part B 3 marks

Reprice the frozen opening book using consistent market closes and compare it with the actual P&L bridge. Examine time-stamped volatility estimates and factor movements to determine whether risk forecasts adapted slowly.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

What exactly does a Kupiec test examine, and which model failure could pass it?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

It tests whether the overall observed exception frequency is compatible with the model's stated frequency under its likelihood assumptions. Five exceptions in 100 days against 1% give about 8.26, enough to challenge coverage using the usual approximate reference. I would inspect sample size, frozen-position P&L and timing. A model that fails repeatedly in one week but is too conservative elsewhere can look adequate on total count.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A 100-day backtest has exactly one exception, immediately after a major portfolio change. Can the Kupiec result clear the new book?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Its count gives p̂ = p and LR equal to zero. That simply reflects full-sample frequency. The post-change history may be too short, the single loss may be large, and the old book may offer weak evidence about the new exposures. I would analyse the two regimes separately.
