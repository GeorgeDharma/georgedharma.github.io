Define the exposure

Choose a base currency, loss convention, horizon and portfolio perimeter. A daily price loss and an annual credit loss measure different events.

The starting point

If a bank says it could lose a million, ask what it owns, in which currency, and over what period. The exposure is the collection of positions whose value or cash payments can change. A loss is meaningful only relative to a stated starting value and set of contracts.

Define gains and losses consistently. If positive numbers mean losses, a fall in asset value contributes positively. A liability that becomes cheaper to repay offsets that loss. Translate all positions at consistent exchange rates and specify whether the calculation includes income or new trades.

Theory and method

Full revaluation and P&L attribution

Factor interaction · Translation exposure

Can separate bond-price and currency sensitivities exactly explain the euro loss on an unhedged dollar bond?

A euro desk holds a dollar bond. It can lose money because the bond sells for fewer dollars, because each dollar buys fewer euros, or because both move together. Translating the starting dollar value at the new exchange rate isolates one cause, while repricing the bond at the old rate isolates another. Those two single-factor tests omit the joint change. Revaluing the end position directly supplies a control total that a risk analyst can decompose and reconcile.

How the result is built

ΔV = S₀ΔP + P₀ΔS + ΔPΔS

V is euro market value, P the entire bond position's dollar value, S euros per dollar, subscript 0 the start, and Δ the end minus start change in each quantity.

  1. Start from V₁ − V₀ = (P₀ + ΔP)(S₀ + ΔS) − P₀S₀ and multiply out all terms.
  2. For a $1m position whose local value falls $50k and euro-per-dollar falls from 0.90 to 0.86, standalone bond and FX effects are −€45k and −€40k.
  3. The interaction is (−$50k) × (−€0.04/$) = +€2k. The local loss is converted at a weaker final dollar, giving a net value change of −€83k.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use the same fixed holdings and valuation times at both ends. Coupon receipts and new positions should be separately bridged.
  • The quote must be euros per dollar for direct multiplication. Reversing the quotation requires taking its reciprocal before using the expression.
Terms in this lesson
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Counterparty
The other party to a financial contract or trade.
First example

A euro-based fund owns a $100,000 receivable and owes $60,000 at the same date. A fall from €0.95 to €0.90 per dollar reduces its net position by €2,000. Reporting the gross receivable alone would show a €5,000 loss.

What exactly can be lost?

An insurer may lose quoted market value, miss a promised repayment or face an immediate cash call. These outcomes can happen at different dates and affect different decisions. Start by stating the question the loss measure must answer for the person responsible.

Trading valuation asks what existing positions would be worth at the end of a fixed horizon. Funding analysis asks which payments actually settle. An asset can gain value while creating a cash need if collateral must be posted on a related contract first.

Choosing the measurement perimeter

Include all positions whose gains and losses legally and economically belong to the chosen portfolio. An offshore subsidiary's cash may be unavailable to meet the parent company's same-day obligations. A guaranteed invoice and an unguaranteed invoice can have different collection risks.

Distinguish gross exposure, net directional exposure and settlement exposure. Opposite currency amounts may offset movements in mark-to-market value while leaving the firm dependent on two distinct customers paying on time. Enforceable netting of claims requires checking the parties and contracts.

Holding period and convention

For a frozen portfolio, use starting positions and revalue them at the endpoint. An actual daily result may also include new trades, fees, interest and realized cash. State whether these components belong to the comparison so an apparent model failure has a defined meaning.

Express loss consistently as starting wealth minus ending wealth including cash received under the specified convention. Converting each position to a common currency allows aggregation. Applying yesterday's conversion to one leg and today's to another can create an artificial mismatch.

Method

Defining a position report

  1. State the decision

    Pair the reporting currency and horizon with the obligation or control being monitored.

  2. Trace ownership

    List contracts, counterparties, maturities and whether legal or operational restrictions prevent offsets.

  3. Revalue jointly

    Compute gains or losses with common market inputs and show a separate cash calendar when settlement dates diverge.

Worked case

Receipts and payables on different dates

A euro-reporting manufacturer will receive $2m in thirty days and pay $1.5m tomorrow. For a simplified immediate valuation, ignore discounting and counterparty default. The dollar falls from €0.96 to €0.91.

Working

  1. The receivable loses $2m × €0.05 = €100,000. The euro amount of the payable falls €75,000, giving a €25,000 net valuation loss.
  2. The company still needs $1.5m tomorrow. The dollar receipt due in thirty days cannot fund that payment unless it is financed or convincingly accelerated.
  3. A risk report can therefore show net long exposure of $0.5m and a separate next-day currency funding requirement of $1.5m before available cash. Both numbers describe the same contracts.

Interpretation

If the customer defaults before the invoice is paid, the original economic hedge is weakened. A second scenario should revalue the remaining payable without relying on full collection.

A common mistake

Reporting the net dollar position as tomorrow's funding need hides a maturity gap.

How to correct it

Record value sensitivity and settled cash by maturity bucket, including the day's actual available currency balances.

Quick check
A firm will receive $300,000 and pay $250,000 tomorrow. A dollar falls from €0.94 to €0.92. What is the euro loss on its combined commitments?

The net receivable is $50,000. Translating it at a rate €0.02 lower reduces its euro value by €1,000. Matching the currency and date made netting appropriate here.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A firm owns a $900,000 invoice due in sixty days, owes $600,000 tomorrow and has no cash. The dollar rises from €0.88 to €0.92, with all other quantities fixed.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find the immediate net change in euro wealth and the reportable loss under a positive-loss convention.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What must tomorrow's funding report show, and how could default of the invoice affect the exposure?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The net long position is $300,000. Euro wealth rises by €12,000. Under the chosen convention, the loss is minus €12,000.

Part B 3 marks

It must show a gross $600,000 due before collections. A total invoice default removes the long-dollar claim and leaves the payable as a short-dollar position.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can separate bond-price and currency sensitivities exactly explain the euro loss on an unhedged dollar bond?

Compare with a spoken answer

I would start with the €900,000 opening value and the €817,000 closing value. The exact loss is €83,000. A price-only run loses €45,000 and an FX-only run €40,000, but the position's dollar value also shrank before translating it at the weaker dollar. That joint effect adds back €2,000. I would retain it explicitly or assign it according to a disclosed sequential convention.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If you reprice the bond first and then translate the reduced dollar position, how much of the loss is recorded as FX?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The bond step remains minus €45,000. FX on the ending $950,000 position is −€38,000. The two steps total −€83,000 because the €2,000 interaction has been included in the smaller FX step.
