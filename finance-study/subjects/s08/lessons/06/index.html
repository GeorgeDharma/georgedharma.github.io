Liquidity and funding

A solvent institution can face a cash shortfall when assets become difficult to sell. Identify margin calls, withdrawal assumptions and available funding.

The starting point

A lender can own assets worth more than its debts and still run out of cash today. Solvency asks whether assets cover obligations in value. Funding liquidity asks whether cash and drawable funding can meet payments as they fall due.

Use a calendar of cash needs and count funds that are available within each interval. A securities sale may settle too late. Secured borrowing may advance only part of the collateral's value. That deduction is called a haircut.

Theory and method

Basel liquidity coverage ratio

LCR · High-quality liquid assets

Can incoming payments of €90m offset all €100m of stressed thirty-day outflows in a standard LCR illustration?

A bank expects customers to repay loans during the same month that depositors withdraw cash. An internal cash forecast might net almost all the outflows against those expected receipts. A standardized liquidity measure limits reliance on inflows and asks how much eligible liquid asset stock is available under stress. Applying the cap matters particularly for banks showing large contractual receipts. A ratio over the whole month also leaves open whether a payment due on day two can actually settle.

How the result is built

LCR = H / [O − min(I, 0.75O)]

H is eligible unencumbered high-quality liquid assets after applicable haircuts, O stressed thirty-day cash outflows, I eligible stressed inflows, and min chooses the smaller amount. Quantities are in millions of the same currency under the ordinary capped case.

  1. With O = €100m the standard illustration caps recognized inflows at €75m. From €90m expected eligible receipts only €75m enter the denominator.
  2. Net outflows are €100m − €75m = €25m. With €30m eligible liquid assets, the ratio is 30/25 = 120%.
  3. If inflows fall to €60m the cap stops binding. Net outflows become €40m and the same liquid stock covers only 75%.

When the reasoning holds

  • Only cash flows and liquid assets eligible under the applicable rules count. A pledged security cannot be treated as freely available HQLA.
  • The 75% calculation describes the ordinary cap. Local implementations and specified exemptions require a separate regulatory check before interpreting a real institution's ratio.

Terms in this lesson
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Margin
In business accounts, profit expressed relative to sales. In derivatives trading, margin is collateral required to support a position.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
First example

A bank faces €12m of payments today. It has €3m cash and can borrow against €10m of eligible collateral at a 20% haircut. Cash plus €8m of borrowing provides €11m, leaving a €1m same-day gap.

Funding pressure can amplify falling prices

Market liquidity describes the ability to sell an asset near its quoted price in the quantity required. Funding liquidity describes the ability to obtain cash by a deadline. If many institutions sell collateral at once, sale prices can fall and lenders may demand more collateral from the remaining holders.

This feedback can convert a cash shortfall into an economic loss through forced sales. An asset shown at €100 may raise far less in an urgent sale or borrowing arrangement. The effect of a haircut on funding is separate from any accounting write-down.

Constructing a survival horizon

Start with cash genuinely available and add inflows when they settle. Subtract contractual obligations and plausible withdrawals or collateral calls under the selected stress. A cumulative cash balance that first falls below a required floor identifies a stress deadline.

Avoid assuming every uncommitted credit line will be renewed during a shared crisis. Count already pledged collateral once. Consider currencies and entities separately when a surplus in one place cannot reach the deficit before the payment cutoff.

Stock ratios and dated flows

A liquidity coverage measure compares a defined pool of liquid assets with stressed net outflows over a specified period. Its interpretation depends on eligibility, runoff assumptions, inflow limits and local rules. A positive monthly ratio can coexist with a morning payment gap.

Structural funding measures ask whether longer-lived assets have sufficiently stable financing. They answer a different question from today's collateral call. A risk committee needs both the immediate cash calendar and the funding profile beyond the stress window.

Method

Preparing the liquidity ladder

  1. Classify access

    Separate free cash, unencumbered eligible collateral, committed drawable lines and conditional resources. Record settlement delays.

  2. Translate collateral to cash

    Use realizable values and applicable haircuts on each business date. Avoid treating a line secured by the same assets as an extra independent source.

  3. Run cumulative balances

    Add flows due in each interval and identify the earliest breach before later receipts can mask it.

Worked case

A market decline raises cash demands

A broker holds €4m free cash and €10m unpledged collateral. It can draw same-day funds at an initial 10% haircut. Payments due today total €11m and proceeds from customer assets arrive tomorrow.

Working

  1. Under ordinary conditions, collateral raises €9m. Combined resources are €13m, leaving €2m after today’s payments. No tomorrow collection is needed to meet today's amount.
  2. Under stress, collateral prices fall 20% and the haircut rises to 25%. The lender advances 0.75 × €8m = €6m. Cash and secured borrowing now total €10m.
  3. The desk is €1m short even before any additional margin call. Repricing collateral and applying the new haircut sequentially matters because the haircut applies to the reduced market value.

Interpretation

A facility that cannot settle before today's cutoff does not fix this particular gap, even if its nominal size exceeds €1m.

A common mistake

Adding a security's full value, its pledged borrowing proceeds and a later disposal value counts the same resource repeatedly.

How to correct it

Mark each asset as available, pledged or sold in each scenario and enforce the payment and receipt dates.

Quick check
A fund owes €9m today, holds €3m cash and can raise 75% against €8m of unpledged collateral immediately. What is its funding position after the payment?

The collateral raises €6m. With €3m cash, available funding is exactly €9m. Pledging the same collateral a second time would overstate liquidity.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A firm has €2m cash and securities worth €12m. In stress their price falls 25%, lenders apply a 20% haircut, and all resulting borrowing is available today. It owes €10m today and will receive €3m tomorrow.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Find today's borrowing capacity and cash gap.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Would borrowing another €1m against the same already pledged assets be a justified remedy? Explain one workable alternative.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Stressed collateral value is €9m. Borrowing capacity is 80% × €9m = €7.2m, so €9.2m is available and the gap is €0.8m.

Part B 3 marks

No unused collateral capacity is stated. A separately committed unsecured line drawable today or an agreed deferral of at least €0.8m could close the gap.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can incoming payments of €90m offset all €100m of stressed thirty-day outflows in a standard LCR illustration?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Under the ordinary cap only €75m of the €90m can offset the €100m outflow. The denominator is €25m and €30m of fully eligible liquid assets gives 120%. Separately I would check when the payments fall, where the assets sit and how quickly they can be monetized. A month-wide coverage calculation cannot establish that every intraday deadline is funded.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A €12m outflow comes tomorrow but only €9m of liquid assets can be monetized before the cutoff. What would you report?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Report a €3m dated shortfall alongside the thirty-day ratio, before other confirmed funding. Validate immediately drawable resources or an agreed timing change. The remaining liquid assets may support the aggregate calculation yet be unavailable by tomorrow's deadline.
