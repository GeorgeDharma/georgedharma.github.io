Operational and model risk

Data errors, control failures and misspecified models can create losses outside a price model. Specify monitoring, escalation and independent challenge.

The starting point

A pricing model can calculate its formula perfectly and still produce a bad number. An input might be stale, a system may confuse percentages with decimals, or the model may systematically miss an important source of risk. These call for different controls.

Operational risk concerns failures of people, processes or systems and external events. Model risk includes unsuitable assumptions, incorrect implementation and misuse. Independent review, comparisons with outside prices, ownership of data and an escalation path help locate the source of a discrepancy.

Theory and method

Brier score for probability forecasts

Proper scoring rule · Probabilistic model validation

Two credit models rank all borrowers in the same order. How can one issue worse probability forecasts?

A lender needs actual probabilities to set reserves and price loans. An ordering can identify which file looks worse while saying little about whether its default chance is 2% or 20%. The Brier score averages squared differences between stated probabilities and actual binary outcomes. It rewards honest probability forecasts in expectation. In a particular short sample luck still matters, so validation also examines calibration by groups, data definitions and actual use of the predictions.

How the result is built

BS = (1/n) Σ(pᵢ − yᵢ)²

n counts observations, pᵢ is the prior forecast probability between zero and one, yᵢ is one for default and zero otherwise in the specified horizon, Σ sums borrowers, and BS is a unitless mean squared score.

  1. For a borrower whose true event chance is q, expected squared score equals q(1 − p)² + (1 − q)p².
  2. Expanding gives (p − q)² + q(1 − q). The second term is irreducible uncertainty and the first is minimized at p = q.
  3. With outcomes 0, 0, 1 and forecasts 0.1, 0.2, 0.7 the score is (0.01 + 0.04 + 0.09)/3 = 0.0467. Forecasts 0.2, 0.3, 0.5 keep the ranking but score 0.1267.

When the reasoning holds

  • Predictions must be recorded before outcomes, for consistent horizons and default definitions. Revised or immature outcomes can bias validation.
  • Scores must be compared on the same or comparable borrower populations. Different base rates change even an honest model's attainable score.

First example

A feed reports volatility as 20, meaning 20%. The pricing engine expects 0.20. A range check rejects the input before publication, and a reviewer compares the rejected run with independent market prices before approving a correction.

Understanding the failure mechanism

An operations incident can result from incorrect settlement instructions, a failed service provider, misconduct or an external interruption. Loss frequency and loss size may require different controls. A daily reconciliation can find many errors, while a rare regional outage requires a separate continuity plan.

A model may be conceptually wrong, implemented incorrectly or used outside its intended scope. Agreement with another spreadsheet is weak evidence if both rely on the same assumptions, inputs or code. Identify the decision supported and the consequences of systematically wrong estimates.

Independent validation with a specific purpose

Conceptual review asks whether the economic mechanism describes the portfolio. Implementation tests check formulas, units and boundary cases using independent calculations. Outcome analysis compares saved forecasts with later realizations after allowing for changes in data and composition.

The independence of a reviewer matters, but it cannot replace relevant evidence. An independently operated model using the same mortgage prepayment assumptions can repeat the original error. Build a challenger that differs in the mechanism being questioned and compare predictions for relevant borrower groups.

Controls before and after an incident

Preventive controls reduce the probability of an error, while detective controls find errors that pass through. A recovery plan limits the damage and restores service. A formal override can be appropriate when a model is known to be outside scope, provided timing, reason and authority are recorded.

Define thresholds that trigger review, a fallback valuation and who can release a blocked report. Near misses provide evidence even when no booked loss occurred. A large bias that cancels at portfolio level should still be examined within subgroups.

Method

Following an error from source to decision

  1. Reproduce the input

    Trace a small case from the original record through transformations, preserving timestamps, units and overrides.

  2. Challenge the mechanism

    Compare the observed result with an independent calculation or data split designed to distinguish the suspected errors.

  3. Contain and retest

    Estimate affected positions, apply a controlled fallback, document decisions and rerun appropriate historical and boundary tests.

Worked case

A loss forecast cancels in aggregate

A consumer model predicts 2% annual defaults for 500 stable-income customers and 8% for 500 irregular-income customers. Observed defaults are 20 and 30 respectively. Treat this as a retrospective diagnostic rather than a statistical proof.

Working

  1. Predicted defaults are 10 and 40, total 50. Actual defaults also sum to 50, so an aggregate error check reports zero.
  2. The stable-income group underpredicted defaults by 10 and the other overpredicted by 10. Observed rates are 4% and 6%. Opposing subgroup errors cancel.
  3. Investigate sample uncertainty, label definitions, changes in underwriting and whether group characteristics still mean what the original model assumed. Test additional periods before replacing both rates with the observed values.

Interpretation

A correction based solely on a single small sample might worsen calibration next year. A validation owner can require monitoring while limiting decisions most exposed to the error.

A common mistake

A matched total can hide offsetting errors and an apparently good challenger can inherit the same data defect.

How to correct it

Compare meaningful groups and run an independent input lineage check before changing model parameters.

Quick check
Two independently coded models return similar results, but both assume every mortgage prepays at the same rate. Observed prepayments diverge sharply by borrower group. What should be challenged first?

Two implementations can share the same mistaken economic assumption. Compare predicted and observed cash flows by relevant borrower groups and check how the valuation changes.

Written practice · 6 marks

Independent exam problem

A lender forecasts 5 defaults among 100 new customers and 15 among 100 repeat customers. Actual defaults are 12 and 8.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate aggregate and group errors using actual minus predicted defaults.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Design one implementation test and one test of the assumed behaviour.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Total predicted and realized both equal 20. Group errors are +7 for new customers and −7 for repeat customers.

Part B 3 marks

Recompute predictions from verified inputs for selected records to check code and labels. Compare repeated cohorts or an independently specified model to see whether group risks have genuinely shifted.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two credit models rank all borrowers in the same order. How can one issue worse probability forecasts?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would preserve both ranking and probability checks. Model A here predicts 10%, 20% and 70%, and achieves a Brier score of about 0.047. Model B ranks the borrowers identically but scores about 0.127. That matters for expected losses. Three files cannot establish lasting superiority, so I would repeat the comparison out of time and examine observed default rates by predicted-risk bucket.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A portfolio has only one default per thousand loans. A model always forecasts zero. Could it show a low score?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Yes. With 999 zero errors and one squared error of one, the average is 0.001. Compare it with credible base-rate forecasts on the same sample, examine high-risk segments, and test economic consequences. A small raw score by itself says little across very different event rates.
