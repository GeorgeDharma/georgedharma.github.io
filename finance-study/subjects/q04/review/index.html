Trees, estimated payoffs and model checking

Learners compare continuation and exercise by backward induction, discount simulated payoffs, reduce estimator variance and run independent benchmark calculations. These are the building blocks for least-squares exercise across paths.

Valuation equations and numerical error

New and expanded lessons

Q04.11

Value a Bermudan exercise decision with least-squares Monte Carlo

Q04.02 handles a small exercise tree and Q04.03 estimates simulated payoffs. The new chapter learns a continuation function from training paths, freezes an exercise policy and prices its realized cash flows on independent paths.

  • Fit a simple conditional continuation estimate and use it at an early-exercise date.
  • Distinguish a feasible stopping policy's value from an estimate using future information.

Q04.12

Price a European payoff with a Fourier-cosine expansion

Q04.05 prices on a spatial grid and Q04.03 prices sampled paths. This chapter obtains prices from a terminal distribution's characteristic function, computes cosine payoff weights and checks the sign and normalization against a directly integrable claim.

  • Relate the real part of a characteristic function to expected cosine terms.
  • Price a bounded-state call with the COS formula and diagnose truncation and normalization errors.

Q04.13

Choose a simulation scheme for paths and for prices

Q04.03 distinguishes sampling and time-grid error, and Q04.10 studies numerical convergence. This extension derives the scalar Milstein correction, contrasts strong and weak accuracy, and checks both schemes against an exact solution using the same Brownian increment.

  • Compute Euler–Maruyama and Milstein updates with correct time scaling.
  • Design coupled grid tests and distinguish path accuracy, expectation bias and Monte Carlo uncertainty.

Public benchmarks

ENSAE Paris, Institut Polytechnique de Paris, France

Numerical Methods in Financial Engineering

The course connects no-arbitrage pricing to tree, PDE and simulation approaches, and lists regression-based methods for American exercise, including Longstaff–Schwartz.

UCLA Anderson School of Management, United States

Francis A. Longstaff and Eduardo S. Schwartz, Valuing American Options by Simulation: A Simple Least-Squares Approach

The researchers use least squares to approximate conditional continuation cash flows, allowing exercise decisions along simulated paths in models with multiple state variables.

Delft University of Technology, Netherlands

Fang Fang and Cornelis W. Oosterlee, A Novel Option Pricing Method Based on Fourier-Cosine Series Expansions

The paper derives a Fourier-cosine expansion for European claims using the transition characteristic function and payoff coefficients, and investigates truncation and numerical accuracy.

Columbia University, United States

Martin Haugh, IEOR E4703 Monte-Carlo Simulation, Simulating Stochastic Differential Equations

The lecture compares Euler and Milstein discretizations, defines strong and weak convergence, and explains the consequences of paths crossing barriers between grid points.
