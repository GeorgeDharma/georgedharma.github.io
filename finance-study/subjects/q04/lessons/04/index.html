Variance reduction

Antithetic draws or a control variate can reduce estimator variance without changing the intended expected payoff when implemented consistently.

The starting point

If one simulated outcome lands unusually high, another related observation can sometimes counterbalance its noise. Antithetic sampling pairs a symmetric random draw with its opposite. The pair average has lower variance than two independent draws when the paired payoffs are negatively correlated. Analyze independent pair averages to estimate sampling uncertainty.

A control variate uses a related simulated quantity with a known expectation. From the target sample mean subtract β times the difference between the control’s sample mean and its known mean. A suitable coefficient takes advantage of their correlation. Choosing β in advance or from an independent pilot avoids complications from fitting it on the same draws.

Theory and method

Rao–Blackwellization by conditional Monte Carlo

Conditional expectation estimator · Law of total variance

How could a desk reduce simulation variance by removing one simulated coin flip from every path?

A path simulation first identifies a market state and then simulates a final default to determine a payout. If the conditional default probability is available analytically, the final random draw adds avoidable noise. Replace the terminal draw by its average payout within the already simulated state. The outer market state remains random. This conditional Monte Carlo argument uses the same conditional-expectation principle as Rao–Blackwellization.

How the result is built

Y = E[X | G], E[Y] = E[X], Var(X) = E[Var(X | G)] + Var(Y)

X is the discounted euro payoff; G is the information in the simulated outer state; Y is its conditional mean. E and Var are expectation and variance under the pricing simulation. Assume square-integrability.

  1. Split X into the conditional mean and a residual. The residual averages to zero within each state and is uncorrelated with the conditional mean.
  2. Simulate two equally likely states with a €100 payment probability 0.1 or 0.5. Conditional payoffs are €10 and €50. The common mean is €30.
  3. Raw payoff variance is 100² × 0.3 × 0.7 = 2,100. Conditional means have variance [(10−30)² + (50−30)²]/2 = 400, removing 1,700 squared euros per path.

When the reasoning holds

  • The conditional expectation must be correctly computed under the same model. An approximate conditional payoff can introduce bias.
  • Efficiency also depends on computation time. A very expensive inner analytic evaluation may offset some variance saving per unit of wall-clock time.
Terms in this lesson
Correlation
A measure of how closely two series move together in a linear pattern.
Option
A contract giving the buyer a right to buy or sell on stated terms. The buyer pays a premium for the right.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Standard error
An estimate of how much a statistic would vary across repeated samples under the assumed sampling process.
How to read the notation
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.
A slope or exposure · β
In a regression a coefficient describes how the fitted outcome changes with one unit of its input, holding the other included inputs fixed. A market beta of 1.2 gives a fitted change of 1.2 percentage points for a one-point change in market return.
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.

All notation examples

First example

The sample mean option payoff is €8.40. A discounted, non-dividend-paying stock averages €101 on those paths and its known pricing expectation is €100. With fixed β = 0.5, the adjusted estimate is 8.40 − 0.5 × (101 − 100) = €7.90.

Remove noise using a related random quantity

A control variate is a simulated quantity Y with a known pricing expectation μᵧ. If X and Y move together across paths, part of the sample error in X can be predicted from the deviation of the sample mean of Y from μᵧ. Define X* = X − b(Y − μᵧ).

For a fixed coefficient b, E[X*] = E[X] because the subtracted term has expectation zero. The variance is Var(X) + b²Var(Y) − 2bCov(X,Y). Differentiating identifies the optimal coefficient b = Cov(X,Y)/Var(Y) when Var(Y) is positive.

How much variance can be removed

Substitution of the optimal coefficient leaves Var(X)(1 − ρ²), where ρ is the correlation between X and Y. A highly correlated control can therefore reduce the number of paths needed for a target precision. A poor sign or scale for b can instead increase noise.

The control’s expectation must match the implemented model, measure and discount convention. Estimate b on an independent pilot or justify any adjustment when fitting on the same paths. Replacing a known mean with an incorrect historical estimate introduces bias.

Pair opposite shocks

Antithetic sampling simulates each random input together with a symmetric opposite input and averages the two payoffs. Both paths must retain the required marginal distribution. For equal payoff variance σ² and pair correlation ρ, the pair-average variance is σ²(1 + ρ)/2.

At a fixed number of payoff evaluations antithetics improve over independent pairs if the within-pair correlation is negative. Error estimates should treat pair averages as individual independent observations if pairs are generated independently. Some nonmonotone payoffs produce little benefit.

Method

Design a variance experiment

  1. Choose a suitable control

    Identify a known pricing expectation and use the identical simulated market path for control and target.

  2. Check correlation and scaling

    Estimate sign and magnitude on pilot data. Compare the adjusted and original variances at equal computational cost.

  3. Report the sampling unit

    Identify whether standard errors are computed across raw independent paths, independent pairs or other independent batches.

Worked case

Reducing the cost of simulation

An option’s discounted payoff has variance 25, a control’s variance is 16 and their covariance is 12. The control mean is known to be 40. The target and control sample means on a new production sample are 8.50 and 41.20.

Working

  1. The optimum fixed coefficient is 12/16 = 0.75. Correlation is 12/(5 × 4) = 0.60.
  2. The adjusted estimate is 8.50 − 0.75 × (41.20 − 40) = 7.60. The adjustment removes the part of the observed target mean associated with the above-average control.
  3. The adjusted path variance is 25(1 − 0.6²) = 16. With 400 independent paths the standard error falls from 5/20 = 0.25 to 4/20 = 0.20.

Interpretation

Both estimates remain noisy. A single adjusted value closer to a benchmark is weaker evidence than repeated variance comparisons with a correctly centered control.

A common mistake

Use a control with a strong observed correlation even if its theoretical mean is unknown.

How to correct it

A control requires a correct centering value or a carefully designed independent estimation method with propagated uncertainty. A fixed centering error shifts the resulting price estimate.

Quick check
If the control averages €98 instead, its known mean is €100 and β is 0.4, how does the adjustment change the unadjusted payoff mean?

Subtracting 0.4 × (98 − 100) adds 0.80. The adjustment responds to the discrepancy from the known control mean, with a sign determined by β.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

Two antithetic payoffs each have variance 9 and correlation −0.5. A production run generates 100 independent pairs.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate the pair-average variance and standard error of the overall estimate.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Compare with 200 independent unpaired evaluations and describe when the advantage disappears.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Pair variance is 9(1 − 0.5)/2 = 2.25. With 100 independent pair averages, the standard error is √2.25/10 = 0.15.

Part B 4 marks

Independent evaluations give 3/√200 ≈ 0.212. At zero within-pair correlation the equal-cost benefit disappears. Positive within-pair correlation can be worse than independent evaluations.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How could a desk reduce simulation variance by removing one simulated coin flip from every path?

Compare with a spoken answer

I would simulate the market state and replace the final payoff draw by its state-specific mean. The tower property preserves the €30 price, and the variance decomposition shows exactly which noise disappears. A fixed number of outer paths now gives about 44% of the previous standard error. I would compare runtime as well as variance before putting it into production.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Would replacing both state-specific values by an unverified €30 guess produce an even better estimator?

Compare with the follow-up answer

A constant has no sample variance, but its value must be the actual model expectation. If that number is already known the pricing problem is solved. An arbitrary constant discards state dependence and can produce an entirely biased price with a misleading zero standard error.
