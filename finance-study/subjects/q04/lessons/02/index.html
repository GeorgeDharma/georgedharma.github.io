Trees and early exercise

Price backward through a lattice. At an American exercise date compare the immediate payoff with the discounted continuation value.

The starting point

A binomial tree describes two possible market moves per time step. Begin at maturity, where the payoff is known at every final node. Step backward. At each earlier node, use the model’s pricing weights to average the two future values and discount the result. This computes the value of continuing to hold the option.

An American option allows exercise on or before maturity. At each allowed exercise node, its value is the larger of immediate exercise value and continuation value. The comparison must use amounts valued at the same node, and future child values must already reflect any later exercise rights.

Theory and method

Snell envelope and optimal stopping

American exercise · Backward induction

Why does giving a holder the choice to wait force the American value to be at least the European value?

A borrower or option holder sometimes controls when a contract ends. At each permitted date the holder can exercise immediately or preserve the right to act later. The right to decide after seeing the next state is economically valuable. Backward induction values that flexibility by choosing the better action separately in each future state, then averaging. Under a fixed positive numeraire the discounted value process is the Snell envelope of discounted exercise payoffs.

How the result is built

Yₜ = max(Gₜ, Eᵠ[Yₜ₊₁ | Fₜ])

Gₜ is immediate exercise payoff measured in numeraire units, Yₜ the American value in those same units, Fₜ current information, and Eᵠ the conditional expectation under the associated pricing measure. The recurrence applies at an exercise date.

  1. At expiry the only remaining value is exercise payoff, so value and exercise payoff coincide at that final date.
  2. At the previous permitted date compare exercise with the conditional expected value of the next optimally managed position. On non-exercise dates use continuation alone.
  3. At a node with exercise 8 and continuation 6, retain 8. At another with exercise 1 and continuation 4, retain 4. With equal weights their parent continuation is 6 in numeraire units.

When the reasoning holds

  • Exercise must be legally allowed at the node. A Bermudan calendar excludes exercise on all other dates, regardless of immediate intrinsic value.
  • Continuation must already incorporate later optimal choices. Averaging fixed-date payoffs first can lose the state-dependent value of waiting.
Terms in this lesson
Option
A contract giving the buyer a right to buy or sell on stated terms. The buyer pays a premium for the right.
Martingale
A process whose expected future value, given current information, equals its current value under the specified probability measure.
Replicating portfolio
A combination of traded claims that delivers the same future cash flows as a contract in every state covered by the model.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
How to read the notation
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

All notation examples

First example

At a node a stock is €80, a put has strike €100 and cash grows 5% over the step. Next stock prices €100 and €60 have pricing weights 0.60 and 0.40. Continuation gives (0.60 × 0 + 0.40 × 40)/1.05 = €15.24. Exercise pays €20 now, so exercise is optimal there.

Construct the one-step lattice

A stock at S can reach Su or Sd after one step, where u > d. Cash accumulates by gross factor R. In a frictionless model with d < R < u, the cash-measure up probability is q = (R − d)/(u − d). This choice makes the discounted stock a martingale.

Starting from a known terminal payoff, calculate continuation as [qVᵤ + (1 − q)V𝒹]/R. This is equivalent to pricing the one-step replicating portfolio. Values at the next level must already incorporate any exercise opportunities that remain after that node.

Model the exercise decision

For a European holder, exercise is allowed only on specified terminal or contractual dates. An American holder can compare immediate exercise value E with continuing value C at every allowed node. The node value is max(E,C). A Bermudan option permits that maximum only at a subset of dates.

The maximum must be taken before averaging at the previous level. Different later states may have different optimal exercise decisions. Calculating the expected payoff under one exercise date imposed across all states can undervalue flexibility.

Read the approximation as a model

A finite tree approximates continuous market dynamics through chosen step sizes, movement factors and probabilities. Some parameter combinations give invalid probabilities or poor convergence. Raising the step count while keeping a consistent annual model is a numerical test. Changing annual volatility changes the underlying model.

Write the asset’s dividend assumptions and whether an exercise payoff includes a dividend or accrued interest. An apparently unexpected early-exercise boundary may follow from those contractual features or from a poorly constructed grid.

Method

Roll back prices

  1. Fill the final nodes

    At maturity compute the contractual payoff in every attainable stock state.

  2. Take the correct node action

    For each previous node calculate discounted continuation first, then compare with exercise only where exercise is allowed.

  3. Validate the root

    Compare with a European version, an immediate exercise lower bound and a finer consistent lattice where appropriate.

Worked case

A two-step American put

A stock starts at 100. Each step multiplies it by 1.2 or 0.8. Cash grows 5% per step. A put with strike 105 can be exercised at either step or immediately. Use the same factors at every node.

Working

  1. The pricing up weight is (1.05 − 0.8)/0.4 = 0.625. Terminal stock prices are 144, 96 and 64, giving put payoffs 0, 9 and 41.
  2. At stock 120 continuation is (0.625 × 0 + 0.375 × 9)/1.05 ≈ 3.2143, better than exercise at zero. At stock 80 continuation is (0.625 × 9 + 0.375 × 41)/1.05 = 20, below exercise value 25.
  3. The root continuation is (0.625 × 3.2143 + 0.375 × 25)/1.05 ≈ 10.8418. This exceeds current exercise value 5, so the American put is worth approximately 10.84 in this tree.

Interpretation

Early exercise occurs only after the first down move. The root price incorporates that future choice even though exercising immediately is suboptimal.

At the board

Replicate one option with a share and a loan

A share at 100 finishes at 120 or 80 after one year. A call with exercise price 100 pays 20 or zero. The annual risk-free rate is 5%. Assume frictionless trading, borrowing and lending at the stated rate, and no dividends.

The hedge ratio is (20 − 0) / (120 − 80) = 0.5 share per option. Borrow just enough for the down-state payoff to be zero. The net cost 50 − 38.0952 is the no-arbitrage call price in this two-state model. If it were possible to buy the same two payoffs more cheaply elsewhere, the expensive position could be sold against the cheaper one.

A common mistake

Use observed historical up frequencies directly as lattice weights and discount the risky expected payoff at the cash rate.

How to correct it

For a replicable claim, match traded stock and cash prices to obtain pricing weights. Physical probabilities can describe realized trends without yielding arbitrage-consistent discounting.

Test the assumptions in the interactive model

Quick check
At another permitted exercise node, immediate exercise is worth €8 and the discounted continuation value is €9.30. What value enters the tree?

Exercise and continuation are alternative actions at that node. Backward induction selects the higher value. A positive immediate payoff alone does not make immediate exercise optimal.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

At a put node stock is 75, strike 90 and one-step stock outcomes are 90 or 60. Cash grows by 4%.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Calculate the up pricing probability using current and terminal stock values.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Compare immediate exercise with continuation, and give the node’s value for American and European contracts.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

Expected stock at the cash growth rate is 75 × 1.04 = 78. Solve 90q + 60(1 − q) = 78 to obtain q = 0.60.

Part B 4 marks

Terminal payoffs are zero and 30. Continuation = 0.4 × 30/1.04 ≈ 11.54. Immediate exercise pays 15. The American value is 15 and the European value here is about 11.54.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why does giving a holder the choice to wait force the American value to be at least the European value?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Holding an American claim includes the feasible strategy of waiting until the European exercise date. The optimal stopping value must therefore be at least the value of that strategy, provided every other contractual term is identical. The example adds one full unit at the parent because the holder can exercise only on the branch where waiting destroys value.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The root itself offers exercise for 6.40 in numeraire units. What should the holder do?

Compare with the follow-up answer

At the root the value becomes max(6.40, 6) = 6.40. The earlier branch calculations still correctly value continuation. A closing rule prohibiting root exercise would instead leave the value at 6.
