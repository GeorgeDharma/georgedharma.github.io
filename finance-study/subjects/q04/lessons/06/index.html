Curves and swaps

Bootstrap discount factors from specified instruments. Price fixed and floating legs with conventions consistent with their discount and forecast curves.

The starting point

A discount factor Dₜ is today’s price of one unit of currency paid at time t by the assumed risk-free or collateralized cash account. Bootstrapping extracts factors in maturity order. Each new traded instrument contributes an equation, and earlier factors already determined can be used to solve it.

For a swap, value each fixed payment and each forecast floating payment under the relevant conventions. In a simple single-curve, annual, unit-accrual example beginning on a reset date, the par fixed rate is (1 − Dₙ) divided by the sum of the payment-date factors. Many real swaps use separate forecasting and discounting curves.

Theory and method

Forward-rate identity and swap-annuity weighting

Par swap rate · Discount-factor bootstrapping

A swap trader calls the par coupon an average forward rate. Which average, and can its longest rate dominate?

A swap exchanges several future floating payments for fixed payments. At inception the par coupon equalizes the two present values. An early forward payment and a late forward payment can have different accrual fractions and discount factors, so equal arithmetic weights usually misprice the fixed leg. The present-value weight of each unit fixed coupon determines how much each projected floating rate contributes to the par rate.

How the result is built

K = ΣⱼwⱼFⱼ, wⱼ = δⱼDⱼ / A, A = ΣⱼδⱼDⱼ

K is the decimal annual par rate, Fⱼ annual forecast or known floating rate for period j, δⱼ its accrual year fraction, Dⱼ the payment-date discount factor and A the fixed-leg annuity per unit of notional in year units.

  1. Per unit of notional, the fixed leg is KA and the floating leg is ΣδⱼDⱼFⱼ. Set these equal to obtain the weighted mean.
  2. For three equal annual accruals with discount factors 0.96, 0.90 and 0.84, A=2.70 and weights are 0.35556, 0.33333 and 0.31111.
  3. For forwards of 2%, 4% and 6%, contributions are 0.7111, 1.3333 and 1.8667 percentage points, giving par K=3.9111%.

When the reasoning holds

  • The simple weights presume aligned payment and accrual schedules for fixed and floating legs. Different frequencies require separate leg schedules.
  • When forwards and discount factors come from different curves, use the actual forwards. The single-curve floating-leg identity 1 − Dₙ may no longer hold.

Terms in this lesson
Swap
A contract to exchange payment streams, such as fixed interest for floating interest.
Discount factor
The price today of one unit of currency payable at a particular future date under specified risk assumptions.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Coupon
The interest payment specified in a bond’s terms.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Forward
A contract to exchange an agreed amount at a set price on a future date.
How to read the notation
Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.
Summation · ∑
The symbol asks you to add the terms for the listed items. For weights of 0.6 and 0.4 and returns of 5% and 2%, ∑ wᵢrᵢ means 0.6 × 5% + 0.4 × 2% = 3.8%. A standalone capital sigma (Σ) can also name a covariance matrix in portfolio equations.
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.

All notation examples

First example

A one-year 4% deposit implies D₁ = 1/1.04 ≈ 0.96154. A two-year bond at par pays annual coupons of 5% and principal 1 at maturity. Solving 1 = 0.05D₁ + 1.05D₂ gives D₂ ≈ 0.90659. Those factors imply a two-year par swap rate of 5% under the stated single-curve assumptions.

Bootstrap rather than average yields

A discount factor D(t) prices one currency unit delivered on a specified date. A fixed-coupon bond trades as the sum of each payment times its date-specific factor. If earlier factors are known, the next instrument’s price provides an equation for its final missing factor.

For annual payments with coupon c per unit of principal and price P, the final factor is [P − cΣD(earlier dates)]/(1 + c). Forward rates implied by a single curve follow from the ratio D(t₁)/D(t₂). Their units depend on the accrual and compounding convention.

Price the two swap legs

For notional N, accrual δⱼ and fixed rate K, fixed-leg present value is NKΣδⱼDⱼ. A projected floating payment at date j has present value NδⱼFⱼDⱼ if Fⱼ is the contract-consistent forward for that period or the known fixing where applicable.

Equating legs gives par K = ΣδⱼFⱼDⱼ / ΣδⱼDⱼ. If forwards also come from those same discount factors and the contract starts now on a reset date, δⱼFⱼDⱼ = Dⱼ₋₁ − Dⱼ, so the floating sum telescopes to 1 − Dₙ.

Separate forecast and discount curves

Collateral terms may imply one discount curve while floating payments reference a different term rate. Then the single-curve telescoping identity generally fails. Retain the explicit sum of projected or fixed payments times their payment-date discounts.

A market par swap rate makes a new matching swap’s value zero. An existing off-market fixed rate can create positive or negative value even with no exchanged principal. Write the payer or receiver sign before interpreting the result.

Method

Reconcile a swap valuation

  1. Build each date row

    Show accrual fraction, discount factor, fixed coupon and floating projection or known fixing.

  2. Compute the annuity

    Add δⱼDⱼ and multiply by notional to measure the present value of a unit fixed rate.

  3. Check par and sign

    Solve the equal-leg rate and compare with the contractual rate. Reconstruct the result using both leg values.

Worked case

A swap with separate curves

A two-year €1m swap pays fixed 4.3% and receives annual floating. Accruals are one. Discount factors are 0.97 and 0.93. Forecast annual forward rates supplied by a different projection curve are 4% and 5%.

Working

  1. Fixed-leg annuity per euro is 0.97 + 0.93 = 1.90. Its present cash value is €1m × 0.043 × 1.90 = €81,700.
  2. Floating present value is €1m × (0.04 × 0.97 + 0.05 × 0.93) = €85,300. The receive-floating, pay-fixed position is worth €3,600.
  3. Par rate is 0.0853/1.90 ≈ 4.4895%. Paying 4.3% is favorable because the contractual coupon is below the new par rate.

Interpretation

Substituting 1 − 0.93 for the floating present value would omit the difference between the projection and discount curves.

A common mistake

A yield quoted for a coupon-paying bond is its discount factor for every cash payment.

How to correct it

Start with the actual instrument price and cash-flow equation. A quoted yield to maturity is a single internal discount rate. A curve contains date-specific present-value factors.

Quick check
In that bond equation, why does the second discount factor multiply 1.05?

Each discount factor applies to the total cash due on its date. The first coupon is paid a year earlier and receives its own discount factor.

Independent exam problem

A one-year discount factor equals 0.98. A two-year bond pays annual coupons of 0.03 and principal 1, and trades at 1.01.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Bootstrap D₂ and verify the price.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Using a single curve and annual accruals, compute the two-year par swap rate and explain the floating-leg shortcut.

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

D₂ = (1.01 − 0.03 × 0.98)/1.03 = 0.9806/1.03 ≈ 0.95204. Substituting gives 0.0294 + 1.03D₂ = 1.01.

Part B 4 marks

K = (1 − D₂)/(D₁ + D₂) ≈ 2.48%. Forward payments discounted on the same curve telescope across reset dates. With a different projection curve use the separate forward sum.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A swap trader calls the par coupon an average forward rate. Which average, and can its longest rate dominate?

Compare with a spoken answer

It is an annuity-weighted average under these matched schedules. The longest forward here has the smallest weight but the largest rate, so it contributes about 1.87 points to a 3.91% coupon. For a hedge I would bump the relevant curve and rebuild all affected discount and forward rates, because these simple weights hold those other inputs fixed.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if only the third forward rises by ten basis points and all discount factors stay fixed?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The coupon moves by its annuity weight times the forward change, or 0.31111 × 10 = 3.1111 basis points. A real calibration-instrument bump may affect several forwards and discount factors and therefore produce a different sensitivity.
