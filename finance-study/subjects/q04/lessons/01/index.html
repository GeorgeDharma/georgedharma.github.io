Contract and conventions

Write down payoff, exercise rights, calendar, currency and collateral rules. Two accurate engines can disagree because they priced different contracts.

The starting point

Before pricing a derivative, translate its legal promises into a timeline. Write who pays what, in which currency, on which dates and under which conditions. Distinguish the date used to observe a price, the date a holder may exercise a right and the date cash or securities actually change hands.

Conventions can change value even when the quoted rate agrees. A day-count rule converts elapsed dates into a fraction of a year. A holiday adjustment can change the payment date and sometimes the accrual period. Collateral terms also matter for choosing a discounting framework. Resolve the contract inputs before comparing numerical methods.

Theory and method

Clean price, dirty price and accrued interest

Full price · Day-count convention

A bond dealer quotes 99.40 but the settlement system debits more than par. Can both amounts be correct?

A coupon-bearing bond earns interest between coupon dates. A buyer settling halfway through a coupon period generally acquires the right to the next full coupon. Under the ordinary cum-coupon convention, settlement compensates the seller for the fraction already accrued. Market screens can quote a clean price that excludes this amount, while the actual invoice uses the dirty price. The choice of accrual start, end, settlement and ex-coupon treatment determines which fraction is owed.

How the result is built

Pdirty = Pclean + C × a

Both prices are currency units per 100 face. C is the full coupon per 100 for the current coupon period, and a is the accrued fraction under the contractual day-count convention.

  1. A 5% annual coupon paid semiannually delivers 2.50 per 100 face each period. Identify the previous and next coupon dates using the terms.
  2. With a contractual fraction of 73/182, accrued interest is 2.50 × 73/182 = 1.00275 per 100.
  3. Add 1.00275 to the clean quote of 99.40 to obtain 100.40275. A €2m face purchase invoices about €2,008,054.95.

When the reasoning holds

  • The ordinary cum-coupon assumed here gives the buyer the full upcoming coupon. Ex-coupon periods can change the entitlement and accrued treatment.
  • Price, coupon and face must use consistent per-100 units. Settlement date rather than trade date can determine the accrual fraction.
Terms in this lesson
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Derivative
A contract whose value depends on something else, such as a currency, an interest rate or a share price.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Discount factor
The price today of one unit of currency payable at a particular future date under specified risk assumptions.
Swap
A contract to exchange payment streams, such as fixed interest for floating interest.
Coupon
The interest payment specified in a bond’s terms.
How to read the notation
A change · Δ
ΔS commonly means the new asset price less the old price. A move from €50 to €53 gives ΔS = €3. In options, delta can also name a sensitivity. A delta of 0.6 means a €1 rise in the underlying predicts about a €0.60 rise in the option, for a small move.

All notation examples

First example

A €1 million interest payment accrues at 4% for 45 actual days. Under actual/360 it is €1m × 0.04 × 45/360 = €5,000. Under actual/365 fixed it is about €4,931.51. The €68.49 difference arises solely from the agreed denominator.

Translate the confirmation into cash flows

A pricing engine needs quantities, dates, observations, exercise rights and settlement rules. A date labeled maturity may be the last observation, final exercise date or payment date. Create separate fields so that an amount fixed on Monday but paid Friday receives the appropriate discount factor.

A notional is often a scale for interest rather than an amount actually exchanged. A swap may exchange only coupon differences, while a bond also returns principal at redemption. Confusing those conventions can produce a larger pricing difference than any numerical approximation.

Apply accrual and business-day rules

For simple floating interest on notional N, coupon = N × L × δ, with fixing L and day-count fraction δ. Actual/360 uses actual elapsed days divided by 360. A contract may adjust payment dates for weekends while retaining unadjusted accrual endpoints, or it may adjust both.

Observation and settlement conventions must be read literally. A rate fixed in advance is known at the beginning of its accrual period. A compounded overnight coupon is assembled from successive fixings. Historical known fixings and model forecasts enter different parts of a valuation.

Separate known and uncertain values

At valuation time reconstruct already fixed amounts exactly, then use the applicable model for remaining uncertain quantities. When payments are collateralized and in one currency, a collateral-consistent discount curve is often relevant, under the contract and funding assumptions. Reporting currency conversion can be another layer.

Two implementations should first reconcile payment dates, accrual fractions, fixing source and rounding. Comparing Monte Carlo paths when projected cash flows differ does little to identify the cause. A small hand-built schedule often locates the discrepancy.

Method

Trace one coupon

  1. Resolve dates

    Write accrual start, end, actual fixing date and payment date, noting which dates move under the stated holiday convention.

  2. Compute amount

    Apply the notional, fixing, spread and day-count formula. Add principal only if required by the contract.

  3. Apply valuation convention

    Multiply the owed amount by its payment-date discount factor and record the currency and known-versus-forecast status.

Worked case

A payment moved to Monday

A €5m coupon accrues at 3.6% simple actual/360 over 90 days. Its contractual period ends Saturday. Payment moves to Monday, two calendar days later, but the stated accrual end remains Saturday. The Monday discount factor is 0.992.

Working

  1. The accrual fraction is 90/360 = 0.25, regardless of the two-day payment delay. The coupon is 5,000,000 × 0.036 × 0.25 = €45,000.
  2. The current value of the known coupon is €45,000 × 0.992 = €44,640. There is no separate principal payment in this example.
  3. An engine accruing through Monday would use 92 days and compute €46,000 before discounting. Its €992 present-value excess comes from dates, not interest-rate volatility.

Interpretation

A different contract might adjust accrual end as well. The worked answer follows the specific date roles supplied here.

A common mistake

A derivative with a notional of €5m must pay €5m at maturity.

How to correct it

Read whether the notional is exchanged, returned or used only as a multiplier. Reconstruct every actual cash flow before applying any discount factor.

Quick check
What cash interest accrues over 30 days on €2m at 3% under actual/360 simple interest?

Multiply the €60,000 full-year amount by 30/360. Using a 365-day denominator would price a different contractual convention.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A €3m coupon fixes at 4.2% plus 0.3 percentage point and accrues for 80 days on actual/360. Payment occurs 85 days after period start and its discount factor is 0.99. No principal is exchanged.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find coupon and present value.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    An engine uses 85/360. Quantify the undiscounted discrepancy and identify its cause.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 4 marks

The contractual rate is 4.5% and the accrual fraction 80/360. Cash is 3m × 0.045 × 80/360 = €30,000. Present value is €29,700.

Part B 3 marks

It adds five days at 3m × 0.045/360 = €375 per day, or €1,875. It confused elapsed time to settlement with contractual accrual length.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A bond dealer quotes 99.40 but the settlement system debits more than par. Can both amounts be correct?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would reconcile the quote to the invoice on the settlement date. With semiannual coupon 2.50 and 73 of 182 days accrued, the seller receives 1.00275 in addition to the 99.40 clean price. That puts the invoice above par. I would confirm whether the trade is cum-coupon and whether the invoice includes any separate fees.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if the next coupon belongs to the seller after the ex-coupon date?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Then the buyer no longer receives the full coupon that supported the ordinary accrued-interest bridge. Apply that market’s ex-coupon pricing and negative-accrual or settlement convention if relevant, remove the missing entitlement from the buyer’s cash-flow schedule and compare like-for-like prices.
