Value a Bermudan exercise decision with least-squares Monte Carlo

Fit a simple conditional continuation estimate and use it at an early-exercise date.

Theory and terminology

Least-squares Monte Carlo for early exercise

Longstaff–Schwartz · Bermudan options · continuation value

What you should be able to explain

  • Fit a simple conditional continuation estimate and use it at an early-exercise date.
  • Distinguish a feasible stopping policy's value from an estimate using future information.

Replace a future payoff with its conditional mean

A Bermudan option may be exercised only on named dates. On the last date its payoff is known. One date earlier, exercising produces an immediate value, while waiting produces a random cash flow. A rational holder compares immediate value with the conditional expectation of discounted later cash flows, given information observable then.

A recombining tree aggregates many paths at a state. Monte Carlo paths usually pass through different continuous states. Longstaff–Schwartz fits the relation between those states and discounted realized future payoffs on simulated paths. It moves backward through exercise dates, updating future cash flows to reflect the later policy already learned.

Choose the state and the regression target

Use explanatory variables visible at the exercise decision. A put on several assets may need each relevant price, rates or past averages for a path-dependent contract. The target for a path is the realized subsequent payment under the later policy, discounted back to the current comparison date. Many implementations fit at in-the-money states because only they can justify an immediate positive exercise value on a conventional put.

Fit a small, justified basis first and check its behavior where exercise decisions change. More regressors can reduce approximation error but increase estimation noise. Two states with the same intrinsic value can have different continuation values if rates, volatility or another underlying differs. A compressed state variable needs an economic justification.

Value the strategy on paths it has never seen

Fit on one sample, freeze every coefficient, then apply the exercise rule in chronological order to independent paths. The exercise decision uses the fitted conditional mean. The measured cash flow uses what actually occurs under the chosen action on the valuation path. Substituting the fitted conditional mean into every payoff would hide part of the valuation risk.

Any strategy that only uses information available when it acts is admissible. Its true expected value cannot exceed the optimal stopping value. A finite simulation estimate of that strategy may still exceed the true optimum through sampling noise. Permitting each path to see tomorrow before exercising creates a different, better-informed problem.

Method

Build the result

Cₜ(x)=Eᴽ[Dₜ,ᵤ CFᵤ | Xₜ=x]; exercise if Hₜ(x)≥Ĉₜ(x)

Xₜ is the observable state at exercise date t, Hₜ is immediate cash, CFᵤ is cash at a later stopping date u, and Dₜ,ᵤ discounts u to t. C and its fitted estimate Ĉ are in date-t currency. Q denotes the pricing measure.

  1. At expiry set each path's future cash flow to its terminal payoff. Move back one permitted date and discount those payments to that date.
  2. Regress the discounted future payments on a basis of the current state. Evaluate the fitted conditional mean only from that date's inputs.
  3. When immediate cash wins, replace the later cash flow with immediate exercise. After training, value this frozen strategy on independent pricing paths.
Worked case

A two-date exercise rule without hindsight

A claim can be exercised at date 1 or paid at date 2. There is no date-0 exercise. All date-2 amounts below have already been discounted to date 1. The four training intrinsic values x are 2, 4, 6, 8 and their corresponding future payments are 6, 5, 5, 4. Fit an intercept and x. Date-0-to-date-1 discount is 0.98.

Working

  1. Training means are x̄=5 and ȳ=5. The sum of cross-deviations is −6 and squared x deviations sum to 20, so slope is −0.30. Intercept is 5−(−0.30×5)=6.50.
  2. Exercise when x≥6.50−0.30x, or x≥5. The four independent valuation paths have the same current x values, and their future payments are 0, 8, 2, 11.
  3. The rule calls for continuation at 2 and 4 and exercise at 6 and 8. Realized date-1-equivalent cash flows are 0, 8, 6, 8 with a mean of 5.50.
  4. Discount the sample mean to give 0.98×5.50=5.39. Looking ahead on each valuation path would instead select 2, 8, 6, 11, giving 6.615 in present value.

Decision and interpretation

Use 5.39 as this tiny sample's policy valuation. The hindsight figure is infeasible. A desk would train and test on much larger samples, record sampling error and check boundary stability.

Quick check
An independent path's fitted continuation is 7, immediate exercise pays 5 and its later discounted cash turns out to be 2. Which value enters the policy estimate at the exercise date?

The fitted 7 determines the action using current information. The realized cash for that action is 2 on this valuation path.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A separate desk trained Ĉ(x)=5.2−0.1x. Its independent paths have current intrinsic values 3, 6 and 7 and later date-1-equivalent payments 8, 2 and 12. D₀,₁=0.97. Price the rule and compare perfect hindsight.

Reveal the worked solution
  1. Solving x≥5.2−0.1x gives exercise for x≥4.7273. The decisions are continue, exercise, exercise.
  2. Cash under the policy is 8, 6 and 7. Mean is 7, and discounted value is 0.97×7=6.79.
  3. Perfect hindsight takes 8, 6 and 12, giving 0.97×26/3=8.407. Its extra value arises from seeing a future payment before the decision.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How can an options analyst decide whether to exercise today when each Monte Carlo path shows only one possible future?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I estimate the expected future cash flow given today's state across many training paths. I exercise if the immediate value is at least that conditional estimate. On new paths I use only today's state to decide, then collect the cash flow produced by that frozen policy.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What changes when a lagged average affects the payoff?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The current spot alone may fail to identify continuation. Include the running average or another sufficient path statistic, retain its correct history on every path and test the revised stopping boundary independently.
