Independent validation

Benchmark a closed-form special case, test extreme inputs and document reproducibility. State where the evidence supports use and where a restriction is warranted.

The starting point

A validator asks whether a model is fit for the decisions it will support. Separate the contract definition, mathematical assumptions, implementation and data. A second implementation can detect coding mistakes, especially if its logic and test data were built independently. Matching two implementations with the same mistaken convention offers weaker evidence.

Use cases with known answers, convergence checks, extreme but coherent inputs and tests against traded instruments. Record tolerances, software versions, market-data timestamps and seeds. State which products, maturities and market conditions the evidence covers. A use restriction can be justified even if the model succeeds on ordinary examples.

Theory and method

Richardson extrapolation and convergence order

Grid convergence study · Numerical model verification

Two grids give close prices. How can you test whether the agreement supports the claimed order of convergence?

A validator can run the same contract on three grids with a fixed refinement ratio. If the leading error behaves like a constant times a power of the step size, the changes between successive prices should shrink at a predictable rate. Eliminating that leading term produces a sharper limiting-price estimate. The argument needs measurements inside the asymptotic range, where payoff kinks, boundaries and other errors do not dominate.

How the result is built

Vext = V(h/2) + [V(h/2) − V(h)]/(2ᵖ − 1)

V(h) is a price on spacing h, p the leading convergence order, Vext the extrapolated limiting price in euros, and h/2 the refined spacing.

  1. Assume V(h)=V*+chᵖ plus higher-order terms. Then V(h/2)=V*+chᵖ/2ᵖ to first order.
  2. Subtract the two approximations and solve for the leading error on the fine grid, yielding the extrapolation formula.
  3. Prices 5.160, 5.040 and 5.010 on successive halvings change by −0.120 and −0.030. Their ratio is 4, implying p≈2. The latest extrapolation gives 5.010 − 0.030/3=5.000.

When the reasoning holds

  • Keep the model, contractual boundary and other numerical settings consistent. An apparent convergence order can reflect overlapping sources of error.
  • Reliable extrapolation needs a stable leading error coefficient and sufficiently fine grids. Oscillating errors or a kink moving across grid nodes can defeat the simple ratio.
Terms in this lesson
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.
Option
A contract giving the buyer a right to buy or sell on stated terms. The buyer pays a premium for the right.
Discount factor
The price today of one unit of currency payable at a particular future date under specified risk assumptions.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
How to read the notation
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.

First example

For a one-year non-dividend-paying European call, assume zero volatility, spot €102, strike €100 and deterministic cash growth of 5%. In the model its value is max(102 − 100/1.05, 0) ≈ €6.76. A simulator giving €2 may have subtracted the undiscounted strike from spot.

Validate use, model and code separately

A validator starts from the decision the model informs. Pricing a liquid plain call, managing an early-exercise book and reporting an illiquid long-dated exposure may require different tests. State allowed instruments, input ranges, market regimes and output tolerances before selecting benchmark comparisons.

Implementation verification asks whether the code solves its specified equations correctly. Conceptual validation asks whether those equations and assumptions suit the economic use. Input checks ask whether the supplied curves, prices and conventions are timely and appropriate. A failure in any layer can matter to the same reported number.

Choose targeted invariants

A benchmark with a known answer isolates one mathematical property. European call-put parity for matching contracts on a non-dividend-paying stock is C − P = S − KD at a known deterministic maturity discount D. The relation follows from equal terminal payoffs of long call and short put versus stock minus discounted strike.

Other tests isolate boundary conditions, positive homogeneity, unit scaling, monotonicity or zero-volatility limits under appropriate assumptions. An American option’s value should be at least its immediate exercise payoff. Passing any one relation covers only the inputs and features exercised.

Report numerical and economic significance

Keep a dated test record with inputs, expected answer, error measure and acceptance threshold. A one-cent per-contract discrepancy can be material on millions of contracts. For stochastic tests compare differences relative to simulation uncertainty as well as monetary materiality.

When tests fail, distinguish reproducible deviations from sampling noise, isolate the affected component and state an interim use restriction. A second implementation that shares the same source of incorrect dates may reproduce the same wrong answer. Independence of reasoning matters.

Method

Plan an independent test

  1. Derive expected behavior

    Use a closed-form special case or dominance condition and document the assumptions that make it applicable.

  2. Vary one input at a time

    Change time grid, path count or a contract feature in controlled comparisons, including stressed values.

  3. Quantify the claim

    Translate deviations to portfolio size and identify the model uses for which the evidence supports or limits reliance.

Worked case

A parity breach before a large trade

A no-dividend stock trades at 100, strike is 100 and the maturity discount factor is 0.96. A pricing system returns European call 8.15 and European put 3.90, both with identical maturity and settlement. Assume deterministic rates and consistent inputs.

Working

  1. The theoretical call-put difference is 100 − 100 × 0.96 = 4.00. The reported difference is 8.15 − 3.90 = 4.25.
  2. The residual is +0.25 per matched pair. For 10,000 one-share pairs that corresponds to €2,500 before fees. It is a validation discrepancy in the specified setup.
  3. Recheck dividends, contract styles, quote currencies, maturity discount and timestamps. If those align, compare independent European benchmarks and convergence for both legs.

Interpretation

A discrepancy between theoretical outputs is evidence about the model. Calling it a tradable market arbitrage would additionally require executable prices, funding and trading assumptions.

At the board

Measure simulation error against a price you can check

Price a one-year European call. Share and strike are both 100, the continuously compounded risk-free rate is 3%, volatility is 20%, and there are no dividends. For each independent standard normal draw z, the terminal share is 100 × exp(0.03 − 0.20²/2 + 0.20z). Discount the positive part of terminal share minus strike. from math import exp, sqrt
from random import Random
from statistics import fmean, stdev

rng = Random(1729)
payoffs = []
for _ in range(16384):
    final = 100 * exp(0.03 - 0.20**2/2 + 0.20 * rng.gauss(0, 1))
    payoffs.append(exp(-0.03) * max(final - 100, 0))
print(round(fmean(payoffs), 3), round(stdev(payoffs)/sqrt(len(payoffs)), 3))

The Black–Scholes model price for these inputs is 9.413. Under independent draws, the price standard error is the sample standard deviation of discounted payoffs divided by the square root of the path count. Approximately four times as many paths halve the standard error. A confidence band quantifies sampling error inside this model. It says nothing about a weak volatility assumption or a miswritten payoff.

A common mistake

A model matching competitors’ mid-prices can be approved for all its supported volatility and maturity inputs.

How to correct it

Define the tested region and stress behavior. Multiple implementations can share benchmark inputs or modeling assumptions, and a good cross-sectional fit may conceal poor numerical convergence or hedge dynamics.

Quick check
A pricing engine passes a plain European call benchmark. Which claim can the validator support from that test alone?

A European call benchmark exercises a specific calculation. Early-exercise logic and data timing need targeted evidence of their own.

Independent exam problem

In a one-year zero-volatility model with S₀ = 90, K = 100 and deterministic cash growth 10%, a non-dividend European call is reported at 0.40. A European put is reported at 1.30.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Find the model benchmark values for both claims.

  2. Part B · 4 marks

    Assess parity and explain why one parity check alone would be insufficient.

Part A 4 marks

The final stock is 99. Call payoff is zero and put payoff is 1, giving prices 0 and 1/1.10 ≈ 0.9091. These deterministic limits should be approached consistently as volatility tends to zero.

Reported C − P = −0.90 versus 90 − 100/1.10 ≈ −0.9091, a small discrepancy despite both individual prices being wrong by roughly 0.4. Add absolute known-price tests alongside parity.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Two grids give close prices. How can you test whether the agreement supports the claimed order of convergence?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would run at least a third grid and compare successive differences. A fourfold contraction under halving supports second-order behavior in this example, yielding an extrapolated €5.000. I would still compare with an independent benchmark and shift boundaries. A solver can converge beautifully to the wrong boundary-value problem or contractual payoff.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A fourth refinement gives 5.020. Should the validator retain the second-order conclusion?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The sequence has reversed direction rather than continuing toward approximately 5.0025. Recheck boundary treatment, interpolation near the evaluation point, grid alignment with payoff features, convergence tolerances and any stochastic noise. Suspend the order claim until the discrepancy is explained.
