Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A contract schedule and an off-market swap

A known coupon accrues on €2m at 3.6% actual/360 for 60 days and is paid three days after accrual ends. Its payment-date discount factor is 0.99. Separately, a €2m annual two-payment swap receives floating and pays fixed 5%. The discount factors are 0.96 and 0.91, and projected annual floating rates are 4.5% and 5.5%. Accruals on the swap are one.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A lattice and an independent grid calculation

A stock costs 90 and can finish at 108 or 72 after one period with gross cash growth of 1.04. A put has strike 100 and may be exercised now or at expiry. Separately a validator tests a simple explicit diffusion grid Vτ = aVₛₛ with a = 0.5, k = 0.1, h = 0.5 and left, center, right values 4, 5, 8.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

A control variate and a disputed hedge

A Monte Carlo run with 1,600 independent paths has payoff mean 5.20 and standard deviation 8. A control has known mean 10, simulated mean 10.5, variance 16 and covariance 16 with the payoff. Treat these moments and the implied fixed coefficient as supplied. Separately the base option is 5.00 with values 4.50 and 5.60 at underlying bumps −1 and +1.

Calibration and the meaning of validation

Under a proposed historical short-rate model the current rate is 7%, long-run level 3% and κ = ln 2 per year. A separate option quote is 4.26 versus a model price 4.00 at 20% implied volatility, with local vega 0.13 per percentage point. A desk claims success at fitting today’s prices validates historical forecasts and future hedges.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A contract schedule and an off-market swap

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the coupon amount and present value and specify the date convention.

Cash is €2m × 0.036 × 60/360 = €12,000 (1), worth €11,880 (1). The payment delay affects discount timing, not the stated 60-day accrual (1).

Part B 4 marks

Calculate the swap’s fixed and floating leg values and the position value.

Fixed = €2m × 0.05 × 1.87 = €187,000 (1). Floating = €2m × (0.96 × 0.045 + 0.91 × 0.055) = €186,500 (2). Position value = −€500 (1).

Part C 3 marks

Derive the par rate and explain why blindly using 1 − 0.91 for the floating value could fail.

Par rate = 0.09325/1.87 ≈ 4.9866% (1). The shortcut requires forwards derived from the same discount factors (1) with compatible resets/accruals. The supplied projections may use another curve (1).

Case 2 · 10 marks

A lattice and an independent grid calculation

Part A 3 marks

Compute the lattice pricing probability and the European put value.

Solve 108q + 72(1 − q) = 90 × 1.04 = 93.6 to obtain q = 0.60 (2). Terminal put pays 0 or 28, so European value is 0.4 × 28/1.04 ≈ 10.7692 (1).

Part B 3 marks

Determine the American value and give two independent validation inequalities for the result.

Exercise gives 10, so continuing yields approximately 10.7692 and is optimal (2). American value must be at least immediate exercise and at least the European value for an otherwise identical option (1).

Part C 4 marks

Update the grid center, then examine stability after halving h without changing k. Give a repair.

Initial λ = 0.5 × 0.1/0.25 = 0.2 (1). Updated center = 0.2 × 4 + 0.6 × 5 + 0.2 × 8 = 5.4 (1). New λ is 0.8, exceeding the simple explicit limit of 0.5 (1). Reduce k, for instance to 0.025 to preserve λ = 0.2 (1).

Case 3 · 10 marks

A control variate and a disputed hedge

Part A 3 marks

Calculate raw standard error, optimal control coefficient and adjusted price.

Raw standard error is 8/√1600 = 0.20 (1). Optimal coefficient = 16/16 = 1 (1). Adjusted estimate = 5.20 − 1 × 0.5 = 4.70 (1).

Part B 3 marks

Calculate adjusted variance and standard error and state one reason the adjustment could produce a biased price in production.

Adjusted variance is 64 + 16 − 32 = 48 (1). Standard error = √48/40 ≈ 0.1732 (1). An incorrectly centered control or incompatible discounting introduces bias (1).

Part C 4 marks

Compute centered delta and gamma and hedge 100 sold one-share options. Estimate the curvature loss on a two-unit move.

Delta = (5.60 − 4.50)/2 = 0.55 (1). Gamma = 5.60 − 10 + 4.50 = 0.10 (1). Buy 55 shares (1). Combined gamma −10 implies local curvature loss ½ × 10 × 2² = 20, ignoring other effects (1).

Case 4 · 10 marks

Calibration and the meaning of validation

Part A 3 marks

Find the model’s one- and two-year conditional mean rates and distinguish them from discount factors.

Initial deviation is four points and halves annually (1), giving 5% and 4% (1). A bond price uses a pricing-measure expectation of discounted paths, distinct from historical conditional mean rates (1).

Part B 4 marks

Compute the first implied-volatility correction, explain its local character and give a practical problem when vega is very small.

Correction = 0.26/0.13 = 2 percentage points and updated volatility is 22% (2). Reprice because vega changes (1). Small vega can turn bid–ask noise into a wide implied-volatility interval (1).

Part C 3 marks

Propose three targeted validation tests beyond matching calibration quotes.

Award one mark each for tests of independent behavior, such as withheld strikes or later quotes, realized hedge errors under later market moves, and benchmark prices or numerical convergence with prespecified tolerances (3).
