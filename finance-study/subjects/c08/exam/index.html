Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

A specialist lender and delegated oversight

Forty households can each spend €80 duplicating borrower screening. A bank’s shared screening costs €1,400 fixed and €12 per household with a further €350 independent oversight cost. After origination, monitoring costs €15,000 and reduces default from 14% to 8% on a €400,000 loss conditional on default. The bank retains 40% of that exposure after sale.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

Loan origination, payment and a fire sale

Bank A initially has €18m reserves and €82m loans. Its liabilities are €90m and equity €10m. It grants a €12m new loan by deposit credit. The borrower transfers €10m to Bank B. A B customer independently sends €3m to an A customer. Later A must pay another €15m of existing deposits. It can sell remaining loans at 80% of carrying value.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Liquidity insurance, a run and the public backstop

One hundred depositors each have €1 claims. A bank holds €25 liquid and invests €75 in a project paying €1.40 per retained euro late. Early liquidation pays €0.60 per euro. Normal early withdrawals are €25. A committed short-term facility may lend €15 now, repayable at €16.50 late. Ignore other claims.

Case 4 of 4 · 10 marks

Group funding, margins and trapped cash

A group subsidiary has €70m immediately repricing loans, €100m immediately repricing deposits and other fixed funding. A 2-point reference rise passes through fully to the loans and 50% to deposits. It owes US$14m tomorrow, has US$4m cash, a US$3m receivable due tomorrow and a confirmed parent transfer capped at US$4m. Tomorrow’s total USD obligations are fixed. Ignore other balances for these stresses.

Case 1 · 10 marks

A specialist lender and delegated oversight

Part A 3 marks

Compute direct and delegated screening costs and the saving.

Direct costs €3,200. Delegated cost is €1,400 + €480 + €350 = €2,230. Saving €970. Award one mark per amount.

Part B 4 marks

Find the expected gross monitoring gain for the entire loan and for the bank. Assess incentives.

Whole gain is (0.14−0.08)×€400,000=€24,000, net €9,000 after cost. Bank retains €9,600 gross and loses €5,400 net from the monitoring decision. Award marks for total gross, total net, bank gross and private conclusion.

Part C 3 marks

Find the minimum retained share for monitoring to break even and explain why many formally separate borrowers might not diversify the bank.

Share must be at least €15,000/€24,000 = 62.5%. Separate borrowers may rely on the same buyer or sector, leaving correlated losses and a fragile aggregate repayment promise. Award marks for ratio, percentage and mechanism.

Case 2 · 10 marks

Loan origination, payment and a fire sale

Part A 4 marks

Give A’s loans, reserves, liability and equity positions after origination and the two transfers.

Loans rise to €94m. Net reserve outflow is €7m leaving €11m. Liabilities rise €12m then fall net €7m, leaving €95m. Equity remains €10m. Award one mark per result.

Part B 3 marks

Find assets A must sell to meet the additional payment and the loss on sale.

It needs €15m−€11m=€4m. It sells €4m/0.80=€5m book loans, realizing €1m loss. Award one mark per result.

Part C 3 marks

Reconcile closing A and distinguish system deposit creation from the initial interbank transfer.

Closing reserves zero, loans €89m, liabilities €80m and equity €9m. The original loan created €12m system deposits. The first interbank transfers redistributed reserves and deposits. Award marks for assets, financing and distinction. The destination of the later payment is unnecessary for A’s own balance sheet.

Case 3 · 10 marks

Liquidity insurance, a run and the public backstop

Part A 3 marks

First assume €40 must withdraw. Find late payment per remaining depositor with forced sales and no facility.

Gap €15 requires sale of €25 project principal. Remaining €50 pays €70 late. Sixty late customers receive €70/60≈€1.167 each. Award a mark per stage.

Part B 4 marks

With the facility instead, compute late payment and compare a full immediate run with a patient outcome absent that facility.

Keeping the project pays €105. Less €16.50 facility leaves €88.50 for 60, or €1.475 each. A full immediate run without it accesses €25+€75×0.60=€70, below €100. Under normal waiting, €105 for 75 late customers yields €1.40 each. Award a mark per main calculation and comparison.

Part C 3 marks

Explain how credible protection can change the queue and one trade-off of providing it.

If coverage is trusted and usable in time, covered patient depositors have less reason to withdraw solely because others may run. Reduced depositor monitoring can alter risk-taking. Supervision, capital or risk-sensitive fees can counter that incentive. Award marks for mechanism, cost and mitigation.

Case 4 · 10 marks

Group funding, margins and trapped cash

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the annualized change in NII on the euro positions, holding balances fixed.

Loan income rises €70m×0.02=€1.4m. Deposit cost rises €100m×0.01=€1m. Net increase €0.4m. Award a mark per calculation.

Part B 4 marks

Compute tomorrow’s dollar gap and the loan face amount sold to bridge it at 75% of face. What if the parent transfer fails?

Available US$11m leaves a US$3m gap. Sell US$4m face to raise it. Without the parent line the gap is US$7m, requiring about US$9.333m face if sufficient marketable loans exist. Award marks for initial gap, initial sale, stressed gap and stressed sale.

Part C 3 marks

Why can positive euro earnings and positive group capital coexist with dollar payment failure? State one action that addresses the exact gap.

Earnings and capital are different from cash due by a deadline. The currency and legal entity holding funds matter, as do transfer limits. Confirm a timely dollar line or executable transfer/sale available to the subsidiary before tomorrow. Award one mark per distinction/action.
