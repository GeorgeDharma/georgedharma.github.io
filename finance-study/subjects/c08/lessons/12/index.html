How a non-life insurer earns premiums, reserves for claims and uses reinsurance

Reconcile written premiums and paid claims with their earned and incurred amounts.

Theory and terminology

Non-life underwriting and reinsurance

earned premiums · claims reserves · combined ratio · ceded risk

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile written premiums and paid claims with their earned and incurred amounts.
  • Calculate how quota-share reinsurance and commissions alter underwriting profit and retained loss.

Recognize revenue while coverage is provided

A property insurer may collect a full year’s premium before providing a full year’s cover. Written premium records business written in the period under the stated reporting basis. Unearned premium is the portion supporting future coverage. A growing unearned balance means some current written premium belongs to a later period.

For this management exercise, earned premium equals written premium plus opening unearned premium minus closing unearned premium. This simplified reconciliation omits cancellations, currency movements and accounting adjustments. Investment income is separately earned on assets held while claims remain unpaid.

Recognize claims when incurred

Cash paid this year can relate to accidents in an earlier year. Add the increase in claims reserves to paid claims to estimate claims incurred during the reporting period. Reserves include estimated unpaid reported claims and estimates for losses incurred but not yet reported. Prior estimates can develop favorably or adversely.

Use a consistent expense and premium convention. Here claim-handling costs are included in incurred losses, while other underwriting expenses are separate. Both ratios use earned premium. A combined ratio below 100% indicates a positive underwriting result on that basis. Investment returns and financing expenses can change total company profit.

Identify what the reinsurer shares

A quota-share treaty transfers an agreed fraction of covered premiums and covered losses to a reinsurer. A ceding commission paid by the reinsurer offsets some of the primary insurer’s acquisition or operating expense. The insurer generally remains obligated to policyholders and gains a separate claim against the reinsurer under the treaty.

An excess-of-loss treaty instead responds above an agreed loss retention up to a limit, subject to contract terms. Equal premium costs on two reinsurance contracts do not guarantee equal protection against concentrated storms. Assess exclusions, reinsurer credit quality and collection timing as well as the net income statement.

Method

Build the result

EP = W + U₀ − U₁. IL = P + B₁ − B₀. CR = (IL + X)/EP. Net profit = (1 − q)(EP − IL) − X + gqEP.

W is written premium, U₀ and U₁ opening and closing unearned premiums, EP earned premium. P is claims paid, B₀ and B₁ opening and closing unpaid-claim estimates, IL incurred loss including claim handling, and X other underwriting expense. CR is the combined ratio, q the quota ceded and g the ceding commission as a fraction of ceded earned premium. All amounts cover the same reporting period.

  1. Reconcile earned premium and incurred losses on the same reporting perimeter. Use the movement in reserves only once.
  2. Compare incurred losses and underwriting expenses with earned premiums. Keep investment income outside this measure.
  3. Apply the treaty quota to earned premiums and incurred losses. Keep original expenses, then deduct the ceding commission from net expense under the case convention.
Worked case

A regional property insurer closes its year

Amounts are €m. Written premium is 60, opening and closing unearned balances are 22 and 24. Claims paid are 32 and opening and closing claim reserves are 15 and 24. Other underwriting expenses are 14. The whole portfolio is covered by a 40% quota-share treaty. The ceding commission is 25% of ceded earned premium. Assume full, timely reinsurance performance.

Working

  1. Earned premium is 60 + 22 − 24 = 58. Incurred claims are 32 + 24 − 15 = 41.
  2. Gross underwriting profit is 58 − 41 − 14 = 3. The gross combined ratio is 55/58 = 94.83%. Taking only premiums written less claims paid would omit both reserve movements and expenses.
  3. Cede 23.2 of earned premium and recover 16.4 of claims. Commission is 0.25 × 23.2 = 5.8. Retained earned premium is 34.8, retained claims 24.6 and net expense 8.2.
  4. Net underwriting profit is 34.8 − 24.6 − 8.2 = 2.0, and net combined ratio is 32.8/34.8 = 94.25%. The reinsurer’s simple margin is 23.2 − 16.4 − 5.8 = 1.0 before its own costs.

Decision and interpretation

If closing gross claims estimates rise by another 10, retained losses rise by 6. The net result falls from +2 to −4, provided the treaty covers that development. Investigate reserve evidence and the collectibility of the reinsurance asset.

Quick check
Opening unpaid claims are 14, closing unpaid claims 19 and paid claims 22. What is incurred loss on the stated basis?

Cash paid plus the five-unit increase in unpaid claims gives incurred losses of 27.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

An insurer writes 50 and moves unearned premiums from 10 to 12. Paid claims are 24 and claims reserves move from 12 to 17. Expenses are 12. It cedes 25% of premiums and losses and receives 20% commission on ceded earned premium. Amounts are millions. Find gross and net underwriting results.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Earned premiums are 50 + 10 − 12 = 48, and incurred losses are 24 + 17 − 12 = 29.
  2. Gross profit is 48 − 29 − 12 = 7. Gross combined ratio is 41/48 = 85.42%.
  3. Retained premium is 36 and retained claims are 21.75. Commission is 0.20 × 12 = 2.4, giving net expense 9.6. Net profit is 4.65 and net combined ratio is 31.35/36 = 87.08%.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can an insurer appear profitable on its cash receipts and still lose money on the coverage it actually provided?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. Written premiums may finance future coverage, and paid claims can omit losses that have already occurred. I would adjust for the unearned premium and claims reserves before comparing expenses with earned premium. In the example, a further €10m of gross reserve development changes retained profit from €2m to a €4m loss under the quota share.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A reserve review adds four million in covered gross losses without changing expenses. What happens to estimated net underwriting profit?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The treaty covers one million of the additional loss and the insurer retains three. Profit falls from 4.65 to 1.65 million if that recovery is collectible. Cash settlement may occur in a different period.
