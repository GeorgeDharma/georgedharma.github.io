Customer due diligence, beneficial ownership and transaction monitoring

Trace interests to natural persons, aggregate independent paths and test control by other means.

Theory and terminology

Customer and beneficial-owner due diligence

KYC · AML · beneficial ownership · ongoing monitoring

What you should be able to explain

  • Trace interests to natural persons, aggregate independent paths and test control by other means.
  • Use an evidence-based customer profile to resolve or escalate unexpected account activity.

Establish the customer and its business purpose

A certificate confirming that a company exists does not reveal who ultimately owns or controls it. Customer due diligence connects the legal customer, persons authorized to act, ultimate natural persons and the activity expected through the account. Distinguish evidence supplied by the customer from independent corroboration where required.

The FATF Recommendations provide an international standard implemented through local law. Recommendation 10 addresses identification, verification, purpose and continuing diligence. Local legislation and bank policy determine exact thresholds, documentation and actions. The illustrative ownership threshold below is a classroom policy, not a universal legal test.

Look through ownership and control

Multiply ownership fractions along a chain and add genuinely independent paths that reach the same individual. Do not count a subsidiary and its parent as two separate underlying interests in the same shares. Trace nominee arrangements and distinguish voting, economic and appointment rights.

A person with a small economic interest can control the customer through voting agreements or rights to appoint its leadership. An individual’s percentage is therefore one part of the analysis. Record shareholder registers, relevant constitutional documents, the authority of signatories and any unresolved inconsistencies.

Turn knowledge of the customer into a monitoring decision

Build an account profile from the stated business model, major customers, countries, expected amounts and purpose of payments. Compare later activity with this baseline. An alert prompts inquiry and documented reasoning. An unusual payment alone does not prove an offense.

Under the applicable reporting framework, the designated officer assesses suspicion, reporting duties, timing and confidentiality, including restrictions on disclosing a report to the customer. Preserve the factual trail and follow lawful local instructions for continuing, restricting or declining activity. An outstanding credit approval or an attractive deposit cannot replace the required diligence.

Method

Build the result

Effective indirect interest = sum over distinct ownership paths of the product of the ownership fractions on each path. Diligence decision = ownership evidence + control evidence + purpose and activity evidence + documented local policy decision.

An ownership fraction is a direct economic stake expressed as a decimal. Each path starts at a natural person and ends at the customer. Control evidence concerns decision rights even without majority economics. The activity profile states expected counterparties, turnover, transaction patterns and supporting business rationale.

  1. Begin at the customer and identify its direct owners, then continue through holding entities until the underlying individuals or other relevant control arrangements are understood.
  2. Multiply stakes along each valid path and aggregate by individual. Review voting and appointment rights separately, and document the source and date for each conclusion.
  3. Compare proposed or observed transactions with the corroborated profile. Record what additional evidence is needed, who owns the decision and what actions the governing policy permits pending review.
Worked case

A wholesaler with two holding-company owners

Atlas Trading requests an operating account. Cedar Holdings owns 60% and Drift Partners owns 40%. Lena and Omar own respectively 60% and 40% of Cedar. Omar and Poppy each own 50% of Drift. Atlas’s shareholder agreement gives Poppy the independent right to appoint a majority of its board. The fictional bank requires verification of persons with at least 25% indirect interest and of anyone exercising effective control. Its policy requires this before activation.

Working

  1. Lena’s effective interest is 0.60 × 0.60 = 36%. Omar holds 0.40 × 0.60 = 24% through Cedar and 0.50 × 0.40 = 20% through Drift, totaling 44%.
  2. Poppy owns 0.50 × 0.40 = 20%. The three interests sum to 100%. Her board-appointment power independently brings her within the control review.
  3. Verify all three identities and the ownership chain, corroborate the agreement and record signatory authority. If the agreement or identity evidence cannot yet be verified, retain the application for review without activating the account under the stated policy.
  4. After satisfactory verification, Atlas expects €400,000 in monthly receipts from domestic wholesalers, with normal individual receipts below €40,000. It later receives €360,000 from a previously unknown overseas consultant in five days. Record counterparties, dates and mismatch, and obtain lawfully available business evidence for the payment.

Decision and interpretation

The alert merits review of the underlying transaction, business explanation and prior activity. Route unresolved concerns to the designated officer for a confidential decision under local law. The amount alone cannot establish an offense or determine the reporting deadline.

Quick check
A signed document confirms a person with no shares can unilaterally appoint most of a customer’s board. How should the banker handle this under the worked policy?

Control over board appointments can matter even without share ownership. The legal basis and actual ability to exercise the right need corroboration.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A company is 70% owned by HoldCo and 30% by Amina personally. HoldCo is 60% owned by Bao and 40% by Amina. Separate documents grant Cai, who holds no shares, unilateral appointment of a majority of the company’s board. Under the same illustrative policy, list the persons to verify and the evidence behind the decision.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Bao owns 0.60 × 0.70 = 42% indirectly. Amina owns 0.40 × 0.70 + 0.30 = 58%. Their ownership interests sum to 100%.
  2. Verify Bao and Amina through ownership. Verify Cai through control, and corroborate the appointment power and its continuing legal effect even though Cai has no economic percentage.
  3. Obtain reliable ownership records, identity and authority evidence, and the account purpose and expected activity. If verification required before activation remains incomplete, retain and escalate the file under the specific policy.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

What should a banker do when the company named on an account is verified but its owners, controllers or payment activity remain unclear?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would distinguish the legal customer from the people who own or control it, trace each path and verify the authority of the people signing. In Atlas, Omar owns 44%, Lena 36%, and Poppy’s 20% economic stake comes with control over board appointments. Once those issues are resolved, account activity still needs monitoring against a documented profile.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A new customer payment is much larger than forecast, but the customer presents an invoice. Is the monitoring alert resolved?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The invoice is one piece of evidence. Assess its authenticity, the payer’s relationship, consistency with the business purpose, payment route and other activity. Document whether the discrepancy is explained and send unresolved suspicion to the authorized officer under the applicable local procedure.
