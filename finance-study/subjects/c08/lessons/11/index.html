Theory and terminology

Bank resolution and creditor bail-in

loss absorption · recapitalization · critical functions

What you should be able to explain

  • Separate loss recognition from the conversion of creditors into new equity.
  • Test the liquidation safeguard and a post-resolution cash gap using distinct valuations.

Identify the function that has to continue

An authority may need to preserve deposit access, payments and other critical services if an institution becomes nonviable. Recovery measures seek to restore viability under the institution’s own plan. Resolution uses statutory powers in the applicable jurisdiction. Possible tools include a sale, a transfer to a temporary bridge institution, or creditor writedown and conversion.

An emergency loan can buy time if the institution can repay. It cannot by itself erase an asset loss. Determine the legal entity, actual creditor hierarchy, eligible liabilities, possible exclusions, enforceability and operational systems before applying any numerical plan. The hierarchy and capital target below belong to an invented jurisdiction.

Distinguish loss absorption from recapitalization

A writedown cancels part of a claim, absorbing past losses. A conversion exchanges a creditor claim for shares to rebuild positive capital. Junior claims typically bear loss first subject to governing law. Converting more creditors into equity changes their exposure and ownership without generating settlement cash.

A balance sheet that just reaches zero equity has no cushion for new losses. The recapitalization target directs how much additional eligible debt becomes new equity. Check the resulting legal claims, regulatory eligibility and prudently valued risk-weighted assets before declaring the bank viable.

Run the liquidation and liquidity comparisons

Under the international FSB framework, the safeguard compares treatment with ordinary liquidation under applicable law. It uses credible realizable value, costs and timing in that alternative. A creditor receiving shares cannot automatically count their face or book value as its economic recovery.

Separately rebuild a dated cash ladder. Depositors can withdraw and payment systems can require settlement before assets can be sold. A bridging funding source needs sufficient eligible collateral, an available limit and usable cash at the right time. Capital restoration, creditor safeguards and liquidity each answer a different question.

Method

Build the result

Gap = max(0, L − E). Eligible debt intervention = Gap + T. Creditor safeguard check = fair value received in resolution versus estimated liquidation recovery.

L is recognized asset loss, E opening common equity, Gap losses beyond old equity and T the new common equity required after losses. The second expression assumes sufficient eligible claims, unchanged remaining liabilities and no newly injected capital. Compare recoveries per equal-ranking claim in the chosen legal entity and currencies.

  1. Reduce assets by the loss. Eliminate old equity first where the stated hierarchy requires. Allocate uncovered losses by rank.
  2. Convert further surviving eligible debt sufficient to produce the new equity target. Reconcile assets to remaining liabilities plus new equity.
  3. Independently allocate liquidation proceeds to the same creditor classes. Compare market-equivalent consideration and build tomorrow’s cash ladder separately.
Worked case

A weekend resolution over a €1bn balance sheet

Amounts are €m. Assets 1,000 are funded by protected and operating claims 800, eligible senior debt 110, subordinated debt 60 and equity 30. An independent review recognizes losses of 120. Under the stated hierarchy equity, then subordinates, then eligible senior absorb loss. The authority targets 80 of new equity against 800 risk-weighted assets. The 800 protected and operating claims have priority here. Liquidation would distribute 845 after costs. Independent fair value of the new shares is 72.

Working

  1. Assets fall to 880. Old equity absorbs 30. Subordinated notes absorb 60 and senior notes absorb 30, leaving 80 of eligible senior principal.
  2. Convert that 80 into new common equity. The balance sheet is now 880 assets = 800 continuing liabilities + 80 book equity. The simple capital ratio is 80/800 = 10%.
  3. In liquidation the priority claims recover 800 and senior creditors share 45. In resolution the same senior class receives shares worth 72, a modeled improvement of 27. Subordinates receive zero in both comparisons.
  4. The reopened bank faces 65 cash payments tomorrow. Unencumbered cash of 20 plus certain timely committed funding of 30 leaves a 15 shortfall.

Decision and interpretation

The recapitalization balances and the example’s senior comparison passes. Obtain at least €15m further timely liquidity or alter the execution plan. Real safeguards require the applicable creditor-by-creditor valuation and legal process.

Quick check
A senior creditor receives shares carried at 80 in the new bank but independently valued at 72. Which amount is relevant to the simplified liquidation comparison?

The comparison asks what the creditor economically receives relative to liquidation. A book recapitalization entry does not establish that recovery value.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A bank has 500 of assets, 380 senior protected claims, 70 eligible senior notes, 40 subordinate notes and 10 common equity. All figures are currency millions. Assets lose 65. The target new equity is 35. Follow the same assumed ranking. Liquidation returns 425 total and the new shares have an independently estimated value of 28.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. After the loss, assets are 435. Old equity absorbs 10, subordinate debt absorbs 40 and senior notes absorb the remaining 15, leaving 55 senior debt.
  2. Convert 35 of surviving senior debt to equity. Ending liabilities are 380 + 20 = 400 with 35 new book equity, reconciling to 435 assets.
  3. Senior receives 20 retained debt, assumed worth par, and shares worth 28, total 48. In liquidation senior would receive 425 − 380 = 45. The class comparison shows a 3 improvement. Subordinates recover zero.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a failing bank reopen capitalized, satisfy the assumed creditor safeguard and still lack cash for tomorrow?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. The example finishes with €80m equity and senior creditors receive shares worth more than their modeled liquidation recovery. Tomorrow’s cash payments still exceed the available funds by €15m. Converting debt changes claims on the bank. It does not deposit additional money in the settlement account.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If an independent valuation reduces the shares’ fair value from 28 to 22, what changes?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Senior economic consideration falls to 42, below the liquidation estimate of 45 by 3. Reassess the safeguard and any compensation or revised treatment under local law. The simplified book capital of 35 does not itself resolve that valuation difference.
