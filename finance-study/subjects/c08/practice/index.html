Theory midterm · 10 marks

What settles when a borrower pays a supplier?

After lessons 1–5, allow 20 minutes. Explain the reasoning behind your answer.

Bank A makes a new loan of 100 and credits the borrower’s current account. The borrower immediately pays the full amount to a supplier who banks at Bank B. Assume gross settlement in central-bank reserves.

  1. Part A · 4 marks

    Write the two balance-sheet changes at Bank A, starting at loan origination.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    What happens at Bank B and across the banking system?

  3. Part C · 3 marks

    Why can Bank A still face a constraint?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the midterm marking guide

Part A · 4 marks

Origination adds a loan asset of 100 and a deposit liability of 100. Payment removes 100 of reserves and 100 of the borrower’s deposit. The loan asset remains. Award marks for the four signed positions.

Part B · 3 marks

B gains reserves of 100 and a supplier deposit liability of 100. Across both banks the reserve transfer cancels. Relative to the starting position, the system has one additional loan and one additional deposit.

Part C · 3 marks

It must deliver reserves or obtain settlement funding when the transfer is due. It also bears borrower default risk and needs a capital cushion. Deposit creation by bookkeeping does not guarantee settlement liquidity.

Problem 1

A solvent bank has 2 cash and faces withdrawals of 6 today. What is the immediate shortfall?

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

The cash gap is 4 before new funding or asset sales. That gap alone does not measure equity or the final loss.

Problem 2

Why might deposit insurance reduce runs yet change risk-taking incentives?

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

Depositors who expect repayment have less reason to withdraw or monitor risk. Supervision, pricing and capital requirements can influence the resulting incentives.

Assessment criteria
  • Apply the right theory and state the assumptions.
  • Show each step with consistent units, dates and currency.
  • Interpret the result, test an alternative and explain what could change the decision.
Suggested academic marking scheme

Use these weights when assessing the complete subject. The four problem sets collect the written problems from individual lectures. You can use the 10-mark midterm and the 40-mark subject paper with the weights below.

Problem sets 25%
Four sets per standard subject. Show reasoning, calculations, units and checks. The two practice problems in each subject are worked samples for these sets.
Theory midterm 20%
After the first half of the subject. Explain or derive a model, identify assumptions and state what would change if one failed.
Seminar paper or laboratory 25%
During the second half. Apply the theory to a case, dataset or implementation. Address an alternative explanation and document how to reproduce the result.
Individual final examination 30%
At the end of the subject. Solve an unfamiliar problem, interpret the result and critique a claim. M&A and lending each include a 30-mark examination question with a marking guide.
