Funding and the cross-currency basis

Compare local loans, foreign borrowing and swapped funding. Credit, collateral and funding constraints can create gaps from frictionless parity.

The starting point

A treasurer comparing two loan markets needs the final payment in the same currency. A foreign loan can have a lower coupon but a costly exchange back into the repayment currency. A swap or forward can lock those exchanges. Fees, collateral and the borrower’s credit spread also belong in the comparison.

The cross-currency basis is a spread adjustment in a currency swap relative to a reference-rate comparison. Its quoted sign depends on which leg carries it. Work from the actual cash flows and report the all-in cost, including the basis once.

Theory and method

Cross-currency basis and synthetic funding cost

Covered interest parity deviation · FX swap basis · Balance-sheet constraints

A dealer says the three-month dollar basis is negative. Why is that insufficient information for a European treasurer to choose its funding currency?

The meaning of a quoted basis depends on which currency leg gets the spread and which leg the company pays. A treasurer can bypass the sign ambiguity by writing the initial currency received and every future currency delivered. Heavy demand for hedged funding and limits on dealer balance sheets can keep market forwards away from a frictionless reference even when institutions attempt to arbitrage. The achievable price depends on their actual funding and collateral terms.

How the result is built

1 + iˢʸⁿᴴ = (F/S)(1+iᶠᵇ)

iˢʸⁿᴴ is effective home borrowing rate over the period, iᶠᵇ the foreign borrowing rate, S home per foreign received at spot, and F home per foreign needed at the forward settlement. This bullet-loan comparison excludes fees unless added separately.

  1. Borrow Q foreign units and convert to QS home today. At maturity the foreign repayment is Q(1+iᶠᵇ).
  2. To obtain that amount under a fixed conversion, total home repayment is Q(1+iᶠᵇ)F. Divide by QS and subtract one to obtain the synthetic home borrowing rate.
  3. If S = 1 and foreign borrowing costs 3%, a forward of 1.02 gives synthetic cost 5.06%. Compare actual local cost, collateral and fees before selecting the route.

When the reasoning holds

  • Use executable spot proceeds and the correct forward purchase price. A reference midpoint can imply a cheaper funding route than the treasury desk can trade.
  • The basis may already be included in the dealer's all-in quote. Adding it again double-counts the same price adjustment.

Terms in this lesson
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Coupon
The interest payment specified in a bond’s terms.
Swap
A contract to exchange payment streams, such as fixed interest for floating interest.
Credit spread
Extra yield over a chosen reference yield. Credit risk, liquidity and market conditions can all affect it.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
First example

A company needs €100 and can repay €104 domestically. Alternatively it borrows $120, converts at $1.20 per euro and owes $126 in a year. A locked rate of $1.20 per euro makes that €105. Its all-in cost is 5% before fees, versus 4% domestically.

Compare loans after matching their currency

The cost advertised on a foreign loan is the cost in that currency. A parent needing euros must convert the initial advance into euros and obtain enough foreign currency for every future payment. A year-end forward locks a single payment. A cross-currency swap can exchange multiple interest payments and principal over time.

A swap can transform a stream of payments without eliminating the borrower’s credit spread. Determine what the firm still pays to its original lender and what it receives and pays under the swap. On each date cancel only equal and opposite cash flows in the same currency.

Understand the basis as a contract term

In a frictionless benchmark covered interest parity relates spot, forwards and comparable interest rates. In markets, funding pressures, collateral rules, balance-sheet costs and different credit terms can put the effective swapped rate away from a simple rate comparison. A currency-basis quote specifies an adjustment to a particular swap leg.

The sign is meaningful only alongside its convention. A quoted negative spread on the foreign leg may affect a euro borrower differently depending on which leg it pays. A cash-flow comparison avoids implying that every negative quote automatically saves the firm money.

Use net proceeds and an equivalent annual cost

For a one-year bullet loan with no intermediate payment, all-in cost equals final home payment divided by initial usable home proceeds minus one. An up-front fee reduces the amount available today. When there are multiple payments, compare present values at a common curve or solve for an equivalent borrowing rate on the entire schedule.

Company treasury should also compare whether lines remain available, counterparties can post collateral and market liquidity supports an early exit. A small saving in quoted interest can be outweighed by a funding mismatch or a cash collateral need.

Method

Prepare a funding comparison

  1. Net advance

    List gross principal, immediate exchange, fees and usable home-currency funds at inception.

  2. Matched service

    Calculate each loan interest and principal payment and the exact swap or forward deliveries that offset them. Include the basis in the quoted cash flow once.

  3. Equivalent cost

    Compare matched home-currency payment streams with the same maturity and initial cash. Distinguish amount, percentage of principal and annual rate.

Worked case

A foreign loan with a fee

A firm can borrow 100 home units and repay 104 after a year. The foreign alternative advances 125 foreign units when spot is 1.25 foreign per home, charges a 1-home-unit fee now, and requires 130 foreign after one year. A forward locks 1.27 foreign per home.

Working

  1. The foreign advance buys 100 home units, but usable cash after the fee is 99. The foreign route supplies less cash than the direct loan at the same stated principal.
  2. Buying 130 foreign forward costs 130/1.27 = 102.3622 home at maturity. Express the rate against usable 99.
  3. Effective cost is 102.3622/99 − 1 = 3.396%. The direct route costs 4%. If all terms scale proportionally, the foreign route remains cheaper for equal net proceeds.

Interpretation

The firm should check scaling, collateral and early-repayment conditions. A fixed fee that does not scale would require a new calculation for the desired advance.

A common mistake

Adding a basis spread again to a firm quote that already incorporates it.

How to correct it

Identify whether the dealer supplies a raw reference rate plus basis or a final executable payment. Build the signed schedule using only the cash actually payable.

Quick check
Which locked future rate would make the dollar route cost exactly €104 when the dollar repayment is $126?

Dividing the dollar repayment by the euro repayment gives $126/€104 = $1.2115 per euro.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A company needs home currency for one year. Local borrowing costs 5%. The foreign route yields net proceeds of 200 home after fees, requires 224 foreign at maturity and locks a quote of 1.08 foreign per home.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Compute the foreign route's annual home cost.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find the forward quote making this route tie the local rate.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Why might the cheaper quoted route still need cash planning?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

Buying 224 foreign costs 224/1.08 = 207.4074 home. The effective rate is 207.4074/200 − 1 = 3.70%.

Part B 3 marks

Local repayment for 200 is 210. The threshold solves 224/quote = 210, so quote = 224/210 = 1.06667 foreign per home.

Part C 2 marks

A collateral agreement may generate interim margin calls even on a hedge held to maturity. Determine their timing and liquidity source separately from the final effective rate.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A dealer says the three-month dollar basis is negative. Why is that insufficient information for a European treasurer to choose its funding currency?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask which leg carries the basis and which side of an executable quote we receive. Then I would compare identical home funding proceeds and all future home payments. At 1.02 forward, spot 1 and a 3% foreign loan, home cost is 5.06% before fees. A direct 5% facility is cheaper on those assumptions, even though the foreign loan's headline coupon is lower.

Interviewer’s follow-up

A dealer's price reduces the synthetic cost to 4.90% but introduces a 0.20% upfront fee on principal. Is it still cheaper for one year?

Compare with the follow-up answer

If gross proceeds are 100 and the fee is 0.20, usable cash is 99.80. Repayment is 104.90. Effective cost is 104.90/99.80 − 1 = 5.11%, above the direct 5% before any other differences.
