Foreign Exchange and Multinational Financial Management
Core finance / Lesson 7 of 13

Multinational investment

Discount a project’s local cash flows at a local rate or convert consistently to the parent currency. Mixing conventions can create a fictitious gain.

The starting point

A foreign project can be valued in the currency where it earns cash, or in the parent’s currency. First write the currency and date on every cash flow. Then choose a discount rate appropriate to its currency and risk. Higher nominal interest can reflect higher expected inflation in that same currency.

For a certain foreign-currency payment, a forward can translate it at a known future rate. Discount that locked home-currency amount at the matching home risk-free rate. For uncertain business cash flows, risk and currency assumptions also need to match.

Theory and method

Currency-consistent capital budgeting and covariance

Home-currency valuation · Foreign-currency valuation · Joint cash-flow scenarios

A parent multiplies expected foreign cash flow by expected spot to value an uncertain plant. What assumption is it making?

A plant may sell more units precisely when its currency weakens. The euro amount earned by a euro parent equals foreign cash multiplied by the exchange rate in each state. Averaging the cash and the exchange rate separately discards their association. The discrepancy matters even before choosing a discount rate. For a known foreign payment, by contrast, the exchange sensitivity can be locked exactly using a matched forward.

How the result is built

E[SC] = E[S]E[C] + Cov(S,C)

S is future home currency per foreign unit, C future cash in foreign units and SC realized home cash. E is the probability-weighted average and Cov is covariance in home-currency units, measuring how S and C move together around their means.

  1. List joint outcomes. In equally likely states (S,C) of (0.8,120) and (1.2,80), home cash is 96 in each state.
  2. Their separate means are 1.0 and 100. Multiplying those means gives 100, although average home cash is 96. Covariance contributes −4.
  3. Choose a home discount rate consistent with the actual home risk. In this particular example home cash is certain, so a 4% risk-free rate gives 96/1.04 = 92.31 today.

When the reasoning holds

  • Probability and discount treatment must be consistent. Risk-neutral state prices and ordinary probabilities with a risk-adjusted discount are distinct valuation methods.
  • Currency-by-currency valuations agree only when cash, exchange and risk assumptions align. A fixed forward on mean foreign volume cannot remove volume uncertainty.
Terms in this lesson
Net present value (NPV)
Today’s value of incoming cash minus today’s value of outgoing cash.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Discount factor
The price today of one unit of currency payable at a particular future date under specified risk assumptions.
Covered interest parity
A relationship connecting cash exchange rates, forward rates and interest rates in two currencies under matched, idealized funding terms.
How to read the notation
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.

All notation examples

First example

A project pays a certain 110 foreign units in one year. The foreign rate is 10%, so the value today is 100 foreign units. Spot is €2 per foreign unit, giving €200. At the parity forward of €2 × 1.05/1.10, the payment converts to €210. Discounting at 5% also gives €200.

Value a foreign project in its own currency

Begin with dated cash flows available to the party being valued. For a foreign subsidiary these might be local cash generated by operations. A parent might instead require dividends actually remittable after taxes and restrictions. Identify which series is being valued before choosing a currency.

If certain foreign-currency cash flows are discounted at the corresponding foreign risk-free rate, the result is a foreign amount today. Converting that present amount at today's spot rate gives home value today. This final conversion differs from converting every future payment at today's spot.

Reproduce the result in the parent's currency

A certain foreign amount at each date can be sold forward for a certain home amount on that date. Discount each home amount with the matching home risk-free discount factor. Covered interest parity makes the two routes agree when the borrowing, lending and forward contracts share the stated terms.

In an uncertain business, expected local cash and future conversion rates can move together. Expected home proceeds depend on this joint pattern. Simply multiplying independent forecasts is justified only if their interaction is negligible or appropriately modelled. The discount rate must also match the risk of the actual home cash flow.

Treat inflation and financing consistently

Nominal local cash forecasts should incorporate local price growth and a nominal local discount rate. Real cash forecasts use a real rate on a consistent price basis. A foreign market's higher nominal interest can reflect inflation differences and need not imply a worse underlying project.

If debt, tax shields or guarantees are valued separately, keep them out of the base operating cash flows. A company deciding whether to accept the project compares the value of future benefits with the initial investment on the same currency and claimant basis.

Method

Value then reconcile

  1. Local route

    List the dated foreign cash, discount each at its appropriate local rate and translate today's total at spot.

  2. Hedged home route

    For known receipts, apply the dated forward to each amount and discount each resulting home receipt at the home rate.

  3. Decision

    Reject inconsistent values caused by a mixed currency convention. Compare the agreed present value with the initial cost and stress the commercial assumptions.

Worked case

A two-year equipment lease

A foreign customer promises certain lease payments of 52.5 foreign units in year 1 and 55.125 in year 2. The foreign annual rate is 5%, home rate 2% and spot is 3 home per foreign. Ignore default and transaction costs.

Working

  1. The local present value is 52.5/1.05 + 55.125/1.05² = 100 foreign units. At spot this is 300 home units.
  2. Year 1 forward is 3 × 1.02/1.05. Year 2 forward is 3 × 1.02²/1.05². These lock home receipts of 153 and 156.06 respectively.
  3. Discount the home receipts to 153/1.02 + 156.06/1.02² = 300. If the equipment costs 290 home units now, the lease has NPV of 10 home units.

Interpretation

Both routes price the same certain payments. A separately negotiated lease fee or guarantee could change economic value but calling the payment euros or foreign currency cannot.

A common mistake

Applying the home discount rate to unconverted foreign receipts and calling the result home value.

How to correct it

Inspect each term's currency first. Converting a future amount requires a dated exchange assumption. Converting today's value uses today's spot.

Quick check
In this example, what goes wrong if 110 is multiplied by today’s €2 and discounted at the euro rate of 5%?

It produces about €209.52 by treating €2 as a future conversion rate. The consistent hedged one-year payment in this model is €210 before discounting.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

A project costs 190 home units now and pays a certain 105 foreign units next year. Spot is 2 home per foreign, the foreign rate is 5% and the home rate is 3%.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    Calculate NPV by discounting abroad first.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Prove parity by building the home route.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Would a forward on 105 eliminate all risk if the business might instead receive only 80 foreign?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

105/1.05 = 100 foreign today. That is 200 home, giving NPV 200 − 190 = 10 home units.

Part B 3 marks

Forward is 2 × 1.03/1.05. Selling 105 foreign forward produces 206 home. Its value 206/1.03 = 200 once again gives NPV 10.

Part C 2 marks

No. The fixed sale still requires delivery of 105. A shortfall of 25 must be acquired or otherwise funded, so volume risk and potentially spot-market exposure remain.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

A parent multiplies expected foreign cash flow by expected spot to value an uncertain plant. What assumption is it making?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I would ask for a joint sales-and-FX scenario. Multiplying the two forecasts assumes zero covariance or treats one quantity as fixed. Here the plant earns 96 home units in both states although the two means multiply to 100. A parent could overvalue the project and hedge the wrong amount. After converting in each state I would match the discount rate to the home-currency payoff that actually remains.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Reverse the volumes so that 80 pairs with 0.8 and 120 pairs with 1.2. What changes?

Compare with the follow-up answer

State payoffs become 64 and 144 home, averaging 104. The covariance contribution is +4. The home payment also becomes risky. Discounting the expected 104 at a risk-free rate would require an additional pricing assumption.
