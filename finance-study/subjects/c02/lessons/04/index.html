Uncovered parity and risk

Removing the forward contract leaves exchange risk. Expected future spot rates and forward rates can differ when investors demand compensation for holding that risk.

The starting point

An investor buying a foreign deposit earns its local interest and ultimately converts the proceeds at a future exchange rate. Leaving that conversion open creates currency risk. A high foreign interest rate alone says little about the return measured in the investor’s own currency.

Uncovered interest parity is a model in which expected currency movements offset interest differences for comparable deposits when investors require no extra compensation for currency risk. A forward quote is observable today. An expected future spot rate is a belief and can differ from the forward when investors price risk.

Theory and method

Uncovered interest parity and the currency risk premium

UIP · Forward premium puzzle · Carry trade

If the one-year forward is 2.00 home per foreign, is an economist predicting spot of 2.08 alleging arbitrage?

A trader can lock the future conversion with a contract or wait and accept whatever price the currency fetches. Investors may demand extra expected compensation for a currency that falls precisely when their labour income or portfolio also falls. Its expected return can then differ from a safe home investment even when the market offers no locked arbitrage. A forecast that later proves inaccurate provides a separate source of realized gains and losses.

How the result is built

ρ = (1+iᶠ)E[S₁]/S₀ − (1+iᴴ) ; under UIP, ρ = 0

S₀ is spot home currency per foreign unit today, E[S₁] the expected future spot under the stated beliefs, iᶠ and iᴴ the one-period foreign and home interest rates, and ρ expected excess home-currency return on the unhedged foreign deposit.

  1. One home unit buys 1/S₀ foreign units. Invest at iᶠ and convert the fixed foreign maturity amount at uncertain S₁.
  2. Take an expectation using the assumed distribution of S₁. Subtract the home gross return to isolate the expected excess.
  3. CIP replaces the uncertain conversion with a traded forward. If CIP holds and ρ equals zero, the forward equals expected future spot under the quote and assumptions. With a nonzero premium the equality need not hold.

When the reasoning holds

  • The expectation must specify whose information and probability distribution are used. Observing a forward alone does not reveal a unique market forecast.
  • In the elementary UIP comparison credit and liquidity risks of deposits are matched. Different default risks complicate attribution to currency risk.

Terms in this lesson
Forward
A contract to exchange an agreed amount at a set price on a future date.
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
Risk premium
Extra expected return for bearing risk, or the amount a risk-averse person would give up to replace a gamble with certainty, depending on context.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
First example

A dollar investor converts $100 into 100 foreign units at $1 per unit and earns 8%. If the final rate is $0.90, the 108 units buy $97.20. If the rate is $1.05, they buy $113.40. The same deposit rate produced two very different dollar returns.

Build a distribution of home-currency returns

Owning a foreign deposit without a forward leaves a known amount of foreign currency at maturity and an unknown home-currency value. For a spot quote in home units per foreign unit, its realized gross home return is foreign gross interest multiplied by future spot divided by initial spot.

Construct possible future rates rather than assuming the average is guaranteed. Conditional on fixed foreign interest, expected home proceeds use the probability-weighted future rate. The distribution, including its downside when the investor needs cash, still matters even if the expected proceeds equal those of a safe deposit.

Separate covered from uncovered reasoning

Covered parity compares contracts whose future conversion is fixed. Uncovered interest parity compares expected returns under assumptions that leave no additional expected compensation for currency risk on otherwise comparable deposits. The second is an economic hypothesis, not the replication proof used for the first.

Forward and expected future spot may then differ. Investors might require a premium for a currency that tends to fall precisely when their other income falls. A high interest differential can compensate for potential depreciation, a risk premium, credit risk or a combination.

Evaluate a carry strategy

A carry strategy funds in a lower-yielding currency and invests in a higher-yielding one without locking the exchange rate. Small regular gains can coexist with an occasional currency fall that erases them. The sign of the realized result is determined after translation and funding costs.

Calculate a break-even future rate at which the investment just repays the funding leg. Compare that threshold with plausible outcomes. If an investor has an offsetting foreign liability, the relevant risk of the same deposit can differ from the risk for a purely domestic saver.

Method

Test a foreign deposit against a home alternative

  1. Set up the choice

    State the initial investment, both interest rates and the exchange-rate units. Treat credit risk as equal only if specified.

  2. Build outcomes

    Convert today, grow at the foreign rate, translate under each future rate and subtract the foregone home payoff.

  3. Find the threshold

    Divide the alternative home payoff by the fixed future foreign amount. Assess the consequences in both favourable and adverse states.

Worked case

A saver faces two currency outcomes

One home investor has 1,000. Spot is 2 home units per foreign unit, the foreign rate is 8% and the home rate 4%. Future spot will be 1.80 or 2.10, each with probability one-half.

Working

  1. The home deposit pays 1,040. The foreign deposit starts at 500 and grows to 540 foreign units.
  2. At the low rate home proceeds are 540 × 1.80 = 972. At the high rate they are 1,134. Expected proceeds are (972 + 1,134)/2 = 1,053.
  3. The expected excess is 13 but the downside relative to saving at home is 68. The break-even rate is 1,040/540 = 1.92593.

Interpretation

The excess expected payoff is not a locked benefit. A saver whose domestic labour income also falls in the low-rate outcome may be especially reluctant to bear it.

A common mistake

Subtracting the two interest rates and calling the difference guaranteed currency profit.

How to correct it

Always show the translated maturity payoff, and label the future exchange rate as locked, expected or realized.

Quick check
With a dollar deposit paying 2%, which final exchange rate makes the foreign investment’s actual dollar proceeds equal $102?

The foreign deposit delivers 108 units. Solve 108 × exchange rate = 102, giving $0.9444 per unit.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

An investor converts 600 home units at 3 per foreign unit and earns 10% abroad. At home she could earn 3%. Possible future rates are 2.60 with probability 25% and 2.90 with probability 75%.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Derive home payoffs in both currency states.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Find expected excess proceeds and the break-even exchange rate.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Does this violate covered parity?

Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The deposit grows from 200 to 220 foreign units. Home payoffs are 220 × 2.60 = 572 and 220 × 2.90 = 638. A home deposit pays 618.

Part B 3 marks

Expected foreign proceeds are 0.25 × 572 + 0.75 × 638 = 621.5. Excess over home is 3.5. The break-even rate is 618/220 = 2.80909.

Part C 2 marks

These are uncertain spot conversions. To test covered parity one would need a forward quote and executable borrowing and lending terms for matched maturities.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Compare with a spoken answer

A forecast above the forward is a view about an uncertain exchange rate. CIP concerns contracts that deliver fixed currency at maturity. UIP adds a restriction on expected unhedged returns under a zero-premium assumption. Before treating the difference as attractive, I would calculate the foreign payoff in adverse states and how it relates to the investor's other risks. No one can lock 2.08 simply by writing it in a forecast.
