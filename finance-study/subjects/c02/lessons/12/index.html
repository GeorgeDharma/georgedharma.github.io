Pricing political-risk cover for an overseas investment

Separate a government-imposed transfer block from a market loss or commercial default.

In this lecture

  1. Identify the event that caused the loss
  2. Read the cash-flow promise
  3. Price the change in cash flows
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. Three states, two payment dates
  6. A transfer block on a distribution
  7. Independent exam problem
  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Political-risk insurance and expected recovery

risk transfer · political risks · policy exclusions

What you should be able to explain

  • Separate a government-imposed transfer block from a market loss or commercial default.
  • Calculate a delayed claim's value and identify the funding the parent still needs during the wait.

Identify the event that caused the loss

Currency inconvertibility means inability to exchange local funds into the covered foreign currency under the relevant contract. Transfer restriction concerns inability to send eligible funds outside the country. A government action can create one without the other. An exchange rate that freely falls makes a remittance less valuable but does not by itself prevent conversion or transfer.

Write one sentence describing what physically happened in each scenario. Was there local money to convert? Could it be converted at a lawful market rate? Could the proceeds leave? A customer who never pays creates a commercial receivables loss, which needs separate cover unless the contract expressly provides it.

Read the cash-flow promise

A policy may cover a fraction of the eligible loss, limit the amount per claim, specify a currency and impose a waiting period. The percentage, as well as any deductible or ceiling, belongs on the eligible loss. An exchange-rate loss on a separate freely transferable receipt does not become eligible just because the same investment also faces political risk.

A payment after a waiting period cannot pay a bill due before that date. Model any bridge financing separately. Document who owns rights to later recoveries. Counting both the full claim and all eventual local recoveries can give the investor cash it has agreed to assign to the insurer.

Price the change in cash flows

Prepare a column for the uninsured parent and one for the insured parent in each possible state. The difference isolates insurance. Discount each incremental receipt from its actual payment date and subtract the premium on the date due. Keep amounts and discount rates in the same currency.

Probabilities and discount rates here are given for an illustrative expected-value decision. A real parent may value prevention of a funding shortfall or a debt covenant breach as well. Conversely, uncertain claims acceptance, legal costs and imperfect coverage can reduce protection. Quantify those items where evidence permits.

Method

Build the result

Incremental PV = sum of discounted probability-weighted claim receipts − premium today.

PV is today's value in the policy currency. For a single payment in year t, multiply the eligible claim by the valuation probability of the covered event, then divide by (1 + r) raised to t, with r the stated annual valuation rate.

  1. List exhaustive states, assign each a probability and calculate the uninsured cash separately.
  2. Apply the policy only to eligible losses according to the policy terms. Schedule the claim and any surrendered recovery rights.
  3. Discount incremental net receipts and compare to the premium. Separately show the largest near-term cash shortage.
Worked case

A transfer block on a distribution

Amounts are €m. At year one, mutually exclusive states yield a normal transfer of €12 with probability 70%, a freely transferable €9 after depreciation with probability 20%, or a government block on €12 with probability 10%. In the last state, the parent receives nothing through the horizon unless insured. Illustrative cover costs €0.50 today and pays 80% of €12 at year two only in the blocked state. Assume no other recoveries or costs, and use these valuation probabilities and 5% for all receipts.

Working

  1. Uninsured expected year-one receipt is 0.70 × 12 + 0.20 × 9 + 0.10 × 0 = €10.20. Its PV is €9.71.
  2. The blocked-state claim is €9.60. Depreciation-state compensation is zero. The expected year-two claim is therefore €0.96.
  3. Incremental value is 0.96/(1.05)² − 0.50 = €0.371. By adding this to unrounded uninsured PV, the insured receipts net of premium are worth €10.085.
  4. Year-one cash in the blocked state is still zero. If the parent owes €7 that year, it needs €7 of independent liquidity even with cover.

Decision and interpretation

The stated policy adds value under the supplied assumptions. The treasurer should show both the €0.371 expected gain and the unresolved €7 funding requirement when seeking approval.

Quick check
Under a contract covering only government transfer restrictions, which event could create an eligible claim?

The government order matches the stated trigger. Default and lawful depreciation have different causes. Remaining policy terms still apply.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

An insurer quotes €0.60m today for a contingent €6m payment at the end of year two. Under the supplied valuation probabilities the sole covered event has a 15% chance, and the discount rate is 8%. No other cash flow changes. Find incremental PV and the event probability at which the parent breaks even.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Expected claim is 0.15 × 6 = €0.90m in year two.
  2. Its PV is 0.90/(1.08)² = €0.772m. After the premium, incremental PV is €0.172m.
  3. Set p × 6/(1.08)² equal to 0.60. The break-even probability is 0.11664, about 11.66%.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Does political-risk insurance pay whenever foreign-currency earnings arrive below budget, and how should a parent decide whether the premium is worth paying?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The contract pays for a covered cause of loss. A lawful depreciation can reduce the value of a freely transferable dividend without triggering transfer-restriction cover. I would identify eligible states, calculate the contractual payment after any limits, discount it from its actual date, and subtract the premium and surrendered recovery rights. I would assess funding during any waiting period separately.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the parent expects to recover €2m itself in the covered state at year two and must give up that right on payment, how does the decision change?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The incremental covered-state receipt is €4m. Its expected discounted value is 0.15 × 4/(1.08)² = €0.514m, below the €0.60m premium. On the stated valuation the cover then loses about €0.086m.
