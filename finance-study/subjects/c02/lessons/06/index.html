Currency hedging

Match a business exposure with a forward, option or natural offset. Imperfect matching of timing and quantity leaves residual risk.

The starting point

Start with a dated schedule of foreign receipts and payments. A natural hedge matches opposite cash flows in the same currency and period. A forward locks a future exchange. A currency option buys the right to exchange on stated terms and requires a premium.

The direction matters. An exporter expecting dollars can sell dollars forward. An importer needing dollars can buy them forward. Matching a forecast with the wrong maturity or a larger amount can create an additional exposure when the original order changes.

Theory and method

Money-market hedging and protective currency puts

Natural hedge · Forward hedge · Currency option floor

An exporter has a fixed foreign invoice. Show how to manufacture a forward sale with ordinary bank accounts and explain what an option changes.

A future dollar receipt can repay a dollar loan. Borrow its present dollar value today, sell the proceeds for euros and invest those euros until the invoice falls due. The dollar receipt then exactly repays the dollar loan, while the euro deposit provides a known amount. A put option instead buys the choice to sell dollars at a minimum rate. That flexibility costs a premium and lets the exporter benefit from a stronger dollar.

How the result is built

Hₘ = [Q/(1+iᶠᵇ)]bₛ(1+iᴴˡ) ; Hₚ = Q max(S₁,K) − P₀(1+iᴴˡ)

Hₘ and Hₚ are maturity home proceeds of the money-market and put hedges. Q is foreign receipt, iᶠᵇ foreign borrowing rate, bₛ spot bid, iᴴˡ home deposit rate, S₁ maturity spot, K put strike and P₀ total premium today. Rates cover the hedge term.

  1. For Q of 104 foreign and foreign borrowing of 4%, borrow 100. Converting at spot bid 0.90 and depositing home at 2% creates a certain 91.80 home units.
  2. For a put struck at 0.90 with total initial premium 2, account for 2.04 of maturity-date premium cost. Gross receipts are at least 104 × 0.90 = 93.60.
  3. The put's net floor is 91.56. If spot reaches 1.00, net proceeds rise to 101.96. Compare with an executable matched forward and with required budget cash.

When the reasoning holds

  • Invoice amount and maturity must match the debt for a locked money-market hedge. Client default leaves the foreign loan outstanding.
  • Option comparisons require a common premium date and correct payoff convention. Compare against net receipt after offsetting foreign bills.

Terms in this lesson
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
First example

A company will collect $500,000 and pay suppliers $200,000 on the same date. Its net dollar receipt is $300,000. Selling that amount forward at €0.92 per dollar locks €276,000 on the matched net receipt, assuming both invoices settle as planned.

Identify the business risk before picking a contract

An importer facing a fixed dollar payment fears a more expensive dollar. Its hedge should deliver dollars at a known or capped home-currency cost. An exporter awaiting dollars fears a cheaper dollar. It needs a floor on the home currency it can receive.

Foreign receipts and payments at the same date naturally offset one another. Net them by currency and date before buying financial protection. If a future receipt is uncertain, distinguish the amount already committed from the possible amount and allow for delays or cancellations.

Compare a forward with a currency option

A forward commits both parties to exchange a fixed amount at a fixed future rate. A purchased option gives the buyer a choice. A dollar call lets its owner buy dollars at a stated rate. A dollar put lets its owner sell them. The premium is the up-front price of that choice.

At maturity a dollar holder with a put chooses the higher of spot and the strike price. Add any unmatched exposure at spot, then subtract the premium on a consistent date. A forward on a matched receipt locks one value but gives up favourable movements on that part.

Compare business outcomes after hedging

A hedge can have a loss considered in isolation and still protect the company if the underlying business position gains. Measure the combined outcome, including the premium, funding costs, future collateral requirements and mismatch in timing or size.

Even two currencies that usually move together may separate under stress. Hedging a currency through a related currency is an approximate hedge whose residual is called basis risk. Show a stress price for the actual invoice and for the instrument instead of assuming a permanent relationship.

Method

Construct the payout table

  1. Net the business exposure

    Subtract firmly matched payments from receipts in each currency and maturity bucket. Identify which side needs protection.

  2. Write each hedge payoff

    For a forward apply the locked rate to the hedged volume. For a put use the higher sale rate, and for a call use the lower purchase rate.

  3. Compare total proceeds

    Include any unhedged balance plus premiums and fees. Recalculate the table when one commercial cash flow fails to arrive.

Worked case

An exporter compares two policies

A euro exporter is certain to receive $200,000 in three months. It can sell forward at €0.94 per dollar or buy a dollar put struck at €0.94 for €4,000. Compare outcomes at €0.85 and €1.00. Ignore premium financing.

Working

  1. The full forward yields 200,000 × 0.94 = €188,000 in either case. Without a hedge the receipt would be €170,000 or €200,000.
  2. At €0.85 use the put and sell at €0.94. Net of the premium the company receives €184,000. At €1.00 let the put expire and receive €196,000 net.
  3. The option buys upside and a lower guaranteed net floor than the forward in this example. The favourable state gives €8,000 more than the forward, while the weak state gives €4,000 less.

Interpretation

The two policies protect different business objectives. A cash budget requiring at least €187,000 would be met by the forward but not by the put under these assumptions.

A common mistake

Reporting the option strike as the guaranteed net cash price.

How to correct it

Deduct the premium and, if relevant, its financing cost. Multiply the strike only by the quantity actually covered.

Quick check
After that hedge is signed, the customer payment falls to $400,000 while the supplier bill stays $200,000. What remains?

The revised net receipt is $200,000, but the company committed to deliver $300,000. The difference is a $100,000 shortfall.

Written practice · 7 marks

Independent exam problem

An importer owes $150,000 and will receive $50,000 on the same date. It can buy the net dollars forward at 0.92 home units or buy a dollar call with strike 0.91 for 2,000 home units. Ignore premium interest.

  1. Part A · 2 marks

    Find the locked home funding need using the forward.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Compare the call's total net cost at spots of 0.85 and 1.00.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    What if the $50,000 receipt is delayed beyond settlement after the original forward is signed?

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 2 marks

The business requires a net $100,000. Buying this amount forward costs 92,000 home units.

Part B 3 marks

At 0.85 allow the call to expire, buy at spot and add the premium, giving 87,000. At 1.00 exercise at 0.91 and add the premium, giving 93,000.

Part C 2 marks

The forward still delivers $100,000, leaving $50,000 to fund at the due date. A bridge, spot purchase or rearranged settlement is required. The later receipt creates a separate exposure.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

An exporter has a fixed foreign invoice. Show how to manufacture a forward sale with ordinary bank accounts and explain what an option changes.

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

I can borrow the present foreign value of the receipt, convert at the executable spot bid and invest the home proceeds. The invoice repays the loan, so I know the home maturity amount today. A put gives a floor and keeps the benefit of a stronger currency. I would include the premium's financing cost, compare dealer sides and test what happens if the customer pays late or less than contracted.

Interviewer’s follow-up

The customer defaults on 20 of the 104 foreign units. What becomes of the hedge?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The foreign loan still matures at 104. Only 84 arrives, leaving 20 foreign units to buy or refinance. The home deposit remains intact, but net proceeds now depend on the cost of obtaining the missing 20 and potential recoveries from the customer.
