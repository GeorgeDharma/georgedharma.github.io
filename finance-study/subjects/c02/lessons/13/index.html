FX settlement risk and payment versus payment

Theory and terminology

FX settlement exposure and payment versus payment

Herstatt risk · replacement cost · PvP

What you should be able to explain

  • Measure unsettled FX principal from the last guaranteed cancellation time to confirmed final receipt.
  • Compare replacement cost, principal exposure and liquidity need, then decide whether a settlement method meets a counterparty limit.

Before either payment is irrevocable

Two counterparties agree on the currencies and amounts on the trade date. They promise to exchange on the value date. If one defaults while the other can still cancel its outgoing instruction, the surviving party can enter a new market trade. Its loss from replacing the original terms depends on how the market rate has moved.

For a buyer of dollars, a rising euro price of dollars makes the original bargain valuable. Replacing it costs the increase in euro price times the contracted dollars. A rate move in the other direction gives no positive replacement exposure on that individual trade, though legal netting rules may affect a portfolio.

The full principal can become exposed

An ordinary separate currency payment can become impossible to cancel with certainty before its cash leaves the bank's account. From that deadline, the payer may owe its entire outgoing amount even if the other side defaults. Exposure lasts until final receipt can be confirmed. A bank that cannot reconcile incoming payments promptly should measure conservatively while their status remains uncertain.

Use a common time zone. Include holidays and the actual correspondent bank's cancellation deadline. The accounting time at which an account is booked can be misleading. Add overlapping outgoing principals when measuring a counterparty limit under the gross assumptions below. Enforceable net settlement or collateral would call for a separately justified measure.

What payment versus payment changes

In payment versus payment, final delivery of one currency depends on final delivery of the other. If the settlement does not occur there is no one-sided final principal delivery through that mechanism. Eligible currencies, participants and cutoffs matter. A trade settled outside those arrangements still needs its own treatment.

Conditional delivery does not produce dollars in time for a separate bill if the trade fails. The desk may need same-day liquidity and may incur replacement costs when arranging a new trade. Set funding and replacement limits as well as any limit on gross principal.

Method

Build the result

Gross exposure at time t = sum of outgoing principals whose cancellation deadlines have passed and whose final receipts are unconfirmed.

Principals are stated in one reporting currency. Separately, for a dollar buyer, positive replacement exposure before irrevocability is max(0, current price − contract price) × contracted dollars. Both prices are euros per US dollar.

  1. Place each guaranteed cancellation deadline and each reliable confirmation on one clock.
  2. Include the entire outgoing principal for each open interval, then add intervals that overlap for the same counterparty.
  3. Compare the peak to the approved limit. Compare replacement risk before release and contingency funding after a failed receipt separately.
Worked case

A treasury desk on two value dates

The desk buys US$10m for €9m by separate payments. Its guaranteed euro cancellation deadline is Monday 10:00. Final dollar receipt is confirmed Tuesday 14:00. Before the cutoff, a dollar costs €0.93 instead of the trade price of €0.90. A second trade with the same bank exposes another €4m from Monday 11:00 to 13:00. All times use one clock. There is no offset, collateral or netting. The counterparty principal limit is €12m.

Working

  1. Before 10:00, replacing the first trade at €0.93 costs (0.93 − 0.90) × US$10m = €0.30m extra.
  2. From Monday 10:00 until Tuesday 14:00, the first trade puts €9m at risk for 28 hours. Its dollar equivalent is a different unit for the same exchange, not a second outgoing payment.
  3. For the two hours between 11:00 and 13:00, gross exposed principal is €9m + €4m = €13m. For the rest of the first trade's window it is €9m.
  4. At its peak the schedule exceeds the limit by €1m. Moving the second trade to an eligible payment-versus-payment arrangement removes its one-sided €4m from this calculation. The remaining €9m then fits.

Decision and interpretation

Change the settlement route or timing before either instruction is irrevocable. Check the alternative's cutoff, funding and confirmation process. An alternative route that receives the required dollars too late can still leave the treasury desk short.

Quick check
A bank loses the guaranteed right to cancel an outgoing currency payment at noon. It debits its own ledger at 15:00 and confirms final receipt at 17:00. How long should it measure principal exposure?

Irrevocability can come before the ledger debit. Exposure ends when the incoming currency's final receipt can be confirmed.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A bank buys US$6m for €5.10m. Just before the euro cancellation deadline it would pay €0.87 per dollar in a replacement trade. The cancellation deadline is Wednesday 11:30. Final receipt is confirmed Thursday 09:00. A second €2m outgoing principal overlaps from 15:00 to 17:00 Wednesday. The gross principal limit is €7m. Calculate the three separate exposures.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. The contracted euro price is 5.10/6 = €0.85 per dollar. Replacing the trade before irrevocability costs (0.87 − 0.85) × 6 = €0.12m extra.
  2. The first principal exposure is €5.10m for 21.5 hours from Wednesday 11:30 to Thursday 09:00.
  3. Peak exposure during the two-hour overlap is €7.10m, which exceeds the limit by €0.10m. The treasury desk would need another route, another time or an authorised limit change before release.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why might a bank with only €0.30 million at risk from an exchange-rate move have €9 million of principal exposed on the same trade?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

The €0.30 million is the extra cost of replacing the currency bargain while the bank can still stop its payment. Once the euro payment cannot be cancelled with certainty, the bank can lose the entire €9 million if the incoming dollars never settle. Payment versus payment links final delivery of both currencies; replacement costs and the need for timely dollar funding may still remain.

Interviewer’s follow-up

Suppose the dollar transfer appears as a provisional credit Wednesday evening. Could the bank close the exposure then?

Compare with the follow-up answer

Only if the payment is final and reliably identified under the arrangements. A reversible provisional credit leaves principal exposed. The desk should keep the exposure open until it can establish final receipt.
