Valuing a foreign project for its parent company

Reconcile foreign operating value to the cash that parent shareholders can receive.

Theory and terminology

Adjusted present value in multinational capital budgeting

parent cash flows · remittance · foreign project APV

What you should be able to explain

  • Reconcile foreign operating value to the cash that parent shareholders can receive.
  • Price a subsidised foreign loan against its market alternative, and check the resulting parent decision with direct cash flows.

Choose whose pocket you are valuing

The subsidiary asks whether equipment creates more local-currency cash than its purchase price. The parent asks how much it must contribute and what it will receive in its own currency. A transfer charge, blocked dividend or cost at headquarters can make the answers differ even when both analysts forecast exactly the same sales.

Write a dated schedule for cash before financing. Put operating receipts, terminal assets and incremental transfer charges on separate lines. An amount left in a foreign account may fund a later purchase or distribution. Value that actual use at its actual date. In the case below, all eligible operating cash can leave immediately after payment of a fixed charge.

Give the lender its own row

A loan advance and repayment buy the lender a claim. At a market interest rate the fair value of that claim equals the advance, so the mere availability of borrowing adds no value under the case assumptions. A below-market loan can add value because repayment has a lower market value than the money advanced.

Match the benchmark loan's currency, maturity, default exposure, security and repayment timing. A comparison with an unsecured borrowing rate can give a spurious subsidy on secured debt. Include any arrangement fee. Debt tax relief, if applicable, needs its own incremental treatment and must appear only once.

Make the two currencies agree

Cash already contracted in foreign units can be translated at an executable forward exchange rate, the price agreed today for exchange on a future date. For the certain cash flows here, home discounting after that conversion agrees with foreign discounting followed by conversion today.

Uncertain operating cash requires an assumption about how exchange rates and business results move together. Multiplying an average receipt by an average rate can misstate its expected home value. Also check the amount and maturity that can actually be hedged before labelling a forecast fixed.

Method

Build the result

Parent APV = remittable operating NPV + market value of loan concession − fees today.

APV means adjusted present value. NPV is present value of receipts less investment. All terms are date-zero parent currency. The concession equals cash advanced minus the present value of promised repayments at a comparable market rate.

  1. Calculate operating NPV as if financed entirely by the parent, including the transfer terms that apply in that comparison.
  2. Discount the concession's foreign repayments at their appropriate foreign market rate, deduct from foreign proceeds, then convert the difference at spot.
  3. Subtract fees and reconcile to actual parent contributions and post-debt distributions. Agreement is a strong check on timing and duplicated benefits.
Worked case

A packaging line and a development loan

All figures are millions. A euro parent pays L200 today for equipment. It generates certain L224 in one year, then closes. A fixed L6 transfer fee applies regardless of financing. Today L1 buys €0.50. Comparable certain cash discounts at 10% in L and 5% in euros. The one-year forward is €0.4772727 per L. A lender advances L80 now for L82 due in a year. The parent pays a €0.60 arrangement fee. No taxes or other costs.

Working

  1. Local operating NPV in euros is (224/1.10 − 200) × 0.50 = €1.82. The transfer fee is worth (6/1.10) × 0.50 = €2.73 today.
  2. Parent operating NPV is (218/1.10 − 200) × 0.50 = −€0.91. The financing concession is (80 − 82/1.10) × 0.50 = €2.73.
  3. Adding unrounded amounts gives APV of −0.9091 + 2.7273 − 0.60 = €1.22.
  4. Direct check: the parent invests €100 − €40 + €0.60 = €60.60. It receives (224 − 6 − 82) × 0.4772727 = €64.91 in a year. Discounting at 5% gives €61.82 today.

Decision and interpretation

Accept on these terms. If the concessional offer lapses and its fee disappears, the same line loses €0.91 for the parent. The positive local figure alone would miss the reversal.

Quick check
A local lender extends a loan at exactly the appropriate market rate, with no fee, tax effect or other benefit. What is its separate contribution to parent APV?

The market value of the repayment promise equals the cash received. The project can still create operating value independently.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A different subsidiary spends L100 and earns certain L111 in one year. The transfer charge is L3. A concessionary loan advances L30 for repayment of L30. Its arrangement fee is €0.50. Spot and one-year forward are both €1 per L. Both market rates are 10%. Find local operating NPV, parent operating NPV and parent APV.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Local operating NPV is 111/1.10 − 100 = €0.91.
  2. Parent operating NPV is (111 − 3)/1.10 − 100 = −€1.82. Loan value is 30 − 30/1.10 = €2.73.
  3. Parent APV is about €0.41 after the fee. Direct check gives 78/1.10 − (100 − 30 + 0.50) = €0.41.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Can a foreign project with a positive local value, a negative remittable operating value and a subsidised loan still earn its parent an adequate return?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Yes. I would value the subsidiary's operations first, then deduct the cost or delay of getting its cash to the parent. I would add only the loan's advantage over a comparable market loan, less fees. The result reconciles to the parent's actual investment and receipts. In the packaging case that gives positive €1.22 million even though remittable operations alone lose €0.91 million.

Interviewer’s follow-up

What if the concessionary lender instead required L33 at maturity?

Compare with the follow-up answer

At the 10% market rate that repayment is worth L30 today, so the concession disappears. With the €0.50 fee still payable, parent NPV becomes approximately −€2.32.
