Policy under uncertainty

A hedge protects a forecast only if the underlying payment occurs. Compare committed and uncertain invoices before setting a coverage ratio.

The starting point

A company choosing its hedge ratio decides how much of an exposure to cover. A signed invoice gives a firmer quantity than a possible sale. A forward commits the firm to exchange currency even if a forecast order is cancelled. An option allows it to walk away at the cost of a premium.

Test both the exchange rate and the volume. Comparing hedge choices only in the scenario where every order arrives hides the cost of overhedging. A policy can set different coverage levels for confirmed and uncertain business, and update the hedge when an order becomes firm.

Theory and method

Minimum-variance hedge ratio and cash-flow-at-risk

Projected exposure · Forecast error · Layered hedging

Would hedging 100% of an export forecast always minimize currency risk?

A fruit exporter might receive fewer dollars precisely when weak commodity prices also hurt its customers. Its unhedged home revenue moves with both invoice volume and the exchange rate. A short forward offsets one unit of exposure for each foreign unit promised. Choosing the amount that minimizes variance depends on how total unhedged cash co-moves with spot. A treasurer protecting a hard cash floor may choose a different amount from the variance minimum.

How the result is built

H* = Cov(X,S)/Var(S) ; Y = X + H(F−S)

X is unhedged maturity home cash, S home per foreign at maturity, F the locked forward rate, H foreign units sold forward, Y hedged home cash. Cov and Var mean covariance and variance. H* minimizes variance over unconstrained H.

  1. The forward's deterministic HF does not affect variability. The random part becomes X − HS.
  2. Expand variation: Var(Y) = Var(X) + H²Var(S) − 2H Cov(X,S). Set the marginal change to zero, giving H* = Cov(X,S)/Var(S) when spot variance is positive.
  3. If foreign volume is fixed at Q and X = QS, covariance equals Q Var(S), giving H* = Q. A stochastic volume can make the optimal amount smaller, larger or even negative.

When the reasoning holds

  • Covariance must describe the future joint exposure reasonably well. A short or shifting historical sample can make H* unstable.
  • Variance treats favourable and unfavourable deviations symmetrically. Delivery constraints, liquidity floors, credit limits and an option premium can make an unconstrained variance-minimum hedge unsuitable.
Terms in this lesson
Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.
Forward
A contract to exchange an agreed amount at a set price on a future date.
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
First example

An exporter has confirmed receipts of $600,000 and possible additional receipts of $400,000. Selling $600,000 forward exactly covers confirmed sales. Selling $1m covers the optimistic forecast but leaves a $400,000 delivery shortfall if the extra order fails.

Define the risk measure and the decision horizon

A hedge policy can protect a minimum operating cash balance, a quoted customer margin or the value of an identified invoice. These objectives can recommend different coverage. Set the currency, amount, time window and outcome against which success will be measured before comparing instruments.

A signed invoice and a sales forecast differ in enforceability. Forecast error can concern volume, date, cancellation or currency. If the invoice never occurs, a binding forward can turn a protective contract into a stand-alone position that the firm must settle.

Map volume and rate together

A useful scenario grid combines more than one currency rate with more than one eligible business volume. For an exporter with a short forward, add the home currency from selling the contracted amount at the forward rate to the home value of the remaining commercial receipts at spot.

If the remaining volume is negative, the company must acquire foreign currency to discharge the contract. An option covers an uncertain receipt without imposing delivery when the business disappears, although its premium is paid even if the option is unused. A small outright hedge and a contingent hedge may be combined.

Build controls around the decision

Treasury needs to know who owns the sales forecast, when an estimate becomes binding, and what happens after a cancellation. A company can layer hedges as orders are confirmed rather than making a single all-or-nothing decision on the first forecast.

Assess the hedge alongside the underlying revenue. A contract showing a loss when the currency strengthens may be offset by higher home proceeds from the sale. A risk committee should receive the net outcome and exception reports covering overhedges or delayed receipts.

Method

Stress a coverage decision

  1. Separate committed and forecast volumes

    Record a defensible minimum, a central forecast and a zero-or-low outcome for uncertain sales. Assign dates and settlement currency.

  2. Compute net proceeds

    For a short forward of H foreign units and receipt V, home proceeds are H × forward + (V − H) × spot, excluding fees.

  3. Compare the objective

    Use the scenario satisfying the business constraint as well as the scenario with the best commercial outcome. Quantify any required cash purchase if V is below H.

Worked case

A seasonal exporter

A euro exporter expects $100,000 to $180,000 in September. It commits to sell $140,000 forward at €0.90 per dollar. Test a spot of €0.80 and €1.00, with the commercial receipt due on the settlement date.

Working

  1. At the low volume and a €0.80 spot, proceeds are 140,000 × 0.90 − 40,000 × 0.80 = €94,000. At €1.00 the same low volume gives €86,000.
  2. At actual volume of $180,000 the remaining $40,000 is sold at spot. Net proceeds are €158,000 if spot is €0.80 and €166,000 if it is €1.00.
  3. The sign of the unhedged position reverses between volumes. A stronger dollar helps the high-volume case but hurts the low-volume case because dollars must be purchased to fill the contract.

Interpretation

If the priority is never having to buy missing dollars, a full commitment above the confirmed $100,000 misses that objective. Other policies may deliberately accept that risk.

A common mistake

Testing only the forecast volume and concluding that a forward removes currency sensitivity.

How to correct it

Recalculate the sensitivity at the lowest and highest volumes. The residual foreign exposure equals actual volume less the committed hedge.

Quick check
Which contract lets the exporter secure a minimum euro value per dollar for the uncertain order while retaining the choice to let the contract expire?

The option protects the sale price if the order arrives. If it does not, there is no requirement to deliver dollars. The premium is a cost in either case.

Written practice · 8 marks

Independent exam problem

A company has €80,000 confirmed foreign-currency receipts and another €40,000 forecast. It sells €100,000 forward at 1.20 home per euro. Evaluate actual sales of €80,000 or €120,000 at spot 1.30.

  1. Part A · 3 marks

    Calculate proceeds when only confirmed sales arrive.

  2. Part B · 3 marks

    Calculate proceeds at the full forecast volume and identify the residual currency sensitivity.

  3. Part C · 2 marks

    Describe a policy protecting both the firm order and uncertain upside without a binding excess delivery.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked marking guide

Part A 3 marks

The company receives 120,000 home from the forward and must buy the missing €20,000 for 26,000 home, leaving 94,000 home net.

Part B 3 marks

Net proceeds equal 120,000 + 20,000 × 1.30 = 146,000 home. A one-cent increase in spot adds 200 home in this case but subtracts 200 in the low-volume case.

Part C 2 marks

Sell the confirmed €80,000 forward and consider put options on some or all forecast receipts. Options have an explicit premium. Revisit coverage as sales become contractually certain.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Would hedging 100% of an export forecast always minimize currency risk?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

A full hedge fits a fixed, dated receipt. For a sales forecast I would measure covariance of business cash with the hedge price and test a range of volumes. The variance-minimizing forward amount is covariance divided by spot variance. I would then check our actual objective. If the board prohibits binding delivery beyond contracted receipts, the mathematically lowest variance may still breach an operating constraint.

Interviewer’s follow-up

In two equally likely states, spot is 0.8 or 1.2 and unhedged home cash is 72 or 144. What forward amount equalizes outcomes, and what delivery issue arises?

Compare with the follow-up answer

The slope is (144−72)/(1.2−0.8) = 180 foreign units. Selling 180 forward removes the difference between these two modeled payoffs. Actual receipts are only 90 or 120 foreign units, so the contract requires a market purchase in both states. A firm can prefer less coverage.
