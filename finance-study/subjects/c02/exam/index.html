Execute a conversion and cover a future import

An importer needs 104 foreign units exactly one year from now. Spot is 2 home units per foreign unit at a single executable rate. Foreign deposits pay 4% and home borrowing costs 5%. A dealer will sell the required currency forward at 2.04 home per foreign. No other costs apply.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

Predict purchasing power and test an unhedged deposit

Spot is 3 home units per foreign unit. Home inflation is projected at 5% and foreign inflation at 2%. A home deposit pays 4% and a foreign deposit pays 6%. A saver has 300 home units. A future spot of 2.85 is possible.

An exporter revises its sales plan

A home-currency exporter has 90,000 foreign units of confirmed sales and 50,000 uncertain sales due on the same date. It also owes a foreign supplier 30,000 on that date. It agrees to sell 100,000 foreign forward at 1.10 home per foreign. Future spot may be 0.95 or 1.20.

Case 1 · 10 marks

Execute a conversion and cover a future import

Part A 4 marks

Construct the money-market hedge and calculate the implied forward.

Deposit 104/1.04 = 100 foreign today. Buy it for 200 home borrowed at 5%. The home repayment is 210. The implied price is 210/104 = 2.01923 home per foreign.

Part B 3 marks

Compare the dealer offer and express the cheaper method's saving in home currency.

The dealer would charge 104 × 2.04 = 212.16. The money-market hedge costs 210, saving 2.16 at maturity on identical delivered currency.

Part C 3 marks

Can the observed dealer price alone support a claim for riskless arbitrage? Identify a missing quote.

The 2.04 is the dealer's selling price. To buy foreign synthetically and sell it dearly would require a dealer bid above the synthetic cost and executable terms for all legs. A costly offer alone does not supply that sale.

Case 2 · 10 marks

Predict purchasing power and test an unhedged deposit

Part A 3 marks

Calculate the relative-PPP forecast and explain which inputs determine it.

The price-based forecast is 3 × 1.05/1.02 = 3.08824 home per foreign unit. It uses relative basket inflation and assumes the initial relative purchasing-power relationship is maintained.

Part B 3 marks

Compute the parity forward and distinguish its interpretation from PPP.

Covered parity gives 3 × 1.04/1.06 = 2.94340 home per foreign unit under matched borrowing and lending terms. It is a locked-price benchmark determined by interest rates. It need not equal the PPP projection.

Part C 4 marks

Compare the unhedged foreign and home payoffs at the stated possible spot. Identify the break-even spot.

Foreign saving grows from 100 to 106 foreign, worth 106 × 2.85 = 302.10 home. The home deposit pays 312, so the foreign route falls short by 9.90. Its break-even is 312/106 = 2.94340. Neither the forward nor PPP guarantees the future spot.

Case 3 · 10 marks

An exporter revises its sales plan

Part A 3 marks

Find the underlying and residual exposures at both sales volumes.

Confirmed net receipts are 90,000 − 30,000 = 60,000. If all sales arrive net receipts are 110,000. After the 100,000 forward, residual positions are short 40,000 or long 10,000 foreign units.

Part B 4 marks

Compute net home proceeds in all four combinations and explain the opposite sensitivity.

The fixed part is 110,000. At low volume, subtract 40,000 × spot, leaving 72,000 at 0.95 and 62,000 at 1.20. At high volume, add 10,000 × spot, giving 119,500 and 122,000. A stronger foreign currency hurts only when the firm must acquire missing units.

Part C 3 marks

Design a policy for a treasurer who must avoid binding commitments beyond confirmed receipts.

Sell forward up to the 60,000 net confirmed amount. Consider an option granting the right to sell additional foreign units, or add forwards as orders become firm. Budget the premium and monitor supplier and customer dates.

Case 4 · 10 marks

A parent values delayed foreign cash

Part A 3 marks

Value the immediate and later distributions in foreign currency and translate present value to home.

Distribute 40 now and 80 × 1.05 = 84 next year. Their foreign present value is 40 + 84/1.05 = 120, worth 240 home today.

Part B 4 marks

Show that selling the later receipt forward and discounting in home gives the same answer.

The parity forward is 2 × 1.03/1.05 = 1.96190. The 84 foreign produce 164.8 home at year end. Discounting at 3% yields 160 today. Add 80 received immediately for 240 in total.

Part C 3 marks

Suppose the later gross remittance suffers a 25% charge. Recalculate value and name an assumption that matters for the hedge.

Only 63 foreign units are distributable later. Foreign present value becomes 40 + 63/1.05 = 100, or 200 home. A binding sale of the old gross 84 would leave a 21-unit delivery shortfall if the remittance alone funds it.

Find incremental PV and the event probability at which the parent breaks even.
