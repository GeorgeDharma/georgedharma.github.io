Professional workshop · F03

Purchasing an industrial heat pump without breaching treasury’s reserve

A manufacturing board is deciding whether to install a heat pump. It can pay cash today or agree to three fixed vendor instalments. The treasury director wants a capital approval that separates the project economics, the price of the financing and the cash that would remain available for payroll. The board also wants to know what a fall in energy savings would do. Work in thousands of euros. The case deliberately holds credit, installation date and service quality equal between payment options so the discounting logic can be inspected.

Your role
Corporate treasury and capital budgeting analyst
What to submit
A capital expenditure approval sheet with a dated net present value calculation, a liability-matching funding schedule, a liquidity test and a recommendation for the capital committee.

From inputs to decision

01

Fix the timeline and valuation boundary

A comparison of supplier options can reverse if a payment is shifted a year or an operating receipt is treated as financing cash.

  1. Put project receipts at years one, two and three of 120, 120 and 140. The 140 includes the distinct residual sale of 20. Record supplier alternatives separately as −300 today or −102, −108 and −125 in their respective years.
  2. Preserve nominal units throughout. Estimated savings are incremental operating cash net of stated operating costs. The given factors are assumed appropriate for the specified project cash estimates in this model. In a live approval, risk treatment would need its own evidence.

The one-schedule operating asset can now be compared with two alternative funding promises.

02

Value the operating asset

Discount each dated receipt at its own maturity price, so the value reflects the actual timing of savings and of the resale.

  1. Year one contributes 120 × 0.96 = 115.20. Year two contributes 120 × 0.90 = 108.00. Year three operating savings contribute 120 × 0.84 = 100.80.
  2. Residual proceeds contribute 20 × 0.84 = 16.80. Operating savings have total present value 324.00, and the full asset including its residual has value 340.80.

The implied maximum upfront outlay at zero NPV on base assumptions is 340.80.

03

Compare supplier prices on a common date

Adding future instalments at face value would ignore the time when cash leaves the company and overstate the economic difference between offers.

  1. Present value of instalments is 102 × 0.96 + 108 × 0.90 + 125 × 0.84 = 97.92 + 97.20 + 105.00 = 300.12.
  2. Paying today costs 300.00, only 0.12 less in present terms, even though the instalment face total is 335. Base project NPV is 40.80 with cash payment and 40.68 with instalments.

Cash is the cheaper price by €120 at these quotes, a small difference beside forecast uncertainty.

04

Build a payment-matching portfolio

The vendor instalment plan postpones cash transfers but still creates obligations. Matching them shows the amount that must be reserved today for complete funding certainty under the case assumptions.

  1. Purchase year-one zeros with €102k face for 97.92, year-two zeros with €108k face for 97.20, and year-three zeros with €125k face for 105.00. At €100 per bond face these are 1,020, 1,080 and 1,250 bonds respectively.
  2. Total funding earmarked is 300.12. Each maturity supplies exactly the instalment due. A high average portfolio value without matched dates would leave reinvestment or sale risk if an individual bill fell due first.

If the board insists on full matching, the deferred option also ties up nearly the entire upfront price.

05

Test the actual reserve

A valuable project can still be hard to fund. Treat cash outside the earmarked portfolio as the immediately available payroll reserve and compare alternative funding policies explicitly.

  1. The cash purchase leaves 330 − 300 = 30 available, with headroom 5 over the required 25. An instalment commitment with its full zero portfolio leaves 330 − 300.12 = 29.88, with headroom 4.88.
  2. If treasury signs the instalment plan without earmarking funds, unrestricted day-zero cash is 330 because no vendor payment is due today. It then carries future payment and funding risk. This option must be labelled as an unhedged policy and cannot be treated as the economic equivalent of holding the matching zeros.

The €10k unrelated debit would leave 20 under cash purchase, a 5 shortfall, or 19.88 under full matching, a 5.12 shortfall.

06

Calculate the operating break-even

A board should see the savings needed for approval and how robust the result is to engineering estimates. Apply the same decline to comparable operating flows and keep the independently stated resale separate.

  1. For constant annual savings S, asset value equals S × (0.96 + 0.90 + 0.84) + 16.80 = 2.70S + 16.80. Covering a 300 cash price requires S = (300 − 16.80)/2.70 ≈ 104.8889.
  2. The base 120 can fall by about 12.59% before cash-purchase NPV reaches zero. A 15% reduction gives S = 102, asset PV 102 × 2.70 + 16.8 = 292.20 and cash-purchase NPV −7.80. The instalment price would give −7.92.

The engineering downside reverses both base approvals. Supplier negotiation worth 0.12 is secondary to the savings assumption.

07

Write an executable funding condition

A recommendation should specify the actual gate before payment, including what the board should do if the independent treasury stress materializes.

  1. Request measurement evidence for baseline energy use, the unit cost avoided and the maintenance assumptions embedded in the 120 annual savings. Confirm the installation and sale dates used in the valuation.
  2. Before choosing the cash offer, confirm the unrelated €10k debit or secure at least 5 of extra committed liquidity if the debit occurs. For full matching under instalments the required liquidity top-up is 5.12. Exclude that unrelated debit from project NPV while including it when testing cash headroom.

Approve the cash offer conditionally on evidence supporting the operating threshold and on preserving the day-zero reserve.

Completed document

Capital approval memorandum to the committee

Investment economics

Authorize the heat pump conditionally. On the stated base case it creates 40.80 of value at the cash price. Annual savings of approximately 104.89 are required for break-even if the residual sale of 20 is realized. A 15% miss produces a 7.80 loss in present-value terms.

Supplier negotiation

The fixed instalments cost 300.12 at quoted maturity-specific prices. The cash option saves only 0.12. Negotiate installation acceptance and reliable savings measurement before focusing on the small financing spread. These benefits and liabilities share a currency and stated timing in this comparison.

Liquidity control

Cash payment leaves 30 unrestricted versus a minimum of 25. An unrelated debit of 10 would breach the limit by 5. Request confirmation or committed liquidity before authorization. A fully matched instalment strategy ties up 300.12 and leaves slightly less available than paying cash today.

Residual exposures

Full payment matching can protect the instalment schedule if the bonds pay and are held to maturity. It cannot ensure operating savings or resale proceeds. Assign engineering the savings validation and treasury the reserve confirmation. Rework the decision if dates or cash terms move.

Run a different case

Use the changed facts in each task. Enter numbers in the units shown, using a decimal point or comma and no thousands separators.

Task 1

If the year-three zero price rises from 84 to 88 per €100 face, holding the other prices and vendor cash schedule fixed, what is the new present value of instalments?

See the full solution

Only the last factor changes. New PV is 97.92 + 97.20 + 125 × 0.88 = 305.12. This question isolates the liability price; a full project revaluation at the changed curve would also revisit year-three receipts.

Task 2

With the original curve, what minimum additional cash or committed day-zero funding is required after a €10k debit if the company buys the full matching bond portfolio and must retain 25?

See the full solution

Unrestricted cash is 330 − 10 − 300.12 = 19.88. The gap to 25 is 5.12. Any funding instrument must actually be available by the debit date to address that gap.

Task 3

If the year-three sale produces zero, what constant annual savings make the cash purchase break even on the original curve?

See the full solution

With no residual value, annual savings S must satisfy 2.70S = 300. Therefore S = 111.1111, about 111.11 each year, compared with 104.89 under the base residual assumption.

Task 4

Why can the board choose a slightly more expensive instalment arrangement while still making a reasoned treasury decision?

See the full solution

If immediate funding is constrained and the firm accepts or separately manages future payment risk, deferred cash may preserve present liquidity. The board should show its extra 0.12 economic price and the risk or cost of any later shortfall. A fully funded matching portfolio offers essentially no day-zero liquidity relief in this example.
