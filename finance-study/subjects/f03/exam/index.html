Case 1 of 4 · 10 marks

Comparison of borrowing quotes

A firm needs €4,200 for two years. Loan A charges 10% effective annually with two equal end-year instalments and no fees. Loan B quotes 10% nominal compounded semiannually. For the rate comparison assume annual payments. No other charge applies. A separate bridge line accrues simple actual/360 interest.

Case 2 of 4 · 10 marks

A grant fund’s future spending

A fund begins with €1,000. It gains 10% in year one, then receives a €200 contribution, and gains 5% in year two. A separate grant commitment promises €600 in today’s purchasing power one year from now. Assume deterministic inflation of 4% and a real discount rate of 5%.

Case 3 of 4 · 10 marks

Capital allocation with an opportunity cost

A company owns machine time that could be leased today for €30. Project M uses it and incurs a new €170 outlay, producing €242 in one year. Project N uses a different resource, costs €500 today and pays €575 in one year, leaving the machine free to lease. Choose one project or neither. Compare incremental returns relative to leasing the time and doing no project. The discount rate is 10%.

A small fixed-income trading book

Two comparable default-free instruments trade with no costs or short-sale restrictions. A one-year zero with €100 payoff costs €96. A two-year coupon note costs €103.20, paying €5 in one year and €105 in two years. A dealer also quotes €96 for a separate claim paying only €100 in two years.

Case 1 · 10 marks

Comparison of borrowing quotes

Part A 4 marks

Size loan A’s payment and separate year-one principal and interest.

The two-year annuity factor is 1/1.1 + 1/1.1² = 210/121. Instalment €4,200 × 121/210 = €2,420. Interest €420, principal €2,000 and balance €2,200. Award for factor, instalment, allocation and balance.

Part B 3 marks

Compare the two equivalent annual rates and explain which loan has the higher level annual payment if principal and maturity are identical.

B’s effective rate is 1.05² − 1 = 10.25%, compared with 10% for A. A higher rate reduces the payment annuity factor and requires a higher instalment for the same principal. Award for rate, ranking and explanation.

Part C 3 marks

If €4,200 is instead outstanding on the bridge for 45 days at 7.2% simple actual/360, find interest and the extra charge from a 30-basis-point rise.

Interest €4,200 × 0.072 × 45/360 = €37.80. A 30-basis-point increase is 0.003 and adds €4,200 × 0.003 × 45/360 = €1.575, or €1.58 after rounding.

Case 2 · 10 marks

A grant fund’s future spending

Part A 3 marks

Find the final balance and distinguish gains from contributions.

Year-one close is €1,100. With the deposit, year two begins at €1,300 and ends €1,365. Investment gains are €1,365 − €1,000 − €200 = €165. The original fund’s compounded two-year return is 1.10 × 1.05 − 1 = 15.5%. Award for the balances and separation.

Part B 4 marks

Value the grant by both nominal and real calculations.

The nominal payment is €624. Nominal growth factor 1.05 × 1.04 = 1.092, so €624/1.092 = €571.43. Real calculation €600/1.05 = €571.43. Award for cash, matched rate and both agreeing values.

Part C 3 marks

Another commitment pays €105 nominal at the ends of years two and three. At a 5% nominal annual discount rate, value it today and identify the date at which an ordinary annuity formula first gives value.

At year one the two-year annuity is €105/1.05 + €105/1.05² ≈ €195.24. Discount once more to today for approximately €185.94. Directly discounting payments from years two and three gives the same value.

Case 3 · 10 marks

Capital allocation with an opportunity cost

Part A 4 marks

Calculate incremental NPVs and choose the better feasible project.

M’s economic outlay is €170 + €30 foregone lease = €200. NPV €242/1.10 − €200 = €20. N leaves lease income intact relative to the baseline. Its NPV is €575/1.10 − €500 ≈ €22.73. Both are positive, and N adds about €2.73 more. Award one for opportunity cost, one for each NPV and one for ranking.

Part B 3 marks

Compare the one-year economic IRRs and explain the apparent conflict.

M yields €242/€200 − 1 = 21%. N yields €575/€500 − 1 = 15%. Different scale gives N the larger euro NPV despite its lower percentage return. Award for both rates and the explanation.

Part C 3 marks

If M’s €242 payment is nominal and inflation proves 10%, find its future purchasing power in today’s euros. Why would using the original nominal discount rate on this deflated amount be inconsistent?

Realized purchasing power is €242/1.10 = €220. A deflated amount is in base-date purchasing-power units and needs a matching real discount rate for valuation. Using the nominal rate on it would incorporate inflation twice.

Case 4 · 10 marks

A small fixed-income trading book

Part A 4 marks

Bootstrap the two discount factors and the implied one-year rate from year one to year two.

D₁ = €96/€100 = 0.96. Early note payment value €4.80. D₂ = (€103.20 − €4.80)/€105 ≈ 0.937142857. The implied forward rate is 0.96/0.937142857 − 1 ≈ 2.439%. Award for first factor, subtracting coupon, second factor and forward.

Part B 3 marks

Price the dealer’s claim and specify an executable hedge, giving quantities in the traded instruments.

The claim’s fair price is €93.7143. Buy 100/105 coupon notes to receive €100 at year two and €4.7619 at year one. Short 4.7619/100 = 0.047619 one-year zeros to cancel the year-one coupon. This replica costs about €93.7143. Award for value, notes and short zero.

Part C 3 marks

Give the complete trade and describe one change that could eliminate its profit.

Sell the dealer claim for €96 and buy the replica for €93.7143, collecting approximately €2.2857 now. All later net payments are zero. A sufficient bid–ask spread, missing ability to short the dealer claim, or a meaningful credit difference could remove this executable gain.

Additional exam cases

After the core paper, test the additional lectures below. Each case opens with an answer space and its own worked solution. Use the worked solutions to assess these cases separately from the 40-mark paper.

  1. F03.11 · How much did unevenly timed receipts actually earn?

    A lender pays out a £5,000 face-value loan and retains a £100 origination fee immediately. The only collections are £1,000 on day 90 and £4,300 on day 365. At an 8% hurdle, assess value and bracket the dated yield between two whole percentage rates.

  2. F03.12 · Value a pension paid only while someone is alive

    A different two-year plan uses a 90% first-year survival chance, then 95% conditional survival in year two. It pays £2,000 in arrears for each year survived. The discount rate is 4%. Value it, then find the increase from guaranteeing the second payment to the estate if needed.

  3. F03.13 · Can a reserve repay a bullet loan at maturity?

    A two-year £41,600 bullet charges 6% annual interest. Deposits occur at each year-end, and the originally assumed reserve yield is 8%. After the first deposit, the available second-year reserve rate falls to 2%. Calculate the revised final funding need.
