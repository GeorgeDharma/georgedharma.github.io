How much did unevenly timed receipts actually earn?

Write a dated valuation equation from an investor's signed cash ledger.

In this lecture

  1. Start with one person's cash movements
  2. Price a calendar rather than a row number
  3. Find and interpret the zero
  4. Rebuild the calculation
  5. Each exponent belongs to a cash date
  6. A short invoice and a later collection
  7. Independent exam problem
  8. Explain it in an interview
Theory and terminology

Dated internal rate of return

XIRR · money-weighted yield · irregular cash flows

What you should be able to explain

  • Write a dated valuation equation from an investor's signed cash ledger.
  • Bracket its annual yield and determine whether the investment clears a specified hurdle.

Start with one person's cash movements

An invoice purchaser records the price paid as negative and collections as positive. An arrangement fee paid from the investor's pocket on the purchase day also belongs in that day's outlay. For a lender retaining a fee from the borrower, the initial cash advanced can instead be smaller than the stated principal. Draw one consistent viewpoint before choosing signs.

Combine amounts on the same date. Use settlement or payment dates matching the question, keep one currency and sort dates so the earliest investment is the valuation date. An annual effective rate multiplies wealth over a full year by one plus that rate. Fractional exponents apply the same compound-growth convention to part of a year.

Price a calendar rather than a row number

Under the actual-days/365 convention used here, day 100 receives exponent 100/365, even when the next spreadsheet row represents day 365. A period-based IRR treats adjacent rows as equally spaced. Its output has units of return per row interval and has no defensible annual interpretation when the rows have unequal lengths.

A leap day counts as an actual day under this specified fixed denominator. A contract specifying another convention may give a different answer. Spreadsheet XNPV uses calendar dates on this basis. For actual currencies and dates, a row-based expression is amount divided by (1 + annual rate) raised to ((cash date − start date)/365).

Find and interpret the zero

At a required return the dated net present value tells whether the cash flows offer a surplus in today's money. A positive number means the quoted investment costs less than those discounted receipts. To get the dated internal yield, try two rates at which the present value changes sign and narrow the interval.

With one initial outflow followed only by positive receipts at later dates, present value falls as the rate rises above −100%. A root, if bracketed, is unique. If later signs switch repeatedly, this monotonicity argument fails. Keep the dated NPV at the decision-maker's hurdle in view even when a spreadsheet finds a root.

Method

Build the result

V(r) = Σ Cᵢ/(1 + r)^[(dᵢ − d₀)/365]. The dated yield solves V(r) = 0.

Cᵢ is signed cash in currency on calendar day dᵢ, d₀ is the earliest date, r is an annual effective decimal rate greater than −1, and V is net present value in the same currency.

  1. Put the initial net investment at day zero, so its discount exponent is zero.
  2. Discount each future cash flow by its actual day interval and sum with the initial outlay for a trial rate.
  3. For a conventional sign pattern, raise the trial rate when V is positive and lower it when V is negative. Retain and narrow a sign-changing bracket.
Worked case

A short invoice and a later collection

An investor pays €980 for a receivable and a €20 fee on day 0. The desk collects €330 on day 100 and €760 on day 365. It requires a 10% annual effective return and uses actual days/365.

Working

  1. The initial net cash movement is −€1,000. The later time weights are 100/365 and 1.
  2. At 10%, V = −1,000 + 330/(1.10)^(100/365) + 760/1.10 = €12.40.
  3. At 11% V is €5.38. At 12% it is −€1.52. The root lies between those rates.
  4. Repeating the calculation gives about 11.7786% a year. Treating the collections as years one and two instead would give about 5.23% per such invented period.

Decision and interpretation

The investment clears the stated hurdle by €12.40 of present value if the collections occur as described. A disputed or delayed invoice needs a revised cash forecast.

Quick check
A £120 payment is received 73 days after the start date. Under a fixed actual-days/365 effective-rate calculation, which exponent discounts it?

The exponent is 73/365 = 0.20 of an annual period. The row position does not determine the time.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A lender pays out a £5,000 face-value loan and retains a £100 origination fee immediately. The only collections are £1,000 on day 90 and £4,300 on day 365. At an 8% hurdle, assess value and bracket the dated yield between two whole percentage rates.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Use −£4,900 on day zero and positive collections at 90/365 and one year.
  2. At 8%, V = −4,900 + 1,000/(1.08)^(90/365) + 4,300/1.08 = £62.68. This is positive at the hurdle.
  3. At 9% the NPV is still positive and at 10% it is −£14.14. The root between them is about 9.63% annually.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

How do you compare a payment received after 100 days with one received after a full year?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Discount each by its own fraction of a year, then add both present values. The annual rate that makes them equal to the cash invested is the dated yield under that day-count convention.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the first £1,000 arrived on day 180 and the final collection stayed unchanged, would the yield rise or fall?

Compare with the follow-up answer

At the old positive yield the £1,000 would now have a lower present value. The balance would become negative, requiring a lower yield to bring it back to zero.
