Value a pension paid only while someone is alive

Convert conditional annual survival probabilities into payment probabilities.

Theory and terminology

Actuarial present value

survival probabilities · life annuity · term insurance

What you should be able to explain

  • Convert conditional annual survival probabilities into payment probabilities.
  • Value a temporary pension and death benefit and explain the limits of an expected-value price.

Translate a contract into events on dates

A temporary life annuity in arrears pays at each year-end only if the member is alive then. A two-year term life policy pays on death during either of two policy years. If benefits are paid at the end of the year of death, the first policy year's death benefit shares a date with the first pension payment but is triggered by the opposite event.

A probability quoted for someone alive at the start of next year is conditional. Multiply successive conditional probabilities to determine the chance, today, of being alive at a later date. The chance of dying in the second year counts only those reaching the start of that year. Mixing conditional probabilities with unconditional ones can overstate benefits.

From dated probabilities to value

For each date take the amount paid if the event occurs and multiply it by the probability of that event. Discount the expected amount to today, then sum across dates. A pension can pay more than once to the same member, so its payment probabilities across years need not sum to one. A single death can trigger at most one death payment.

Payments in advance change timing. A first payment due immediately to a member known to be alive has probability one and no discount. A contract guaranteeing payments to an estate for the first two years instead prices those guaranteed payments with probability one even if the member dies.

What an expected present value leaves out

The calculation below is a net actuarial value with a fixed discount rate and a stipulated mortality model. It values an average over many possible lifetimes. An individual member will receive specific payments or none. A provider also needs to fund expenses, capital and adverse experience when setting a commercial premium.

Probabilities should fit the covered population, age and selection of members. Healthy people disproportionately buying income for life can make a general population table inappropriate. For long contracts, stronger-than-assumed survival raises expected pension cost while reducing the near-term cost of death benefits.

Method

Build the result

EPV = Σ bₜ P(Eₜ)/(1 + i)^t. S₂ = p₁p₂. P(death in year 2) = p₁(1 − p₂).

EPV is expected present value in currency, bₜ is the conditional benefit paid at year t, and Eₜ is its triggering event. i is the annual effective discount rate. p₁ and p₂ are one-year survival probabilities conditional on being alive at each year's start. S₂ is survival through two years.

  1. Mark the benefit dates and specify who receives money if the member has died.
  2. Convert conditional survival assumptions into the probability of each dated payment event.
  3. Multiply the benefit by each event probability, apply its own discount factor and add. Test a different survival assumption separately from a different interest rate.
Worked case

A two-year retirement plan and a separate death policy

An illustrative retiree has a 96% chance of surviving year one and, if alive then, a 90% chance of surviving year two. A temporary pension pays €1,000 at each year-end while alive. A separate policy pays €5,000 at year-end on death during that year. Use 5% annually and ignore expenses.

Working

  1. Survival probabilities at the payment dates are 0.96 and 0.96 × 0.90 = 0.864.
  2. The pension's expected cash is €960 and €864. Present values are €914.29 and €783.67, totaling €1,697.96.
  3. Death probabilities are 0.04 in year one and 0.96 × 0.10 = 0.096 in year two. Their sum is 0.136, equal to 1 − 0.864.
  4. The death policy's expected cash is €200 and €480. Present values are €190.48 and €435.37, totaling €625.85.

Decision and interpretation

The net values price different triggers using the same assumptions. Actual benefit pricing also depends on which members enroll, timing conventions and the provider's costs.

Quick check
A member survives year one with probability 0.8 and year two conditional on reaching it with probability 0.9. What is today's probability of a payment triggered only by death in year two?

The member must survive the first year and then die in year two. Multiply 0.8 × 0.1.

Written practice

Independent exam problem

A different two-year plan uses a 90% first-year survival chance, then 95% conditional survival in year two. It pays £2,000 in arrears for each year survived. The discount rate is 4%. Value it, then find the increase from guaranteeing the second payment to the estate if needed.

Saved on this device as you type.
Reveal the worked solution
  1. Survival probabilities at year ends are 0.90 and 0.90 × 0.95 = 0.855. Expected amounts are £1,800 and £1,710.
  2. The pension value is 1,800/1.04 + 1,710/1.04² = £3,311.76.
  3. Guaranteeing only the second payment adds the expected shortfall, (2,000 − 1,710)/1.04² = £268.12. The revised value is £3,579.88.

Interview practice

Explain it aloud

Why does adding a guarantee to pay the estate if a pension member dies increase the value of a two-year pension?

Saved on this device as you type.
Compare with a spoken answer

Without the guarantee, each payment is weighted by the chance that the member lives to its due date. Paying the estate when the member has died makes that payment certain, so its expected present value increases.

Interviewer’s follow-up

If the provider pays immediately on death instead of at the year-end, can the two annual death probabilities give an exact dated value?

Compare with the follow-up answer

They identify the chance of a death during each year, but not its time within that year. Supply a within-year mortality assumption or more granular data. Earlier payments have a larger present value at a positive rate.
