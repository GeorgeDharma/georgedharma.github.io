Chile and Brazil · International case

Sovereign finance

A government’s debt ratio compares debt with annual national output. The primary deficit excludes interest payments. Foreign-currency debt adds a valuation channel when domestic currency weakens. The numbers here are for two hypothetical contracts in a regional comparison.

Case data

InputSimulated value
Starting debt for each fictional economy60% of GDP
Nominal interest rate and GDP growth8% and 5%
Primary deficit1% of current GDP
FX debt shares for simulated Chile and Brazil scenarios40% and 10% respectively

All case figures are simulated for teaching.

Make the decision

Find next year’s debt ratio before a currency shock. Then estimate the first-order revaluation on opening debt if the domestic price of foreign currency rises by 10%.

Saved on this device as you type.
Show the worked solution

Before revaluation each debt ratio is 60% × 1.08 / 1.05 + 1% = 62.71%. On opening GDP, the foreign-currency revaluations are approximately 60% × 40% × 10% = 2.4 points and 60% × 10% × 10% = 0.6 points. For the exact closing ratio convert every added amount to the same GDP denominator and model repayments. The maturity calendar determines near-term refinancing needs.

Extend the analysis

Apply a common export-price shock to different debt structures. Does the binding constraint appear in next year’s refinancing or in the longer debt path?

What to produce

Dated debt ratios, maturity assumptions, currency composition and two explicit shock channels.
