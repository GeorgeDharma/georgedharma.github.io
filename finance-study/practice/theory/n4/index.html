Mathematical finance · Theory note

Why diversification changes variance

Portfolio profits add across holdings. Their variances generally do not, because losses may arrive at the same time or offset one another.

The model

Var(portfolio) = w² Var(A) + (1 − w)² Var(B) + 2w(1 − w) Cov(A,B)

w is the share invested in asset A. The rest is in B. Covariance is the average product of the two returns’ departures from their respective means.

Derivation

1. Form the return

Portfolio return is w × return A + (1 − w) × return B. This uses starting wealth weights and a common measuring currency.

2. Subtract the mean

The portfolio’s deviation from its mean is the same weighted combination of each asset’s deviation.

3. Square and average

Expanding a squared sum creates two squared terms and a cross-product. The expected cross-product is covariance, producing the final term in the formula.

4. Connect to asset pricing

Market beta equals covariance with the market divided by market variance. Under CAPM, expected excess return equals beta times the expected market excess return.

Worked check

Two equal-weight holdings with 20% volatility and zero correlation have 14.14% portfolio volatility. At correlation one they have 20%. The same individual volatilities produce different portfolio outcomes.

When the result changes

Estimated correlations can change in a crisis. CAPM also requires assumptions about investors, tradability and the market portfolio. A historical beta is estimated with error.
