Mathematical finance · Theory note

Present value, merger surplus and the maximum bid

Paying a seller moves wealth between owners. It creates economic value only if the combination changes the future cash flows or the risk attached to them.

The model

Buyer gain = net synergy − (price paid − standalone target value)

Value every term at the same date, in the same currency and on the same claim basis. Net synergy includes the present value of incremental operating benefits after integration costs.

Derivation

1. Start separately

Let B and T be standalone equity values of buyer and target. If incremental cash flow in a future year is 11 and its suitable one-year discount rate is 10%, that cash is worth 11/1.10 = 10 today.

2. Combine the assets

With net synergy S, combined equity value before paying sellers is B + T + S. Assume an all-cash acquisition and consistent treatment of cash and debt.

3. Allocate the value

If sellers receive P in cash, continuing buyer owners are left with B + T + S − P. Subtract their original B and rearrange. Sellers gain P − T. Both gains sum to S.

4. Find the threshold

Set buyer gain equal to zero. The maximum price is T + S at these assumptions. A buyer requiring a margin for execution risk can set a lower ceiling.

Worked check

Suppose T = 80, net synergy = 18 and the price is 95. Sellers gain 15 and buyer owners gain 3. If the buyer inadvertently counts the same savings twice, both its estimated surplus and its price ceiling are overstated.

When the result changes

If the offer is a fixed number of shares, P changes with the combined firm’s value. The share-exchange seminar shows how to solve that allocation directly.
