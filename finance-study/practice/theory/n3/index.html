Mathematical finance · Theory note

How loan losses enter the promised interest rate

The borrower promises a rate on the full loan. The bank collects that promised interest only in the states where it is paid.

The model

(1 − p)(1 + r) + pR = 1 + k

Normalize the loan principal to one. Here p is the one-period default probability, r the promised rate, R the amount recovered per unit in default at maturity, and k the all-in funding and operating requirement.

Derivation

1. Write the two states

If the borrower performs, the lender gets 1 + r. In default it gets R and no interest. Both payoffs are expressed at the same maturity date.

2. Calculate the mean

Weight each payoff by its probability. To break even under these assumptions, expected proceeds must equal 1 + k.

3. Solve for r

Rearranging gives r = (1 + k − pR)/(1 − p) − 1. Expected principal loss is p × (1 − R), which is only part of the pricing calculation.

4. Let behaviour change

When raising r changes the applicant mix or the projects borrowers choose, p also changes. Expected proceeds can then decline as the promised rate rises.

Worked check

With p = 2%, R = 40% and k = 4%, r ≈ 5.306%. If p = 0 the model reduces to r = k. In both cases, the usable duration of capital and timing of recoveries must match the assumed period.

When the result changes

This is a one-period teaching model. Economic capital, taxes, fees, drawn exposure, recovery delays and borrower selection require extensions. Expected loss, a break-even price and required capital answer different questions.
