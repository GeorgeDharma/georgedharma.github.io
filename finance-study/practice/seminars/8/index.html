Lending and Credit Analysis · Case seminar

What changes when the borrower changes?

Underwriting a company, a household and a private-bank client all involves repayment and loss. The relevant evidence differs. Start by naming the expected source of repayment, then choose the measure that tests it.

How to approach the case

Household mortgage

A household earns €4,000 gross per month and would owe €1,100 on a mortgage plus €400 on other debts. Monthly debt-to-income is 1,500/4,000 = 37.5%. Disposable income, variability of earnings and the applicable rules also matter.

Property collateral

A €160,000 loan secured on a €200,000 home starts at 80% loan-to-value. A 15% house-price fall leaves a value of €170,000 and raises the ratio to 94.12%. The instalment and the borrower’s income need not change at the same time.

Securities-backed line

A portfolio worth €100,000 is eligible at a 60% advance rate. The borrower has drawn €50,000. If securities fall 25%, allowable borrowing falls from €60,000 to €45,000, creating a €5,000 deficiency under this fictional contract.

A mortgage’s loan-to-value ratio asks how much asset value backs the debt. A household debt-service ratio asks what share of income is committed to payments. A portfolio advance rate answers how much can be borrowed against today’s eligible collateral.

Work the case

How could the private-bank client remedy the €5,000 deficiency if the advance rate remains 60%? Why would a credit committee still examine income or other liquidity?

Paying down €5,000 restores compliance. Posting at least €5,000/0.60 = €8,333.34 of additional eligible securities also suffices after rounding up to cents. The borrower must be able to act before further losses or a forced sale. Contractual thresholds are case inputs.
