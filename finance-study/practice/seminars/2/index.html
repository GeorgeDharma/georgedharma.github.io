Mergers and Acquisitions · Case seminar

How can earnings rise when a buyer loses value?

A bidder trading at a high earnings multiple issues shares to buy a lower-multiple target. Reported earnings per share rise mechanically. The buyer still has to ask how much of the combined business it gave away.

How to approach the case

Before the offer

The bidder has 10m shares worth €20 each and annual profit of €10m. The target has standalone equity value of €60m and annual profit of €6m. Assume no synergies, integration costs, financing changes or tax changes.

The contract

The buyer issues 3.5m new shares. At the old bidder price they have a headline value of €70m. With a fixed number of shares, the target’s final consideration changes when the bidder’s share price changes.

Two measurements

Earnings per share uses an accounting numerator and share count. Shareholder value depends on the economic value of the business and the fraction of that value the old owners retain.

Case figures

MeasureCalculationResult
Combined earnings per share€16m / 13.5m shares€1.1852
Post-deal fair price€260m / 13.5m shares€19.2593
Old bidder shareholders10m × €19.2593€192.59m
Target shareholders3.5m × €19.2593€67.41m

The bidder’s old earnings per share was €1. Earnings per share rises about 18.52%, although the old shareholders’ stake falls from €200m to €192.59m. With no gains from combining the firms, the sellers’ €7.41m gain equals the buyers’ loss.

Work the case

How many new shares can be issued before existing bidder shareholders give up value under these assumptions? How would a fixed €70m cash offer change the seller’s exposure?

Show the solution and interpretation

Issuing 3m shares leaves 13m shares worth €20 each. Old owners retain €200m and target owners receive €60m. A fixed cash offer gives the seller €70m if completed and transfers the €10m overpayment to existing buyer owners, ignoring financing effects.
