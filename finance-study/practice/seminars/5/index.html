Lending and Credit Analysis · Case seminar

Why can a higher interest rate earn a bank less?

A bank cannot observe every borrower’s type. Changing its rate changes which applicants accept. This is the core intuition of equilibrium credit rationing and is distinct from the question of whether one already-approved borrower can afford a rate increase.

How to approach the case

Two borrower types

Each loan advances €100 for one year. Safe borrowers repay with certainty. Risky borrowers succeed 80% of the time and pay zero in default. At a 10% rate, the applicant pool is 80% safe and 20% risky.

Adverse selection

At 20%, suppose safe borrowers choose their outside option and only risky borrowers accept. The bank has quoted a higher amount due, but the probability of collecting it has fallen.

Moral hazard

There is a second mechanism even with a fixed pool. After funding, limited liability can make a riskier project attractive to equity owners. Covenants, monitoring and borrower equity can affect that choice.

At 10%, expected receipt is 0.80 × €110 + 0.20 × (0.80 × €110) = €105.60. At 20% it is 0.80 × €120 = €96. If more people apply at the first rate than the bank can fund, charging more need not clear the market profitably.

Work the case

Suppose the risk mix stayed at 80% safe and 20% risky at both prices. What would the bank collect at 20%? What observation would distinguish a change in the applicant pool from a change in borrower behaviour after funding?
