Lending and Credit Analysis · Case seminar

How large a loan can this exporter carry?

A lender is considering a new euro facility for an exporter. All amounts are annual and in millions of euros. The lending policy in this fictional case requires cash available for debt service to be at least 1.25 times scheduled interest and principal.

How to approach the case

Build available cash

Cash operating earnings are €3.20m before tax and reinvestment. Deduct €0.40m cash tax, €0.50m maintenance investment and €0.50m additional operating working capital. Available cash is €1.80m under the case definition.

Separate two tests

Existing service is €1.20m, and the requested facility adds €0.30m. Cash may be enough to pay the instalment yet leave insufficient margin to satisfy the required coverage ratio.

Examine security independently

Eligible receivables are €1.50m at an 80% advance rate and eligible inventory €0.80m at 50%. The borrowing base is €1.60m. After €1m already drawn, €0.60m remains available under that limit.

Maximum total service consistent with the base-case covenant is €1.80m/1.25 = €1.44m. This leaves €0.24m for new annual service. That is payment capacity. Translating it into a loan principal still requires a tenor, amortization pattern and interest rate.

Work the case

Would you approve the requested structure? Could the €0.60m borrowing-base availability alone justify it? Give one redesign and one early-warning measure.

Show the solution and interpretation

The proposal breaches the policy even in the base case. A smaller or differently amortized facility can reduce service, but downside capacity still needs a remedy. Consider borrower equity, a verified operating improvement or a lower amount. Monitor collections and covenant headroom.
