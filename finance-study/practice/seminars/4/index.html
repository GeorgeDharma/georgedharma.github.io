Mergers and Acquisitions · Case seminar

An overseas bid with a fixed euro budget

A French buyer agrees a headline price of IDR 1.8 trillion for an Indonesian target. Its euro financing commitment is €105m, and closing fees use €3m. Suppose all funds are needed on closing day. The currency in the signed offer determines the buyer’s funding exposure.

How to approach the case

Translate the offer

The quote is rupiah per euro. Divide the rupiah amount by that quote to get euros. If the euro weakens, the fixed rupiah price consumes more of the euro facility.

Model the timing

Competition approval or a shareholder vote can delay closing. A forward dated to the initial closing day may need to be rolled. If the deal fails, the hedge may survive after the acquisition obligation disappears.

Revisit economic value

A larger euro cash payment can also coincide with a change in the euro value of target operations. Revalue the target’s currency cash flows separately before declaring that a funding gap equals a valuation loss.

The buyer can spend at most €102m on the offer after fees. Its break-even exchange rate is IDR 1.8 trillion / €102m = about 17,647.06 rupiah per euro. Lower quotes create a cash deficit under the assumed committed financing.

Work the case

The bid is strategically attractive, but the regulator can delay completion by three months. Which items belong in the board’s financing schedule and which belong in the valuation model?

Show the solution and interpretation

The funding schedule needs payment currency, committed amounts, fees, hedge dates, extension or cancellation costs and liquidity headroom. Valuation needs currency-consistent target cash flows, synergy, integration cost and completion scenarios. Reconcile shared inputs to avoid counting the same risk twice.
