Uruguay · October 2022

Uruguay’s bond and the price of a climate target

Calculate how environmental performance can change a sovereign coupon and read the denominator of an emissions target.

What the record shows

In October 2022 Uruguay issued a US-dollar bond due in 2034. Its initial annual coupon was 5.75%. Under the issue terms, adjustments starting in 2027 can take it as high as 6.05% if both targets are missed, or as low as 5.45% if both are outperformed. The two indicators concern greenhouse gas emissions intensity and native forest area. These were the terms announced at issuance.

  • Uruguay Ministry of Economy and Finance, February 2023 sovereign debt report
  • Uruguay, sovereign sustainability-linked bond framework

Work through the figures

No net adjustment5.75%$575,000
Both indicators trigger maximum step-down5.45%$545,000

The holding and probabilities in this exercise are invented. The table illustrates the original coupon scenarios without predicting which outcome occurs.

One basis point is 0.01 percentage point. On $10m face value, 30 basis points change a full year’s coupon by $30,000. The climate test also needs a denominator. In simplified units, if emissions increase from 100 to 108 while real output rises from 100 to 120, emissions per unit of output fall from 1 to 0.9. Intensity falls 10% while absolute emissions rise 8%. Forest area is a separate test.

Test your reasoning

For an illustrative full year after adjustment, assign a 25% chance to the highest coupon, 60% to no adjustment and 15% to the lowest. Calculate the expected coupon in dollars. What other information would you need to value the entire bond?

Compare your answer

The weighted coupon is 0.25 × $605,000 + 0.60 × $575,000 + 0.15 × $545,000 = $578,000. The $3,000 increase above the base case comes from giving the higher coupon more probability than the lower coupon. The market price also depends on when all coupons and principal arrive, credit risk, market discount rates and the prices investors assign to the different states. One expected coupon cannot price the entire bond.
