Euro area · 2014 and 2022

The ECB and negative interest rates

Use dated policy decisions to understand how a negative rate can coexist with a positive bond price.

What the record shows

The ECB set its overnight deposit facility rate at −0.10% effective June 11, 2014. In July 2022 it raised its three key rates by 50 basis points, taking the deposit facility rate to 0.00% effective July 27. These observations are eight years apart. They are not the before-and-after rates of a single policy move.

  • ECB, introduction of a negative deposit facility rate, June 2014
  • ECB, monetary policy decisions, July 2022

Invented positions and yields illustrate two instruments. An overnight policy rate is not the one-year market yield.

A basis point is 0.01 percentage point. A change of 50 basis points adds 0.50 percentage point to a quoted annual rate. A negative yield means an investor who pays the quoted price and receives the promised cash can earn a negative holding-period return. The price itself remains positive. An overnight bank deposit and a one-year bond differ in maturity, market risk, and how interest is quoted.

Test your reasoning

A zero-coupon bond matures at €1,000 and sells for €1,004. Find its one-year effective yield. Would a 50-basis-point policy increase necessarily move its own yield by exactly that amount?

Compare your answer

The effective yield is 1,000 / 1,004 − 1 = −0.3984%, or about −39.84 basis points. Its price can change before maturity. Its one-year yield depends on the expected path of short rates, risk and demand for that instrument. A perfectly anticipated policy decision may add little new information about that path.
