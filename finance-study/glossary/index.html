How to read financial formulas

114 terms

A

Accretion and dilution
In merger analysis, a rise or fall in the buyer’s earnings per share after the transaction. Shareholder value is a separate calculation.
Adverse selection
A contract can attract a different mix of applicants when people know more about their own quality or risk than the other side knows.
Agency problem
People acting on behalf of someone else can have different goals and better information about their own decisions.
Asset
Something owned or controlled that can provide future value, such as cash, an invoice owed to you or equipment.
Asset pricing
Studying how risk, time and market conditions relate to the prices and expected returns of investments.

B

Backtest
A historical simulation of decisions made under an explicit rule, using information available at each date.
Balance of payments
A country’s record of economic transactions between its residents and the rest of the world during a period.
Basis point
One hundredth of a percentage point. A rise from 4.00% to 4.01% is one basis point.
Benchmark
A reference chosen in advance for comparison, such as a market index or an equal-weight portfolio.
Bid-offer spread
The gap between the highest quoted buying price and the lowest quoted selling price.
Blended finance
Combining public or philanthropic funding with commercial funding to support a development objective.
Bond
A loan that can be bought and sold. Its terms say when the borrower must pay interest and repay the amount borrowed.
Bookbuilding
Collecting indications of investor demand at proposed prices before deciding the terms and allocations of a securities offering.
Bookrunner
The bank responsible for managing an offering’s order book and coordinating the pricing and allocation process.
Borrowing base
A lending limit calculated from eligible collateral after applying agreed advance rates and deductions.

C

Calibration
Choosing model parameters so that the model reproduces specified market observations as closely as possible.
Capital asset pricing model (CAPM)
A model that relates an asset’s expected return above the risk-free rate to its exposure to the wider market. That exposure is called beta.
Cash buffer
Money kept available to meet upcoming bills or unexpected payments.
Cash flow
Money actually entering or leaving an account over a period.
Collateral
Cash or assets pledged to protect the other party if an obligation is missed.
Compound interest
Interest earned on earlier interest as well as the starting amount.
Constant currency
Recalculating results at a common set of exchange rates to examine operating changes across periods.
Convexity
Curvature. In fixed income it describes how a bond’s price sensitivity to yield changes as the yield moves.
Correlation
A measure of how closely two series move together in a linear pattern.
Counterfactual
What would probably have happened without the event, decision or policy being studied.
Counterparty
The other party to a financial contract or trade.
Coupon
The interest payment specified in a bond’s terms.
Covenant
A promise or condition in a financing agreement, such as maintaining an agreed level of cash.
Covered interest parity
A relationship connecting cash exchange rates, forward rates and interest rates in two currencies under matched, idealized funding terms.
Credit rationing
A lender limits the amount available even when some applicants would accept the quoted terms or offer to pay more.
Credit spread
Extra yield over a chosen reference yield. Credit risk, liquidity and market conditions can all affect it.
Currency quote
The number of units of one named currency exchanged for one unit of another. The order determines whether to multiply or divide.

D

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
Cash available to meet scheduled debt payments divided by the principal and interest due for the period.
Derivative
A contract whose value depends on something else, such as a currency, an interest rate or a share price.
Discount factor
The price today of one unit of currency payable at a particular future date under specified risk assumptions.
Discount rate
Rate used to express a future payment in today’s money, reflecting time and the relevant risk.
Discounted cash flow (DCF)
A valuation method that brings a series of future cash flows back to a common date.
Diversification
Combining exposures that do not always move together to reduce dependence on a single outcome.
Dividend
A payment a company makes to shareholders from available resources.
Duration
A measure used to estimate how strongly a bond price responds to a small interest-rate change.
DV01
Change in value associated with a one-basis-point shift in a specified rate or curve. Document the sign convention.

E

EBITDA
Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Check how the seller calculates and adjusts this measure.
Eigenvector
A direction that a linear transformation stretches or shrinks without changing its direction. In finance it can describe common movements in several assets.
Enterprise value
Value of the business operations attributable to its capital providers. A bridge adjusts it to the amount due to shareholders.
Enterprise-to-equity bridge
A schedule showing how the value of business operations becomes the amount attributable to common shareholders.
Equity
The owners’ claim after liabilities are deducted from assets. It also means ownership shares.
Exchange-rate regime
How authorities manage their currency, ranging from a fixed link to a market-driven rate.
Expected credit loss
For a simple single-period example, default probability multiplied by exposure and the share lost if default occurs.
Expected shortfall
The average loss in a specified worst fraction of outcomes.
Expected utility
The probability-weighted average satisfaction from possible outcomes. It permits comparing choices when money has different value in different states.
Exposure at default (EAD)
Amount at risk when a borrower defaults. It can include draws on a previously unused facility.

F

Financial due diligence
Checking a business’s earnings, cash needs and debt before a transaction.
Foreign exchange (FX)
The conversion of one currency into another. Always state how the rate is quoted.
Forward
A contract to exchange an agreed amount at a set price on a future date.
FP&A
Financial planning and analysis. The team builds budgets and forecasts and explains business performance.
Free float
The shares considered available for public trading under the venue or index provider’s definition.

G

Goodwill
In a simple purchase of an entire company, the part of the purchase price above the measured value of identifiable net assets.
Greeks
Measures of how an option’s value changes with an input. Delta measures its sensitivity to the underlying price.
Greenshoe
An overallotment option that allows underwriters to buy additional shares on agreed terms to cover an overallocation.

H

Hedge
A position or contract intended to reduce an identified exposure, such as the cost of paying in a foreign currency.

I

IFRS and US GAAP
Two sets of financial reporting rules. IFRS stands for International Financial Reporting Standards. US GAAP means US Generally Accepted Accounting Principles.
Initial public offering (IPO)
A company’s first public offering of shares. The shares may be newly issued, sold by existing holders, or both.
Interest rate
The price of borrowing money or the return for lending it, quoted as a percentage over a stated period.
IPO underpricing
In empirical IPO studies, a positive return from the offer price to the first trading close. The meaning depends on the price benchmark.

L

Leveraged buyout (LBO)
A purchase funded partly with debt, usually with the acquired business’s cash helping repay it.
Liability
Something owed to another party, such as a loan or an unpaid supplier bill.
Liquidity
Access to cash when payments fall due. For a security, it also describes how easily it can be traded at a reasonable price.
Look-ahead bias
Accidental use of information in a test before a decision-maker could have known it.
Loss given default (LGD)
The fraction of exposure lost if a borrower defaults, after the recoveries included in the chosen definition.

M

Margin
In business accounts, profit expressed relative to sales. In derivatives trading, margin is collateral required to support a position.
Market microstructure
How orders, trading venues and dealer behaviour affect the prices and costs of a trade.
Martingale
A process whose expected future value, given current information, equals its current value under the specified probability measure.
Monte Carlo simulation
Estimating an outcome by generating many possible paths and summarizing the results.
Moral hazard
A contract can change behaviour after it is signed when the other party cannot fully observe or control those actions.

N

Net present value (NPV)
Today’s value of incoming cash minus today’s value of outgoing cash.
New issue concession
The extra yield a new bond offers compared with the estimated fair yield on comparable outstanding debt.
No-arbitrage
A pricing condition that rules out a feasible trade producing a certain gain for no net investment and with no risk of loss.

O

Option
A contract giving the buyer a right to buy or sell on stated terms. The buyer pays a premium for the right.
Overfitting
Learning patterns peculiar to one sample that fail to hold up on new data.

P

Payable
Money a business owes a supplier for goods or services already received.
Percentage point
The difference between two percentages. A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises one percentage point.
Perpetuity
A stream of payments assumed to continue indefinitely under the model.
Present value (PV)
An amount at a future date expressed in today’s money using a rate suited to its timing and risk.
Primary balance
Government revenue minus non-interest spending under the sign convention used in this course.
Primary repayment source
The cash a lender expects will repay a loan in the normal course, such as operating receipts or household income.
Primary shares
New shares issued by the company. Their sale brings money into the company and increases the outstanding share count.
Principal
The amount borrowed or invested before interest is added.
Probability of default (PD)
The chance that the borrower meets the specified default definition over the stated horizon.
Profit
Revenue left after the expenses recognized for the period.
Purchasing power parity
A comparison of what a common basket costs in different currencies. It offers a reference point for comparing price levels.

R

Receivable
Money a customer owes for a sale already made.
Regression
A way of estimating how an outcome varies with one or more observed inputs.
Replicating portfolio
A combination of traded claims that delivers the same future cash flows as a contract in every state covered by the model.
Revenue
Amount earned by selling goods or services. The customer may pay later.
Rights issue
An offer allowing current shareholders to subscribe for new shares in proportion to an existing holding, under the stated terms.
Risk factor
A common source of variation in returns, such as market movements or a change in interest rates.
Risk premium
Extra expected return for bearing risk, or the amount a risk-averse person would give up to replace a gamble with certainty, depending on context.
Risk-neutral valuation
A way of pricing by taking a discounted expectation under a probability model consistent with traded prices.
Rule of priority
The assumed order in which claims receive available proceeds. In a real case, contract terms and local law matter.

S

Secondary shares
Existing shares sold by their holder. The sale transfers ownership and the seller receives the proceeds.
Sensitivity analysis
Varying an assumption to see how the answer or decision changes.
Settlement
The final transfer of cash or securities needed to complete a transaction.
Share
A unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder may receive dividends and may gain or lose money if its price changes.
Standard error
An estimate of how much a statistic would vary across repeated samples under the assumed sampling process.
Stochastic calculus
Mathematics for working with quantities that change continuously and randomly.
Sukuk
Islamic finance certificates linked to an underlying asset, use or investment. The contract determines holders’ rights and payments.
Swap
A contract to exchange payment streams, such as fixed interest for floating interest.
Synergy
Extra value expected from combining businesses, such as a saving attainable only after a merger.

T

Theoretical ex-rights price (TERP)
The model price per share after a rights issue, combining the old equity value with subscription cash and dividing by the new share count.

V

Value at risk (VaR)
For a chosen horizon and probability, a threshold in the loss distribution. Larger losses remain possible.
Volatility
How widely returns vary over a stated time interval.

W

Weighted average cost of capital (WACC)
A blend of the required returns on debt and equity, weighted by how much each supplies. It can be used to discount cash flows available to both.
Working capital
Money tied up in day-to-day operations. For a deal, agree which receivables, stock and payables count.

Y

Yield curve
The relationship between interest rates and time to maturity for a specified market and type of instrument.

Reading financial formulas

Each symbol below has a small example. The letters in a particular equation take their meaning from the definitions in that lecture.

Subscripts · S₀ and S₁
A small index names the time or item. If S₀ = €40 and S₁ = €42, the price moves from €40 at time 0 to €42 at time 1. A subscript i can label a company instead of a date.
Powers · (1 + r)²
The small 2 means multiply the factor by itself. Two years of 5% compound growth turn €100 into €100 × 1.05 × 1.05 = €110.25. Plain text also writes the power as ^2.
Basis points and percentage points
A rate rising from 4% to 5% rises by one percentage point, or 100 basis points. Its increase relative to the original rate is (5 − 4) ÷ 4 = 25%.
Summation · ∑
The symbol asks you to add the terms for the listed items. For weights of 0.6 and 0.4 and returns of 5% and 2%, ∑ wᵢrᵢ means 0.6 × 5% + 0.4 × 2% = 3.8%. A standalone capital sigma (Σ) can also name a covariance matrix in portfolio equations.
Expected value · E[X]
Multiply each possible outcome by its probability and add the results. With equal chances of €10 and €30, E[X] = €20. A single outcome can differ from the average.
Conditional probability · P(A | B)
Read the vertical bar as “given.” If four of 100 late-paying customers default, P(default | late payment) in that group is 4%. To find P(late payment | default), start with the group of customers who defaulted.
Mean, variance and standard deviation · μ, σ², σ
The mean is the average. Variance measures squared distances from that average. Its square root, the standard deviation, is back in the original units. If equally likely returns are 2% and 6%, the mean is 4% and the population standard deviation is 2 percentage points.
A change · Δ
ΔS commonly means the new asset price less the old price. A move from €50 to €53 gives ΔS = €3. In options, delta can also name a sensitivity. A delta of 0.6 means a €1 rise in the underlying predicts about a €0.60 rise in the option, for a small move.
A partial derivative · ∂V/∂S
Read it as “how quickly V changes as S moves, holding the other inputs fixed.” If a €0.10 increase in S raises V by about €0.04, the local sensitivity is about 0.4 per euro.
Transpose and multiplication · wᵀr
A transpose turns a column into a row. Multiplying that row by a column pairs their entries and adds the products. Weights (0.75, 0.25) multiplied this way by returns (4%, 8%) produce a portfolio return of 5%.
Natural logarithm · ln
The log return is ln(ending price ÷ starting price). From €100 to €105 it equals ln(1.05) ≈ 0.04879. Log returns add across successive periods. The ordinary percentage gain for this period is 5%.
Integral · ∫
An ordinary time integral adds a rate across small time intervals. A constant payment flow of €3 per month for four months gives €12. When the final term is dW instead of dt, the increments being accumulated are random. The stochastic-calculus lectures show how that changes the rules.
Time and random increments · dt, dW
Over a short interval Δt, a standard Brownian increment has mean zero and standard deviation √Δt. If a year is one time unit, a quarter-year increment has standard deviation 0.5. A drift term scales with elapsed time itself.
The choice that optimizes · arg min, arg max
If two bids of €10 and €13 cost the buyer €12 and €15 after fees, the minimum total cost is €12. The choice that achieves that minimum, the arg min, is the €10 bid.
A slope or exposure · β
In a regression a coefficient describes how the fitted outcome changes with one unit of its input, holding the other included inputs fixed. A market beta of 1.2 gives a fitted change of 1.2 percentage points for a one-point change in market return.
Approximately equal · ≈
The two numbers match to the precision shown. €100 ÷ 3 ≈ €33.33. Keep more digits while calculating, then round the final answer to the precision requested.
